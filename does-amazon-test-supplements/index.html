Does Amazon Test the Supplements It Sells?

What Amazon's lab rule checks and what it does not, what the FDA found when it bought supplements on Amazon, and why a fake could ship from a real brand's page.

Does Amazon test supplements before it sells them?

Quick answer: Amazon does not run the tests itself. It makes sellers use an outside lab that it approves.

In April 2024, Amazon changed its rules for supplement sellers. The trade site NutraIngredients reported that Amazon now wants test results sent "straight from the lab to Amazon."

Before that, a seller could upload its own lab report. Now Amazon is "no longer accepting CoAs directly from sellers," the site reported. A CoA, or certificate of analysis, is a lab's report on what it found in a product.

By January 2026, the rule covered every supplement seller. The law firm Epstein Becker Green wrote that Amazon was requiring "all sellers of dietary supplements" to show their products had been tested. The firm counted seven testing companies on Amazon's approved list.

One more detail matters. The firm wrote that Amazon will not take a company's own audit in place of the outside check. It will not take an FDA inspection either: "nor will Food and Drug Administration (FDA) inspections."

Sources: NutraIngredients, Amazon updates its dietary supplements seller's policy, April 5, 2024; Epstein Becker Green, Amazon's Third-Party Supplement Testing Mandate Explained, January 28, 2026, first published in SupplySide Supplement Journal. Amazon posts these rules on its site for sellers. The lines above come from the trade press and a law firm.

Which supplements get an extra test?

Five kinds get a stricter check: supplements for bodybuilding, joint health, sexual enhancement, sports nutrition and weight management. The law firm says these must also meet an outside standard called NSF/ANSI 173, or its equal.

That standard checks that a supplement "contains the ingredients claimed on the label." It also checks that it "does not contain specific undeclared contaminants."

Two of those five kinds are sexual enhancement and weight management. The FDA found hidden drugs in both kinds when it bought them on Amazon. More on that below.

Source: Epstein Becker Green, Amazon's Third-Party Supplement Testing Mandate Explained, January 28, 2026.

What does Amazon's lab check not tell you?

Quick answer: it is not a government review, and it is not a test of your bottle.

  • It is a store rule. Amazon sets it, and private labs carry it out. No government agency signs off first. Under the 1994 supplement law, the FDA says it "does not have the authority to approve dietary supplements before they are marketed."
  • It works product by product. Sellers set up a test for each product, NutraIngredients reported. Nothing in the rule, as reported, says every bottle that ships gets tested.
  • It does not prove a product is safe for you. Researchers who tested supplements bought on Amazon wrote that a third-party seal helps show the label matches what is inside. But they added that it "does not ensure a product's effectiveness or safety."
  • It does not tell you whose bottle you got. For years, that was a separate problem.

Sources: FDA, Questions and Answers on Dietary Supplements; Crawford and others, Analysis of Select Dietary Supplement Products Marketed to Support or Boost the Immune System, JAMA Network Open, 2022.

Can a fake bottle come from the real brand's Amazon page?

Quick answer: for years, it could. Amazon pooled the same product from different sellers in one bin.

Amazon called this commingling. It explained it in a post to sellers. Orders were filled with "exact product matches from the closest available inventory," Amazon wrote, "even if that inventory belonged to a different seller."

In plain words: you clicked the brand's own page. Amazon could ship the closest matching bottle it had. It might have come from a different seller.

Amazon said it would end this for stock shipped in on or after March 31, 2026. The trade site PPC Land reported that stock already in Amazon's warehouses was "grandfathered under the previous system." So a bottle from the old shared bin could still ship after that date.

PPC Land also wrote that fake units, expired products or items in damaged packaging could get into the old pool. Once there, they looked just like the real stock.

Sources: Amazon, Commingling practices will end effective March 31, 2026, post on Amazon's Seller Forums; PPC Land, Amazon ends FBA commingling on March 31, March 24, 2026.

What has the FDA found in supplements sold on Amazon?

Quick answer: hidden drugs, in products sold as supplements and shipped by Amazon.

On July 26, 2021, the FDA sent Amazon a letter. It said FDA staff bought 26 sexual enhancement products on Amazon between December 2019 and February 2020. FDA labs found hidden drug ingredients in all 26. They were the same drug ingredients used in prescription pills for erectile dysfunction. None was on the label. The letter says 25 of the 26 were sold as supplements. The other one was a coffee.

In March 2021, the FDA bought a weight loss product called "Miss Slim" on Amazon. Its lab found sibutramine, which was not on the label. Sibutramine was once a prescription diet drug. Its approval as a drug was withdrawn in 2010, after data showed a higher risk of heart attack and stroke, the FDA letter says.

The letter says these products were shipped through Fulfillment by Amazon. Amazon told the FDA it had "restricted" the first 26 products and their sellers. Then, in January 2021, the FDA bought one of the same products from the same seller again. The FDA wrote that it was "concerned that your filters are inadequate."

These are the FDA's lab findings and its own words. They came before Amazon's April 2024 lab rule.

What did researchers find in supplements bought on Amazon?

A study in JAMA Network Open in 2022 looked at 30 top-listed "immune" supplements bought on Amazon in May 2021. Seventeen of the 30 had labels that did not match the lab results. In 13, ingredients on the label were not found. In 9, the lab found things the label did not list.

The bottles were bought after Amazon began asking sellers for a lab report in December 2020. The authors also said their 30 products do not stand for everything sold on Amazon.

Sources: FDA, letter to Amazon.com, Inc., July 26, 2021; Crawford and others, JAMA Network Open study, 2022.

Common questions about Amazon and supplement testing

Does Amazon test every bottle of a supplement?

Nothing in Amazon's rule, as the trade press and a law firm describe it, says every bottle is tested. The rule makes sellers have each product checked by an outside lab that Amazon approves. That is a check on the product, not on each bottle that ships.

Does the FDA approve supplements before Amazon sells them?

No. The FDA says it "does not have the authority to approve dietary supplements before they are marketed." The companies that make and sell supplements are responsible for making sure their products follow the law.

Can I still get a fake from a brand's own Amazon page?

Amazon said it would end its shared bin for stock shipped in on or after March 31, 2026. But stock already in its warehouses before then was left under the old system, PPC Land reported. So a bottle from the old shared bin could still ship after that date. The product page looks the same either way.

Is a supplement that passed Amazon's lab check safe?

Not necessarily. Amazon's check is a store rule carried out by private labs, not a government safety review. Researchers who tested supplements bought on Amazon wrote that a third-party seal "does not ensure a product's effectiveness or safety."

Sources for these answers: Epstein Becker Green (2026); FDA, Questions and Answers on Dietary Supplements; PPC Land (2026); JAMA Network Open study (2022).

What should I do instead?

Quick answer: buy from the company that makes it, not from a middleman.

Start with the bottles you take every day. Those are the ones that matter most.

Buy them straight from the company that makes them. That way your bottle does not pass through a shared bin with a stranger's stock. One maker. One chain from start to finish. One name to call if something goes wrong.

Ask the maker who tests its products and what the tests look for. If a maker will not tell you, that is your sign to switch to one that will.
