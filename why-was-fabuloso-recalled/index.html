Why Was Fabuloso Recalled?

What the government notice said, what the company says went wrong, how the buyers' court case ended, and what to keep under your sink instead.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years. Published October 16, 2026.

Fabuloso was recalled on February 8, 2023, because some bottles could contain Pseudomonas bacteria, germs found in soil and water. The company says a preservative was not added at the intended levels during manufacturing. The recall covered about 4.9 million bottles made from December 14, 2022 to January 23, 2023. Buyers sued, and in April 2024 a federal judge approved a class action settlement without ruling on whether their claims were true.

The 2023 Fabuloso recall was about what could grow inside the bottle, not what was on your floor.

Why was Fabuloso recalled?

Quick answer: some bottles could carry germs, and the company says it was because a preservative came up short.

On February 8, 2023, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) posted the recall. Colgate-Palmolive, the company that makes Fabuloso, recalled "About 4.9 million (In addition, about 56,000 in Canada)" multi-purpose cleaners.

The notice says the bottles "can contain Pseudomonas species bacteria, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Pseudomonas fluorescens, which are environmental organisms found widely in soil and water."

So why were germs in a cleaner? The company answers that on its own recall page. It says the recall happened "because a preservative was not added at the intended levels during manufacturing." It adds: "With inadequate preservative, there is a risk of bacteria growth in the recalled products."

A preservative is what keeps germs from growing in a bottle of cleaner. The company says the recalled bottles were made from December 14, 2022 through January 23, 2023, "when the manufacturing issue was corrected."

Sources: CPSC, Colgate-Palmolive Recalls Fabuloso Multi-Purpose Cleaners Due to Risk of Exposure to Bacteria, recall 23-115, February 8, 2023; Colgate-Palmolive, Fabuloso recall page.

Who could the germs hurt?

Quick answer: mainly people with weak immune systems, lung problems or certain medical devices, the notice says.

The CPSC says people "with weakened immune systems, external medical devices, or underlying lung conditions who are exposed to the bacteria face a risk of serious infection that may require medical treatment."

It says the germs "can enter the body if inhaled, through the eyes, or through a break in the skin." Think about wiping a counter with a small cut on your hand.

Here is the other side, straight. The same notice says "People with healthy immune systems are usually not affected by the bacteria." And under injuries it says "None reported."

Which Fabuloso bottles were recalled?

Quick answer: five scents, many sizes, all made in a window of about six weeks.

  • Scents: Lavender, Refreshing Lemon, Passion of Fruits, Spring Fresh and Ocean.
  • Lot codes: 2348US78 through 2365US78, and 3001US78 through 3023US78.
  • Where they sold: Amazon and other websites, and Dollar General, Family Dollar, The Home Depot, Sam's Club, Walmart and other stores, from December 2022 through January 2023, for $1 to $11.
  • Not included: "No Fabuloso Antibacterial variants or other Fabuloso products are impacted by this recall," the notice says.

The company also says "Some 3.9 million of these bottles," about 80 percent, "were never released for sale."

Was there a Fabuloso lawsuit?

Quick answer: yes. Buyers sued, and the case ended in a settlement, not a verdict.

One day after the recall, on February 9, 2023, a buyer sued Colgate-Palmolive in federal court in New York. Her complaint alleged that the packaging did not tell buyers the cleaner could contain the germ. It alleged that cleaners with the germ "are in no way safe to use for cleaning and are entirely worthless." These were allegations, not findings.

Two other suits, one more in New York and one in North Carolina, were folded into the same deal. On November 14, 2023, the judge gave early approval to one settlement. It covered the claims about lost money in all three cases.

On April 25, 2024, Judge Vincent L. Briccetti signed the final approval. His order says "There have been no objections to the Settlement or any of its terms." He weighed "the risks of establishing liability and damages." And he approved "the dismissal with prejudice of the claims asserted against Defendants."

So the judge approved a deal. He did not decide whether the buyers' claims were true. The dollar amount is set in the settlement agreement. I could not find a free public copy of it, so I do not give a number here.

Sources: Patora v. Colgate-Palmolive Co., U.S. District Court, Southern District of New York, No. 7:23-cv-01118-VB: Class Action Complaint, filed February 9, 2023; Order Granting Preliminary Approval, November 14, 2023; Order Granting Plaintiffs' Motion for Final Approval of Class Action Settlement, April 25, 2024.

Is Fabuloso safe to use now?

Quick answer: a recall cannot answer that. It only tells you what was pulled.

The company says the problem was fixed on January 23, 2023. A search of the CPSC recall database on October 16, 2026 lists one Fabuloso recall: this one, from February 2023.

But a recall is a record of one thing that went wrong. It is not a promise about the next bottle. And a product that has never been recalled has not been stamped safe by anyone.

What were people told to do with a recalled bottle?

The CPSC told people to "immediately stop using" the recalled cleaners and contact the company for a refund or a free replacement. They were to take a picture of the UPC and lot code, then "dispose of the product in its container with household trash." And one more line: "Do not empty the product prior to disposal."

Think about that. You were told not to pour your own floor cleaner down your own drain.

What can I use instead of Fabuloso?

Quick answer: one simple cleaner, mixed fresh, from a maker who tells you what is in it and picks up the phone.

  • One cleaner, not six. One concentrated all-purpose cleaner you mix with water can do floors, counters and the bathroom.
  • Mix it fresh. Make only what you need for that job in a clean bucket or bottle. Pour out what is left when you finish.
  • Dry the mop. Rinse the mop head and let it dry all the way before you put it away.
  • Smell is not clean. A strong scent tells you nothing about what is growing in the bottle. Pick a cleaner that does not need a big perfume to feel like it worked.
  • Know who made it. Pick a maker that lists everything in the bottle on its website and has a real person who answers the phone. If something goes wrong, you want to hear fast.

The Antibacterial Fabuloso was not part of the recall. That does not mean you need a germ-killing spray for every job. Before you switch to one, see Are quats in cleaners safe?

Common questions about the Fabuloso recall

Why was Fabuloso recalled in 2023?

Some bottles could contain Pseudomonas bacteria, germs found in soil and water. Colgate-Palmolive says a preservative was not added at the intended levels during manufacturing, so germs could grow in the bottle. The CPSC announced the recall on February 8, 2023.

How many Fabuloso bottles were recalled?

About 4.9 million in the United States and about 56,000 in Canada, the CPSC says. The company says the bottles were made from December 14, 2022 through January 23, 2023, and that about 3.9 million of them were never released for sale.

Did anyone get hurt from the recalled Fabuloso?

The CPSC notice lists injuries as "None reported." It says people with weakened immune systems, external medical devices, or lung conditions who were exposed faced a risk of serious infection. It also says people with healthy immune systems are usually not affected.

Was there a Fabuloso class action settlement?

Yes. Buyers sued in 2023. On April 25, 2024, a federal judge in New York gave final approval to a class action settlement and wrote that there were no objections. The judge approved a deal. He did not rule on whether the buyers' claims were true.

Sources for these answers: CPSC recall notice (2023); Fabuloso recall page; Order Granting Final Approval (2024).
