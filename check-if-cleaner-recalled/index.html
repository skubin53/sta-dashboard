How Do I Find Out If a Cleaner I Own Was Recalled?

Where to look, how to get recall emails, what a recall notice tells you to do, and what a recall list can never tell you.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years. Published November 18, 2026.

Search the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission's recall list at cpsc.gov/Recalls by brand or product name. To hear about new recalls, use the same page's link to subscribe for recall emails. If your bottle is on the list, stop using it and follow the steps in its notice.

A recall list only shows the problems someone has already found. A bottle that is not on it has not been cleared.

Where do I check if a cleaner was recalled?

Quick answer: the CPSC recall search at cpsc.gov/Recalls.

The CPSC is the U.S. agency that handles recalls of household products, like the sprays under your sink.

When I read its page on November 18, 2026, it was headed "Recalls & Product Safety Warnings." It has a search box called "Search Recalls & Product Safety Warnings." You can narrow your search with "Date from," "Date to," "Hazard" and "Category."

Here is how to use it:

  1. Pull out the cleaners you use most. Start with the ones you spray close to your face.
  2. Type the brand name into the search box, one at a time.
  3. If a notice comes up, open it and check that the product and size match yours.
  4. Write down what you checked and the date, so you know where you left off.

Source: U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, Recalls & Product Safety Warnings, read November 18, 2026. The CPSC can change how this page looks.

Can I get recall alerts by email?

Quick answer: yes, from the CPSC.

The CPSC recall page says "Never miss a recall" and links to "subscribe for recall emails." Each recall notice also has a link called "Choose Your Recall Emails." Both go to the CPSC subscription page.

One thing to know. Emails tell you about new recalls. They do not look back at the bottles already in your cabinet. So search once for what you own, then let the emails watch for you.

Sources: CPSC, Recalls & Product Safety Warnings, and recall 26-201, both read November 18, 2026.

What does a recall notice tell me to do?

Quick answer: stop using it, get the refund the way the notice says, and throw it out the way the notice says.

Here is a real one. On January 22, 2026, the CPSC posted recall 26-201 for about 1,500,000 bottles of Angry Orange Enzyme Stain Remover, sold by Thrasio. The notice says the bottles "can contain bacteria, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa." It lists injuries as "None reported."

The notice told buyers to:

  • Stop using it right away.
  • Mark the bottle. Write "recalled" and your initials on it in marker, take a photo, and email it to the company for a refund.
  • Throw it out whole. Put it in the household trash in its bottle. "Do not empty the product prior to disposal." "Do not attempt to recycle the product bottle."

Every notice has its own steps. This one also lists the company's own recall page and phone line, so you can ask the company directly.

Source: CPSC, Thrasio Recalls Angry Orange Enzyme Stain Removers Due to Risk of Exposure to Bacteria, recall 26-201, January 22, 2026, read November 18, 2026.

What can a recall list not tell me?

Quick answer: it cannot tell you a bottle is safe.

A recall is posted after a problem is found. A product that is not on the list may just not have been looked at. The recalled Angry Orange spray was sold from March 2019 through December 2025, the CPSC notice says.

What do I use instead?

Quick answer: a spray from a company that makes its own.

Look for a cleaner where the name on the front is the company that made it. When the seller is the maker, you know who stands behind what is in the bottle.

Start with one bottle. Pick the spray you use closest to your face, and swap that one first.

Common questions about cleaner recalls

How do I find out if a cleaner I already own was recalled?

Go to the CPSC recall list at cpsc.gov/Recalls. Type the brand or product name into the search box. You can narrow it by date, hazard and category. If you find a match, open the notice and follow its steps. If you find nothing, that only means no recall has been posted. It does not mean the bottle was checked.

Can I get an email when a cleaner is recalled?

Yes. The CPSC recall page says "Never miss a recall" and links to "subscribe for recall emails." Each recall notice also has a "Choose Your Recall Emails" link. The emails cover new recalls, so search the list once for the bottles you already have.

What should I do if my cleaner is on a recall list?

Stop using it and do what the notice says. Each notice has its own steps. The Angry Orange notice said to mark the bottle "recalled" with your initials, send a photo for a refund, and throw it out in its container. It said, "Do not empty the product prior to disposal."

Where do I report a cleaner that hurt me or made me sick?

The CPSC points you to SaferProducts.gov. The Angry Orange recall notice says, "Report a dangerous product or product-related injury on www.SaferProducts.gov." You can also search that site for reports other people have filed.

Sources for these answers: CPSC, Recalls & Product Safety Warnings; CPSC, recall 26-201; SaferProducts.gov; all read November 18, 2026.

What can I do this week?

Pick the three cleaners you use most. Search each one at cpsc.gov/Recalls. Then sign up for the recall emails so the next one comes to you.
