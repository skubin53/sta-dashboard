Is the Aluminum in Antiperspirant Dangerous?

The honest answer to the breast cancer rumor, the real reasons women switch, and the cleaner deodorant swap.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping over 100 women switch to safer, American made home and personal care products.

The viral claim that the aluminum in antiperspirant causes breast cancer is not supported by the research, and you deserve the honest version instead of a scare. The real reasons a lot of women move to a natural deodorant are added fragrance and skin irritation, not cancer. Here is what the science actually says, and the cleaner swap if you want one.

Does antiperspirant cause breast cancer?

No, the evidence does not support it. A case-control study published in the Journal of the National Cancer Institute in 2002 compared 813 women with breast cancer to 793 women without it and found no increased risk from antiperspirant or deodorant use, including using it soon after shaving. The National Cancer Institute and the American Cancer Society both say there is no conclusive research linking antiperspirant use to breast cancer. The rumor spread online long before there was ever a study behind it. The related claim that antiperspirant causes Alzheimer's is also unproven.

Source: Mirick, Davis, Thomas, "Antiperspirant Use and the Risk of Breast Cancer," Journal of the National Cancer Institute (2002). Read the study.

The scary version of this story travels faster than the science. The honest reason to switch is not cancer, it is what you want to put on your skin every day.

So why do so many women switch anyway?

Because the real reasons hold up even when the cancer scare does not. The first is added "fragrance," a single word that can legally stand in for dozens of undisclosed ingredients, applied to a sensitive area every single day. The second is plain skin irritation, which is common under the arms. The third is simple preference: a lot of people just want a short, readable ingredient list on something they use daily. None of that requires a rumor to be a good enough reason.

What is the difference between deodorant and antiperspirant?

They are not the same thing. Deodorant masks or neutralizes odor. Antiperspirant uses aluminum salts to temporarily plug your sweat glands so you sweat less. If your goal is to skip the aluminum, what you actually want is a deodorant, not an antiperspirant. Sweat itself is odorless, so for many people a good deodorant is all they needed.

What should I look for in a safer deodorant?

  • Fragrance-free or naturally scented. This is the change most people can feel, and it removes the undisclosed-ingredient question entirely.
  • A short ingredient list you can actually read. Fewer synthetic actives, nothing you cannot pronounce or look up.
  • American made. A domestic supply chain and a clear label, and concentrated formats that come out cheaper per use than the drugstore names.

Give a new formula a couple of weeks. Your skin goes through a short adjustment when you stop plugging your sweat glands, and most people settle in quickly.
