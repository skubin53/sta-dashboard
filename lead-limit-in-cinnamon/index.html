Is There a Legal Limit for Lead in Cinnamon?

Who draws the line for lead in cinnamon, where the lines sit, how much is too much, and what to buy instead.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years. Published October 19, 2026.

No, there is no US federal limit for lead in cinnamon, and New York's agriculture department says so too. New York uses 1 part per million as its line to ask for a recall, and a new world standard sets 2.5 for cinnamon. Those are lines for taking action, not a promise that a jar is safe.

A cinnamon jar can test under the new world line for lead and still land on the FDA's throw-it-out list.

Is there a federal limit for lead in cinnamon?

Quick answer: no. Not for cinnamon, and not for any spice.

New York's agriculture department said so in June 2026. It set new spice rules "in the absence of finalized, specific federal limits for lead in spices." As of October 2026, I found no federal limit finalized since.

The FDA's cinnamon alert says the jars on its list have "elevated levels of lead." It names no cutoff. The FDA's main page on lead in food lists a lead limit for bottled water, 5 parts per billion. It lists none for spices.

That does not mean the FDA cannot act. In March 2024 it said it "does not need action levels or guidance to take action when the level of a contaminant, such as lead, is unsafe." So it works case by case. When tests find high lead, it asks the company to recall those jars.

Sources: New York State Department of Agriculture and Markets, New Action Levels for Heavy Metals in Spices, June 5, 2026; FDA, public health alert on ground cinnamon, content current as of September 28, 2026; FDA, Lead in Food and Foodwares; FDA, FDA Takes Steps to Ensure Safety of Cinnamon Products Sold in the US, March 6, 2024.

Who draws the line for lead in cinnamon?

Quick answer: New York has a recall line. A world food body has a new standard. The US government has no finalized limit for spices.

These lines are counted in parts per million. That means parts of lead in every million parts of cinnamon. A level of 2.5 milligrams per kilogram is the same as 2.5 parts per million.

What is New York's line for lead in cinnamon?

Since 2016, New York has used 1 part per million of lead as its line to ask a company to recall a spice. Its line for the most serious kind of recall is 25.

In June 2026, the state lowered the line to 0.6 for seven spices: curry, dill seed, dried basil leaves, dried non-bell pepper, nutmeg, sesame seed and turmeric. That starts January 1, 2028. Cinnamon is not one of the seven.

The state says "Sampling and assessment of additional spices, such as cinnamon and ginger, will continue." Its long-term goal is 0.21 parts per million.

What is the new world line for lead in cinnamon?

Codex is the United Nations food standards body. At its meeting of November 10 to 14, 2025, it adopted a maximum level of "2.5 mg/kg for lead in spices, dried bark." The UN's food agency, the FAO, names dried bark as cinnamon.

Codex is not US law. Codex says its texts "are not mandatory unless they are adopted by or incorporated into national food legislation."

Sources: New York State Department of Agriculture and Markets, June 5, 2026; FAO, 48th Session of the Codex Alimentarius Commission adopts new standards, November 2025; Codex Alimentarius, About Codex.

How much lead is too much?

Quick answer: no safe level of lead has been found. The lines above tell officials when to act. They do not tell you how much is safe.

The FDA says it plainly: "no safe level for lead exposure has been identified."

Lead can be hard to notice. Galil Importing Corp recalled one lot of Lior cinnamon in September 2026. Its own notice says "increased blood lead levels may be the only apparent sign of lead exposure."

Time matters too. The FDA's cinnamon alert warns that eating elevated lead over "months to years" could add to health problems. The FAO lists harms of lead that include kidney trouble, high blood pressure and heart and blood vessel disease.

Cinnamon goes on oatmeal and coffee every morning, and into pies all fall. That is why the jar you use every day matters more than the one you use once a year.

Sources: FDA, Lead in Food and Foodwares; Galil Importing Corp, recall notice for Lior Cinnamon Ground Seasoning, September 2026; FDA, public health alert on ground cinnamon; FAO release on the 48th Codex session.

What do the cinnamon recall numbers mean?

Quick answer: every jar the FDA named tested at more than twice New York's line. Some still tested under the new world line.

The FDA has named 24 cinnamon brands in three lead alerts since March 2024. The jars tested from 2.03 to 20 parts per million.

The top number, 20, came from one El Servidor jar. New York's agriculture department tested it, the FDA said in July 2024.

The newest brand is Lior Gourmet Spices, added on September 28, 2026. Maryland's health department collected the sample. The FDA lists it at 2.18 parts per million. That is under the new world line of 2.5. The FDA still told people "to throw away and not to buy" the jars on its list.

So a jar can sit under one line and still get pulled. That is why a limit is not a safety promise.

What should I buy instead?

Quick answer: whole cinnamon sticks from a seller who can tell you where they grew and whether they were tested for lead.

  1. Pull every cinnamon jar out tonight. The FDA says these jars have "a long shelf life," so an old one may still be in your cupboard.
  2. If the brand is on the FDA list, throw it out. I would not hunt for the lot code.
  3. Buy whole sticks from a person you can ask. A farmers market seller is a good start. Ask where the sticks grew and who packed them.
  4. Ask if they test for lead, and if they will show you. If they cannot answer, keep walking.
  5. Grate the sticks yourself. A small grater does it in a minute.

The list does not tell you that. It only names jars that state and federal labs tested and found high in lead. The FDA says no safe level for lead exposure has been identified. For your next jar, buy from someone who can tell you where it grew and whether it was tested for lead.
