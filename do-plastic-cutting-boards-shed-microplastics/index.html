Do Plastic Cutting Boards Shed Microplastics?

What did the 2023 study find?

Quick answer: chopping wears tiny bits of plastic off the board, and some of it ends up with the food.

The team at North Dakota State University chopped on two common board plastics, polyethylene and polypropylene, and measured what came off. The longer the chopping went on, the more the way people chop made a difference.

Polypropylene boards gave off more than polyethylene boards: 5 to 60 percent more by weight and 14 to 71 percent more by count. Most of the pieces were smaller than 100 micrometers.

Chopping carrots on a polyethylene board released more than chopping on it without carrots. Real food changes how the board wears.

From that, they estimated a person could take in 7.4 to 50.7 grams of plastic a year from a polyethylene board, and 49.5 grams from a polypropylene board.

Source: Yadav and colleagues, Cutting Boards: An Overlooked Source of Microplastics in Human Food?, Environmental Science and Technology (2023), 57(22):8225 to 8235. Also listed on the University of Southern Denmark research portal.

What the study does not tell you

The authors say the yearly numbers are "based on our assumptions" about how people chop. Their own early lab test of the polyethylene pieces found no harm to mouse cells over 72 hours. So the study shows the plastic comes off. It does not show what that plastic does in your body.

Are microplastics in food harmful?

Quick answer: nobody knows yet, and that is the honest answer.

The World Health Organization said in 2019 that, based on the limited information it had, microplastics in drinking water did not appear to pose a health risk at current levels. It also said more research was needed.

In 2022 it published a wider review of microplastics in food, water and air. It reviewed the data up to the end of 2021 and listed the research still needed to settle the open questions.

You do not have to wait for that research to make an easy change in your own kitchen.

What is the simple swap?

Quick answer: chop on wood or bamboo every day.

The study tested plastic boards. A board that is not plastic cannot shed plastic. Pick one good wood or bamboo board you like using, and make it the one you reach for.

Wash it by hand and let it dry standing up. Oil it now and then so it lasts for years.
