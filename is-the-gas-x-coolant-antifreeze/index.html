Is the Gas-X Coolant Antifreeze?

What leaked into four lots of Gas-X softgels, what propylene glycol is, what Class I means, and what to do if your box matches.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years. Published November 4, 2026.

Haleon, the company behind Gas-X, calls it a "diluted propylene glycol-based coolant" that leaked from a packing machine. It does not use the word antifreeze. But a federal health agency, the Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry, says propylene glycol "is used to make antifreeze and deicing solutions for cars, airplanes, and boats." So in plain words, the coolant was made with a chemical that is used as antifreeze. Four lots of softgels were pulled over the chance it got into them.

The coolant in the Gas-X recall was propylene glycol-based, and propylene glycol is a chemical used to make antifreeze.

What leaked into the Gas-X softgels?

Quick answer: Haleon says a packing machine leaked a "diluted propylene glycol-based coolant."

Haleon posted its recall on June 4, 2026. It covers four lots of Gas-X Extra Strength Softgels, 125 mg. Those are the green softgels in the box with the green band.

Haleon says the lots were recalled "due to potential contamination with a diluted propylene glycol-based coolant from a machine leakage during the packaging process."

  • 120 count box: lots TL8K, YH9X and YH9Y
  • 72 count box: lot X78N
  • All four expire: November 30, 2028

The federal recall record, D-0595-2026, gives the reason as "Chemical Contamination." It counts 35,883 boxes, sent out nationwide.

Note the word "potential." Haleon does not say how much coolant, if any, reached a softgel. It also does not say what else was in the coolant, or how diluted it was.

Sources: Haleon, company recall announcement, June 4, 2026, as posted by the FDA. The FDA says it posts these "as a public service" and "does not endorse either the product or the company." FDA enforcement record D-0595-2026.

Is the coolant in the Gas-X recall antifreeze?

Quick answer: Haleon does not call it antifreeze. But propylene glycol is used as antifreeze.

Neither Haleon's notice nor the federal record uses the word antifreeze. They both say "propylene glycol-based coolant." Neither one says more about what it was.

The federal Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry, or ATSDR, tells us what propylene glycol is used for. The first key point on its fact sheet says: "Propylene glycol is a clear liquid used in antifreeze and deicing solutions."

Its longer health statement adds one more line. Propylene glycol "is used by the chemical, food, and pharmaceutical industries as an antifreeze when leakage might lead to contact with food."

So some industries pick it on purpose, as an antifreeze, for places where a leak could touch food. Haleon has not said why its packing machine used it.

Sources: ATSDR, ToxFAQs for Propylene Glycol; ATSDR, Public Health Statement for Propylene Glycol.

What is propylene glycol?

Quick answer: a clear liquid used in antifreeze and deicing, and also used to hold moisture in some medicines, cosmetics and foods.

ATSDR says it is a "clear, colorless, slightly syrupy" liquid at room temperature. It says it is "practically odorless and tasteless."

It has a lot of jobs. ATSDR lists these:

  • Making antifreeze and deicing solutions for cars, airplanes and boats
  • Making polyester compounds
  • As a solvent in the paint and plastics industries
  • To "absorb extra water and maintain moisture in certain medicines, cosmetics, or food products"
  • As a solvent for food colors and flavors

If it is in some medicines, why was Gas-X recalled?

Because this coolant was not meant to be there. Haleon says it came from a machine leak while the softgels were being packed. The federal record calls it "Chemical Contamination."

Haleon's notice says swallowing a softgel with the coolant in it "may result in adverse events such as nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain and diarrhea." The same notice says, "To date, Haleon has not received any reports of adverse events related to this recall."

What does Class I mean for the Gas-X recall?

Quick answer: it is the most serious of the FDA's three recall classes.

The FDA ranked this recall Class I on June 17, 2026. The FDA uses Class I when there is "a reasonable probability that the use of or exposure to a violative product will cause serious adverse health consequences or death."

That is a ranking of the risk. It is not a report that anyone got sick. Haleon says it has had no reports of that.

Sources: FDA, Recalls Background and Definitions; FDA enforcement record D-0595-2026, classified June 17, 2026.

What should I do if I have Gas-X at home?

Quick answer: match the lot. If it matches, stop taking it and call Haleon.

  1. Find the lot number on your box. Hold it next to TL8K, YH9X, YH9Y and X78N.
  2. If it matches, stop. Haleon's notice says to "stop taking the product immediately."
  3. Call Haleon at 1-800-245-1040. The line is open Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern. Ask them to set up the return and pay you back.
  4. If you felt sick after taking it, the notice says to contact your doctor. Report it to Haleon at the same number, too.

Haleon says to check if you bought Gas-X Extra Strength Softgels on or after April 13, 2026. Do it before the next big holiday meal.
