Is BPA Banned in Food Cans?

Where the US and Europe stand, what "BPA free" on a can really tells you, and the simple swap I made in my own kitchen.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years. Published October 9, 2026.

Is BPA banned in food cans in the US?

Quick answer: no. The FDA still allows BPA in the lining of food cans.

BPA is a chemical used to make some hard plastics and resins. The FDA's own page says BPA "is also found in epoxy resins, which act as a protective lining on the inside of some metal-based food and beverage cans."

The FDA also says BPA "has been used in food packaging since the 1960s."

The lining does a real job. The FDA says BPA-based liners in cans "form a barrier between the food and the can surface that prevents corrosion of the can and migration of metal into the food." It also says "very small amounts may migrate from the food packaging into foods or beverages."

So the question is not whether a can needs a lining. It is what the lining is made of.

Sources: FDA, Bisphenol A (BPA): Use in Food Contact Application; FDA, Questions and Answers on BPA Use in Food Contact Applications.

Didn't the FDA ban BPA back in 2012?

Not in cans, and not for safety reasons. In 2012 and 2013, the FDA granted two requests to stop allowing certain BPA materials "in baby bottles, sippy cups, and infant formula packaging because these uses have been abandoned."

Abandoned means makers had already stopped using it there. The FDA says plainly that a change like this "is not based on safety."

Food cans were not on that list.

Source: FDA, Questions and Answers on BPA Use in Food Contact Applications.

Is anyone asking the FDA to take BPA out of cans?

Yes. On May 2, 2022, the FDA filed a petition from health, consumer and environmental groups and scientists. It asks the FDA to "remove or restrict authorizations for the use of bisphenol A (BPA)." The groups behind it say it asks the FDA to revoke approvals for BPA in "adhesives and can coatings."

The FDA's own petition list was last updated on August 19, 2026. It still shows the status as "under review." The FDA says it will publish its answer in the Federal Register "once we have completed our review."

Sources: Federal Register, Filing of Food Additive Petition, July 11, 2022; FDA, Petitions Under Review, FAP 2B4831; FDA, Bisphenol A (BPA); Environmental Defense Fund, January 12, 2026.

Did Europe ban BPA in food cans?

Quick answer: yes. The ban started January 20, 2025, with time for makers to switch.

On December 19, 2024, the European Commission adopted a ban on BPA in food contact materials "due to its potentially harmful health impact." It said the ban covers things "such as the coating on metal cans."

The rule is Commission Regulation (EU) 2024/3190. It covers BPA, its salts and "other hazardous bisphenols," not just BPA.

Why did Europe act? The Commission says the ban "takes into account the latest scientific assessment from the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA)." In the Commission's words, "EFSA notably concluded that BPA had potentially harmful effects on the immune system."

Europe gave makers time. Most products had 18 months. That main deadline passed on July 20, 2026. Some cans used for fruit, vegetables and fish have until 2028. And cans made under the old rules may still be filled for up to 12 months, then sold until they run out.

Sources: European Commission, Commission adopts ban of Bisphenol A in food contact materials, December 19, 2024; European Commission Access2Markets, EU prohibition on the use and trade of Bisphenol A from 20 January 2025; Food Packaging Forum, EU BPA ban reaches main transition deadline, July 20, 2026.

Does "BPA free" on a can mean it is safe?

Quick answer: it means one chemical is not in the lining. The can still has a lining.

"BPA free" tells you what is not in the coating. It does not tell you what is.

California's environmental health office wrote a report on BPS in October 2023. It says BPS "is in protective coatings inside some food cans." BPS is a close cousin of BPA. The report also says that after limits on BPA, "manufacturers have been gradually replacing BPA with BPS and other analogs." It says that about products in general, not cans alone.

Since then, California has put BPS on its Proposition 65 list for three kinds of harm, on three dates:

  • December 29, 2023, for female reproductive toxicity
  • January 3, 2025, for male reproductive toxicity
  • December 8, 2025, for developmental toxicity

A listing is a warning rule, not a ban. Under Proposition 65, businesses must give a "clear and reasonable" warning before knowingly exposing people to a listed chemical above a set level.

Is there another side to this?

Yes, and you should hear it. California's same report quotes a Canadian study that tested meat samples from 2008 to 2020. It found no trend in BPS levels over those 13 years. The authors wrote that this "does not support the speculation that BPS is being used to replace BPA in food packaging."

So the science on BPS in cans is not settled. But a "BPA free" can still has a lining, and those words on the front do not tell you what it is made of.

Sources: California Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment (OEHHA), Evidence on the Female Reproductive Toxicity of Bisphenol S, October 2023; OEHHA, Notice of listing, December 8, 2025, which also gives the 2023 and January 2025 dates; OEHHA, Proposition 65 Fact Sheet.

What can I use instead of canned food?

Quick answer: change the two or three cans you open most. Leave the rest for now.

  • Beans: buy them dry. Soak them overnight and cook a big pot.
  • Tomatoes: buy them in glass jars. Glass has no lining. The metal lid can still have a coating, so this is a smaller step, not a perfect one.
  • Everything else: leave it for now. You do not have to empty your pantry today.

Common questions about BPA in food cans

Is BPA banned in food cans in the United States?

No. The FDA's 2012 and 2013 changes did not cover food cans. In 2022, health, consumer and environmental groups and scientists asked the FDA, in a formal petition, to remove or restrict its approvals for BPA. The FDA's petition list still shows that petition as "under review."

When did Europe ban BPA in food cans?

The European Union's ban applies from January 20, 2025. Most products had until July 20, 2026 to switch. Some cans used for fruit, vegetables and fish have until 2028.

Does "BPA free" mean the can has no lining?

No. It means BPA is not in the lining. The can still has a coating inside. California's environmental health office says BPS, a close cousin of BPA, "is in protective coatings inside some food cans."

Is BPS banned in California?

No. California's Proposition 65 list names BPS for three kinds of harm. They were added in December 2023, January 2025 and December 2025. A listing means businesses must give a warning before knowingly exposing people above a set level. It is not a ban.

Sources for these answers: FDA, Questions and Answers on BPA; FDA, FAP 2B4831; European Commission Access2Markets; OEHHA, October 2023; OEHHA, December 8, 2025.

What can I do this week?

Open your pantry. Find the two cans you buy most. Next time you shop, buy one of them dry or in glass instead.
