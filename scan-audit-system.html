THE ONE RULE THAT GOVERNS THIS ENTIRE SYSTEM:

The buyer conversion path is a PHONE CALL (the shopping audit). There is no link to send. There is no store URL to share. Shannon's words, verbatim: "We never send a link. There is no link to send. We do shopping audits."

Every step in this system exists to get a warm, ready person onto a phone call with Shannon or Stacy.

What This System Is

Switch to America runs a shopper funnel that converts warm leads into enrolled members of the private membership store (Melaleuca, which is never named publicly). This funnel has five steps. Each step prepares the person for the next. The final step is a personal phone call — a shopping audit — where Shannon or Stacy walks them through swapping their home products and enrolls them.

Cameron's job in this system is to move leads through the steps, book the phone calls, and keep Shannon's calendar filled — without Shannon having to manage it manually.

Section 12: How Cloudflare Fits In

Cloudflare sits between the internet and Shannon's websites. Think of it as a traffic cop and a speed layer. It does not host the content — it caches and routes it. Understanding this explains why changes sometimes take a few minutes to appear and why certain domains can't be touched without going through Cloudflare first.

scan.ismyhometoxic.com (The Scan App)

Hosted on: GitHub Pages (the sta-dashboard repo at github.com/skubin53/sta-dashboard)

Cloudflare's role: Cloudflare sits in front of GitHub Pages as a CDN (content delivery network) and proxy. When a visitor loads the scan app, they're actually hitting Cloudflare's servers first. Cloudflare serves a cached copy of the page.

Why this matters for editing: When Cameron pushes a change to GitHub, it does NOT show up instantly for visitors. Cloudflare's cache has a TTL (time-to-live) of approximately 10 minutes (max-age=600). This is why after a push it can take 1-10 minutes before the live site reflects the change. This is normal and expected — not a bug.

Important clarification: scan.ismyhometoxic.com is GitHub Pages with Cloudflare in front — it is NOT Cloudflare Pages. There is a dead folder called scorecard-cloudflare/ in the workspace that was an old experiment. Ignore it.

shift.theshannonnicole.com (The $11 Tripwire / Quiz Server)

Hosted on: The Mac Mini in Scottsdale, exposed to the internet via a Cloudflare Tunnel

Cloudflare's role: A Cloudflare Tunnel (DNS CNAME pointing to cfargotunnel) creates a secure connection from the Mac Mini's quiz-server to the internet without opening firewall ports. The URL shift.theshannonnicole.com routes through this tunnel directly to the Node.js quiz-server running on the Mac Mini.

What lives here: The $11 Shift tripwire checkout, the Stripe webhook, the quiz-server, The Shift access page, the builder quiz.

theshannonnicole.com (The Apex Domain — Locked)

The problem: GHL (GoHighLevel) has the bare apex domain theshannonnicole.com registered as a Cloudflare-for-SaaS Custom Hostname. This lives at Cloudflare's EDGE layer — meaning DNS changes alone cannot override it. GHL owns that hostname at the infrastructure level.

What this means practically: The $11 tripwire cannot live at theshannonnicole.com/the-shift. It has to live at shift.theshannonnicole.com. To move it to the apex, either GHL support would need to release the custom hostname, or Shannon would need to move her GHL funnel's primary domain off the apex (e.g. to home.theshannonnicole.com). Until that changes, shift.theshannonnicole.com is the correct URL and should not be changed.

Section 13: All the Follow-Up Conversations — Texts, Voice Notes, and How Everything Gets Into GHL

Every time a lead touches the scan funnel, information flows automatically into her GHL contact record. And after the scan, a sequence of follow-up touchpoints fires — some automated, some requiring Shannon's approval, none going out without the right context. Here is exactly how it all works.

Step 1: What Gets Written to GHL the Moment Someone Completes the Scan

The moment a lead submits her scorecard answers, the quiz-server on the Mac Mini fires two writes to GHL simultaneously:

Write 1: The Scorecard Note (appears in the contact's Notes tab in GHL)

A structured note is posted to the contact's GHL record. It contains:

  • Timestamp of the scan
  • Her total Toxic Home Score (e.g. 67/100)
  • Her letter grade and zone (Red Zone / Caution Zone / Clean Zone)
  • Her top flagged room
  • The specific products she flagged in each room
  • Her pain text — her own words for what bothers her most (this is what drives the follow-up SMS template)
  • Whether she received the Switch List SMS
  • Whether she engaged (clicked, watched, etc.)

This note is what every subsequent follow-up reads before doing anything. Cameron never drafts a reply to a scorecard lead without reading this note first.

Write 2: The Score Custom Field (updates the contact's data fields in GHL)

The contact's custom field called "score" is updated with her numeric score. This allows GHL pipelines, filters, and automations to sort and segment leads by their actual scan score without having to read the note.

Special case: Score Reveal note (fires when she sees her score but does NOT buy)

If a lead views her score on the results page but does not purchase the $7 audit, a second note is automatically written to her GHL record:

"SCORE REVEAL — [date]. Score: X/100. Lead saw this score but did not purchase the $7 audit. Red-hot — follow up directly."

This flags her as someone who is aware of her problem but hasn't committed yet. She is warm and the right message at the right time will close her.

Step 2: The Immediate SMS After the Scan (Auto-fires)

After a qualifying scan is submitted, GHL automation fires an SMS to the lead with her Switch List — a summary of her top flagged products and the first recommended swap. This is a GHL workflow that Shannon approved and published. It fires automatically without Cameron's involvement.

Step 3: The 2-Hour Follow-Up Text (Approval Required)

Two hours after the lead receives her Switch List SMS, Cameron runs a check:

  1. Read her scorecard note from GHL to find her pain text — the specific product category that bothers her most

  5. Shannon reviews, approves, edits, or skips
  6. Nothing sends until Shannon taps approve

This runs every 2 hours via an automated scheduler on the Mac Mini.
