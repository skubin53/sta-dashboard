Does Woolite Color Renew Work?

What the bottle promised, what the ad industry's own referee and the court record say, what the settlement does and does not mean, and the free habits that slow fading on dark clothes.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years. Published October 7, 2026.

No court ever ruled on whether Woolite Color Renew brings color back. In August 2019, the ad industry's own referee recommended that Reckitt stop saying Woolite "revives color", and Reckitt agreed. Buyers sued, Reckitt said the soap worked, and the case ended in a $3,275,000 settlement with no ruling on who was right.

The Woolite Color Renew case ended with a settlement, not a verdict, so no court ever decided if the soap brought color back.

What did Woolite Color Renew promise?

Quick answer: the bottle said the soap "revives colors." Many bottles also said "COLOR RENEW."

In 2017, Reckitt changed the formula of Woolite Darks and Woolite Gentle Cycle. Then it sold them with a new promise. The soap would "renew" and "revive" color.

A federal judge wrote in 2022 that every one of those bottles said "revives colors" on the back. About 55 percent also said "COLOR RENEW" on the front, inside a rainbow badge.

The back of the bottle showed how it was supposed to work. The soap "smooths rough fibers" and "removes pilling and fuzz." The end result, it said, is that it "revives colors."

The same order says Reckitt raised its wholesale prices for those two soaps along with the new campaign. The exact amount is blacked out.

Source: US District Court, Northern District of California, Order Granting Plaintiffs' Motion for Class Certification, Prescott v. Reckitt Benckiser LLC, No. 20-cv-02101-BLF, filed July 29, 2022, pages 1 and 2.

What did the ad industry's referee say about "revives color"?

Quick answer: in August 2019, it recommended that Reckitt drop "revives color." Reckitt agreed.

A rival detergent company took Woolite's claim to the National Advertising Division. The rival said "revives color" was misleading, because the soap does not add color back to fabric.

In August 2019, the division recommended that Reckitt stop saying Woolite "revives color." Reckitt agreed to stop, the court record says.

Even so, by the court's account, Reckitt kept shipping bottles with those labels until April 2021. That is more than a year and a half later.

The first buyer's complaint went further. As the judge summed it up in 2020, the complaint said the division found Reckitt's data did not support "Brings Back the Color" and "Revives Color." It said the division recommended both claims "be discontinued." That is what the complaint said, as told by the court.

What is the National Advertising Division?

It is the ad industry's own referee. The court record calls it an arm of the Council of Better Business Bureaus. Companies use it to settle fights over each other's ads.

Here is the fair part. Quoting an earlier case, the court described the Council as "a private organization" that offers a "voluntary" way to settle ad fights between competitors. It is not a court, and it is not a government agency. So its decision was a recommendation, not a ruling.

Sources: US District Court, Northern District of California, Order Granting Plaintiffs' Motion for Class Certification, Prescott v. Reckitt Benckiser LLC, No. 20-cv-02101-BLF, filed July 29, 2022, page 2 and footnote 1; same court, Order Granting in Part and Denying in Part Motion to Dismiss First Amended Complaint, December 3, 2020, page 2. The 2020 order says its background facts are drawn from the buyer's complaint.

Did anyone test whether Woolite brings color back?

Quick answer: the buyers' expert did. In his test, the shirts kept losing color.

The buyers brought in a chemistry expert, Dr. Randy Meirowitz. He designed a test, and an accredited lab ran it.

The lab washed eight cotton T-shirts, four navy and four red, in Woolite Darks. It used cold water, as the bottle said, and washed them 25 times. A machine measured the color along the way.

Judge Beth Labson Freeman summed up his report: "All eight t-shirts lost a substantial amount of color after 10 washes." They kept losing color through wash 25.

Reckitt pushed back. It said a new shirt has no lost color to bring back. The judge wrote that by wash 10, the shirts "no longer qualified as new." Color kept washing out after that. She let the test in.

Letting a test in is not the same as ruling that the soap failed. The judge wrote that how much weight to give an expert's opinion is a question "for the jury." No jury ever heard this case.

Source: US District Court, Northern District of California, Order Granting Plaintiffs' Motion for Class Certification, Prescott v. Reckitt Benckiser LLC, No. 20-cv-02101-BLF, filed July 29, 2022, pages 10 to 12.

Is there anything to Reckitt's side?

Quick answer: some. Fuzz and pills can make clothes look dull.

Reckitt said its soap had "technology to renew and revive color in clothing," and that it did so. That is in the court notice.

The idea is not silly. A Kansas State University extension page passes on advice from the American Cleaning Institute, the cleaning industry's trade group. It says pilling has a "dulling effect." So less fuzz could mean less dull color. That was the idea behind the picture on the back of the bottle.

But looking less dull is not the same as getting lost color back. The buyers said the bottle promised the second one. That fight never got decided.

What does the $3,275,000 Woolite settlement mean?

Quick answer: Reckitt agreed to pay to end the case. No court decided who was right.

The court-ordered notice put it plainly: "The Court has not made any determination as to who is right."

The settlement only covered people who lived in California, New York or Massachusetts. Purchases counted up to May 1, 2023. Claims closed December 19, 2023. Woolite Delicates was not part of it.

On March 28, 2024, the judge approved the deal as "fair, reasonable, and adequate." From the fund, she approved $982,500 for the buyers' lawyers, $369,499.27 for their costs, and $337,000 to run the settlement. Together, that is more than half of the $3,275,000.

If you lived in any other state, the settlement did not cover you.

How do I keep dark clothes from fading?

Quick answer: cold water, inside out, smaller loads, and less time in the dryer. These habits cost nothing.

Kansas State University's extension office lists them:

  • Keep darks together. Sort them out from the rest of the wash.
  • Use cold water. The page says "cold water is best for protecting darks."
  • Turn them inside out. Do it before washing and before drying. It cuts down on rubbing and the dull look of pills.
  • Do not stuff the washer or dryer. Clothes need room to move.
  • Do not over dry. Take them out while they are still a little damp.
  • Hang them inside out. If you dry them outside, leave them inside out.

Source: K-State Research and Extension, Ford County, Laundry, "How can I prevent my dark colors from fading?" The page credits the American Cleaning Institute's Consumer Affairs and Outreach Committee.

What laundry soap should I use instead?

Skip any bottle that promises to bring color back. Do not count on a soap to put back color that already washed out.

Pick a plain laundry soap sold straight from the manufacturer, so you know who made it. That is why I send women to Switch to America, a private membership store. We are the manufacturer, and there are no middlemen.

Switching will not bring back the color you lost. What changes is that you know who made your soap.

Common questions about Woolite Color Renew

Did a court rule that Woolite Color Renew does not work?

No. The court notice says, "The Court has not made any determination as to who is right." The buyers said the soap did not bring color back, and Reckitt said it did. The case ended with a $3,275,000 settlement.

Was I part of the Woolite Color Renew settlement?

Only if you lived in California, New York or Massachusetts and bought a bottle that said "Color Renew" or "revives colors" in the covered years. Those years started in 2017 or 2018, depending on the state, and ran to May 1, 2023. Claims closed December 19, 2023.

When did Woolite stop saying "revives colors"?

By the court's account, Reckitt stopped shipping bottles with those labels in April 2021. It had agreed in August 2019 to drop "revives color," after the ad industry's referee recommended it.

Will a different soap bring my faded clothes back?

Do not count on it. Plan to protect the color you still have. Cold water, washing inside out, smaller loads and less time in the dryer help slow fading from here.

Sources for these answers: Court-ordered settlement notice (2023); Class certification order (2022); Final Order and Judgment (2024); K-State Research and Extension, Laundry.

What can I do this week?

Tonight, turn the dark clothes in your hamper inside out. On wash day, use cold water and pull them from the dryer while they are a little damp.
