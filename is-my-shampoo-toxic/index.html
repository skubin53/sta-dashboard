Is Your Shampoo Toxic?

The honest truth about sulfates, who should actually skip them, and the cleaner swap.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years.

Not in the way the internet says. Sulfates like SLS are strong cleansers, not a proven poison, but they are harsh enough that dermatologists use SLS as the benchmark irritant in skin testing, which is exactly why they can dry out hair and bother a sensitive scalp. The bigger honest flag on most shampoo is the undisclosed word "fragrance."

Are the sulfates in shampoo toxic?

No. Sulfates, mainly sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS) and sodium laureth sulfate (SLES), are detergents. They are what makes shampoo foam and lift oil and dirt off your hair. The viral claim that SLS causes cancer is not supported by the evidence. So a bottle of sulfate shampoo is not poisoning you, and the honest version of this story is more useful than the scare.

So why do so many women switch to sulfate-free?

Because sulfates really are strong, and that part is true. Sodium lauryl sulfate is so reliably irritating to skin that dermatologists use it as the standard irritant control in patch testing, the known benchmark they measure other reactions against. In routine patch-test data, roughly one in five people show an irritant reaction to it. That is not a toxin story, it is a harshness story: on dry, color-treated, or sensitive scalps, sulfates can strip color faster and leave hair dry and squeaky. If that is you, a gentler sulfate-free formula genuinely helps.

Source: Forkel et al., "Strengths and Limitations of Sodium Lauryl Sulfate as an Irritant Control in Patch Testing," Contact Dermatitis (2026). Read the study.

Sulfates are not a poison. They are a strong detergent, and dermatologists literally use one of them as their benchmark for irritation.

What is the real thing to watch for?

Fragrance. On a shampoo label that single word can legally stand in for dozens of undisclosed ingredients, the same loophole that runs through air fresheners and cleaners, and it is a common trigger for an itchy scalp or a headache in the shower. Chasing "sulfate-free" while ignoring a long mystery-fragrance list misses the bigger flag. A short, readable ingredient list matters more than any one buzzword.

What should I look for in a shampoo?

  • Sulfate-free if your hair needs it. Dry, color-treated, curly, or a reactive scalp are the real reasons to skip sulfates. If your hair is fine and happy, you do not have to.
  • Fragrance-free or lightly, naturally scented. This removes the undisclosed-ingredient question, which is the honest win for most people.
  • A short label you can read, and American made. Fewer synthetic extras, a clear supply chain, and concentrated formats that come out cheaper per wash than the salon names.

Where to start

You do not need to throw out the bottle in your shower tonight. When it runs low, if your scalp or color has been struggling, try a fragrance-free, sulfate-free, American made shampoo and give it two or three washes to settle. If your hair has been fine, just move to a fragrance-free version and keep the rest simple.
