# Builder Nurture Knowledge Base

**Why this exists and why it is first.** Lanny's rule from the 2026-07-01 Wealth Engine
session: build the knowledge base first, then write the instructions against it, because the
instructions have to leverage the knowledge base. Writing the emails first and back-filling
the facts is how you end up with a sequence that sounds right and says nothing.

**What triggers this.** As of 2026-08-24 the application form on theshannonnicole.com works
for the first time in its life. A woman who applies gets `source: theshannonnicole.com
application` and the tag `builder-application`, and then nothing happens to her. This document
is step one of fixing that.

---

## 1. WHO IS ON THE OTHER END

She is **not** a warm-market contact and she is **not** an existing shopper. Both of those are
ruled out permanently and neither gets revisited.

She is a woman roughly **40 to 60** who already sells, leads or owns something, and whose
income structure just changed or has hit a ceiling. She reads Hormozi and Eker. She is hunted
by **what she consumes**, not by her job title.

**The four shapes she usually arrives in:**

| Shape | What just happened to her | What she is actually asking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The repriced creator | An affiliate program, platform or payout got rewritten under her | "What do I build on that nobody can change?" |
| The orphaned builder | Her company closed, merged or restructured | "Who is going to lead me now?" |
| The commission-only seller | Her split moved, her market slowed, her rates changed | "Where does the ceiling stop moving?" |
| The trapped owner | She built something and it now owns her | "How do I own an asset instead of a job?" |

**What all four have in common:** something broke recently. That is the highest-intent moment
there is, because nobody has to convince her that a change happened. She already knows.

**She is braced for an MLM pitch before she reads a word.** Assume it. Never write a line that
would sound reasonable to someone who was not braced.

**The comparison that carries the whole pitch:**

- 100 shoppers is roughly **$11.6K a year**.

- That is why builders are the bottleneck and shoppers are not.

### THE HARD COMPLIANCE LINE ON ALL OF THE ABOVE

Every number in this section is **Shannon's own result over seven years**. Not one of them may
appear in copy aimed at a prospect in a way that implies she will earn it, or that it is
typical, or that it is achievable in any timeframe.

**Practical rule:** Shannon may describe her own life. She may never describe the reader's
future income. "Here is what seven years built for me" is a story. "Here is what you could
make" is an earnings claim. The first is legal and it is also better copy.

Photographs follow the same rule and it is easy to break by accident. **A photo implying income
IS an earnings claim.** No cash, cheques, wallets, payment apps, dashboards, charts,
calculators, new cars, suitcases or beaches in any builder image.

## 4. THE OBJECTION LIBRARY

Fifteen objections. Each one carries what she actually said, what is underneath it, the answer
in Shannon's voice, and the line that must never be used. The **underneath** column is the one
that matters. Answering the literal words of an objection is how you lose someone who was
asking something else.

### 4.1 "Is this an MLM?"

**Underneath:** she is asking whether she is about to be embarrassed in front of people she
knows. It is a social risk question wearing a business question's clothes.

**Answer:** Do not dodge it and do not get defensive. Name the structure, then move
immediately to the thing that is actually different: she is not required to recruit her
friends, there is no monthly buy-in, and she pays what a brand new customer pays. Then hand
her the vetting checklist and invite her to use it on Shannon first.

**Never:** "it's not an MLM, it's a..." Every woman on earth has heard that sentence and it
ends the conversation. Never say "the wellness company", which reads as evasion.

### 4.2 "I am not calling my friends and family"

**Underneath:** she has watched somebody do this and lose people over it.

**Answer:** Good, because that is not the model here. Shannon does not have a warm market
strategy and does not teach one. There is no list of ten people you know. The whole approach is
built on reaching strangers who are already looking.

**Never:** any version of "just start with the people who love you most."

### 4.3 "I do not have time"

**Underneath:** usually not literally time. It is "I do not believe this will work, and I am
not going to say that out loud."

**Answer:** Give a real hours range and the honest catch that goes with it. Then answer the
real question by showing what the work actually is, because "I do not have time" evaporates the
moment she believes it works.

**Never:** "you only need an hour a day." It is unfalsifiable and it sounds like every ad she
has scrolled past.

### 4.4 "I tried something like this before and I lost money"

**Underneath:** she is not asking to be reassured. She is testing whether Shannon will
acknowledge that it happens.

**Answer:** Agree with her. It does happen, and the specific things that make it happen are
nameable: a monthly buy-in she could not sell through, a comp plan that paid the top, a company
that closed. Then go through each one and say plainly which apply here and which do not.
Acknowledging the failure mode is what separates this from the pitch that burned her.

**Never:** "you just had the wrong upline." It blames her judgement.

### 4.5 "I would have to be on social media all day"

**Underneath:** she does not want to become a person her friends mute.

**Answer:** Shannon does not go on camera either. Say that out loud, then say what she does
instead. This is credible precisely because it is true and because it is unusual for someone to
admit it.

**Never:** pretend it is possible with no visibility at all.

### 4.6 "What does it actually cost to start?"

**Underneath:** she has been asked this question a dozen times by other people and been given a
runaround.

**Answer:** Answer it first, plainly, with the number. The willingness to answer it before she
has to ask twice is itself the differentiator.

**Never:** "let's get on a call and I'll walk you through it." That is the runaround she is
braced for.

### 4.7 "Is there a monthly minimum?"

**Answer:** No. Say it in one word and then stop talking. Adding qualifiers to a clean no makes
it sound like a yes.

### 4.8 "How much can I make?"

**Underneath:** the real question is "is this worth my time", not "what is the number".

**Answer:** This is the sharpest compliance edge in the entire sequence. Shannon may describe
her own seven years. She may explain the structure, how the bonuses work, and what a Director
is. She may not answer the question as asked. **Redirect to the income disclosure statement and
teach her to read it.** Teaching a woman to read the document most people never open builds
more trust than any number would, and it works in Shannon's favour because it is the same
document that exposes the companies that burned her.

**Never:** any figure attached to her, any timeframe, any "women in your position typically".

### 4.9 "I am not a salesperson"

**Underneath:** often said by people who are excellent at this. It usually means "I am not
willing to be pushy."

**Answer:** Separate the two. Nobody is asked to be pushy. Note that the best people at this
are usually the ones who refuse to be.

### 4.10 "What happens to my customers if I quit?"

**Underneath:** the ownership question underneath everything. She is asking whether she is
building an asset or renting one.

**Answer:** Answer it honestly and specifically. Most models take the customer back. This is
the single best question a prospect can ask and she should be told so.

### 4.11 "Why you?"

**Underneath:** she is deciding whether to trust a person, not a company.

**Answer:** Seven years. 100+ women launched. She built theshannonnicole.com herself. She does
not go on camera and still built it. And she will hand over the vetting checklist and invite
its use on her.

### 4.12 "I already have a business"

**Underneath:** this is usually the best prospect on the list, not the worst.

**Answer:** She is not being asked to leave it. She already has the hard part, which is an
audience and the credibility to speak to it. She is currently renting that audience to
companies that keep the customer.

### 4.13 "Isn't the market saturated?"

**Answer:** Answer with the category, not with enthusiasm. Household products get bought every
month by every household regardless of who else sells them. Saturation is a real problem for a
niche product and not a real problem for laundry detergent.

### 4.14 "My husband will think this is a scam"

**Underneath:** she is asking for something she can show him.

**Answer:** Give her the artefact, not the argument. The income disclosure, the checklist, the
plain cost, the no-monthly-minimum. Written things she can hand over. Never coach her on how to
persuade him, which is exactly the manipulation she is watching for.

### 4.15 "How is this different from what I already promote?" *(the creator and affiliate case)*

**Underneath:** she has just been repriced and is asking what stops it happening again.

**Answer:** The difference is whether the payout is a policy somebody else can rewrite. Amazon,
LTK and TikTok Shop rates are all policies. This is the single strongest angle for the repriced
creator and it is already the spine of the builder blog.

## 5. VOICE RULES FOR EVERY LINE IN THE SEQUENCE

Non-negotiable, all previously set by Shannon:

- **No em dashes or en dashes anywhere.** Commas, periods, or the word "to". She reads them as
  an AI tell. Hyphens are fine.
- **No hedges.** Never "in my opinion", never a not-a-doctor disclaimer. Delete the hedge and
  keep the sentence.
- **Real texting, not marketing copy.** Short bubbles. No hype words. No emoji. Copy that
  sounds like marketing gets her blocked.
- **Never name a competitor brand.** The swap is always Switch to America's own. Say "brand",
  not "maker". Naming the villain brand in a blog post is still required, but that is the blog,
  not this.
- **Never say "the wellness company."** It reads as MLM. Say Switch to America.
- **No P.S. blocks.**
- **Guru substance, never guru style.** Hormozi, Brunson, Worre and Cardone are studied for
  what they know. Everything gets translated into Shannon's warm plain voice before it is used.

## 6. WHAT THE SEQUENCE MUST NOT DO

- **Enroll anyone carrying a hard stop.** `not interested`, STOP and DND are permanent. STOP
  and DND cannot be undone by Shannon at all, and the only exit from Not Interested is the
  person re-entering her own details through a form.
- **Touch the 44,000 cold contacts.** They are not the audience for this and never will be.
- **Make a single earnings claim**, in words or in a photograph.
- **Present an allegation as a fact.** Lawsuits, recalls and fines are attributed as
  allegations. Always.
- **Fire before a human being has seen the first send.** Every automation gets one supervised
  run against one real applicant before it runs on its own.

Things this knowledge base does not yet have and cannot invent:

1. **Shannon's own freedom story.** Six questions only she can answer. Blocked since July. It

2. **Named customer testimonies.** 20,000+ customers and not one usable named account, because
   inventing one is not allowed and she has not yet named any. A woman describing her own life

3. **The real hours range.** Objection 4.3 needs a true number. Shannon has it and I do not.
4. **The current start cost figure**, stated to the dollar, for objection 4.6.
5. **What a builder's first 30 days actually look like**, step by step, for the first-action
   sequence.

Items 3, 4 and 5 are small and can come out of one conversation. Items 1 and 2 are the ones
that change everything.
