# Builder Application Follow-Up

Written against [[KNOWLEDGE-BASE]], not alongside it. Research came before the writing, per the
undated Wealth Engine session: do not tell it what to build, let it research first, then
combine.

## What the research changed

The FTC published proposed rules in January 2025 aimed squarely at this category. Two things in
them matter for every line below:

1. Earnings claims require **written substantiation held at the time the claim is made**, and
   the proposal would require that substantiation be handed to a consumer on request, in the
   language the claim was made in.
2. The proposal would specifically prohibit **providing participants with recruitment marketing
   materials that contain deceptive earnings claims.**

Point 2 is the one that binds hardest. Anything written here is material Shannon may hand to
somebody she recruits, so it is not enough that Shannon herself is careful. **The sequence has
to be safe in the hands of a builder who is not.**

The direction of travel is stricter, not looser. So: **no income figure appears anywhere in
this sequence, in any email, in any image, stated or implied.** Not hers, not a range, not a
typical, not a hypothetical. Shannon's seven years are told as her life, never as a forecast.

That constraint made the copy better. Every email below has to earn the Zoom on honesty and
specificity, which is the only thing that works on a woman who is braced for a pitch anyway.

## Design principles

- **Seven touches over eleven days, then stop.** She applied because something just broke. That
  window is short and it does not reopen by nagging.
- **One question per email.** Lanny's hard rule for anything conversational.
- **Answer the question she has not asked yet.** Emails 2, 3 and 4 pre-empt the three objections
  that kill this before a call ever happens.
- **Never sell the company. Sell the twenty minutes.**
- **Every email is short enough to read on a phone in a school pickup line.**

## Sending

- From: Shannon, first person, her name.
- No P.S. anywhere.
- No em dashes. No hedges. No hype words. No emoji.
- Hard stop check before every send: `not interested`, DND, STOP.
- **The first real applicant gets watched by a human before this runs unattended.**

## EMAIL 1 — immediately

**Subject:** your application

> I got your application.
>
> Before I send you anything else, one question, and it is the only one I will ask by email.
>
> What changed recently?
>
> Most women who fill that form in are not browsing. Something moved. A payout got rewritten, a
> company folded, a market went quiet, or you looked at what you built and realised it owns you.
>
> Tell me which one it was and I will not waste your time on the other three.
>
> Shannon

*Why:* acknowledges within minutes so the silence never happens. Asks exactly one question. The
answer routes everything after it, and a reply also tells us she is real.

## EMAIL 2 — day 1

**Subject:** yes, and here is what that means

> You are wondering if this is an MLM.
>
> It is direct sales. I am not going to dance around that, because you would spot it and we
> would be finished.
>
> What I will tell you is what usually makes that a bad word, and whether it is true here.
>
> There is no monthly minimum. Nothing to buy through, nothing to hit, no month where you order
> product you do not need to stay active.
>
> You pay what a brand new customer pays. I do not have a special price and I would not offer
> you one, because anyone who discounts you at the start is buying a customer, not building
> a partner.
>
> There is no list of ten people you know. I do not teach a warm market and I do not have one.
>
> If any of those three had been the other way round, I would not be doing this either.
>
> Shannon

*Why:* the first objection is social risk, not business risk. Naming it before she has to ask is
the whole play. Answering "is it an MLM" with "no, it's a..." ends the conversation.

## EMAIL 3 — day 2

**Subject:** what it costs

> People ask me this third and I would rather answer it first.

**[GAP: the exact start cost to the dollar. Shannon has it, I do not. This email does not send
until that number is in.]**

> That is the number. There is nothing behind it, no second tier, and no upgrade conversation
> two weeks later.
>
> Shannon

*Why:* answering the money question plainly, unprompted, and early is the single clearest
signal that this is not the runaround she is braced for. **Blocked until the figure is
confirmed. Do not approximate it.**

## EMAIL 4 — day 4

**Subject:** the question I wish more people asked me

> If you walked away in two years, what happens to the customers you brought in?
>
> Ask that of anything you are ever offered. Most models take them back. That is the difference
> between building an asset and renting one, and almost nobody asks it until it is too late to
> matter.
>
> Ask it of me on the call and I will answer it properly, with the actual terms, not a slogan.
>
> Shannon

*Why:* she is asking, underneath everything, whether she owns anything. Teaching her the
question rather than answering it is more persuasive than answering it, and it is the same
instinct as handing somebody the income disclosure and showing them how to read it.

## EMAIL 5 — day 6

**Subject:** seven years

**[GAP: Shannon's story. Blocked on the six questions only she can answer. This is the emotional
spine of the sequence and there is no honest way to write around it.]**

Structural requirements when it is written:

- Her life, past tense, first person. What it was like before, what the decision cost, what it
  is like now.
- **Not one number.** Not the $700K, not the 100 women, not a product point. The moment a figure
  appears in a recruitment email it becomes an earnings claim, and the FTC's 2025 proposal aims
  directly at recruitment materials that carry them.
- It ends on who she became, not on what she earns.
- It can be a video built from photographs and a voice note. No camera required.

*Why:* everything before this is logic. A woman does not rebuild her income on logic. This is
the email that makes the Zoom feel like a conversation rather than a sales call.

---

## EMAIL 6 — day 8

**Subject:** use this on me

> Before you say yes to anything, including me, here is what to ask.
>
> What does it cost to start, all in.
> Is there a monthly requirement of any kind.
> What happens to my customers if I stop.
> Where is the income disclosure and can I read it.
> Who makes money when I join, and how.
>
> Take that list to every opportunity in front of you. Bring it to me on the call and I will go
> through all five out loud.
>
> If somebody will not answer one of them, that is your answer.
>
> Shannon

*Why:* the strongest possible trust move is inviting the vetting. It also does real work for
her, which means it is worth reading even if she never books. Points 4 and 5 are the ones that
disqualify our competitors, and pointing her at the income disclosure is both the most honest
thing available and the safest thing to say under the earnings rules.

## EMAIL 7 — day 11, last one

**Subject:** last one from me

> This is the last email I will send you about this.
>
> Twenty minutes, on video, no slides. You ask, I answer. If it is not for you I would rather
> find that out on the call than have you sit on a list.
>
> [book the twenty minutes]
>
> If it is a no, reply with the word no and I will take you off. No hard feelings and no
> follow-up.
>
> Shannon

*Why:* a clean exit offered sincerely converts better than a deadline, and it protects the list.
A woman who replies "no" is worth more than a woman who silently rots, because she can be
suppressed properly instead of guessed at.

## WHAT HAPPENS ON A REPLY

Any reply at any point **stops the sequence** and goes to Shannon. This is the line from the
7/29 session about automating the early prospect conversation, held at the right place: the
opener is automated, the conversation is not. If a woman writes back about her closed company
and gets email 4 on schedule the next morning, everything above is undone.

## WHAT IS NOT IN HERE, ON PURPOSE

- **No countdown, no scarcity, no "spots".** She has seen all three and they mark the sender.
- **No income figure**, hers or anyone's, in text or in a picture.
- **No named customer testimony.** There is not one cleared to use, and inventing one is not on
  the table.
- **No competitor named.** The villain in a blog post is named. In a recruitment email it just
  looks like scrapping.
- **No SMS in v1.** Her rule is that outreach must read like a real text or she gets blocked,
  and seven automated texts to a stranger is not a real text. SMS gets added later for the one
  case that earns it, which is a reminder to a woman who has already booked.

## BLOCKED, AND THE SEQUENCE CANNOT SHIP WITHOUT THEM

1. **Email 3**, the exact start cost.
2. **Email 5**, the six story answers.

Emails 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7 are complete and could run as a five touch sequence today. That is the
recommendation: ship five now, add the two when the answers land, rather than let an applicant
sit in silence for another week waiting on a story.
