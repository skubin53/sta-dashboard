What did UC Davis find in the bottles?

Quick answer: most of the 22 bottles they tested in 2020 were stale or mixed with other oils.

Selina Wang's lab at UC Davis tested 22 domestic and imported avocado oils. They found at least 82 percent were either stale before the expiration date or mixed with other oils.

In three cases, bottles labeled "pure" or "extra virgin" contained near 100 percent soybean oil. Those are the exact words a careful shopper looks for.

To know what real avocado oil looks like chemically, the team bought avocados, extracted their own oil in the lab, and compared the store bottles against it.

Source: UC Davis, news release on the 2020 avocado oil study, June 15, 2020, on the lab's study in Food Control.

What about chips, mayo and dressings made with avocado oil?

Quick answer: in 2026 the same lab found most of them were mixed with cheaper oils.

In July 2026, UC Davis reported that 48 of the 54 avocado oil-labeled products were adulterated with cheaper oils. That included 93 percent of the chips, 71 percent of the mayonnaises and 100 percent of the salad dressings they tested.

The team gave the samples a 10 percent margin to allow for natural differences between oils, and 89 percent still failed. When they ran the same tests on 20 olive oil foods, only one failed.

This is the part most answers miss. You never see the oil in a bag of chips, so you cannot even try to judge it by color or smell.

Source: UC Davis, That Avocado Oil Chip You're Eating May Not Be Made With Pure Avocado Oil, July 15, 2026, on the study in Applied Food Research.

Why can't the label tell you?

Quick answer: there is no federal definition of avocado oil for a label to meet.

UC Davis noted in 2020 that the FDA had not adopted a "standard of identity" for avocado oil. In the release's words, those are "basic food standards designed to protect consumers from being cheated by inferior products."

That gap is still open. On October 3, 2026, a search of the federal regulations (eCFR) for "avocado oil" in Title 21 (food and drugs) and Title 7 (agriculture) returned no rules at all.

The only definition is international. In November 2024 the Codex Alimentarius Commission agreed to add avocado oil to its Standard for Named Vegetable Oils. The avocado oil industry's own trade group, the Avocado Oil Manufacturers Association, calls that a voluntary reference standard, not binding legislation.

So "100% pure," "extra virgin" and "cold pressed" on a US bottle are the seller's words. No federal rule says what they must mean for avocado oil.

Do the color and smell tips work?

They help with freshness. Selina Wang said fresh, virgin avocado oil tastes grassy, buttery and a little bit like mushrooms. She also said to store it away from light and heat, buy a size you will finish, and pick the bottle closest to its harvest date.

But the blends were found with lab chemistry. In 2026 UC Davis measured fatty acids and sterols, the chemical "fingerprints" of each oil. Your nose cannot do that in the aisle.

What does the industry say?

The Avocado Oil Manufacturers Association called the adulteration findings unacceptable. It also cautioned that variety and climate can change the chemistry of an authentic oil, and that one study is not a verdict on the whole industry. That is fair, and it is why this page states every number as what UC Davis measured in its own samples.

What can I do instead?

Quick answer: stop trusting the front of the package, and cook with a fat you can trace.

Start with the foods where you never see the oil. Chips, mayo and dressings that say "made with avocado oil" are the foods the 2026 testing found mixed most of the time.

If you keep a bottle, buy a small one, check for a harvest date, and store it in a cool, dark cupboard.

The surer answer is a fat whose source you know. Beef tallow or butter from a farm you can name does not depend on a stranger's label. I go deeper on this in Is avocado oil fake? and Are seed oils bad for you?

Common questions about avocado oil

Can you tell if avocado oil is real just by looking at the bottle?

Not reliably. In UC Davis testing in 2020, three bottles labeled "pure" or "extra virgin" avocado oil contained near 100 percent soybean oil. The researchers found the mixes with lab chemistry, not by reading the label.

Is there a federal standard for avocado oil in the United States?

No federal one that we could find. UC Davis noted in 2020 that the FDA had not adopted a standard of identity for avocado oil, and a search of the federal food and agriculture rules (Titles 21 and 7 of the eCFR) on October 3, 2026 found no rule that mentions avocado oil. The only definition is an international Codex standard from 2024, which the avocado oil industry's own trade group calls voluntary.

Are avocado oil chips and mayo really made with avocado oil?

Often not only avocado oil. In a 2026 UC Davis study, 48 of 54 processed foods labeled with avocado oil were mixed with cheaper oils, including 93 percent of the chips, 71 percent of the mayonnaises and 100 percent of the salad dressings tested. That was one sample of products, not a verdict on any single brand.

What does fresh avocado oil taste like?

UC Davis researcher Selina Wang described authentic, fresh, virgin avocado oil as tasting grassy, buttery and a little bit like mushrooms. Taste can help you spot stale oil, but it was lab testing that caught the blends.

Sources for these answers: UC Davis (2020); UC Davis (2026); eCFR; FAO (2024); FreshFruitPortal (2026).

What can I do this week?

Look in your pantry for anything that says "made with avocado oil" on the front. Read the ingredient list, and know that even that list is the company's word. Then pick one fat you can trace for everyday cooking. Choose from an American made company or a farm you can trust.
