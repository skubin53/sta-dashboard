Is It Safe to Sleep With an Electric Blanket On All Night?

The plain answer, what to check on an older blanket, how to look up any brand for a recall, and what keeps you warm with no plug at all.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years. Published October 23, 2026.

The safest answer is no. Warm the bed first, then switch the blanket off and unplug it before you go to sleep. If yours is a JKMAX, stop using it now: the US Consumer Product Safety Commission posted a recall of about 195,000 JKMAX heated blankets and heating pads on September 17, 2026.

A blanket that is not on a recall list is not proven safe. The safest heated blanket is one that is switched off while you sleep.

Is it safe to sleep with an electric blanket on all night?

Quick answer: no. Use it to warm the bed, then turn it off and unplug it.

All night is hours of heat on your body while you are asleep and not checking it.

Oklahoma's state-owned electric utility, the Grand River Dam Authority, put it plainly in a January 2020 safety column. It was passing on tips from ESFI, an electrical safety nonprofit. The column says heated blankets and pads "should never be left unattended or used while sleeping."

A burn nurse points to time, too. Brad Wiggins, a nurse manager at the University of Utah Health Burn Center, was talking about heating pads. He named "falling asleep on it" as a danger. He said it can cause "a second degree burn over just a few hours of time of exposure."

And he named age. "Maybe as you get older you don't feel as much in certain areas of your body," he said.

Sources: Grand River Dam Authority, Safety tips for electric blankets, heating pads, January 10, 2020, passing on ESFI tips; University of Utah Health, Heating Pads Can Cause Second-Degree Burns, November 23, 2018.

What should I check on an older electric blanket?

Quick answer: look for dark, charred or frayed spots on the blanket and the cord, and think about its age.

Lay it flat on the bed in good light. Run your hand along the cord. Then go down this list:

  • Dark, charred or frayed spots. The ESFI tips say to check the blanket and its cord for them before you use it. If you find any, replace it.
  • A scorch mark, or one spot that runs too hot. The UC Davis burn center says to unplug it and not use it.
  • Folding or tucking. The ESFI tips say "Blankets that are folded or tucked in could overheat and cause a fire." Keep it flat while it is on.
  • Anything on top. Other blankets or a pet on top can make it overheat, the tips say. Nothing goes on top while it is on.
  • Two heated things at once. The UC Davis burn center says not to use a heated blanket and a heated mattress pad at the same time.

How old is too old for an electric blanket?

Ten years is the line to watch. The 2020 column passed on ESFI figures: heating pads and electric blankets were "the source for almost 500 fires each year." In almost every one, "the blanket or pad was more than ten years old."

That number comes from a 2020 column. Treat it as an older figure, not this year's count.

The UC Davis burn center adds two more points about older blankets. Blankets made before about 2001 "may not have a shut-off mechanism." And older blankets "washed and dried over and over" could be a fire hazard, because their wires become brittle.

Sources: Grand River Dam Authority, Safety tips for electric blankets, heating pads, January 10, 2020; Firefighters Burn Institute Regional Burn Center at UC Davis Medical Center, The BurnNet News, February 2018, question and answer on heated blankets.

How do I know if my electric blanket is recalled?

Quick answer: search the brand name on the CPSC recalls page.

  1. Find the brand name. Look on the remote, the cord, or the box.
  2. Go to the CPSC recalls page.
  3. Type the brand into the box marked "Search Recalls & Product Safety Warnings."
  4. If a recall comes up, stop using it and follow the steps in the notice.

If nothing comes up, that does not prove your blanket is safe. It only means you did not find a recall. In the ESFI figures, almost every fire involved a blanket or pad more than ten years old. So do the checks above either way.

What did JKMAX recall?

Every heated blanket and heating pad it sold. The CPSC notice, recall number 26-778, covers "all heating pads and heated blankets sold by JKMax." They were sold on "Amazon.com from September 2022 through July 2026."

The notice says "The internal wire heating elements of the blankets and pads can overheat, posing fire and burn hazards to consumers." It says JKMax "is aware of 577 reports" of the blankets smoking, melting or burning. Those reports include 53 burn injuries.

The blankets and pads are blue, gray, brown or red, with a white remote. The model number is on the back of the remote, and the box shows it with the JKMAX name. The CPSC says to stop using it and contact JKMax "for a full refund."

These were not old blankets. They were sold from 2022 to 2026. So newer is not proof of safe, either. I wrote more about this recall in Are electric blankets safe to use all night?

What keeps me warm instead?

Quick answer: a heavy blanket with no wire inside and no plug.

Put a plain wool blanket or a thick cotton blanket on top of your bed. It holds your own body heat. Nothing in it can overheat, because nothing in it plugs in.

If you keep your heated blanket, use it the safe way. Turn it on while you get ready for bed, with nothing on top of it. When you climb in, switch it off and unplug it. Then pull your wool blanket over you to keep the warm in.

Common questions about electric blankets at night

Can I leave my electric blanket on low all night?

Low is still heat on you for hours while you sleep. The UC Davis burn center told a burn survivor to use the low setting to cut the chance of a burn you cannot feel. But the ESFI tips say a heated blanket should never be used while sleeping. So warm the bed, then switch it off and unplug it before you lie down.

How old is too old for an electric blanket?

In ESFI figures passed on by an Oklahoma state utility in 2020, almost every heated blanket and pad fire involved one more than ten years old. The UC Davis burn center says blankets made before about 2001 may have no shut-off. If yours is over ten years old, or has dark, charred or frayed spots, retire it.

If my blanket is not on a recall list, does that mean it is safe?

No. It only means you did not find a recall for it. In the ESFI figures, almost every fire involved a blanket or pad more than ten years old. So check yours for wear and age, keep it flat, and switch it off before you sleep.

Is an electric blanket safe for my mom?

Check hers the same night you check yours. The UC Davis burn center says not to use heated bedding on anyone who is insensitive to heat. A Utah burn nurse, talking about heating pads, said that as you get older, you may not feel as much in some parts of your body.

Sources for these answers: Grand River Dam Authority, passing on ESFI tips (2020); UC Davis burn center, BurnNet newsletter (February 2018); University of Utah Health (2018).

What can I do tonight?

Pull the heated blanket off the bed and lay it flat. Check it and the cord for dark, charred or frayed spots. Think about how old it is. Search the brand on the CPSC recalls page. Then switch the heat off and unplug it before you sleep, and put a wool blanket on top.
