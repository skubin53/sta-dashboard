Is There a Legal Limit for BHA and BHT in Food?

What the federal rules say, whether stuffing mix has a limit of its own, what the box has to tell you, and what to make on Thanksgiving instead.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years. Published November 5, 2026.

Yes. For food in general, the federal rules for BHA and BHT say the total of all antioxidants can be no more than 0.02 percent of the fat or oil in the food. The rule on added preservatives says the food must name each one and say what it does, but it does not ask for the amount.

A legal limit tells you how much a company may add, not that the amount is safe.

Is there a legal limit for BHA and BHT in food?

Quick answer: yes. It is one shared limit, and it is tied to the fat or oil in the food.

BHA and BHT each have a short federal rule. The two rules use the same words. Each one allows the use when "the total content of antioxidants is not over 0.02 percent of fat or oil content, including essential (volatile) oil content of food."

The FDA said it again in plainer words this year. Its notice on BHA says "the total antioxidant content cannot exceed 0.02% of the total fat or oil content of the food." Its notice on BHT says the same thing.

In plain numbers, 0.02 percent is 2 parts for every 10,000 parts of fat or oil.

What does "0.02 percent of fat or oil" mean?

  • One total, not one each. BHA, BHT and any other antioxidants in the food all count toward the same 0.02 percent.
  • It is measured against the fat or oil. The limit is a share of the fat or oil in the food. It is not a share of the whole box.
  • Some oils from flavor count too. The rule counts what it calls "essential (volatile) oil" along with the fat or oil.

Sources: 21 CFR 182.3169 (BHA) and 21 CFR 182.3173 (BHT), eCFR; Federal Register, "Butylated Hydroxyanisole (BHA); Request for Information", February 11, 2026; Federal Register, "Butylated Hydroxytoluene (BHT); Request for Information", May 13, 2026.

Does stuffing mix have a limit of its own?

Quick answer: no. Stuffing mix is not on either federal food list for BHA and BHT.

Two more rules, 21 CFR 172.110 for BHA and 21 CFR 172.115 for BHT, list certain foods with their own limits in parts per million. Dry breakfast cereals and potato flakes are on both lists. Each has a limit of 50 parts per million, for BHA and BHT together.

Stuffing mix is not on either list. The BHA notice says the 0.02 percent rule covers BHA "in food generally."

Nothing I read says any maker has gone over this limit. And none of these records says how close any one box comes to it.

How much BHA and BHT is in a box of stuffing mix?

Quick answer: the public records I read give no number for any one box.

Kraft Heinz's own page for its Stove Top Stuffing Mix for Turkey ends the ingredients with "WITH BHA, BHT, AND ROSEMARY EXTRACT AS PRESERVATIVES." It names both, and it names their job.

In both notices, the FDA asked food makers how much they use. But both notices say: "We do not need information about individual products and their recipes, but rather data about the levels of use in general product categories." Both say sending that data in is voluntary.

So these records hold no number for any one box. I will not guess one for you.

Does the box have to say how much BHA or BHT is in it?

Quick answer: no. The federal rule asks for the name and the job, not the amount.

The rule is 21 CFR 101.22(j). A food with an added chemical preservative must state "both the common or usual name of the ingredient(s) and a separate description of its function." Its examples of a function include "preservative" and "to retard spoilage." The rule has a few exceptions.

That is why a box can say "BHA" and "as preservatives" and stop there. The rule says nothing about how much.

Why are BHA and BHT under federal review?

Quick answer: the FDA is taking a new look at the safety of both in food. It asked for safety data on BHA in February 2026 and on BHT in May 2026. Nothing has been ruled yet.

The BHA notice came out on February 11, 2026. Comments closed April 13. The BHT notice came out on May 13, 2026. Both notices say these preservatives are used "to prevent the spoilage of fats and oils."

The FDA keeps a list of food chemicals under review. As of October 29, 2026, it shows no action yet on either one. Both are still legal in food today.

What does the federal cancer report say about BHA?

The National Toxicology Program calls BHA "reasonably anticipated to be a human carcinogen." That rests on studies in animals, and BHA has been on the report since 1991.

Here is the other side, straight. The same report calls the human data "inadequate." It also notes one Dutch study that found no rise in stomach cancer at typical levels in the diet.

What can I make instead of boxed stuffing?

Quick answer: stuffing from a loaf you bake yourself, with celery, onion and sage from a farm near you.

Stuffing made in your own kitchen does not need a preservative at all. You make it in the morning and eat it that afternoon.

  1. Monday: bake a plain loaf of bread.
  2. Tuesday: cut it into cubes. Let them dry overnight on a sheet pan.
  3. Before Thursday: get the celery, onion and sage from a farm near you. Ask the farmer how they grow it.
  4. Thursday: cook the onion and celery in butter until soft. Toss in the bread, the sage and some broth. Bake it while the turkey rests, until the top turns golden.

Common questions about BHA and BHT limits

How much BHA is allowed in food?

For food in general, the federal rule says the total of all antioxidants, BHA included, can be no more than 0.02 percent of the fat or oil in the food. That is 2 parts for every 10,000 parts of fat or oil. A few foods, like dry breakfast cereals and potato flakes, have their own limits in parts per million.

Does the box have to say how much BHA or BHT is in it?

No. The federal rule on added preservatives, 21 CFR 101.22(j), asks for the name of the preservative and a few words on its job, like "preservative." It does not ask for the amount.

Is there a separate limit for stuffing mix?

No. Stuffing mix is not on either federal food list for BHA and BHT. Those lists name foods like dry breakfast cereals, potato flakes and potato granules. For food in general, the limit is the 0.02 percent of fat or oil rule.

Does a legal limit mean BHA and BHT are safe?

No. A limit tells you how much a company may add. It is not proof that the amount is safe. The FDA asked for safety data on BHA in February 2026 and on BHT in May 2026, and nothing has been ruled yet.
