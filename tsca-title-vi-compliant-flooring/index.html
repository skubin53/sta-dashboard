What does the TSCA Title VI sticker mean?

Quick answer: the composite wood inside the product was certified to meet the federal formaldehyde limit.

TSCA Title VI is the federal law that sets formaldehyde limits for composite wood. That means plywood, particleboard and fiberboard. Many laminate and engineered floors have a core made of these boards.

EPA says that from June 1, 2018, composite wood products sold in the US had to be labeled CARB ATCM Phase II or TSCA Title VI compliant. After March 22, 2019, they must be labeled TSCA Title VI compliant.

CARB ATCM Phase II is California's rule. EPA says the two are set at identical levels.

Source: EPA, Formaldehyde Emission Standards for Composite Wood Products, last updated February 12, 2026.

What are the actual limits?

Quick answer: between 0.05 and 0.13 parts per million, depending on the type of board.

Type of wood panelFederal formaldehyde limit
Hardwood plywood (veneer or composite core)0.05 ppm
Particleboard0.09 ppm
Medium-density fiberboard (MDF)0.11 ppm
Thin medium-density fiberboard0.13 ppm

The limits are measured with a lab chamber test called ASTM E1333-14. The rule says they apply whether the board is a panel, a part, or built into a finished product.

Source: 40 CFR 770.10, Formaldehyde emission standards.

Why doesn't the sticker tell me about the floor in my home?

Quick answer: the limit is set for the board in a lab test, not for the air in your room.

EPA answers this in its own question and answer page on laminate flooring. Its words: "These emissions standards are not directly applicable to finished products, such as the laminate wood flooring in your home."

Your room is not a test chamber. It has its own heat, humidity and air flow, and maybe hundreds of square feet of new floor. EPA says keeping temperature and humidity low reduces formaldehyde emissions from some products.

Source: EPA, Questions and Answers Regarding Laminate Flooring, last updated November 17, 2025.

Can a floor labeled compliant still be over the limit?

Quick answer: it has happened.

The California Air Resources Board says laminate flooring samples bought between September 2013 and May 2015 "were labeled as compliant" and were being sold at Lumber Liquidators stores across California. Those samples exceeded California's formaldehyde limits.

Lumber Liquidators settled with the state for $2.5 million in March 2016. It agreed to audit its suppliers and to randomly test the cores of its flooring.

That was one company and one time period. It does not mean your floor is over the limit. It does show that a label is a promise, not a test of the box you bought.

Source: California Air Resources Board, Lumber Liquidators, Inc. Settlement.

What can I do the week a new floor goes in?

Quick answer: fresh air, a cooler and drier house, and simpler materials next time.

EPA's own advice for people worried about formaldehyde is to increase ventilation, keep temperature and humidity low, and choose products with less formaldehyde in the future. If anyone in the home has symptoms, EPA says to talk to a doctor and your local health department.

Next time, ask what the core is made of. Solid wood, tile and stone have no composite wood core, so this sticker is not about them. Ask about the finish and the glue too.

Common questions about the TSCA Title VI label

What does TSCA Title VI compliant mean on flooring?

It means the composite wood inside the product (plywood, particleboard or fiberboard) was certified to meet the federal formaldehyde limit for that type of panel. After March 22, 2019, composite wood products sold in the United States must be labeled TSCA Title VI compliant.

Does a TSCA Title VI label mean my floor is safe?

It means the wood panel met a limit in a lab test. EPA itself says the emission standards "are not directly applicable to finished products, such as the laminate wood flooring in your home." The label does not measure the air in your room.

What is the formaldehyde limit for composite wood?

Under 40 CFR 770.10, measured with test method ASTM E1333-14: 0.05 ppm for hardwood plywood, 0.09 ppm for particleboard, 0.11 ppm for medium-density fiberboard and 0.13 ppm for thin medium-density fiberboard.

Can flooring labeled compliant still be over the limit?

It has happened. California regulators found laminate flooring samples bought between September 2013 and May 2015 that were labeled as compliant but exceeded California's formaldehyde limits. Lumber Liquidators settled with the California Air Resources Board for $2.5 million in March 2016.

Sources for these answers: EPA (2026); EPA (2025); 40 CFR 770.10; California Air Resources Board.

What can I do this week?

If you have a newer floor, open the windows when you can, run the fans, and keep the house cool and dry. If you are shopping for one, ask what the core is made of before you look at the color.
