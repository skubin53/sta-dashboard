PDF #1: TOP 5 TOXIC PRODUCTS HIDING IN YOUR HOME

Revised Dossier Draft, Editor Pass 1, 2026-06-20

Voice: soft-mom. Reading level: grade 2. Zero em-dashes. Zero en-dashes. Zero EWG. Zero Melaleuca. Zero income claims.

1. THREE OPENER HOOKS

HOOK 1 (LEAD, post-booking page 1)

"Right now, while you are reading this, your two year old is breathing air the EPA says can be up to 10 times worse than the air outside. The worst of it is coming from the bottle under your kitchen sink." [Grade 2]

Citation: U.S. EPA, Volatile Organic Compounds Impact on Indoor Air Quality, https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/volatile-organic-compounds-impact-indoor-air-quality (accessed 2026-06-20). Qualifier: "many VOCs," not all indoor air. EPA also notes up to 1,000 times higher during specific activities like paint stripping (not used here, qualifier-only).

HOOK 2 (page 2, 78% pivot)

"In one federal study, 78% of the female mice fed a chemical that hides in baby shampoo got liver cancer. The chemical does not have to be on the label. The EPA has known since 1985. Nobody told you." [Grade 2]

Citation: NCI Technical Report No. 80 (1978), Bioassay of 1,4-Dioxane for Possible Carcinogenicity; replicated in Kano K et al., "Carcinogenicity studies of 1,4-dioxane administered in drinking water to rats and mice for 2 years," J Toxicol Sci. 2009;34(3):293-307. EPA IRIS Summary 0326, https://iris.epa.gov/static/pdfs/0326_summary.pdf. FDA, 1,4-Dioxane: A Manufacturing Byproduct, https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/potential-contaminants-cosmetics/14-dioxane-cosmetics-manufacturing-byproduct. Caveat (footnote, 6pt): Animal bioassay data. Exact ratios pending source-verification against NCI 1978 Technical Report No. 80. If ratios cannot be confirmed in the primary, drop to qualitative "large dose-response increase in liver tumors."

HOOK 3 (page 3, dryer vent pivot)

"1 in 8 grown ups in America reported getting sick from the smell of their neighbor's dryer vent. If they have asthma, it is 1 in 3. The smell crosses the wall." [Grade 2]

Citation: Steinemann A., "Fragranced consumer products: exposures and effects from emissions," Air Quality Atmosphere & Health, 2016, DOI 10.1007/s11869-016-0442-z. Survey of 1,136 US adults. Self-report data. Defensible wording: "reported," not "experienced," not "are harmed."

2. FIVE PRODUCT CATEGORIES

Each category below leads with what the product is doing to her kid right now. Not what is in it. Outcome first, chemistry second, source third.

CATEGORY 1: WHAT YOUR BABY SHAMPOO IS QUIETLY LEAVING BEHIND

(1,4-Dioxane in personal care)

(a) Ubiquity. GAP A pending. Statista and P&G 10-K still to pull. Hold the "in your home tonight" line until filled.

(b) Chemicals of concern by name. 1,4-Dioxane.

(c) What it does in the body. [Grade 2] "The EPA says it likely causes cancer in people." (EPA IRIS Summary 0326, https://iris.epa.gov/static/pdfs/0326_summary.pdf, accessed 2026-06-20.) "The U.S. government expects it to give people cancer." (NTP, 15th Report on Carcinogens, "reasonably anticipated to be a human carcinogen," https://ntp.niehs.nih.gov/sites/default/files/ntp/roc/content/profiles/dioxane.pdf.) "Another world health group calls it possibly cancer-causing." (IARC Group 2B.) "Big doses can hurt your eyes, nose, throat, kidneys, and liver." (ATSDR ToxFAQs, https://wwwn.cdc.gov/TSP/PHS/PHS.aspx?phsid=953&toxid=199.) Footnote (6pt): At very high industrial exposure levels, 1,4-dioxane has caused worker deaths. The amounts in consumer products are much lower. Federal regulators still classify it as likely to cause cancer in humans.

(d) What happens to the air when product is used. [Grade 2] "1,4-dioxane is not on the ingredient label. It sneaks in during the factory process. The companies do not have to tell you it is there." "Flip your bottle over. See a word ending in eth or oxynol? That is where it hides." (FDA Cosmetics, link above. Look for PEG, polyethylene glycol, polyoxyethylene, SLES.)

(e) Regulatory status. EPA IRIS: "likely to be carcinogenic to humans." NTP: "reasonably anticipated to be a human carcinogen" (15th Report). IARC: Group 2B. California Prop 65: listed 1988. (GAP H, OEHHA list to confirm date.) FDA: published manufacturing byproduct page, ongoing review. EU REACH Annex XVII: GAP N to confirm scope.

(f) Visual concept. A baby shampoo bottle, a laundry jug, a dish soap bottle on a bathroom counter. Red arrows circle the words "sodium laureth sulfate," "PEG-40," "polyoxyethylene." Sidebar: "This is how the bubble makers get into your shampoo. The leftover is a chemical the EPA says likely causes cancer."

CATEGORY 2: THE STUFF YOU PUT IN THE DRYER WITH HER PAJAMAS

(Dryer sheets and scented laundry products)

(a) Ubiquity. GAP A pending. Bounce, Snuggle, Downy class. Statista pending.

(b) Chemicals of concern by name. Acetaldehyde (direct emission). Benzyl acetate. Limonene and pinene (terpenes that form formaldehyde indoors). 1,4-dioxane (in the detergent, carried into the dryer load).

(c) What it does in the body. [Grade 2] "Acetaldehyde is the chemical that gives a grown up a hangover. Scientists found it puffs out of dryer sheets." (Steinemann A., 2015, "Volatile emissions from common consumer products," Air Quality Atmosphere & Health, DOI 10.1007/s11869-015-0327-6.) Footnote: claim limited to "is emitted by." Not "causes hangovers in users." "When the scent meets the air in your house, it can turn into a poison called formaldehyde. Formaldehyde is what funeral homes use on bodies." (Steinemann 2015, plus indoor terpene-ozone chemistry. Defensible wording: "When fragrance chemicals like limonene react with ozone already in your indoor air, formaldehyde forms.")

(d) What happens to the air when product is used. [Grade 2] "One scientist tested 37 smelly products from the store. She found 156 different bad fumes coming out of them. About 15 in each bottle. Almost half puffed out fumes the government says cause cancer." (Steinemann 2015.)

(e) Regulatory status. Acetaldehyde: Prop 65 listed 1988 (GAP H confirm). EPA HAP under Clean Air Act. Formaldehyde: NTP "known to be a human carcinogen." Prop 65 listed 1988 (GAP H confirm). 1,4-dioxane: see Category 1. EU REACH Annex XVII: GAP N.

(f) Visual concept. A residential dryer vent on the outside of a house. White steam puffing out. The vent labeled with 5 chemical names in plain English. A neighbor's stroller in the next yard. Caption: "Every load of laundry sends these out the vent. Your neighbor's baby breathes it in."

CATEGORY 3: THE SPRAY YOU USE ON THE COUNTER WHERE SHE EATS

(Disinfectant sprays and multi-surface cleaners with quats)

(a) Ubiquity. GAP A pending. Lysol, Pine-Sol class.

(b) Chemicals of concern by name. Quaternary ammonium compounds, also called quats. Common ones: benzalkonium chloride, didecyldimethylammonium chloride.

(c) What it does in the body. [Grade 2] "Studies link this chemical to asthma and trouble getting pregnant." (NIEHS-funded work, Hrubec TC et al., Melin VE et al. GAP B to fill with exact study titles and DOIs before draft ships. Required pattern: "Linked to [outcome] in [study name + DOI]." No orphan claims.)

(d) What happens to the air when product is used. [Grade 2] "You spray it on the counter. The cloud floats. Your toddler is three feet away. She breathes it in."

(e) Regulatory status. GAP B + Gap N to fill. EU REACH Annex XVII restrictions on certain quats. CDC/NIOSH workplace exposure guidance.

(f) Visual concept. A spray bottle puffs a cloud over a kitchen counter. A pregnant mom holds a toddler 3 feet away. Red arrow from cloud to her lungs. Caption: "Studies link this chemical to asthma and trouble getting pregnant."

CATEGORY 4: THE PLUG IN IN YOUR HALLWAY

(Air fresheners, plug-ins, gels, sprays)

(a) Ubiquity. GAP A pending. Glade, Febreze, Yankee class.

(b) Chemicals of concern by name. Phthalates (DEHP, DBP, DEP). Benzene. Formaldehyde (secondary). Limonene and pinene (terpenes).

(c) What it does in the body. [Grade 2] "A nonprofit lab tested 14 air fresheners from a normal grocery store. 12 of them had a chemical called phthalates inside." (GAP D, NRDC 2007 "Clearing the Air," to pull directly from NRDC. Not from EWG.) "California lists phthalates as a chemical known to cause birth defects." (Prop 65: DEHP listed 1987, DBP listed 2005, BBP listed 2005. GAP H confirm.)

(d) What happens to the air when product is used. [Grade 2] "The EPA says plug in air fresheners spray bad stuff into your home all day. They never stop." (EPA verbatim: air fresheners release pollutants "more or less continuously." U.S. EPA, Inside Story Guide to Indoor Air Quality, https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/inside-story-guide-indoor-air-quality.)

(e) Regulatory status. DEHP, DBP, BBP: Prop 65 listed (dates above pending GAP H confirm). Benzene: Prop 65 listed 1987. Formaldehyde: NTP "known to be a human carcinogen." EU REACH Annex XVII restricts DEHP, DBP, BBP in many uses (GAP N to scope).

(f) Visual concept. A row of 14 air fresheners on a store shelf. 12 of them have a red X stamped across them. Headline: "12 out of 14 had a chemical called phthalates inside."

CATEGORY 5: THE CANDLE ON THE COFFEE TABLE

(Scented candles)

(a) Ubiquity. GAP A pending.

(b) Chemicals of concern by name. Linalool. Around 60 different airborne chemicals total in one study.

(c) What it does in the body. [Grade 2] "In one peer reviewed study from 2025, scientists found that scented candles puffed out about 60 different chemicals into the air. Plain candles puffed out about 20. Linalool was the most common one." (Single-sample study, Tabuk, Saudi Arabia. Frame as "one study found." PMC12488666.)

(d) What happens to the air when product is used. [Grade 2] "Candle smoke is so tiny you cannot see it. It floats 20 feet across the room. It hangs in the air for half an hour after you blow it out." (Yun et al., 2025, Scientific Reports, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-95010-0. 6 meters converted to 20 feet for the mom reading this.)

(e) Regulatory status. Linalool: EU 2023/1545 expanded fragrance-allergen labeling (cosmetics only, see Section 3). Other VOCs identified in the study: case-by-case. GAP N for any candle-specific EU restriction.

(f) Visual concept. A blueprint style overhead view of a living room. A candle is lit on the coffee table. Concentric gray rings expand out, labeled "smoke at 3 feet," "smoke at 10 feet," "smoke at 20 feet." A child reading on the couch 15 feet away has a dotted line from the candle ring to her nose. Caption: "You light it. The smoke reaches her in seconds. It hangs in the air for 30 minutes after you blow it out."

3. WHAT EUROPE BANNED THAT AMERICA STILL SELLS

Only categories with a defensible EU restriction citation appear here. Cosmetics-only correction applied.

3.1 Fragrance allergen labeling (COSMETICS ONLY)

"Europe just made companies list 80 scent chemicals on the bottle. The U.S. still says 24." [Grade 2] Citation: EU Regulation 2023/1545, expanding fragrance allergen labeling from 24 to 80 substances under EC 1223/2009. Transition: 31 July 2026 (placing) and 31 July 2028 (availability). https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32023R1545 SCOPE CORRECTION (caveat in 8pt under the headline): This European rule covers personal care products only. Laundry soap, dryer sheets, and surface sprays are under different European rules (EC 648/2004 for detergents, REACH 1907/2006, CLP 1272/2008). Dish soap is NOT a cosmetic. It is a detergent under 648/2004 and CANNOT be claimed under 2023/1545.

3.2 Phthalates (DEHP, DBP, BBP) in non-cosmetic uses

GAP N pending. EU REACH Annex XVII restricts DEHP, DBP, and BBP in many consumer uses. Confirm exact entry number and scope before writing copy.

3.3 Triclosan in antibacterial soaps

GAP E pending. FDA banned 19 antibacterial soap ingredients in September 2016 (Federal Register). EU restrictions under Biocidal Products Regulation. Confirm specific EU citation before writing copy.

All other "banned in Europe" claims for laundry, dryer sheets, surface sprays, and air fresheners are HELD until a specific REACH Annex XVII entry, CLP classification, or 648/2004 restriction is documented with regulation number, year, scope, and Official Journal link.

5.7 ACETALDEHYDE hangover line: keep limited to "is emitted by." Do not extend to "causes hangover symptoms in users of dryer sheets."
