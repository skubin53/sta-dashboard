Go look at the bread on your counter.

If it has been sitting there a week and still has not molded, I do not want you to feel proud of how fresh it looks. I want you to feel a little uneasy. Because real food is alive. My bread molds in about four days. Store bread can sit out for weeks and stay soft, and that is not magic. That is chemistry built to keep it from rotting.

One of the reasons has a name. Potassium bromate. It is a dough conditioner that shows up in a lot of store flour and store bread. It is banned for food in the EU, the UK, Canada, and Brazil. Here in the US it is still perfectly legal. The World Health Organization group that studies this calls it possibly cancer causing, and California does not ban it until 2027.

The part that made me sit down was this. On July 13th Florida's Attorney General subpoenaed General Mills over potassium bromate in Pillsbury and Gold Medal flour. I want to be fair with you, because he was. His words were, nobody is getting sued today, yet. This is an investigation, not a lawsuit, and nothing is proven. But he chose to ask about this, in the flour that is in millions of kitchens right now.

And the thing that gets me most is that you cannot read your way out of it. The ingredient you would want to avoid is often not even printed on the label. So flipping the bag over and squinting at the fine print will not save you.

I never trusted this stuff. I figured it out young, with nobody guiding me, and it took me years of reading and asking questions most people never ask. You do not have to do it alone the way I did, because I already walked it.

So do not go shop the store looking for a better loaf. Go find a farm and get to know your farmer.
