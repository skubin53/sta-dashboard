## Fact-check pass, and one correction that mattered

Every claim was checked against the cited source rather than trusted. Results:

| Claim in post | Source says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| July 15 2026, *Applied Food Research*, lead author Selina Wang | matches | OK |
| 54 products tested, 89% adulterated | 48 of 54 = 89% | OK |
| 93% chips, 71% mayonnaise, 100% salad dressings | matches | OK |
| 20 olive oil products, only one failed | matches | OK |
| 2020: 82% rancid or mixed with other oils | matches | OK |
| Private label: 31% pure, 36% of advertised quality | matches | OK |
| Substitutes are sunflower, safflower, soybean | 2020 study names all three | OK |
| Aldi suit filed Sept 18 2024, N.D. Ill., Simply Nature | 1:24-cv-07095, matches | OK |
| **"In some tested bottles, no detectable avocado oil at all"** | **not in any of the three UC Davis sources** | **REMOVED** |

That last line was mine and it was not supported by anything we cite. It was
replaced with a claim the study does support: every salad dressing tested came back
mixed with something else. A second softer overstatement in the opening ("or not
really avocado oil at all") was tightened to "cut with cheaper oils that cost a
fraction of what you paid."

This is the whole reason the receipts get re-read before publish. The numbers were
all correct; the one invented flourish was the thing that would have cost trust.
Commit 992ddbce1.
