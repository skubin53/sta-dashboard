# The STA Story Framework

How every Switch to America blog post is built. One idea underneath it: fear gets the click, but the post has to walk her from scared to free before she will act. Every section moves her one step up. End on fear and she freezes and leaves.

## Two rules that never bend

1. **The reader is the hero. Shannon is the guide.** The mom does the thing. Shannon points at the path she has already walked and says "here is what I would do, and you are the kind of woman who will do it." Shannon never fixes it for her.
2. **Fear opens the door but never closes the post.** Safety fear is what makes her click. So every scary beat is followed by a step up: a plan, other moms doing it, a win, or who she becomes.

## The four steps up (in her language, not Maslow's)

You walk her from one felt state to the next, one section at a time.
1. "Is my family safe from something I cannot see?" The felt need behind the click. Meet her here first, every time.
2. "I am not the crazy one. Other moms see it too." What the community sells.
3. "I am the mom who catches what others miss." Self-respect. Where the low-ticket offers convert.
4. "I am a woman who protects her family and lives free." The summit. Not a product. The whole point.

## The daily 8-step checklist (start here every morning)

1. Pick ONE villain and ONE real receipt for the Proof section. Web-verify the link and quote first.
2. Answer capsule: her real kitchen this morning, then the crack. Lead with how it feels, not the chemical. Relieve the pressure, do not pile on.
3. H2 #1: name the villain plainly, land the shock, hand the receipt to the Proof section.
4. H2 #2: say her real objection out loud ("it is in everything, I cannot fix it all"), agree, then shrink it to "find your worst room first."
5. Comparison table: villain on the left, cleaner swap on the right.
6. Shannon's Advice box: the guide beat (see below). NOT a fake conversion story.
7. FAQ: clear the leftover fear, then plant "you are not the crazy one," "you will be the one who caught it," and "this is who you become."
8. Close on the identity line, then the scan CTA. Run every voice guardrail before publishing.

## What each part of the post is FOR

**Answer capsule (first 100 words) = her kitchen, then the crack.** Put her in her own kitchen this morning. The clean counter, the "healthy" label, the lunch she packed. Everything looks like a good mom did it right. Lead with the feeling, that quiet worry she cannot name, then the short true answer with the crack in it: the fine-looking thing is the threat. Name the villain so the dread has a target. Relieve fast, do not stack fear.

**Question-form H2s = the climb, one step each.**
- H2 #1 (the shock): "What is actually in ___?" Name the villain, point to the receipt. Safety, made concrete.
- H2 #2 (her objection, on purpose): "Isn't this in everything anyway?" Agree, then shrink the mountain to "your worst one thing first." This is where you rescue her from paralysis, the real sale-killer.
- H2 #3 (understand and decide): the comparison table lives here. She can see the trade.
- H2 #4 (you are not alone): other moms are doing this. Belonging.
- H2 #5 (who you become): the swap makes her the capable one. Self-respect, climbing toward free.

**Comparison table = proof the road is walkable.** Villain on the left, cleaner swap on the right. She sees the trade, so it stops being a cloud of dread and becomes a choice she can make.

**Proof section = the receipt.** Real lawsuits, recalls, fines. Category-matched, hyperlinked, attributed as allegation, never as settled fact. Turns an invisible threat into something she can point at. Carries the authority so Shannon's voice never has to cite anyone.

**Shannon's Advice box (dark) = meeting the guide.** Empathy first, but TRUE to Shannon. She never bought this stuff, so never write a fake "I used to buy it, then I stopped" story. Her empathy: she figured it out young, on her own, with no one to guide her, and she knows how rigged it is against a busy mom. Her authority: 34 years off the grocery stores, sourcing from local farms. Then the one swap she would make. She points at the path, she does not walk it for the reader. No agencies in her voice, that flips her from trusted mom to institution and breaks the "not alone" feeling.

**FAQ = clear the rest, then plant the higher steps.** Knock out the leftover safety worries. Then use two or three answers to plant "you are not the crazy one," "you will be the one who caught it," and "this is who you become."

**Scan CTA = her first real step out the door.** Every post drives to scan.ismyhometoxic.com/now. Frame it as "you do not overhaul your house tonight, you get 60 seconds of truth about your worst room." Small, free, no shame.

**Close on identity, not the offer.** End every major post on who she becomes, then the scan link. "This isn't about a cleaner ___. It is about being the kind of woman who protects her family and lives free."

## Voice guardrails (the no-AI-tell lock)

- Talk in a story with lived specifics. A real morning, a real swap, a real receipt.
- One villain per post, plainly named. One receipt, not ten. Ten toxins triggers paralysis.
- Reader is the hero. Shannon guides, never rescues.
- No em-dashes. No rule-of-three tricolons. No "here's the thing." No fake build-up with a bow on it.
- Shannon never cites agencies in her own voice. The receipts live in the linked Proof section.
- Never tell her to "read the label" as the fix. The label is a lie. The fix is always the same: find a LOCAL FARM and get to know the farmer, ask how they grow it, because that relationship is how she knows her food is safe. NEVER send her to a mill, a bakery, a co-op, or a store.
- Lead the emotional hook with the internal problem (the worry, the guilt, feeling out of control), not the chemical.
- Full voice rules: see INSTRUCTIONS-FOR-BLOG-CLAUDE.md and the Shannon's Advice voice guidance.

## Worked example: the potassium bromate in flour post

One villain: potassium bromate. One receipt (web-verify a live, category-matched source, attributed as allegation).

**Answer capsule (her kitchen, then the crack):** You made toast this morning. Maybe a sandwich for the lunchbox. The bread says "enriched," the flour bag says "unbleached," and you have bought both for years without a second thought. Here is the short version. A lot of that flour and bread has potassium bromate in it, a dough conditioner that makes the loaf rise higher and look prettier on the shelf. Most of the world will not let bakers use it in food anymore. It is still legal here, still in bags sitting in American pantries right now, and most moms have no idea it is the thing they have been trusting.

**H2 #1 (the call):** What is potassium bromate, and why is it in my flour? Plain answer: it is a chemical that lets factories rush bread and get a taller, whiter loaf for less money. That is the whole reason it is in your kid's sandwich. Name it, land the shock, hand it to the Proof section.

**H2 #2 (her objection, defused):** "Isn't this in everything? Why bother?" You are right that you cannot fix it all tonight. So do not. You do not overhaul your pantry. You find your worst one thing first, the one you use every single day. For a lot of families that is the bread and the flour. Start there.

**Comparison table:** bromate flour and factory bread on the left, a clean bag of real flour from a local farm on the right.

**Proof section:** the real ban or state action, hyperlinked, attributed as allegation.

**Shannon's Advice box (meeting the guide, in Shannon's true story, no fake conversion):**
Nobody handed me this. I figured it out young, on my own, back when there was no one to ask and no label worth trusting. What I learned early is that the answer is never in the aisle. The stuff you would want to avoid is not on the label, and they stamp "natural" on the front and count on you being too busy to fight it. So I stopped fighting it. I do not buy my food from those places at all. My flour is spelt, straight from a farm, the same grain people baked with for thousands of years before a factory ever touched it. My kids grew up on real food and they are healthy. You do not have to fix your whole kitchen by Friday. Find one local farm near you and get to know the farmer who grows your food. That is the first switch, and you are already the kind of woman who will make it, or you would not have read this far.

**FAQ:** clear the leftover worry (does baking cook it off, is my brand affected), then plant it: you are not paranoid, a lot of us eat this way; you are about to be the one in your family who caught it; this is the start of a home you actually trust.

**Close (identity, then CTA):** This was never really about a bag of flour. It is about being the woman who protects her family and does not hand that job to a factory.
