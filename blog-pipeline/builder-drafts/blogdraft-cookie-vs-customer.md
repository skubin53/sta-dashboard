**body:**

A creator I know woke up one morning in April to the same audience she had in March and about half the income. She had not changed a thing. Amazon had.

Around March 9, Amazon Associates cut commission rates across a long list of categories, some of them close to half. Then on April 14 they changed the rule underneath everyone. You now earn only on the exact item a person clicks, the specific product, and nothing else they load into the cart on that same visit. For years, that cart credit was where a lot of affiliate income quietly lived. One policy update, and it was gone.

If that happened to you, read this part slowly. The problem was never your content. The problem is what you were being paid on.

## What a cookie actually is

An affiliate link drops a cookie. Someone clicks, maybe they buy, and you get paid once. Then the window closes and you are back at zero, waiting on the next click.

That is the whole model, everywhere you look right now.

Amazon pays on a single item, one time. LTK and ShopMy pay you when the sale happens, and then the transaction is finished. TikTok Shop commissions swing with the platform and whatever promotion is running that week. Different logos, same shape. You are renting attention to a company that keeps the customer and can rewrite your cut whenever it suits them.

You did the work of the recommendation. They kept the relationship.

## What if you were paid on the customer

Now picture the other model.

Instead of a click that expires, you introduce someone to a product they genuinely like. They set up their own shop, they reorder next month because they ran out, and again the month after that. You stay credited as the person who brought them in for as long as they keep shopping. You are not chasing a fresh click every day just to stand in place. You did the work once and it keeps counting.

Here is the honest comparison.

| | The cookie | The customer |
|---|---|---|
| You earn on | the one item clicked | what your customer reorders |
| How long it lasts | a short cookie window | as long as they keep shopping |
| Next month | back to zero | the customer is still there |
| Who sets the terms | the platform, overnight | a relationship you keep |
| What you are building | traffic you re-earn daily | a customer base that stays |

I am not going to hand you an income promise, because that is exactly the kind of talk that gets creators burned. What I am pointing at is structure. One model pays you for a moment. The other pays you for a relationship.

## Residual is not magic either

Getting paid on reorders is not a golden ticket by itself. Plenty of companies built on that promise have fallen apart. Modere shut down without warning in April 2025. Sipology closed this spring and told its sellers the commissions they were owed would not be paid. Forever Living ended new US enrollment on May 1 and froze its legacy bonus programs.

So the model is not the whole answer. What matters is whether real people actually reorder the products because they want them, and whether the company behind it has been around long enough, and is honest enough about its numbers, to still be standing in ten years.

## You are not the only one who felt this

If April rattled you, look around. Every corner of the creator world is quietly re-reading its own dashboards right now. You are not behind and you are not naive. You built a real audience on a payout system that was never built to be loyal to you. That is worth sitting with, and it is worth fixing.
