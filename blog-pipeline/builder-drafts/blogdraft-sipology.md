If you were a Sipology consultant, you already know how this one ended, and I'm sorry.

The company closed in April 2026, and consultants were told that outstanding commissions would not be paid. That's a hard thing to say out loud, so let me say it plainly for you: you did the work, and money you earned isn't coming. Being told that after you showed up, hosted the parties, and took care of your customers is a real loss, and it's okay to be angry about it.

I'm not going to dress this up or tell you it happened for a reason. Sometimes a company just closes and the people who built it from the ground level are the last ones considered. What I can do is talk honestly about what comes next, whenever you're ready to think about it.

## Grieve it first. Then take inventory.

Before you jump into anything, give yourself a minute. You're allowed to be done for a week. When you're ready, take a quiet inventory of what you're actually walking away with, because it's more than you think.

You have a customer list of people who trusted your taste and your recommendations. You have the ability to host, to follow up, to make a newcomer feel welcome, to keep a team going on a slow month. You know how to sell without being slimy about it. None of that closed in April. All of it came home with you.

## What to look for before you trust another company

The hard part about starting over is that trust feels expensive now. So use that. Be harder to win over than you were the first time. Here's what I'd hold out for:

**Products people buy because they work, not because there's a party.** Look for everyday household items a family actually finishes and reorders on their own. If sales would keep happening even when no one is "running a business," the demand is real. That's the difference between a product and a promotion.

**A structure that rewards helping customers, not just recruiting.** Ask directly: where does the money come from? If an opportunity mostly pays for signing up the next person, that's the fragile kind. If it pays you for taking care of real customers who stick around, that's the steadier kind.

**Recurring referral income you can count on.** The version of this work that lasts pays you month after month when the people you introduced keep buying things they genuinely use. It's not fast and it's not flashy, but it doesn't vanish overnight either.

**A company that answers straight questions.** After what you have been through, you have every right to ask for plain answers before you commit to anyone. Ask who makes the products, whether there is a monthly requirement, and what happens to your customers if you walk away. If someone dodges that, believe the dodge.

## You get to choose on purpose this time

There's no rush here. The consultants who come out of a closure in good shape are usually the ones who slowed down, asked sharper questions, and picked the next thing deliberately instead of grabbing the first hand that reached out. You're allowed to take your time and be choosy. After April, you've more than earned it.

And you don't have to sort it out by yourself.
