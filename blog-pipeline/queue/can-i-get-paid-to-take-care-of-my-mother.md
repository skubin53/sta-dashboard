<h2 id="medicare">Will Medicare pay you to take care of your mother?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no. Since 2024 it pays the person who trains you, and that is all.</p>

<p>Your mother fell in October. You live closest, and so it is you.</p>

<p>You leave work early on Tuesdays now. You drive to her house, run her bath and warm her soup. You sit with her until she nods off in the chair. Her week runs your week, not the other way around.</p>

<p>So you type the question into your phone at her kitchen counter. Will anyone pay me for this?</p>

<p>Medicare says no. Its <a href="https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/home-health-services">home health page</a> also lists "24-hour-a-day care at your home" as not covered.</p>

<p>There is one change. <a href="https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2024-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule">Since January 1, 2024</a>, Medicare pays when "practitioners train caregivers." That money goes to her doctor or therapist. It does not go to you.</p>

<h2 id="medicaid">Who does pay a daughter to care for her mother?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: some state Medicaid programs. The state sets the rules, and a payroll company cuts the check.</p>

<p>Some states have a program for this. Your mother hires her own helper, and the helper can be you.</p>

<p>New York calls it CDPAP. <a href="https://www.health.ny.gov/health_care/medicaid/program/longterm/cdpap/">The state health department says</a> the helper "can include a friend or family member." A spouse cannot be hired. A daughter can, as long as she is not also her mother's designated representative.</p>

<p>Your mother must be on Medicaid and need help with daily tasks.</p>

<p>And then the state picks who pays you. New York's page says "all CDPAP recipients are required to work with PPL." That is Public Partnerships LLC.</p>

<p>You did not choose PPL. Your mother did not either.</p>

<h2 id="ppl">What happened to the caregivers New York paid through PPL?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: they sued. PPL settled for $162 million and denies any wrongdoing.</p>

<h3>The lawsuit</h3>

<p>Four caregivers sued PPL in federal court in Brooklyn in 2025. The case is Calderon v. Public Partnerships, LLC.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.publicpartnershipssettlement.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Calderon-Long-Form-Notice_Final.pdf">The court notice says</a> they claim PPL "failed to pay workers all legally owed wages on time and at the correct rate."</p>

<h3>Who it covers</h3>

<p>The class is about 200,000 caregivers paid by PPL. They worked in New York City, Long Island or Westchester between March 1, 2025 and April 30, 2026.</p>

<h3>What PPL says</h3>

<p>PPL "disputes the allegations in the lawsuit and denies any wrongdoing." The notice also says the court never decided who was right.</p>

<h3>What the judge did</h3>

<p><a href="https://legalaidnyc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/261110-caledron-final-release.pdf">On November 10, 2026</a>, a federal judge gave final approval to the $162 million settlement.</p>

<p>One caregiver in the case said it plain: "We still had to show up every day because somebody depended on us."</p>

<h3>The second case</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-files-suit-stop-ongoing-medicaid-fraud-related-new-yorks-10-billion-home">On June 16, 2026</a>, the Justice Department sued PPL and New York's health department. It alleges PPL was picked through "a sham bid process."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.homehealthcarenews.com/2026/07/ppl-seeks-to-dismiss-ny-cdpap-case/">PPL has asked the court to dismiss it</a>, calling it "an act of gross overreach." Those are allegations, not findings.</p>

<h2 id="a-paycheck">Isn't a paycheck from the state still a paycheck?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, and for a lot of families it keeps Mom at home. It just is not yours to steer.</p>

<p>I know what you are thinking. Somebody pays you to do what you already do. Why would you turn that down?</p>

<p>You should not, if she qualifies. CDPAP is real and it is legal. It lets a daughter stay close. I will not tell you to skip it.</p>

<p>But look at who holds the pieces. The state sets your hours and your rate. A company you did not pick runs your payroll. And the day her care ends, so does the check.</p>

<h2 id="this-week">What would I do first this week?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: find her state's program, write down what you do, and keep one door that is yours.</p>

<h3>Call the Eldercare Locator</h3>

<p><a href="https://eldercare.acl.gov/">The Eldercare Locator</a> is run by the federal Administration for Community Living. Call 1-800-677-1116. Ask what her state has for a family member who cares at home.</p>

<h3>Ask her Medicaid office one question</h3>

<p>Ask: "Can my mother hire me through a self-directed program?" Then ask who runs the payroll. Write down the name.</p>

<h3>Keep a notebook on her counter</h3>

<p>Each week, write the dates and what you did. The baths, the meals, the rides, the night you sat up with her. If anyone ever asks, your answer is already written.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are women asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>Does Medicare pay family caregivers at all?</strong></p>
<p>No. Medicare does not cover personal care like bathing and dressing when that is the only care she needs. Since 2024 it pays her doctor or therapist to train you. That money goes to them, not to you.</p>

<p><strong>Can I get paid if I am her daughter?</strong></p>
<p>In some states, yes. New York's CDPAP lets a Medicaid member hire a family member, but not a spouse. Each state sets its own rules, so call her Medicaid office.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to quit my job to take care of her?</strong></p>
<p>No. AARP's 2025 report says six in 10 family caregivers also hold a job. Half of those report work disruptions, like going in late or leaving early.</p>

<p><strong>Where do I start if I do not know her state's program?</strong></p>
<p>Call the Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116. It connects you to services for older adults in her area. Ask about programs for a family member who cares at home.</p>
