<h2 id="claim-form">Is there a CeraVe claim form?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no form and no settlement. The judgment went to L'Oreal on September 16, 2026.</p>

<p>You saw the post on your phone about CeraVe and cancer. A CeraVe holiday set was already in your cart. You have trusted that name for years. No shame in that.</p>

<p>And so you stood at the bathroom vanity longer than you meant to, tube in one hand, phone in the other. Am I owed money? Is this safe on my face?</p>

<img src="https://scan.ismyhometoxic.com/blog-images/isthereacer-villain-v1.webp" alt="A tube of acne foaming cream cleanser standing alone on a bathroom vanity in cold, flat light, with nobody in the frame" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px;">

<p>The money first. A claim form comes after a settlement or a win. <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.619298/gov.uscourts.nysd.619298.81.0.pdf">This case had neither.</a> So if a page offers you one, close it.</p>

<p>The plaintiffs, the people who sued, are not finished. <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/68426980/in-re-loreal-benzoyl-peroxide-products-litigation/?order_by=desc">The court record shows they asked the judge to reconsider on September 29.</a> L'Oreal argued against it on October 14. A win there would restart the fight. It would not mail you a check.</p>

<h2 id="why-dismissed">Why did the judge throw the case out?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: L'Oreal won on federal rules. The judge never weighed a test of any tube.</p>

<p><a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.619298/gov.uscourts.nysd.619298.80.0.pdf">Judge Analisa Torres wrote that L'Oreal "concedes that its BPO Products do not contain any mention of benzene."</a> She meant the label. BPO is benzoyl peroxide, the acne medicine.</p>

<p>L'Oreal argued the federal acne rule "does not require any warnings" about benzene. She agreed.</p>

<p>The court calls that kind of rule a "recipe." Think of a recipe card. Follow the card, and no state can make you add a line.</p>

<h3 id="two-ppm">The 2 ppm number you keep seeing</h3>

<p>The plaintiffs said a federal cap held benzene to 2 parts per million, or 2 ppm.</p>

<p><strong>Her answer: "there exists no federal prohibition against the BPO Products containing 2 ppm of benzene."</strong> She called their sources "non-binding guidance."</p>

<p>That number is all over your phone. In this case, nobody had to follow it.</p>

<p>So did L'Oreal prove the tube is clean? It did not have to. At this stage the court "assumes that all facts alleged" by the plaintiffs are true.</p>

<p><a href="https://beautymatter.com/articles/federal-judge-tells-loreal-plaintiffs-to-take-their-ball-and-go-home">BeautyMatter, a beauty trade site, said</a> the ruling "doesn't resolve whether BPO products actually pose a benzene risk." And so your second question stands.</p>

<h2 id="is-it-safe">Is my CeraVe face wash safe?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no court has answered that. Only two CeraVe washes were ever in this case.</p>

<p>The order names one La Roche-Posay treatment and two CeraVe washes. One is Acne Foam Cream Cleanser (4% benzoyl peroxide), and your tube may say Foaming. The other is Acne Foaming Cream Wash (10%).</p>

<p>If your holiday set has neither wash, this case was never about your set. That does not clear it.</p>

<p>If you own one, I cannot tell you what is in it. L'Oreal's label will not either. That is the whole problem.</p>

<h3 id="benzene">Benzene: what the lawsuit alleged</h3>

<p>Benzene is in gasoline fumes and cigarette smoke. <a href="https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/benzene">The National Cancer Institute says</a> exposure "increases the risk of developing leukemia and other blood disorders."</p>

<p>The plaintiffs allege these products hold benzene or break down into it. They blamed heat, in how L'Oreal made and stored them. That is a claim. No judge ruled on it.</p>

<h2 id="recall">Was any of this recalled?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: CeraVe is not on the March 2025 recall notice. One batch of L'Oreal's La Roche-Posay Effaclar Duo is.</p>

<p>The third product in this lawsuit did get pulled. <a href="https://www.nbcchicago.com/news/local/la-roche-posay-proactiv-products-recalled-over-levels-of-cancer-causing-ingredient/3695669/">NBC Chicago reported</a> a March 2025 recall of Effaclar Duo lot MYX46W "due to elevated levels of benzene."</p>

<p><a href="https://fortune.com/well/2025/03/10/loreal-recall-effaclar-duo-acne-treatment-cancer-linked-chemical-benzene">L'Oreal said testing found trace levels in one lot.</a> It pulled the remaining units of that formula from stores.</p>

<p>Stores were told to pull it. Shoppers were not asked to bring theirs back.</p>

<p>CeraVe is not on that list. That tells you what was pulled, not what stayed on the shelf.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for L'Oreal?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, three fair points. One line in the order bothers me more.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> The judge found L'Oreal "complied with its federal labeling requirements." That is a real ruling.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> She called the recall a "one-off voluntary recall of a single lot." No plaintiff claimed to have bought that lot.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> No court has found that any CeraVe tube hurt anyone. And nothing here is a case against treating your acne.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p><strong>Because the same order says L'Oreal "was permitted to add additional, accurate information" to its labels, as long as it fit the federal acne rule.</strong> It could have said more. That doesn't sound right, does it?</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Does CeraVe cause cancer?</strong></p>
<p>No court has found that. The plaintiffs alleged benzene, and the case ended on federal rules before anyone had to answer.</p>

<p><strong>Was there another CeraVe lawsuit?</strong></p>
<p>Yes, but it is closed. <a href="https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/raymundo-v-loreal-complaint.pdf">A California woman sued on July 9, 2026</a> over the price of two CeraVe baby products. <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73598083/raymundo-v-loreal-usa-sd-inc/">She dropped it on September 11.</a></p>

<p><strong>What if an acne product hurt me?</strong></p>
<p>You were right to ask. This case was about money, not injuries. If an acne drug hurt you, you could still sue over the injury. The judge wrote that these federal rules would not block it.</p>
