<h3>Start with Johnson &amp; Johnson</h3>

<p>J&amp;J made the baby powder many of us grew up with.</p>

<ul>
<li>In 2020, J&amp;J stopped selling its talc baby powder in the US and Canada. <a href="https://www.jnj.com/our-company/johnson-johnson-consumer-health-announces-discontinuation-of-talc-based-johnsons-baby-powder-in-u-s-and-canada">J&amp;J's statement</a>. In 2023 it switched to cornstarch worldwide. <a href="https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-consumer-health-to-transition-global-baby-powder-portfolio-to-cornstarch">J&amp;J's announcement</a>.</li>
<li>Tens of thousands of people have sued, saying the talc powder caused their cancer. In July 2026, J&amp;J offered $5.5 billion to settle about 76,000 ovarian cancer claims. The deal is not final yet. <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/200406/000020040626000155/exhibit991.htm">J&amp;J's filing</a>. J&amp;J denies the claims and says its talc is safe.</li>
<li>In December 2025, a Baltimore jury awarded more than $1.5 billion to a woman who said the company's talc gave her mesothelioma, a rare cancer. J&amp;J is appealing. <a href="https://thedailyrecord.com/2025/12/23/baltimore-jury-johnson-johnson-talc-mesothelioma-verdict/">The Daily Record</a>.</li>
<li>In 2021, J&amp;J recalled five Neutrogena and Aveeno spray sunscreens after its own tests found low levels of benzene in some samples. Benzene is known to cause cancer. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/johnson-johnson-consumer-inc-issues-voluntary-recall-specific-neutrogenar-and-aveenor-aerosol">The recall notice</a>.</li>
