<h2 id="if-you-stop">What happens to your paycheck if you stop for 90 days?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it stops. Federal law can hold a job for 12 weeks, and that leave is unpaid.</p>

<p>Start with a job. <a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-825.100">The federal family leave rule gives you up to 12 workweeks a year</a>, if you qualify. It covers caring for a sick parent, or yourself. It calls that time "job-protected, unpaid leave."</p>

<p>So your desk waits. Your paycheck does not, unless you saved up paid leave first.</p>

<h2 id="what-they-sold">What did Publishing.com promise would keep paying you?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: "substantial passive income" from books, the FTC alleged. It says the course alone usually cost $1,995.</p>

<p>Publishing.com sold courses on writing e-books and audiobooks to sell on Amazon and other sites. Since 2018, the FTC says, thousands bought them.</p>

<p>The pitch often started with a free video, the FTC says. <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2423055c4836publishingcomfinalcomplaint.pdf">The FTC's complaint quotes one from May 2024:</a> "One upload away from, say, leaving your 9 to 5 job forever."</p>

<p>Hear that from the hospital parking lot. It sells you the thing you are scared you do not have.</p>

<p>Then coaches offered an add-on called Publishing Accelerator, the FTC says, usually for $9,800.</p>

<p>And the result? The FTC alleged most buyers "never made the income the company promised in its advertising." Its complaint quotes one buyer: "I didn't make any money from their program." Most is not all. The charge is about the promise.</p>

<p>Publishing.com agreed to pay $1.5 million, without admitting or denying the charges. Then there was the guarantee.</p>

<h2 id="refund">What did "no questions asked" turn out to mean?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: for a while, a refund after day three meant publishing a whole book first, the FTC says.</p>

<p>The FTC quotes emails promising a 12 month guarantee, "no questions asked." It says the real terms sat deep inside a long terms of service page.</p>

<p>From October 2024 to March 2025, the FTC says, the terms set a hard rule for buyers past day three. To be considered for a refund, a buyer had to "complete, produce, and publish a book at least 25,000 words in length." A two hour audiobook also counted.</p>

<p>Two buyers asked for refunds, the FTC says. They were told they would also owe the company 5% of what their books earned. It says that rule was in no ad and not in the terms.</p>

<p>Getting the money back, by the FTC's account, could take a whole book.</p>

<p>The FTC also says some glowing video reviews came from employees, unmarked until at least December 2024. Others, it says, came from a contest with prizes, including cash.</p>

<h2 id="this-week">What would I do this week?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: write down what pays you at day 90, then ask three questions of anything new.</p>

<h3>Write down what still pays you in month three</h3>

<p>Get a pad and a pen. Write what comes in if you stop on Monday. Then what still comes in at day 90. Paid leave you have saved counts. A hope does not.</p>

<h3>Find out where your leave stands</h3>

<p>If you have a job, ask HR in writing. Do I qualify for family leave, and how much paid leave have I saved? Ask now, while nothing is wrong.</p>

<h3>Ask three questions of anything new</h3>

<p>Where are the refund terms, in writing, before I pay? Who are the people in the reviews, and were any of them paid? If I stop for 90 days, whose customer is she?</p>

<p>Take those to every offer, mine included. If somebody dodges one, that is your answer.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are you asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>If I stop working for three months, do I still get paid?</strong></p>
<p>Usually not, unless you saved paid leave or something besides you keeps working. Federal family leave can hold a job for 12 weeks if you qualify, and it is unpaid. Social Security disability does not cover a three month illness.</p>

<p><strong>Is passive income even real?</strong></p>
<p>Income that needs nobody is rare, and the ads know you want it. The FTC alleged most buyers of Publishing.com's system never made the income it promised. Ask for proof before you pay, not after.</p>

<p><strong>What should I ask before I pay for an income course?</strong></p>
<p>Ask for proof of any income claim, the refund terms in writing, and who is in the reviews. The FTC's case against Publishing.com covered exactly those three. If the answers do not come in writing, keep your card in your wallet.</p>

<p><strong>Will my customers wait for me if I take time off?</strong></p>
<p>Some will and some will not. Nobody honest can promise you either way. What you can check today is whether they are yours on paper, or the platform's.</p>
