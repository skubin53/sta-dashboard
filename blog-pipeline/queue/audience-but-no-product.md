<h2 id="who-owns-it">Who actually owns the room you built?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: Meta does. Section 4.2 of its Facebook terms lets it close your account.</p>

<p>You finish the video. You switch off the ring light and set the phone face down on the counter. Somebody out there watched every minute, and you have nothing of your own to hand her.</p>

<figure style="margin:28px 0;">
<img src="https://scan.ismyhometoxic.com/blog-images/audiencebut-villain-v1.webp" alt="An empty modern kitchen island with the ring light switched off, a phone lying face down beside its empty tripod, a wiped cutting board and vegetable peelings" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px;">
</figure>

<p>What you are sitting on is not small. You spent years answering questions at night and remembering names. That trust is the hard part, and it is done.</p>

<p>Nobody reads you the fine print when you open the account. So here it is. <a href="https://www.facebook.com/terms/">Meta's Facebook terms have been in effect since January 2025.</a> Say Meta decides you seriously or repeatedly broke its rules. It makes that call "in our discretion." Then it "may permanently disable or delete your account."</p>

<p>In our discretion means theirs. Not yours.</p>

<p>And so why does Meta want your group on its land at all? <a href="https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results/default.aspx">Meta's own year-end report shows $196,175 million from ads in 2025, out of $200,966 million in total.</a></p>

<h2 id="gets-it-wrong">What happens when Meta gets it wrong?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the room goes dark. By Meta's own count, one to two removals in ten may have been wrong.</p>

<p>This is Meta grading Meta. <a href="https://about.fb.com/news/2025/01/meta-more-speech-fewer-mistakes/">In January 2025 it wrote</a> about December 2024. That month it removed "millions of pieces of content every day." Then this: "we think one to two out of every 10 of these actions may have been mistakes."</p>

<p>That was one piece of content at a time. In June 2025 it was whole groups.</p>

<p><a href="https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/24/facebook-group-admins-complain-of-mass-bans-meta-says-its-fixing-the-problem/">TechCrunch reported</a>, from what admins shared, that thousands of Facebook Groups were suspended in the U.S. and abroad. Groups for dog and cat owners. An interior design group that served millions.</p>

<p>Admins said they got notices about terrorism or nudity their groups had never posted. Meta's spokesperson told TechCrunch, "We're aware of a technical error that impacted some Facebook Groups. We're fixing things now."</p>

<p>A technical error. Your room, dark, and you had done nothing wrong.</p>

<p>Some admins did get help. TechCrunch says some who paid for Meta Verified got through. That plan "includes priority customer support." <a href="https://www.meta.com/meta-verified/">Meta's own page lists the Standard plan at $14.99 a month per profile.</a> One thing it sells is "Chat or email with agents 24/7."</p>

<p>So the room is free until something breaks. Then a person costs you every month.</p>

<p>Even Meta's outside review panel, the Oversight Board, has noticed. <a href="https://www.oversightboard.com/news/board-upholds-ban-in-first-accounts-case-but-calls-out-systemic-human-rights-concerns/">In June 2026 it wrote</a> that it "has due process concerns relating to how Meta disables user accounts." In plain words, you deserve to know the rules and the reasons, and a fair way to appeal.</p>

<p>And so you set the phone down for a minute. Just long enough to decide what is yours.</p>

<h2 id="hand-them">What can you hand them that is yours?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: something they use up, where the customer stays tied to you and not to a store.</p>

<p>You will be offered three things.</p>

<h3>The link</h3>

<p>An affiliate link counts one sale. The store keeps her name, her address and her next order. Next month you start from zero.</p>

<h3>Your own product</h3>

<p>This is real work. You pay up front for stock, jars, labels and shipping. You handle the returns, and if a batch goes wrong, your name is on it.</p>

<p>If that sounds like fun to you, build it. Your people will still be there.</p>

<h3>The reorder</h3>

<p>This is the one I care about. Dish soap, shampoo, laundry soap, the cleaner under the sink. She runs out and orders again, and she stays your customer when she does.</p>

<h2 id="fixing-it">Isn't Meta already fixing this?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly. Meta says its wrong takedowns fell by about half, and the rules are still its own.</p>

<p>Here is the part I know you are thinking. Your group has been fine for years. Meta fixed the June mess. Why move anything?</p>

<p>That is fair. <a href="https://about.fb.com/news/2025/01/meta-more-speech-fewer-mistakes/">Meta reported</a> "a roughly 50% reduction in enforcement mistakes" in the United States from late 2024 to early 2025. In plain words, about half as many wrong takedowns. That is a real change.</p>

<p>And the Oversight Board's first account case went Meta's way. That account had posted severe threats of violence against a journalist. The board said shutting it was correct.</p>

<p>So no, Meta is not coming for your page. Half as many mistakes is still mistakes, though, and the rules are still Meta's to change.</p>

<p>I will not pretend what I do is safe from everything either. A company built on reorders can close too. Any of them can.</p>

<p>What I can tell you is how it works here. Switch to America is the maker. No middlemen, no markup. I will not hand you a number to expect. Nobody honest can.</p>

<p>You are not the only one who worries about losing a page. The Oversight Board says it has had "innumerable complaints" since 2020. They came from users who lost access to their accounts.</p>

<h2 id="this-week">What would I do first this week?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: ask for a way to reach them, then pick the one thing they use up.</p>

<h3>Ask for a spare key</h3>

<p>Put one plain line under your next post. Ask them to message you their email address. Say why: so you can still reach them if this page ever goes dark. It works like a spare key to your own front door.</p>

<h3>Write down what they already ask you</h3>

<p>Go through your comments and messages. Which soap, which shampoo, what you wipe the counter with, what you wash the sheets in. Those questions are your product list, and you did not have to invent it.</p>

<h3>Ask the hard questions first</h3>

<p>Of me too. What does it cost to start, all in. What happens to my customers if I stop. Where is the income disclosure, and can I read it today.</p>

<p>If somebody dodges one, that is your answer.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else might you be wondering?</h2>

<p><strong>Could my group really just disappear?</strong></p>
<p>Meta's terms say it can act "in our discretion." In June 2025 TechCrunch reported thousands of groups suspended in what Meta called a technical error. So yes, it can happen, and it has.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to leave Facebook or Instagram?</strong></p>
<p>No. Keep posting what you post. Just stop keeping the only copy of your people on somebody else's land.</p>

<p><strong>How big does my audience need to be?</strong></p>
<p>Smaller than you think. A link needs a crowd, because it counts once. A reorder needs trust, and you already have that.</p>

<p><strong>What if I want my own product one day?</strong></p>
<p>Then build it. A list you hold comes with you, and so does the trust. Nothing here gets in the way of that.</p>
