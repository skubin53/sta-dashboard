<h2 id="going-away">Is your mom's Medicare Advantage plan really going away?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: maybe. It depends on her plan and her county. Her plan sends the letter that tells her, and it comes in October.</p>

<p>Your mom calls after supper. She has a letter from her plan. Her voice goes small when she gets to the part about next year.</p>

<p>If her plan is leaving, that letter has a name. It is <a href="https://www.medicare.gov/basics/forms-publications-mailings/mailings/costs-and-coverage/change-in-plan-availability">the Plan Non-Renewal Notice</a>. The page for it says what it means: "You must look for a new plan for coverage next year."</p>

<p>There is a second letter, and it is the gentler one. <a href="https://www.medicare.gov/basics/forms-publications-mailings/mailings/costs-and-coverage/upcoming-plan-changes">The Annual Notice of Change</a> comes in September. It lists what changes in January.</p>

<p>So the first job is small. Find out which letter is on her counter.</p>

<h2 id="who-decided">Who decided her county was not worth it?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the insurer did. UnitedHealth Group is leaving 228 counties, and its own report shows fewer seniors and a bigger margin.</p>

<p>These plans are sold one county at a time. Each year, the insurers pick where to sell.</p>

<p>KFF lists the reasons. The last one is <a href="https://www.kff.org/medicare/medicare-advantage-plan-availability-varies-widely-by-county-for-2027/">"the insurers' assessment of profitability."</a> In plain words, is the county worth it to them?</p>

<p>UnitedHealth Group is one of the two biggest sellers of these plans. For 2027, KFF counts it leaving 228 counties and moving into 9. It sold in nearly 80% of counties in 2026. Next year it is about 70%.</p>

<p>Now look at its own words. <a href="https://www.unitedhealthgroup.com/content/dam/UHG/PDF/investors/2026/unh-reports-third-quarter-2026-results.pdf">Its third quarter report</a> came out on October 13, 2026. It says the seniors in its plans "contracted by 935,000 since year-end 2025." That means 935,000 fewer.</p>

<p>The same report says its insurance side kept 3.3 cents of each dollar, up from 2.1 cents a year before. Its reasons include "benefit changes and pricing discipline."</p>

<p>Fewer seniors, and a fatter margin. And so a county becomes a number on a page, and then a letter on your mom's counter.</p>

<p>These are the biggest exits in KFF's count. KFF only charted insurers whose changes touch at least 50 counties, so a smaller exit will not show here. Her card may show a brand name, not the parent. Call the number on her card and ask who runs the plan.</p>

<h2 id="how-it-works">Isn't this just how insurance works?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly. Plenty of counties keep plenty of plans, and the rules give her time to switch.</p>

<p>You might be thinking her county still has lots of plans. That is fair, and it may.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.kff.org/medicare/medicare-advantage-plan-availability-varies-widely-by-county-for-2027/">KFF says</a> the average person on Medicare can pick from 28 of these plans in 2027. Humana still sells them in about 80% of counties. Devoted Health is moving into 342 counties and leaving none.</p>

<p>So the average is not the problem. The average does not live in her county.</p>

<p>And the rules give her a window. <a href="https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/get-more-coverage/joining-a-plan">Open enrollment runs October 15 to December 7.</a> A new plan starts January 1.</p>

<p>Is her plan's deal with Medicare not being renewed? Then <a href="https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/get-more-coverage/joining-a-plan/special-enrollment-periods">the same site lists one more window</a>, from December 8 to the last day of February. If your mom picks nothing before her plan ends, she is put in Original Medicare.</p>

<p>And this post will not save her plan. Nothing I do touches Medicare, and I will not pretend it does.</p>

<h2 id="the-letter">What should your mom do with the letter this week?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: find the letter, look up her county, and call her state's free SHIP before December 7.</p>

<h3>Find which letter she has</h3>

<p>Ask her for every envelope from her plan since September. Look for the words non-renewal. Put that letter and her plan card in one folder.</p>

<h3>Look up her county</h3>

<p>Sit with her and open <a href="https://www.medicare.gov/plan-compare/">Find a Medicare plan</a>. That is the plan finder on Medicare's own site. Look up where she lives, and see what is still sold there.</p>

<h3>Call her state's SHIP</h3>

<p>SHIP is her state's Medicare counseling program. Find hers at <a href="https://www.shiphelp.org/">shiphelp.org</a>. <a href="https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare/get-more-coverage/joining-a-plan">Medicare's page</a> says the help is free, and made for her. It also says, "SHIPs aren't connected to any insurance company or health plan."</p>

<h3>Circle two dates</h3>

<p>December 7 ends open enrollment. If her plan is not renewing, the last day of February is the backstop. Write both on the calendar by her phone.</p>

<p>Only her letter can say for sure. Look for the Plan Non-Renewal Notice, which comes in October. Then look up her county on the plan finder.</p>

<p><strong>What happens if she does nothing?</strong></p>
<p>If her plan is not renewed, she is put in Original Medicare when it ends. That happens unless your mom picks another Medicare Advantage plan first. It is not the same as her plan, so ask her SHIP what it means for her.</p>
