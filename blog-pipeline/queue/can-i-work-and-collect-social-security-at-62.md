<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>You can take Social Security at 62 and keep working. Earn over the line, and Social Security holds back your first checks of the year.</p>
<p>Here is the short version. Under full retirement age all year, Social Security keeps $1 of every $2 you earn over the limit. <strong><a href="https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2027.html">Its own fact sheet sets that limit at $24,480 for 2026 and $25,560 for 2027.</a></strong></p>
<p>Starting at 62 also costs <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/agereduction.html">about 30 percent</a>. That cut is for good, if your full age is 67.</p>
<p>A rule you did not write decides your January. The month you start is the part you still pick.</p>

<h2 id="work-and-collect">Can you work and collect Social Security at 62?</h2>

<h2 id="how-it-takes">How does Social Security take the money back?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: whole checks stop first, starting in January. They stay stopped until it has held back the full amount.</p>

<h3>Where does it start?</h3>

<p>With your guess. When you work, <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/cola/">Social Security</a> usually asks you to guess your pay for the year. It holds back checks from that guess.</p>

<h3>Then whole checks stop</h3>

<p>Its own booklet walks through one case. You file at 62 in January 2026, and Social Security needs to hold back $800.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.ssa.gov/pubs/EN-05-10069.pdf">The booklet says</a> "we would withhold all benefit payments from January 2026 through February 2026." Two whole $600 checks go first. The extra $400 comes back the next year.</p>

<h3>And if the guess was low?</h3>

<p>Then you get paid too much. In Social Security's words, you <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/cola/">"may have to repay benefits received."</a></p>

<p>Its notice asks for "a full and immediate refund." On bills sent since April 25, 2025, it then keeps <a href="https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/reference.nsf/links/08282025031929PM">50 percent of each check</a>. That runs until the bill is paid. You can ask for less, or for a waiver.</p>

<p>The year you reach full age has <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2027.html">its own line</a>, $65,160 for 2026 and $67,920 for 2027. Social Security keeps $1 of every $3 over. It only counts the months before you reach full age.</p>

<p>Both 2027 lines came out with <a href="https://theshannonnicole.com/post/will-social-security-get-a-raise-in-2027">the 2027 raise</a>. Next comes the promise that you get it back.</p>

<h2 id="get-it-back">Do you get the held-back money back?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: slowly, as a bigger monthly check from full age on. Social Security's own watchdog office found most of those raises paid late.</p>

<p>Social Security says money held back is not "lost." <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/rtea.html">At full age, "your monthly benefit will be increased permanently"</a> to count the months it kept.</p>

<p>In February 2024, Social Security's Office of the Inspector General published an audit of this rule, the earnings test. <a href="https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/oig-reports/SSA/08-21-51049.pdf">The audit says</a> "SSA did not accurately or timely pay beneficiaries subject to the earnings test."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/oig-reports/SSA/08-21-51049.pdf">By its estimate</a>, about 176,000 people got their raise late, about $81 million. For the late ones in its sample, the first raised check came 292 days late on average.</p>

<p>The audit faults the bills too. Of about 294,000 such bills in fiscal 2021, it estimates about 47,000 were figured wrong. Those bills total more than $148 million.</p>

<p>By the audit's account, part of the cause was a system built in 1989. It added your raise in August of the year after you reached full age. Social Security agreed with all 10 of its fixes.</p>

<p>And so "you get it back" ran through the system the audit says got it wrong.</p>

<h2 id="own-business">Does a business of your own count against the limit?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. Social Security counts your net profit like a paycheck. A business is no way around the test.</p>

<p>Social Security counts <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/whileworking.html">"your net profit if you're self-employed."</a></p>

<p>I am not going to pretend otherwise. Start your check at 62, and any profit from something you build counts like a wage.</p>

<p>What you still pick is the month you start.</p>

<p>The cost of starting early is on <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/agereduction.html">Social Security's own chart</a>. Born in 1960 or later, starting at 62 pays 30 percent less, for life.</p>

<p>Waiting pays the other way. <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/retirement/planner/delayret.html">Each year past full age adds 8 percent</a>, up to 70.</p>

<h2 id="this-week">What would I do first this week?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: get your own numbers and write down your month. Then make one call at full age.</p>

<h3>Get your own three numbers</h3>

<p>Open your statement online, or call Social Security. Ask what it pays at 62, at 67 and at 70.</p>

<h3>Write your month on the fridge</h3>

<p>Born January 2, 1960 or later? Full age is the month you turn 67. Born on the 1st? The month before. Add your start month.</p>

<h3>Were checks held back?</h3>

<p>Then ask for the raise at full age. The audit says you have the right to ask before the system adds it. Call that month. Do not wait.</p>

<h3>If you already filed</h3>

<p>You can <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/manage-benefits/cancel-your-benefits-application">cancel "up to 12 months after your benefit approval."</a> Only once, and you pay back what you and your family got.</p>

<p>Including of me. What does it cost to start, all in. Is there a monthly requirement. What happens if I stop. Where is the income disclosure. Who gets paid when I start.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are women asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>Does my pension or my savings count against the limit?</strong></p>
<p>No. Social Security counts wages and net profit from working for yourself. It does not count pensions, annuities, interest or investment income.</p>

<p><strong>What is the earnings limit for 2027?</strong></p>
<p>It is $25,560 if you are under full retirement age all year. In the year you reach full age, it is $67,920. Only the months before that month count.</p>

<p><strong>If I start at 62, does the 30 percent cut go away at 67?</strong></p>
<p>No. The early cut stays. At full age, you get credit for any months it held back. That raises your monthly check from then on.</p>
