<h2 id="does-it-count">Does a business you own count for the Medicaid work rule?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. The rule names starting a business and owning one. You do not need a boss.</p>

<p>The letter sits on the kitchen counter, next to your laptop. It is about the Medicaid work requirement, which the rule calls community engagement.</p>

<p>You typed your first thought into your phone. My business is just me. Does that count?</p>

<p>It does. The federal Medicaid agency, CMS, published the rule on June 3, 2026. <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-03/pdf/2026-11094.pdf">Page 33354 says</a> you do not need to be "an employee of a company or organization" to meet it. Self-employment, it says, meets "the definition of work."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-435.552">Its list of what counts as work</a> even names unpaid work. Your hours count, not only the hours that got paid.</p>

<h2 id="what-they-check">What does the state check, and when does it start?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: 80 hours, or income of at least $580 for the month. If you are already enrolled, your first check comes at a renewal.</p>

<p>Each month stands on its own. <a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-435.552">The first door</a> is 80 hours. Work counts. So do community service and a work program, or a mix that adds up to 80. Half-time school counts too.</p>

<p>The second door is income. The line is the federal minimum wage, $7.25 an hour, times 80 hours. That comes to $580.</p>

<p>It is counted "the same as the individual's household income used for financial eligibility for Medicaid." Your state already uses that count.</p>

<p>That $580 is the rule's test, not a promise about what any business brings in.</p>

<p>States must start by January 1, 2027, and <a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-435.559">a state may start sooner</a>. A state can ask CMS for a delay, but it ends by December 31, 2028.</p>

<p>For a January 1 start, the first letters go out in July, August or September 2026. So yours may already be on your counter.</p>

<p>Already on Medicaid? Your first check comes at your first renewal after your state starts. From 2027, most renewals under this rule come every six months.</p>

<p>Each time, the state checks one or more months. Your state picks how many, and it can check more often.</p>

<p>The two doors, side by side.</p>

<h2 id="cant-see">Why might the state not see the hours you work at home?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: its records are built around paychecks. The rule admits some work will not show up, and then the state asks you.</p>

<p>The state tries its own records first, without asking you. <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-03/pdf/2026-11094.pdf">The rule says</a> it "must first attempt" that, "including hours worked."</p>

<p>Then the rule admits the gap: "not all types of work can be verified using currently available financial data sources."</p>

<p>A paycheck leaves a trail. Your kitchen table does not.</p>

<p>And so the state turns to you. It "must seek information from the individual to verify work hours." From January 1, 2028, it must ask for documents when you can reasonably get them.</p>

<p>The rule's first example of a document is a paystub. You do not write yourself a paystub.</p>

<p>Say your income is under $580, and the state has no record of your hours. Then it may divide your income by $7.25 and call that your hours.</p>

<p>So a slow sales month can shrink a full month of work on paper. Your own written hours are how you answer that.</p>

<h2 id="safer-job">Isn't a regular job the safer way to meet it?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a paycheck is easier for the state to see. The hours on it still belong to whoever writes the schedule.</p>

<p>I know what you are thinking. A job with a paystub is simpler. The state can see a paycheck without you lifting a finger.</p>

<p>But somebody else writes that schedule, and somebody else can cut it. In September 2026, <a href="https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm">the Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 4.5 million people</a> working part time who wanted full-time work. Their hours "had been reduced," or they could not find full-time jobs.</p>

<p>And so your manager's slow week can become your short month. You did not pick it.</p>

<p>Work you own runs the other way. You set the hours, and you write them down. Your notebook is your record, on your terms.</p>

<h2 id="this-month">What would I do this month?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: start a notebook and an envelope today. Then find your renewal month and call your state.</p>

<h3>Write your hours down every Friday</h3>

<p>Keep a plain notebook by your laptop. Every Friday, write the date and the hours. Then write what you did: making the product, packing orders, the drive to the post office, the books. One line for each day is plenty.</p>

<h3>Put every receipt in one envelope</h3>

<p>The IRS says <a href="https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/what-kind-of-records-should-i-keep">you may choose any system suited to your business</a> that "clearly shows your income and expenses." One envelope a month holds the receipts. Write the month on the front. Your books must also show your income, so write each sale in your notebook.</p>

<h3>Circle your renewal month</h3>

<p>Find the letter from your state Medicaid office. Write your renewal month on the calendar by the phone.</p>

<h3>Call your state Medicaid office</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.healthcare.gov/medicaid-chip/getting-medicaid-chip/">HealthCare.gov lists every state's Medicaid office.</a> Ask what proof of self-employment hours they take, and when your next check is. Their answer is the one that counts.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are women asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>Does self-employment count for Medicaid work requirements?</strong></p>
<p>Yes. The CMS rule says self-employment from starting or owning a business, or contract work, meets its definition of work. You need 80 hours in the month, or income of at least $580 for it.</p>

<p><strong>What if my business has a slow month?</strong></p>
<p>Each month stands on its own, and hours count whether or not they got paid. So write your hours down in slow months too. The rule's six month income average is only for seasonal workers. Ask your state before you count on it.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to prove it every month?</strong></p>
<p>Not by default. Your first check comes at your first renewal after your state starts. From 2027, most renewals come every six months. Your state can check more often, so keep your record every month.</p>

<p><strong>Is anyone excused from the rule?</strong></p>
<p>Yes. The rule excuses a parent or caretaker of a child 13 or younger, and people who are medically frail, among others. Your state decides who fits, so ask yours.</p>
