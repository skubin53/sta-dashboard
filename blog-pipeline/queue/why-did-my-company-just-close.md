<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>The email never said why. Not one word.</p>
<p>Here is the short version. Modere closed on Friday, April 11, 2025, after 23 years. Its email to staff said <strong><a href="https://www.sltrib.com/news/business/2025/04/15/utah-mlm-modere-closes-suddenly/">"we've been forced to close our doors."</a></strong> Forced by whom, it never said.</p>
<p>The court papers say more. In 2024, Modere told a court a "painful" slump hit direct sales in late 2022. Some of its top sellers left in 2024. In bankruptcy court, it owed far more than it owned.</p>
<p>You built the customers. Modere wrote each rule about them.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened">What happened the day Modere closed?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it pushed a sale on Friday and shut its website later that same day.</p>

<p>It is late. The light over the stove is the only one on. The laptop is shut, and the stool is pushed back where you left it.</p>

<p>The samples in the hall closet, the order you placed Thursday, the customer who texted to ask where her box went, the group chat gone quiet, and that feeling that you built a whole business inside somebody else's house.</p>

<p>On Friday, April 11, 2025, Modere's Instagram pushed a sale on liquid collagen, good all weekend.</p>

<p>Later that day, Modere said it had closed. <a href="https://www.sltrib.com/news/business/2025/04/15/utah-mlm-modere-closes-suddenly/">The Salt Lake Tribune reported</a> that its website became a short thank-you note.</p>

<p>Staff had until 5 p.m. to clear their desks. One person online said sellers got "TWO HOURS AND FIFTEEN MINUTES NOTICE."</p>

<h2 id="why-it-closed">Why did it close when nobody told you?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the email gave no reason. Modere's own court filings give you the pieces, starting in late 2022.</p>

<p>In a 2024 lawsuit, Modere said direct sales boomed during COVID. Then a "painful" slump began in late 2022, and Modere had to "right-size," <a href="https://www.sltrib.com/news/business/2025/04/15/utah-mlm-modere-closes-suddenly/">the Tribune reported</a>.</p>

<p>Then, on February 2, 2024, three sellers quit on the same day and went to a rival. Two of them were among its top sellers.</p>

<p>Modere sued them. <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-utd-2_24-cv-00216/pdf/USCOURTS-utd-2_24-cv-00216-0.pdf">A federal judge in Utah refused to stop them on May 17, 2024.</a> Modere dropped the case weeks later.</p>

<p>Less than eleven months later, the doors closed.</p>

<p>None of it was decided at your kitchen table. The rules about your customers were made somewhere else too.</p>

<h2 id="the-paper">What did Modere's sellers sign about their customers?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: rules that could change without asking them, and a one-year ban on recruiting each customer on their list.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-utd-2_24-cv-00216/pdf/USCOURTS-utd-2_24-cv-00216-0.pdf">That May 2024 decision</a> quotes parts of Modere's seller contract word for word.</p>

<h3>The rules could change without you</h3>

<p>The rules "may be amended at the sole discretion of Modere." Each new version came as a click-through box in the back office.</p>

<p>And getting paid counted as saying yes. Taking "bonuses or commissions" meant you took each change too.</p>

<h3>Your customers stayed behind for a year</h3>

<p>For a year after you left, you could not recruit any customer on your list into a company like it. That held "regardless of the reason." Each try could cost you $2,500.00. Asking the same person twice counted twice.</p>

<h3>The back office lived on their website</h3>

<p>The rules sat in Modere's back office, on Modere's website. On April 11, that website became a thank-you note.</p>

<p>Were you somewhere else? Find your own contract tonight and look for those two lines.</p>

<h2 id="the-money">What was left for the people owed money?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: in January 2026, the trustee filed a "Report of No Distribution." The case closed that June.</p>

<p>On June 2, 2025, Maple Mountain Group filed for bankruptcy in Nevada. The 2024 decision says that company did business as Modere.</p>

<p>It filed under Chapter 7. A trustee takes over, sells what is left, and pays the bills in a set order.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/70440879/maple-mountain-group-inc/">Its papers listed $9,382,549.01 in property.</a> It owed $169,455,141.00 to secured creditors, the ones first in line. Another $12,000,000.00 was owed to everyone else.</p>

<p>Here is the math. For each dollar of property, it listed about eighteen owed to the front of the line.</p>

<p>In December 2025, the court sent a notice to file claims. On January 15, 2026, <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/70440879/maple-mountain-group-inc/">the trustee filed a "Report of No Distribution."</a> The case closed on June 2, 2026.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics/chapter-7-bankruptcy-basics">The US Courts guide to Chapter 7</a> says what a no-asset report means: "there will be no distribution to unsecured creditors." Unsecured means everyone else in line.</p>

<p>That is the end of the paper trail. Your customers still have your number.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly, and here is the part I will not dress up.</p>

<p>Closing a company is not against the law. Neither is Chapter 7.</p>

<p>The judge did not call its contract unfair either. She found the one-year clause <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-utd-2_24-cv-00216/pdf/USCOURTS-utd-2_24-cv-00216-0.pdf">"likely enforceable."</a> Modere lost because it let sellers sign up as businesses, not as people.</p>

<p>So that clause would likely have held up in court. That is the problem.</p>

<p>Switch to America sells straight from the manufacturer. No middlemen and no markup. No minimums, no maximums.</p>

<p>You pay what a brand new shopper pays. Not a penny less. I will not hand you a number to expect. Nobody honest can.</p>

<h2 id="monday">What would I do first on Monday?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: write the names down before they fade, then write down what you are owed.</p>

<h3>Write the names on paper tonight</h3>

<p>Each customer you can remember. The one who reordered each month. Her sister. The neighbor who only bought at Christmas. Go back through your texts.</p>

<p>Put the list in your kitchen drawer. Nobody switches off a drawer from an office in another state.</p>

<h3>Write down what you are owed</h3>

<p>The amount, the dates, and each email. If your company files in bankruptcy court, its case is public. Ask someone qualified in your state whether to file a claim.</p>

<h3>Ask one question first of whatever comes next</h3>

<p>Including of me. What happens to my customers if you close?</p>

<p>Then ask the rest. What does it cost to start? Is there a monthly minimum? Where is the income disclosure? Who gets paid when I start? If someone dodges one, that is your answer.</p>
