<h2 id="what-the-lawsuit-says">What does the Carefree lawsuit say is in the liners?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: PFOA, in one or more of the liner's five layers, the suit says. No judge has ruled yet.</p>

<p>You stood at the kitchen counter longer than you meant to. Your tote was open, and the pink box was in your hand.</p>

<p>No shame in that. You picked a name you trusted for years.</p>

<p>You want to feel fresh all day. That is a fair thing to want. The problem is what the suit says was in the liner.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/carefree-edgewell-essity-jj-gelsons-lawsuit-pfoa-pfas-california/833296/">The suit was filed in Los Angeles County Superior Court, Manufacturing Dive reported.</a> It names Essity, Edgewell and Johnson &amp; Johnson. It also names Gelson's Markets, for selling the liners.</p>

<p>Why three companies? Each one has owned Carefree. <a href="https://tools.prnewswire.com/en-us/live/20823/release/20260202EN76608">Essity bought it from Edgewell in February 2026.</a> Edgewell bought it from Johnson &amp; Johnson in 2013.</p>

<p>The suit points to a 2024 lab test. It says the test found PFOA in one or more of the liner's five layers. It says the liners have been made with it ever since.</p>

<p><a href="https://hoodline.com/2026/10/l-a-county-residents-sue-essity-edgewell-over-pfas-in-carefree-liners/">The people suing say they wore the liners for up to 8 to 10 hours during their cycles.</a> The suit claims cancers and thyroid, liver and hormone harm. Those are claims. None has been proven in court.</p>

<h3 id="pfoa">PFOA, the forever chemical named in the suit</h3>

<p>PFOA is one of the PFAS. They are called forever chemicals because they do not break down easily.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.iarc.who.int/news-events/iarc-monographs-evaluate-the-carcinogenicity-of-perfluorooctanoic-acid-pfoa-and-perfluorooctanesulfonic-acid-pfos/">In December 2023, the World Health Organization's cancer agency classed PFOA as "carcinogenic to humans."</a> That is the top of its scale. And so a liner is the last place you want it. The suit says these stayed on the shelf after California's ban.</p>

<p style="font-size:.9em;">Full name: perfluorooctanoic acid.</p>

<h2 id="how-did-it-stay">How did the liners stay on the shelf after the ban?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: until 2027, California's ban only reaches PFAS a company "intentionally added." Essity says it adds none on purpose.</p>

<p><a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB2515">California's law is AB 2515. It was signed on September 30, 2024.</a> It covers pads and any product "used to collect menstruation and vaginal discharge."</p>

<p>Since January 1, 2025, it bans selling those with "regulated PFAS." For now, that means PFAS "that a manufacturer has intentionally added" to do a job in the product.</p>

<p>So by the law's own words, PFAS that gets in some other way is not covered yet.</p>

<p>In 2027, the law starts counting all PFAS over a limit. The state's toxics department sets the limit. <a href="https://hoodline.com/2026/10/l-a-county-residents-sue-essity-edgewell-over-pfas-in-carefree-liners/">It has not been set yet, Hoodline reported this month.</a></p>

<p>So that is the fight. The suit claims the liners were made with PFOA on purpose. Essity says no.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/carefree-edgewell-essity-jj-gelsons-lawsuit-pfoa-pfas-california/833296/">"We do not intentionally add PFAS chemicals to our products," an Essity spokesperson said.</a> That is about what Essity adds. It does not say what a lab would find.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for Carefree?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, a fair one. No judge has ruled, and Essity denies adding PFAS.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> A lawsuit is one side's story. This one is weeks old, and no court has tested it.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> Manufacturing Dive's report does not say how much PFOA the lab found. And the WHO agency rated the proof of cancer in people as "limited."</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> Essity says "Carefree stands behind its products." It plans to "vigorously respond." Edgewell and Johnson &amp; Johnson did not reply to Manufacturing Dive.</p>

<p>No court has said a Carefree liner hurt anyone. I am not going to pretend one has.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me? Because this is the second time.</p>

<p><a href="https://oag.ca.gov/prop65/60-day-notice-2024-00563">In February 2024, a group called Ecological Alliance sent a notice to California's Attorney General.</a> It named PFOA in Carefree panty liners. <a href="https://www.foxla.com/news/carefree-panty-liners-contain-cancer-causing-forever-chemical-lawsuit-alleges">That September, the group sued Edgewell.</a> It wanted the PFOA out, or a warning on the box.</p>

<p><a href="https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/environment-group-drops-carefree-menstrual-liner-pfas-suit">The group dropped that case in January 2025, Bloomberg Law reported.</a> Now, two years and eight months after that first notice, the same chemical is back in court.</p>

<p>And so the fix is a new company to buy from.</p>
