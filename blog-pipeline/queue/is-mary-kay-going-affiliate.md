<h2 id="going-affiliate">Is Mary Kay going affiliate?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: not by anything Mary Kay has said. The real change is the agreement.</p>

<p>It is late and the house is quiet. One lamp is on, the laptop is shut, and the chair is pushed back.</p>

<p>You know the list. The group chat asking if Mary Kay is going affiliate. The nine pages you were asked to accept. The part near the end about arbitration. The thirty days with no date next to them. And that feeling that the agreement was running your business instead of the other way around.</p>

<h2 id="what-it-says">What does the new agreement actually say?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: four things. You resell. A fight goes private. You bring it alone. You get thirty days to opt out.</p>

<p>Its full name is the <a href="https://pinktruth.com/wp-content/uploads/Consultant-Agreement-0726.pdf">Independent Beauty Consultant Purchase and Sale Agreement</a>.</p>

<h3>Reseller, on page one</h3>

<p>Page one says Mary Kay supplies its products "to independent resellers." Section 2 says, "You operate an independent resale business."</p>

<p>Even the name moved. <a href="https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/163178p.pdf">A federal appeals court in 2017</a> quoted the old one as the "Independent Beauty Consultant Agreement." Now "Purchase and Sale" is in the title.</p>

<h3>A private hearing</h3>

<p>Section 11 is titled "Arbitration Agreement and Class Action Waiver." Arbitration is a private hearing in place of a court. You agree you are "waiving the right to a trial by judge or jury."</p>

<p>The old one, as that court quoted it, sent a fight to "the courts of the State of Texas."</p>

<h3>You bring it alone</h3>

<p>Section 11(e) says a claim is brought "solely on an individual basis, and not as a class." And so you and a friend with the same problem each go it alone.</p>

<h3>Thirty days, in writing</h3>

<p>Section 11(k) says you must opt out "in writing within thirty (30) days of the date of acceptance by the Company."</p>

<p>Section 11(l) says the notice must say you are opting out. It needs your full name and mailing address, signed and dated.</p>

<p>No calendar date is printed. Your thirty days run from the day Mary Kay accepted your agreement. If that was back in July, they may already be behind you. I will not tell you whether to opt out. Read your own copy, then decide.</p>

<h2 id="change-again">Can Mary Kay change the rules again?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. Section 9 lets it change the terms at any time on 10 days' written notice. You accept or you leave.</p>

<p>Section 9(a) opens, "You agree to comply with any changes." That covers "commissions and Active status requirements, at any time."</p>

<p>Section 9(b) says Mary Kay can make you click "accept" before you keep placing orders. Section 9(d) is the other door. "If you refuse to accept any changes, you may terminate this Agreement."</p>

<p>And so you sign or you leave. That is the whole vote you get.</p>

<p>The last row is the one that works for you.</p>

<h2 id="customers">Who owns your customers?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: on paper, names you give Mary Kay for its programs stay yours. Your online shop runs on Mary Kay's website.</p>

<p>Section 8(b) covers names you give Mary Kay for optional programs. It says they "shall remain your sole property." That is a good line. But 8(a) says you keep customer information "secure" and share it only with permission.</p>

<p>Now look at <a href="https://www.marykay.com/en-us/pages/terms-of-use">Mary Kay's website terms</a>. A shopper who does not pick a consultant gets "randomly connected" to one. And Mary Kay can "modify or discontinue parts of or all of the Sites."</p>

<p>That is the site your customer shops on.</p>

<p>And so the list you keep yourself matters more than any page. Her name, her number, what she reorders.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly, and here is the part I will not dress up.</p>

<p>Mary Kay is not closing. In August it said My Shop had just opened in three more countries. That is a company with plans.</p>

<p>The agreement gives you real things too. You get a written opt-out, and names that stay yours. None of this is about the products, or about you.</p>

<p>Now me. Switch to America could change its rules too. Any company can.</p>

<p>We are the manufacturer. No middlemen and no markup. No minimums, no maximums. You pay what a brand new shopper pays.</p>

<p>I will not hand you a number to expect. Nobody honest can.</p>
