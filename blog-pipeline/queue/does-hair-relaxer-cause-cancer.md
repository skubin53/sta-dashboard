<h2 id="what-is-the-lawsuit">What is the L'Oreal Arizona lawsuit?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: Arizona sued L'Oreal in September 2026. The state says it hid a cancer risk.</p>

<p>The suit was filed in Maricopa County, in Arizona state court. It names three L'Oreal companies, including the parent company in France, and SoftSheen-Carson.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.azag.gov/press-release/attorney-general-mayes-files-consumer-fraud-lawsuit-against-loreal-usa">Attorney General Kris Mayes announced it on September 11, 2026.</a> Her words were plain. "L'Oreal knew or should have known about the dangers," she said.</p>

<p>The state says L'Oreal broke Arizona's consumer fraud law. That is the law about what a company has to tell you before you buy.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/arizona-loreal-usa-complaint.pdf">The 25 page complaint</a> asks for money back for shoppers, fines, and real warnings on the box.</p>

<p>And so this is not one shopper suing. It is a whole state.</p>

<h2 id="what-the-state-says">What does the state say was in the box?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: by the state's account, relaxers can hold chemicals that mess with your hormones. Some are tied to cancer.</p>

<p>You know how it goes. You smooth it on the new growth, set the timer, and wait while it tingles.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/arizona-loreal-usa-complaint.pdf">The complaint says you go back for a touch up every four to eight weeks.</a> So it is not one exposure. It is years of Saturdays.</p>

<p>The state says these relaxers can burn the scalp. It says the skin on your scalp soaks things in faster than your arms or hands do.</p>

<p>Then it lists what it says relaxers as a group can hold.</p>
<h3 id="phthalates-and-parabens">Phthalates and parabens</h3>
<p>The state says they can throw off your hormones.</p>
<h3 id="formaldehyde">Formaldehyde</h3>
<p>The state says federal and world health agencies list it as a known or likely cause of cancer.</p>

<p>The complaint says L'Oreal sold these in Arizona as far back as the 1970s. It says some were sold for young girls. That line was called Beautiful Beginnings.</p>

<p>By the state's account, none of that was on the box you bought. That is the whole point of the case.</p>

<p>The state's own words for it are "a callous disregard for the health of Arizona consumers."</p>

<h2 id="what-the-studies-found">What did the studies find?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: two large government studies found more cancer in frequent relaxer users.</p>

<p>The state points to several studies. Two of the biggest came from the National Institutes of Health.</p>

<p><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9949582/">The first, in the Journal of the National Cancer Institute in 2022, followed 33,947 women for about 11 years.</a> Frequent use meant more than four times a year. Those who used it that much had about two and a half times the risk of uterine cancer.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/hair-straightening-chemicals-associated-higher-uterine-cancer-risk">The NIH put it in numbers you can hold.</a> By age 70, they estimated about 1.64% of women who never used straighteners would get uterine cancer. For frequent users, it was 4.05%.</p>

<p><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8561257/">The second, an ovarian cancer study from 2021, followed 40,559 women for about 10 years.</a> Frequent use of relaxers or pressing products was tied to about twice the risk of ovarian cancer.</p>

<p>That is the thing that stings. You trusted the box. The studies came out in 2021 and 2022, and by the state's account the box still says nothing.</p>

<h2 id="what-loreal-says">What does L'Oreal say back?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: L'Oreal says the studies never proved its products cause cancer.</p>

<p>Here is the strongest case on the other side. It is fair to hear it.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://www.kold.com/2026/09/11/unacceptable-arizona-ag-sues-loral-over-alleged-cancer-linked-hair-relaxers/">A L'Oreal spokesperson said the study "made no finding of a causal connection."</a> That is true. The researchers said more work is needed.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The uterine study did not ask anyone which brand they used. So it cannot point at one box.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> Nothing has gone to trial. <a href="https://www.jpml.uscourts.gov/sites/jpml/files/Pending_MDL_Dockets_By_Actions_Pending-November-2-2026.pdf">The federal panel that tracks these cases lists 12,743 of them waiting in Chicago.</a> <a href="https://www.aboutlawsuits.com/wp-content/uploads/2026-09-24-Docket-Entry.pdf">Judge Mary M. Rowland has the first trial on the calendar for September 7, 2027.</a></p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because you were never given the choice. The state says L'Oreal never warned you, so you kept buying the box you trusted.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Which L'Oreal relaxers does the Arizona lawsuit name?</strong></p>
<p>Dark and Lovely, Optimum and Mizani. The complaint says the list is not limited to those three.</p>

<p><strong>Has L'Oreal been found guilty of anything?</strong></p>
<p>No. Everything in the Arizona case is the state's claim. L'Oreal denies it, and no court has ruled.</p>

<p><strong>Can I join the Arizona lawsuit?</strong></p>
<p>The state brought this case itself. If you live in Arizona, you can file a complaint with the state on its website.</p>

<p><strong>Does it matter how often I relaxed my hair?</strong></p>
<p>Yes. In the uterine study, any use in the past year was tied to higher risk, and use more than four times a year was tied to the highest. For ovarian cancer, any use at all did not show a clear link. Frequent use did.</p>
