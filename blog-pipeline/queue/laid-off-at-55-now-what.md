<h2 id="how-long">How long does the search really take at 55?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: of women 55 to 64 still looking, one in five is past six months. Plan for the long end.</p>

<p>You closed the laptop at eleven at night and sat at the kitchen island longer than you meant to. The house was very quiet for a weeknight.</p>

<p>In September, <a href="https://www.bls.gov/web/empsit/cpseea36.htm">228,000 women aged 55 to 64 were out of work</a>. Half had been out about ten weeks or less.</p>

<p>At the other end, 46,000 had been out 27 weeks or more. That is over six months, and about one in five. Those are weeks so far, and some are still counting.</p>

<p>And so plan for the long end, not the middle.</p>

<h2 id="who-reads-it">Who reads your application before a person does?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: often Workday's software. A lawsuit says it turns down applicants over 40 more often. Workday denies it.</p>

<p>The job form you fill out may run on Workday. The lawsuit says its tools sort you before a person decides.</p>

<p>How many job forms? <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.408645/gov.uscourts.cand.408645.397.0.pdf">Workday's own court filing</a> from July 2026 admits the number. In May 2023, "by at least one calculation," its customers used its software to process "2.2 million job requisition transactions." Its words: "nearly 22% of all U.S. job openings that month."</p>

<p>That is more than one opening in five.</p>

<p>The man who brought the case is Derek Mobley. He says he applied to more than 100 jobs at companies using Workday's screening since 2017. He says he was turned down for every one.</p>

<p>Four more applicants over 40 say they applied for hundreds of jobs and were turned down almost every time.</p>

<p>In May 2025, a federal court in California <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.408645/gov.uscourts.cand.408645.128.0.pdf">let the age claim go forward as a group case</a>. Workday guessed the group could reach "hundreds of millions" of people.</p>

<p>The court wrote back: "that is because Workday has been plausibly accused of discriminating against a broad swath of applicants."</p>

<p>In June 2026, <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.408645/gov.uscourts.cand.408645.360.0.pdf">the same court kept the case going</a>, age claims under California law included.</p>

<p>Nobody has proven anything yet. And your money still has to last while the search runs.</p>

<h2 id="your-401k">Can you use your 401(k) at 55 without the extra tax?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: often yes, from the plan at the job that just ended. Not from an IRA.</p>

<p>Take money out of a retirement plan before 59 and a half, and you usually pay an extra 10% tax. <a href="https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-exceptions-to-tax-on-early-distributions">The IRS lists a way around it.</a> The extra tax does not apply when "the employee separates from service during or after the year the employee reaches age 55."</p>

<p>That is often called the rule of 55. Separates from service means your job ended. And the year is what counts. Your last day is in March and you turn 55 that December? You still qualify.</p>

<p>Two catches. You still owe regular income tax. And the same IRS table says no for IRAs.</p>

<p>And so if that 401(k) gets rolled into an IRA first, the rule stops covering it. Here is the record. The third row goes against me, and I left it in.</p>

<h2 id="monday">What would I do first on Monday?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: make one call before any money moves. Then start something beside the search.</p>

<h3>Count the long months</h3>

<p>Add up your savings, any severance and your unemployment checks. Divide that by what one month costs you. Put the number on the fridge. If it covers fewer than six months, cut costs first.</p>

<h3>Make one call before you move a dollar</h3>

<p>Call the number on your 401(k) statement. Ask four things. Does the rule of 55 apply to me? Can I take part of it, or only all of it? How much will you hold back for tax? What changes if I move it to an IRA?</p>

<h3>Start something beside the search, not instead of it</h3>

<p>Write down what people came to you for at work. The problems you fixed. The questions only you could answer.</p>
