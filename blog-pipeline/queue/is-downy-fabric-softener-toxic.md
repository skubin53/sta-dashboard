<h2 id="what-it-leaves">What is Downy leaving on your towels?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a softener that clings to the cotton, and a scent built to last.</p>

<h3>Diethylester dimethyl ammonium chloride: why is the towel so soft?</h3>

<p>Start with P&amp;G's own list. <a href="https://smartlabel.pg.com/00030772119860.html">Its page for the 140 ounce April Fresh jug names the softener.</a> It is "Diethylester Dimethyl Ammonium Chloride."</p>

<p>Chemists call it a quat. <a href="https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jos/56/6/56_6_269/_pdf">A 2007 review in the Journal of Oleo Science explains how it works.</a> It sticks to each fiber with its oily end facing out.</p>

<p>That oily layer is the soft you feel. The same review says these softeners "can make them less absorbent." A towel has one job. It is to drink water.</p>

<h3>Butylphenyl methylpropional: what hides in the word fragrance?</h3>

<p>The ingredient list says fragrance. <a href="https://smartlabel.pg.com/00030772119860.html">P&amp;G's page lists 32 ingredients under that one word.</a></p>

<p>P&amp;G flags six of them itself, as EU fragrance allergens. It also lists one of those six, butylphenyl methylpropional, under "EU CMRs." P&amp;G's own page says that is a European list. It holds chemicals classed as causing cancer, gene damage or harm to reproduction.</p>

<p>This one is on it for reproduction. <a href="https://www.ages.at/en/human/cosmetics/cosmetic-information-for-companies/new-fragrance-regulation-in-cosmetics">Austria's health and food safety agency says Europe banned it from cosmetics on March 1, 2022.</a> The reason it gives is "its classification as CMR (reprotoxic)."</p>

<p>That means lotion, shampoo and makeup. It is still on P&amp;G's list for this jug. And P&amp;G's page carries a warning too.</p>

<h2 id="the-warning">What does P&amp;G's own warning say?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: that liquid softener can make fabric burn more easily. Keep it off terry cloth clothes.</p>

<p>Here it is, word for word, from P&amp;G's page for that jug.</p>

<p><a href="https://smartlabel.pg.com/00030772119860.html">"Liquid fabric softener can increase fabric flammability. Using more than recommended can increase this effect."</a></p>

<p>Then it names the clothes to keep it off. Garments made of "fleece, velour, chenille, and terry cloth." Your terry robe is one of them.</p>

<p>Now read P&amp;G's towel page on the Downy website. <a href="https://downy.com/en-us/fabric-softener-tips/protect-your-clothes/towels-fluffy-and-soft-again">"Can I use fabric softener on towels? Yes."</a></p>

<p>Same company, same terry cloth. The website says yes on your towels. The warning says keep it off terry clothes, because they can burn more easily.</p>

<p>This worry is not new. <a href="https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/liquid-fabric-softener-may-make-clothes-more-flammable-quebec-coroner-1.340282">In February 2002, a Quebec coroner warned about liquid softener on fleece and terry.</a> A Quebec resident had died. Her robe caught fire from a dropped cigarette.</p>

<p>By the coroner's account, it burned fast because it was washed in softener. "Fabric tends to burn much more severely with fabric softener on there," he said. He ordered tests on the robe before giving his final advice. CBC's story named no brand.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">The case against my own story</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: three fair points, and P&amp;G's safety sheet is the strongest.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://assets.ctfassets.net/p9x6nkt33m4y/MvRidIEwHf2UlKvZ2G4wq/bf0046571f3ad1d8a7c26709b5cb2745/21056097_RET_NG_MTR_PGNG_EN">P&amp;G's safety sheet for Ultra Downy April Fresh is dated October 4, 2023.</a> It says the product "is not considered hazardous" under the federal workplace safety rule.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The European ban covers cosmetics, not laundry. <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10613275/">A 2023 study in Scientific Reports says the amount in products "is rather low."</a> In their lab tests, it did not act like estrogen.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> The warning says garments. It does not say towels.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because P&amp;G wrote both pages. The website says yes on your towels. The warning says softener can make fabric burn more easily. And the scent holds a chemical Europe took out of lotion.</p>

<h2 id="what-do-i-use">What do I wash my towels with instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: nothing extra. Leave out the softener, and use less soap.</p>

<p>Start with the habit, because it costs nothing. Leave the softener out of every load, starting with your towels and your robe.</p>

<p>Stiff towels? <a href="https://downy.com/en-us/fabric-softener-tips/protect-your-clothes/towels-fluffy-and-soft-again">P&amp;G's own towel page says too much detergent "can cause buildup."</a> It says that leaves towels "stiff and dull." So use less soap, not more coating.</p>

<p>Dry them all the way before you fold them. That same page says so too.</p>

<p>Your towels will not smell like April Fresh anymore. If you love that smell, that is the part you will miss.</p>
