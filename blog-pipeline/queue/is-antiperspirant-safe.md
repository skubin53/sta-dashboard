<h2 id="what-the-aluminum-does">What is the aluminum doing under your arms?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: plugging your sweat ducts, and the stick carries a kidney warning.</p>

<p>There is a white streak on the black top you just put on. You rub at it with your thumb on the way to the car.</p>

<p>Maybe you have bought the same stick for years. No shame in that. It was sold to you as the plain one.</p>

<p>So what does it do all day? <a href="https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/myths/antiperspirants-fact-sheet">The National Cancer Institute says the aluminum forms "a temporary 'plug' within the sweat duct."</a> And so the sweat stops before it reaches your skin.</p>

<img src="https://scan.ismyhometoxic.com/blog-images/isantipersp-villain-v1.webp" alt="An antiperspirant stick standing alone on a bare bathroom counter under flat cold light, twisted up, its cap off beside it and a chalky white dust on the counter" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px;">

<h3 id="the-salt">Aluminum zirconium, the salt that does the plugging</h3>

<p>In the unscented Secret stick, the plug is an aluminum salt. It makes up 18 percent. <a href="https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e2ae5e41-b216-500d-e053-2a95a90a7f60">P&amp;G's own filing lists it first, with a warning for anyone with kidney disease.</a></p>

<p>Why the kidneys? <a href="https://wwwn.cdc.gov/TSP/ToxFAQs/ToxFAQsDetails.aspx?faqid=190&amp;toxid=34">The Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry, a US health agency, says "some people with kidney disease store a lot of aluminum in their bodies."</a></p>

<p>That would be easy to shrug off if the plug were the only thing in the stick.</p>

<h2 id="what-pg-wrote">What has P&amp;G put in writing about Secret?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: that its "unscented" stick lists fragrance, and that it found benzene in Secret sprays in 2021.</p>

<h3 id="the-fragrance">Fragrance, the last word on an unscented stick</h3>

<p>The front of the stick says unscented. <a href="https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e2ae5e41-b216-500d-e053-2a95a90a7f60">The last word in P&amp;G's own list of what is in it is "fragrance."</a></p>

<p>So the front says one thing. The company's own filing says another.</p>

<p>In May 2026, a buyer took P&amp;G to federal court in Massachusetts over it. <a href="https://www.classaction.org/media/phaneuf-v-the-procter-and-gamble-company-complaint.pdf">Her lawsuit says the sticks "do contain fragrance and are therefore not 'unscented.'"</a></p>

<p>It says buyers "did not know, and had no reason to know." If that is true, it is on the company, not on you. A lawsuit is an allegation until a court rules.</p>

<h3 id="the-benzene">Benzene, in the Secret sprays</h3>

<p>This is not the first time. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/pg-issues-voluntary-recall-specific-old-spice-and-secret-aerosol-spray-antiperspirants-and-old-spice">In November 2021, P&amp;G recalled certain Secret and Old Spice sprays after benzene was found.</a> Its own notice says "Benzene is classified as a human carcinogen."</p>

<p>The sticks were not part of that recall. <a href="https://join.switchtoamerica.com/post/is-spray-deodorant-safe-benzene-recall">Here is what the recall did cover.</a></p>

<p><a href="https://cen.acs.org/policy/litigation/PG-settles-benzene-class-action/100/i44">In 2022, P&amp;G agreed to pay $8 million. It settled the class actions over benzene in its sprays.</a> It did not admit wrongdoing.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for keeping your stick?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. A US cancer agency finds no proof it causes breast cancer, and very little gets through skin.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/myths/antiperspirants-fact-sheet">The National Cancer Institute says "no scientific evidence" ties these products to breast cancer.</a></p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> <a href="https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/myths/antiperspirants-fact-sheet">A 2002 study it cites asked 813 women with breast cancer and 793 without.</a> It found no rise in risk from underarm products.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/s0278-6915(00)00118-6">A 2001 study put tagged aluminum under one arm of two adults.</a> Only 0.012 percent of it went through the skin.</p>

<p>That is a real answer, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.</p>

<p>The case against the aluminum is thinner. <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5514401/">A 2017 study in Austria measured aluminum in breast tissue. It found more in 100 women with breast cancer than in 52 without.</a> It was small, and it relied on what each woman remembered using.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because the front said unscented and the filing says fragrance. And it is the same company that pulled its sprays in 2021.</p>

<p>If you bought it, you are far from the only one. The lawsuit puts the buyers it speaks for in the thousands.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Does the aluminum in deodorant cause Alzheimer's?</strong></p>
<p>Nobody has proven that. A US health agency says some studies of high aluminum point that way and others do not. In its words, "We do not know for certain."</p>

<p><strong>Was my Secret stick part of the 2021 recall?</strong></p>
<p>No. P&amp;G recalled certain Secret and Old Spice aerosol sprays for benzene. Its notice said solid sticks were not affected.</p>

<p><strong>Is the unscented one better for sensitive skin?</strong></p>
<p>You would think so. P&amp;G's own filing lists fragrance in that stick. A May 2026 lawsuit says the word unscented is false. A lawsuit is an allegation until a court rules.</p>

<p><strong>Will I smell if I stop using antiperspirant?</strong></p>
<p>You will sweat, because nothing is plugging the duct. The smell is what a plain deodorant is for. Wear it for a week before you decide.</p>
