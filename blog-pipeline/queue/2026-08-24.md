<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>On 14 July 2021, Johnson &amp; Johnson pulled five spray sunscreens. They were sold as Neutrogena and Aveeno. Testing had found benzene in them.</p>
<p><strong><a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/health/2021/07/15/johnson-johnson-sunscreen-recall-benzene/">Benzene is a known human carcinogen. It was never an ingredient in any of those products.</a></strong> It was found in the finished cans anyway.</p>
<p>Here is the part that matters. It was not on the label. A label only lists what they meant to put in. Nobody in a store could have read their way to it.</p>

<h2 id="what-was-actually-recalled">What was actually recalled, and when?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: five spray cans, pulled in July 2021, after an outside lab found benzene.</p>

<p>Here are the five. Neutrogena Beach Defense. Neutrogena Cool Dry Sport. Neutrogena Invisible Daily Defense. Neutrogena Ultra Sheer. Aveeno Protect + Refresh.</p>

<p>Every one of them a spray can. The kind you keep in a beach bag.</p>

<p>A lab called Valisure started it. They buy things off the shelf and test them.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.consumernotice.org/news/jj-recalls-neutrogena-aveeno-sunscreens-benzene-contamination/">They asked the FDA to act.</a> They said they found benzene in dozens of batches. Some were well over the small amount the FDA allows.</p>

<p>Then it got worse. Consumer Reports got hold of records later. It reported that <a href="https://www.consumerreports.org/health/sunscreens/fda-safety-risks-aveeno-neutrogena-sunscreens-with-benzene-a8377840278/">the FDA knew about the risks and did not say so</a>.</p>

<h2 id="how-does-benzene-get-in">How does benzene get into a sunscreen at all?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: nobody puts it in. It rides in with the spray gas.</p>

<p>Benzene comes out of oil. So does the gas that pushes a spray out of a can.</p>

<p>They come from the same place. If it gets dirty early, it stays dirty.</p>

<p><strong>This is why the recall matters more than the chemistry.</strong> The recipe can be clean and the can can still be dirty.</p>

<p>The problem came in where nobody was looking.</p>

<p>Who tests it. How often. What they do when they find something. All of that is decided in a building you will never see.</p>

<p>Think about how sunscreen gets used. Thick. All over. Again and again. Left on in the heat.</p>

<p><a href="https://caseygerry.com/blog/johnson-johnson-sunscreen-recall-benzene-found-in-neutrogena-and-aveeno-aerosol-sunscreen-products/">That is the argument the lab made</a>. Not much stands between the can and you.</p>

<h2 id="what-is-in-the-bottle">What is in the bottle the rest of the time?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a set of chemical filters. Some go into the blood.</p>

<h3>Benzene</h3>
<p>Not an ingredient. That is why it is never on the list. It is known to cause cancer in people. This is what caused the 2021 recall.</p>

<h3>Oxybenzone</h3>
<p>A chemical filter used for decades. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/drugs/understanding-over-counter-medicines/questions-and-answers-fda-posts-deemed-final-order-and-proposed-order-over-counter-sunscreen">The FDA did not call it safe.</a> It asked the makers for more data instead.</p>

<p>Hawaii and Key West limited it years ago, to protect the reefs.</p>

<h3>Octinoxate</h3>
<p>Another chemical filter. Same FDA data request. Same state limits.</p>

<h3>Homosalate</h3>
<p>Also in the group the FDA asked about. It is cheap and it is common. It is in a lot of bottles.</p>

<h3>Avobenzone</h3>
<p>The workhorse. It breaks down in sunlight. So they add more chemicals to hold it together.</p>

<p>That is how a simple product ends up with a long list.</p>

<h2 id="does-that-mean-it-is-hurting-me">Does that mean it is hurting me?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the recall is a fact. What it did to one person is not.</p>

<p>Johnson &amp; Johnson said the amounts found would not be expected to cause harm. No regulator has said different. That is their side and it belongs here.</p>

<p>The FDA has not banned the three chemical filters above. It asked for more data.</p>

<p>Those are two different things. I am not going to mix them up.</p>

<p>So what is left when you take out all the parts nobody can prove?</p>

<p>Something that causes cancer turned up in five products. Made for skin. In the sun. Bought by people trying to be careful.</p>

<p>An outside lab found it first. Then a regulator sat on what it knew.</p>

<p><strong>I am not asking you to believe a study.</strong> I am asking you to look at how they act when nobody is watching.</p>

<p>Then decide if they get your family's skin every summer.</p>

<h2 id="should-i-stop-wearing-sunscreen">Should I stop wearing sunscreen?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no. Sun protection is not the problem. The maker is.</p>

<p>Skin cancer is real. That risk does not drop because you got angry at a company.</p>

<p>Nothing here is a case for going without.</p>

<p>Every woman my age tells me the same skin story. Forty summers. Two bad burns she still remembers. A few spots she watches.</p>

<p>That is why the bottle has to be one you trust. The answer is a better product, not no product.</p>

<h2 id="what-do-i-use-instead">What do I use instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a mineral sunscreen, from a company that answers to its members.</p>

<p>Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are the two the FDA did back.</p>

<p>They sit on the skin. They bounce the light back. They do not soak in.</p>

<p>And they do not need spray gas to leave the bottle. That was the way benzene got in.</p>

<p>I am not going to send you to the aisle to read fine print.</p>

<p>The 2021 recall shows why that will not save you. The benzene was never on the label.</p>

<p>What protects you is who made it, and what they do when something goes wrong.</p>

<p>That is why my family buys through a private membership store instead. We are the manufacturer. No middleman, no markup. One name on it, and a person who picks up the phone.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Were they taken off every shelf?</strong> No. The recall covered certain lots of five spray products. Other spray sunscreens from other companies stayed on sale.</p>

<p><strong>Is a spray worse than a lotion?</strong> The 2021 recall was all sprays. The spray gas is the likely way the benzene got in. A pump or a lotion has no spray gas at all.</p>

<p><strong>Does this prove sunscreen causes cancer?</strong> No, and I am not saying it does. A cancer causing chemical was found in five products. Nobody measured what it did to one person.</p>

<p><strong>Do mineral sunscreens work as well?</strong> Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are the two the FDA backed as safe and effective. They carry the least argument.</p>
