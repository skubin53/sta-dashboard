<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>On 14 July 2021, Johnson &amp; Johnson recalled five aerosol sunscreens sold under the Neutrogena and Aveeno names after testing found benzene in them.</p>
<p><strong><a href="https://www.washingtonpost.com/health/2021/07/15/johnson-johnson-sunscreen-recall-benzene/">Benzene is a known human carcinogen. It was never an ingredient in any of those products.</a></strong> It was found in the finished cans anyway.</p>
<p>That is the part worth sitting with. It was not on the label, because a label only lists what a company meant to put in. Nobody standing in a drugstore aisle could have read their way to it.</p>

<h2 id="what-was-actually-recalled">What was actually recalled, and when?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: five aerosol sunscreens, pulled on 14 July 2021, after an independent laboratory found benzene in them.</p>

<p>The five were Neutrogena Beach Defense, Neutrogena Cool Dry Sport, Neutrogena Invisible Daily Defense, Neutrogena Ultra Sheer and Aveeno Protect + Refresh. All of them aerosols. All of them the kind of can you keep in a beach bag and spray on a moving child.</p>

<p>The testing that started it came from Valisure, an independent laboratory that buys products off the shelf and analyses them. <a href="https://www.consumernotice.org/news/jj-recalls-neutrogena-aveeno-sunscreens-benzene-contamination/">Valisure filed a citizen petition with the FDA</a> reporting benzene in dozens of sunscreen batches, some of them well above the two parts per million the FDA allows conditionally for drug products where it cannot be avoided.</p>

<p>Then it got worse. Consumer Reports later obtained internal records and reported that <a href="https://www.consumerreports.org/health/sunscreens/fda-safety-risks-aveeno-neutrogena-sunscreens-with-benzene-a8377840278/">the FDA had information about the safety risks and did not make it public</a>.</p>

<h2 id="how-does-benzene-get-in">How does benzene get into a sunscreen at all?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: nobody adds it. It arrives as a contaminant, most often through the propellant in an aerosol can.</p>

<p>Benzene is a solvent. It shows up in petroleum processing, and the pressurised hydrocarbons used to push an aerosol out of a can come from that same world. Contamination anywhere upstream travels downstream into the finished product.</p>

<p><strong>This is the whole reason the recall matters more than the chemistry.</strong> A company can hold a perfectly clean formula and still ship a contaminated can, because the problem entered somewhere it was not looking. What is being tested, how often, and what happens when something is found are decisions made inside a building you will never see.</p>

<p>Valisure's petition made the same point about how much of it reaches you. Sunscreen is designed to be applied thickly, over most of the body, repeatedly, and left there in the heat. <a href="https://caseygerry.com/blog/johnson-johnson-sunscreen-recall-benzene-found-in-neutrogena-and-aveeno-aerosol-sunscreen-products/">The petition argued the exposure route is unusually direct for exactly that reason</a>.</p>

<h2 id="what-is-in-the-bottle">What is in the bottle the rest of the time?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a chemical filter package, and several of those filters are absorbed into the bloodstream.</p>

<h3>Benzene</h3>
<p>A contaminant, not an ingredient, which is why it never appears in the list. Classified as a known human carcinogen. This is the one that triggered the 2021 recall.</p>

<h3>Oxybenzone</h3>
<p>A chemical UV filter used for decades. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-advances-new-proposed-regulation-make-sure-sunscreens-are-safe-and-effective">The FDA's own sunscreen work</a> put it in the group of active ingredients where the agency asked manufacturers for more safety data rather than affirming it as generally recognised as safe and effective. Hawaii and Key West restricted it in the name of reef damage before most shoppers had heard of it.</p>

<h3>Octinoxate</h3>
<p>Another chemical filter, in the same FDA data request, and in the same state level restrictions.</p>

<h3>Homosalate</h3>
<p>Also in the group the FDA asked for more data on. It is common, it is cheap, and it is in a great many of the bottles on the shelf.</p>

<h3>Avobenzone</h3>
<p>The broad spectrum workhorse. It degrades in sunlight, which is why formulas stack other chemicals in to hold it together, which is how a simple product ends up with a long list.</p>

<h2 id="does-that-mean-it-is-hurting-me">Does that mean it is hurting me?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the recall is a fact. What it did to any one person is not something anybody measured.</p>

<p>Johnson &amp; Johnson said at the time that the benzene levels found would not be expected to cause harm, and no regulator has said otherwise. That is their position and it belongs here.</p>

<p>The FDA has not banned oxybenzone, octinoxate or homosalate. It asked for more data. Those are different things and running them together is how a post gets picked apart in the comments.</p>

<p>So what is left when you strip out everything nobody can prove? A carcinogen turned up in five products meant for skin, in the sun, on people who bought them to be careful. The company found it after an outside laboratory went looking. And a regulator held information back.</p>

<p><strong>You are not being asked to believe a study.</strong> You are being asked to look at how a company behaves when nobody is watching, and decide whether you want to keep handing it your family's skin every summer.</p>

<h2 id="should-i-stop-wearing-sunscreen">Should I stop wearing sunscreen?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no. Sun protection is not the problem. The manufacturer is.</p>

<p>Skin cancer is real, and the risk of it does not go down because you got annoyed at a corporation. Nothing in this post is a case for going without.</p>

<p>Every woman I speak to who is my age has the same skin story: forty summers, a couple of bad burns she still remembers, and a growing list of spots she keeps an eye on. That is exactly why the bottle has to be one you trust, and why the answer is a better product rather than no product.</p>

<h2 id="what-do-i-use-instead">What do I use instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a mineral sunscreen from a company that answers to its members rather than to a shelf.</p>

<p>Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are the two filters the FDA did affirm. They sit on the skin and reflect rather than absorbing into it, and neither one needs a pressurised hydrocarbon to leave the bottle, which removes the exact route benzene took in 2021.</p>

<p>I am not going to tell you to stand in an aisle and study the fine print. The 2021 recall is proof that will not save you. The benzene was never on the label. The only thing that protects you is who made it and what they do when something goes wrong.</p>

<p>That is why I stopped shopping in those stores altogether and moved my family onto a private membership store. Not a different aisle. A different supply chain, with a name attached to it and a person I can call.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Were the recalled sunscreens taken off every shelf?</strong> The recall covered specific lots of five aerosol products and Johnson &amp; Johnson asked retailers to pull them. Plenty of other aerosol sunscreens from other companies stayed on sale throughout.</p>

<p><strong>Is a spray sunscreen worse than a lotion?</strong> The 2021 recall was entirely aerosols, and the propellant is the most likely route the benzene took. A pump or a lotion has no pressurised hydrocarbon in it at all.</p>

<p><strong>Does this prove sunscreen causes cancer?</strong> No, and this post does not claim it. A carcinogen was found in five products. Nobody measured what it did to any individual person, and I am not going to blur that line to make a stronger headline.</p>

<p><strong>Are mineral sunscreens as effective?</strong> Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are the two active ingredients the FDA affirmed as safe and effective. They are the ones with the least argument attached to them.</p>
