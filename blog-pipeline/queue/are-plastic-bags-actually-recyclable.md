<h2 id="what-they-promised">What did the bag makers promise you?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: that the bag would get recycled if you brought it back to a store.</p>

<p>You have knelt on the kitchen floor with the cabinet door open. Out came every bag you saved since spring. You stuffed them all into one big bag and hung it on the pantry door.</p>

<p>The next day your knees felt it. You did it anyway, because you were raised not to waste.</p>

<p>You wanted that trip to count.</p>

<p>Now look at what the bag makers printed on the bag.</p>

<p><a href="https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/2025-10-17%20-%20Complaint.pdf">The state's lawsuit shows a Novolex bag sold in California.</a> In bold capital letters it says, "PLEASE RETURN TO A PARTICIPATING STORE FOR RECYCLING."</p>

<p>Under that, in tiny print, it says, "Programs may not exist in your area."</p>

<p>And so the promise was in big letters and the catch was in small ones.</p>

<p>California banned thin checkout bags in 2016. Stores could still sell thicker ones for at least 10 cents.</p>

<p>The law allowed them only if they could be recycled in the state. The makers told the state their bags passed.</p>

<p>Then the state asked them to prove it.</p>

<h2 id="the-store-bin">Where do the bags go after the store bin?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the state says most are never recycled, in California or anywhere else.</p>

<p>California started looking into the bag makers in November 2022. It sent subpoenas. Those are orders to hand over records.</p>

<p>The state says the makers could not show how many bags got recycled at their own plants. And it says they could not show what share of their California bags got recycled at all.</p>

<p>Then the state checked 69 recycling sites. <a href="https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-announces-335-million-settlement-plastic-bag">Only two said they took plastic bags, "but could not confirm the bags were, in fact, recycled."</a></p>

<p>And the bin by the store's front door? The complaint says most bags dropped in those bins "are not recycled, in California or anywhere else."</p>

<p>It says they end up in landfills or burned, or they get shipped to other countries.</p>

<p>You carried them into the store on your hip, on knees that hurt. The state says that trip did not count the way the bag promised.</p>

<p>And so the next bag of bags can stay home. Set it down by the door. I will show you what to do with it.</p>

<h2 id="the-word">Why could they print "recyclable" at all?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the makers signed off on their own bags as recyclable.</p>

<p>There is a national guide for that word. It comes from the FTC, which polices false ads.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-260/section-260.12">The FTC Green Guides set the bar. The plain word is only for things "at least 60 percent" of people can recycle.</a></p>

<p>The fewer people who can recycle it, the louder the warning is supposed to be.</p>

<p>A tiny line under a big bold one is not much of a warning. The big letters did their job, and you believed them like anybody would.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">The case against my own story</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: three fair points, and they are all true.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> Nobody admitted anything. <a href="https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/Novolex%20et%20al.%20-%20Stipulation%20re%20Consent%20Judgment.pdf">The court papers say the deal "shall not constitute an admission of wrongdoing or liability."</a></p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> Some bags do get recycled. The complaint says Novolex runs a plant in Indiana. Novolex says it has turned store bin bags into new bags there since at least 2020.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> This case is about California's own law. It is not a finding that no bag is ever recycled anywhere.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because the promise went on the bag in capital letters. When the state asked for the records behind it, the makers had none to give, the state says.</p>

<p>And you were never the only one saving them. The complaint says these three makers sold more than 4.3 billion of these bags to California stores since 2016.</p>

<h2 id="what-do-i-use">What do I carry my groceries in instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a cloth bag you already own, washed and used for years.</p>

<p>Start with the trip you make every week. That is the one that fills the drawer.</p>

<p>Keep two cloth bags on the hook by the back door. Put them back in the car when you unload. Wash them like a dish towel when they get grubby.</p>

<p>For food, my answer never changes. Find a local farm and get to know the farmer. Carry what they grow home in a basket.</p>

<p>Use up the plastic bags you already have. Line a small trash can with them until they tear.</p>

<p>Do not put them in the blue bin at home. The complaint says most California sorting plants turn them away. Bags get caught in the machines.</p>

<p>Here is what a cloth bag will not do. It will not clean up the ocean. It will only stop the cabinet under your sink from filling back up.</p>

<p>The rest of your house is what Switch to America is for. We are a private membership store and the manufacturer. No middlemen, no markup.</p>

<p>Do the small thing first. Tonight, take the bag of bags off the pantry door. Hang a cloth one in its place.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Can I put plastic bags in my recycling bin at home?</strong></p>
<p>In California, mostly no. The state's complaint says most sorting plants there turn them away. Anywhere else, call your trash company and ask.</p>

<p><strong>Did the bag makers get in trouble?</strong></p>
<p>They settled. In January 2026, Novolex, Inteplast and Mettler agreed to pay California $3.35 million. None of them admitted wrongdoing.</p>

<p><strong>Are plastic bags banned in California now?</strong></p>
<p>At the checkout, yes. <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB1053">Since January 2026, a law called SB 1053 stops stores from handing you a plastic carryout bag.</a> Stores offer recycled paper bags, or you bring your own.</p>

<p><strong>What do I do with the bags I already saved?</strong></p>
<p>Use them up. Line a small trash can with them until they tear. Keep them out of the blue bin.</p>
