<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>Ask five things before you pay anyone a cent.</p>
<p>What does it cost, all in? Can I take a week? Who is in your success stories? What does the guarantee really say? Has anyone sued you?</p>
<p>Here is why. In February 2025 the FTC sued a company called Growth Cave. <strong><a href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/03/ftc-takes-action-stop-sprawling-growth-cave-business-opportunity-credit-repair-scam">The FTC alleged it took about $50 million from buyers "using false promises of huge income."</a></strong></p>
<p>Each of the five answers one move from that case. Use them on anyone. Use them on me first.</p>
</div>

<p>You came home from somebody's pitch with a glossy black gift bag, tissue still in it. You set it on the hall table with the mail and never opened it. Now they want your answer on a call.</p>

<h2 id="what-happened">What did Growth Cave do, according to the FTC?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the FTC says the price came last, and the contract came after the money.</p>

<p>Growth Cave sold a program to build and sell your own online course. It found buyers through YouTube ads, the FTC says.</p>

<p>In one video the FTC cites, the promise came in capital letters: "It is literally IMPOSSIBLE to fail."</p>

<p>Then came the "strategy call." <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/GrowthCave-Complaint.pdf">The FTC's complaint</a> says the price showed up there "for the first time." It ran from $3,500 to $9,800.</p>

<p>And so the money moved fast. The complaint says Growth Cave took payment, or set the buyer up with financing, "during the very same call."</p>

<p>In most cases, the FTC says, buyers got the contract only after they paid.</p>

<h2 id="first-call">Why do they want your money on the first call?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: because a week is the one thing a pitch like that cannot survive.</p>

<p>You know the call I mean. The bonus that ends at midnight, the loan they can set up while you hold, the contract that shows up after the card, the voice that keeps saying your first name, that feeling that the call is running you instead of the other way around.</p>

<p>When the FTC's business opportunity rule covers a sale, the seller must give you a one-page form. <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/selling-work-home-or-other-business-opportunity-revised-rule-may-apply-you-1">It has to reach you "seven days before" you sign or pay.</a></p>

<p>That form has to name certain legal actions against the seller from the past ten years, and its refund policy. It also lists at least 10 past buyers you can call.</p>

<p>The FTC charged Growth Cave with breaking that rule. Its complaint says the company often failed to hand over the form in time.</p>

<p>That rule does not cover every kind of business. So do not wait for the form. Take the seven days anyway.</p>

<p>Think about what a week does. You sleep on it. You tell your sister. You search the name. You read the contract at your own kitchen table.</p>

<p>A pitch that needs your card tonight cannot get through that week.</p>

<h2 id="five-questions">What are the five questions?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: cost, time, proof, the way out and the record.</p>

<h3>1. What does it cost, all in?</h3>

<p>All in means the start fee, the upgrade they offer later, and any loan. The FTC says Growth Cave pushed an "upgraded" program that cost "an additional $30,000 to $50,000."</p>

<p>Ask for the contract before you pay. If it comes after the card, so does every surprise in it.</p>

<h3>2. Can I take a week?</h3>

<p>The only right answer is yes. If the price only lasts until tonight, say thank you and hang up.</p>

<h3>3. Who are the people in your success stories?</h3>

<p>Ask if they work for the company. The FTC says four "clients" in one Growth Cave pitch were <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/GrowthCave-Complaint.pdf">"in fact Growth Cave employees."</a> By the FTC's account, nobody watching was told.</p>

<h3>4. What does the guarantee really say?</h3>

<p>Growth Cave offered a profit guarantee, the complaint says. When buyers asked for refunds, the FTC says, the company turned them down and said it only promised to keep trying to "work with you."</p>

<p>The FTC says that line was "slipped into the Agreement" buyers signed after paying. So get the guarantee in writing, and read it before the card comes out.</p>

<h3>5. Has anyone sued you?</h3>

<p>Ask the seller. Then check it yourself. The FTC says to search the company name with words like "review," "scam," or "complaint." Do the owner's name too.</p>

<p>Here it is on one page. Take a picture of it before your next call.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly. So here is the part that cuts against me.</p>

<p>I know what you are thinking. I am asking you to book a call. Growth Cave asked people to book a call too.</p>

<p>That is fair. So run all five on me first, out loud, on the call.</p>

<p>Switch to America sells straight from the maker. No middlemen and no markup.</p>

<p>And you decide nothing on the call. Take the week. If I push, you have your answer.</p>

<p>Now the limits. Growth Cave's owners <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/GrowthCave-FinalOrder.pdf">"neither admit nor deny" the charges</a>, the court order says. Everything above is what the FTC alleged, not what a jury found.</p>

<p>And a list will not catch everyone. A smooth talker can answer all five. The list just slows the call down, so you can hear the answers.</p>

<p>I will not hand you a number to expect either. Nobody honest can.</p>

<h2 id="before-the-call">What would I do before the next call?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: write the five down, and bring somebody who loves you.</p>

<h3>Write the five on a card</h3>

<p>Put it on the counter next to your coffee. Ask the questions in order, and write each answer down while they talk.</p>

<h3>Bring a friend to the call</h3>

<p>The FTC tells you to <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/when-business-offer-or-coaching-program-scam">talk to someone you trust before you sign up</a>, "a friend, a family member, a neighbor." A straight seller will not mind a second set of ears. A slick one will want you alone.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are women asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>What if they will not let me take a week?</strong></p>
<p>Then you have your answer. The FTC's own advice says an offer that is good today "should be good tomorrow, too." Say thank you and hang up.</p>

<p><strong>What if I already paid?</strong></p>
<p>Ask for your refund in writing and keep a copy. Then report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to your state attorney general.</p>

<p><strong>Do these five questions work on you too?</strong></p>
<p>Yes, and I want you to use them. Ask me every one on the call, out loud. If I dodge one, do not book a second call.</p>
