<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>The pay plan you built under ended on March 31, 2026. The company picked the date. You did not.</p>
<p>So is Younique now Curated? For sellers, yes. Curated's own help page says that after March 31, <strong><a href="https://support.curatedby.you/portal/en/kb/articles/transitioning-from-younique-to-curated-7-5-2026">"commissions on Younique product sales will be earned exclusively through Curated."</a></strong></p>
<p>And the new plan has the same kind of clock on it. Curated's creator agreement says its pay plan can change "at any time."</p>
<p>A rank lives on their paper. A customer who knows your name lives with you.</p>

<h2 id="what-ended">What actually ended when the Younique pay plan did?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the Younique Pay Plan itself. Its last day was March 31, 2026.</p>

<p>It is late and the kitchen is quiet. One lamp is on. The laptop is shut and the stool is pushed back.</p>

<p>You know the list. The live videos at nine at night. The group chat. The friend who only ever wanted the mascara. The box of samples by the door. And that feeling that the plan was running your week instead of the other way around.</p>

<div class="sta-cta">
<h3>Twenty minutes, and you decide nothing on the call</h3>
<p>Working out what comes next? It is a video call about my own business, Switch to America. You sign nothing.</p>
<p><a href="https://link.switchtoamerica.com/widget/bookings/boostyourincome">Book the twenty minutes</a></p>
<span class="micro">You can always say no.</span>
</div>

<p>Then a help page told you how it ends. <a href="https://support.curatedby.you/portal/en/kb/articles/transitioning-from-younique-to-curated-7-5-2026">Curated's page on the move from Younique</a> sets it out by date.</p>

<p>On February 17, 2026, new Younique seller sign-ups closed. Sponsoring ended the same day. March 31 was "the final day of the Younique Pay Plan." After that, Younique sales paid only through Curated.</p>

<p>And so the plan you learned is gone. The products are still for sale.</p>

<figure style="margin:28px 0;">
<img src="https://scan.ismyhometoxic.com/blog-images/youniqueisn-villain-v1.webp" alt="An empty navy and white island kitchen at night, one lamp on, a closed laptop and a half glass of water on the counter, a stool pushed back" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px;">
</figure>

<p>Now the sellers you brought in. Curated has no teams. The page calls the move "an individual decision." Each seller had to sign up alone.</p>

<p>There was one way to carry it over, called the Legacy Program. You had to be on Curated by March 31. Miss that date, the page says, and your old structure "will not carry forward."</p>

<p>Even inside it, there is a catch. Each seller has to keep up a set level of sales over any rolling three months. If she slips, she stays on Curated. You stop being paid on her sales.</p>

<p>None of that was your choice. All of it was on their paper.</p>

<h2 id="customers">What happened to your customers?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: your customers can still buy Younique. A Younique.com order after April 1 carries no seller name.</p>

<p>The same page puts it plainly. "Customers may purchase directly on Younique.com (without seller attribution) or through Creators on Curated."</p>

<p>Then it adds a date. Orders on Younique.com after April 1 "will not be attributed" to a seller.</p>

<p>Think of your customer who reorders the same foundation every spring. Out of habit, the order goes through the old site. The sale is no longer yours. Nobody did anything wrong.</p>

<p>You built the trust. The company decided where the order lands.</p>

<h2 id="change-again">Can Curated change the rules again?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. Its own creator agreement says so in one line.</p>

<p><a href="https://curatedby.you/-/creator-agreement">Curated's 2026 creator agreement</a> says it out loud. "The Pay Plan may be modified or amended at any time, at Curated's sole and absolute discretion."</p>

<p>It says it will tell you in writing or electronically when it does. That is notice. It is not a vote.</p>

<p><a href="https://curatedby.you/-/terms">Its terms of use, last updated June 2026</a>, go further. Curated can change them "at any time and without prior notice."</p>

<p>And so the new plan sits on the same kind of paper as the old one.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly, and here is the part I will not dress up.</p>

<p>Younique did not vanish overnight. Its dates were set out in writing and offered a way to carry some of it over. <a href="https://curatedby.you/">Curated's home page</a> says you can start with "No follower minimum. No sales requirements." That is fairer than some.</p>

<p>Now me. Switch to America could change its rules too. Any company can. If you want somebody to promise you a number, this is not for you.</p>

<p>Here is what I can tell you about us. Switch to America sells straight from the manufacturer. No middlemen and no markup. No minimums, no maximums. You pay what a brand new shopper pays.</p>

<p>I will not hand you a number to expect. Nobody honest can.</p>

<h2 id="this-week">What would I do first this week?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: write down your customers by name. Then ask the next company the same questions.</p>

<p>You close the laptop and look out the window. You do not need every answer tonight. You need two things done by Friday.</p>

<h3>Write down your customers by name</h3>

<p>Every one. The one who reorders the same foundation. Her sister. The friend from church who asked what you use on your skin. No notice from any company can rewrite that list.</p>

<h3>Ask the next company the same questions</h3>

<p>Including me. Who can change the pay plan, and on how much notice? What happens to my customers if I stop? Where is the income disclosure, and can I read it today?</p>

<p>If somebody dodges one, that is your answer.</p>

<p>Then talk it over with a friend who has nothing to sell you.</p>
