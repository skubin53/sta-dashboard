<h2 id="what-got-recalled">What exactly did Thrasio recall?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the enzyme stain spray in two scents, sold from March 2019 through December 2025.</p>

<p>You know the bottle. It is orange and white, with "Angry Orange" and "Stain &amp; Odor Remover" on the front.</p>

<p>The recall covers the Fresh Clean and Orange Twist scents. It covers the 24-ounce, the 32-ounce and the gallon. Some came with a UV light that attaches to the bottle.</p>

<p>So who is Thrasio? A company in Boston. <a href="https://www.thrasio.com/for-sellers/case-studies/angry-orange">By its own case study, it bought Angry Orange in November 2018. Then it added "a ready-to-use spray for stain removal."</a></p>

<p><a href="https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/alert-recall/angry-orange-stain-removers-recalled-due-risk-exposure-bacteria">Canada's recall notice names a different company as the maker, Chempace Corp.</a></p>

<p>So the company behind the name bought the brand, and another company filled the bottle. Remember that when you buy the next one.</p>

<h2 id="how-it-reaches-you">How could a germ in a stain spray get into you?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: through the air, your eyes or a cut, the notice says. And you use this spray on your knees.</p>

<p>Think about how you use it. You spray the wet spot, then you kneel and scrub it with a rag in your bare hand.</p>

<p>Your face is right there. So is the mist.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2026/Thrasio-Recalls-Angry-Orange-Enzyme-Stain-Removers-Due-to-Risk-of-Exposure-to-Bacteria">The recall notice says the germ "can enter the body if inhaled, through the eyes, or through a break in the skin."</a></p>

<p>It says the risk is for people with weak immune systems, external medical devices or lung problems. For them, it warns of serious infection.</p>

<h3>The germ the recall names</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.cdc.gov/pseudomonas-aeruginosa/about/index.html">The CDC says this family of germs lives in "soil and water." It says the infections "typically occur in healthcare settings," and they can be hard to treat.</a></p>

<p>Then there is the line I cannot get past, in the part about throwing it away.</p>

<p><strong>"Do not empty the product prior to disposal. Do not attempt to recycle the product bottle."</strong></p>

<p>You sprayed it on the carpet where you sit. Now you are told not to even pour it down your own drain.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">The case against my own story</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no injuries were reported, and healthy immune systems are usually not affected.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> Nobody was reported hurt. The US notice says "None reported."</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The risk is narrow. The notice says "People with healthy immune systems are usually not affected by bacteria."</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> The Angry Orange odor spray was not part of this. <a href="https://angryorange.com/pages/productrecall">The company's recall page lists its Pet Odor Eliminator as "NOT impacted."</a></p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because it sold for almost seven years. The notice does not say when the germ got in, and so I will not guess.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.classaction.org/media/thrasio-complaint.pdf">One buyer who sued says Thrasio did not check the making of it closely enough.</a> That is her claim. No court has ruled on it. <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72235094/vivian-makar-v-thrasio-llc/">The docket shows the case still open in September 2026.</a></p>

<p><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260122164825/https://angryorange.com/pages/about-us">And on recall day, the company's About Us page still called its products "safe to use around your home."</a></p>

<p>First, the old bottle. Stop using it. The refund steps are in the questions below.</p>

<p>For the next accident, blot first. Press an old towel down hard, then a dry one.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Is the Angry Orange Pet Odor Eliminator recalled too?</strong></p>
<p>No. The company's recall page says its Pet Odor Eliminator is "NOT impacted." Only the Enzyme Stain Remover was recalled.</p>

<p><strong>How do I get my money back?</strong></p>
<p>Write "recalled" and your initials on the bottle in marker. Email a photo of it to productrecall@angryorange.com. Then throw it out in its bottle. Do not empty it first.</p>

<p><strong>I used it for years. Should I be scared?</strong></p>
<p>Not by the notice's account. It lists no injuries. It points the risk at weak immune systems, external medical devices and lung problems.</p>

<p><strong>Is the Angry Orange lawsuit over?</strong></p>
<p>No. Makar v. Thrasio was filed February 4, 2026, in federal court. The docket shows it still open in September 2026. Nothing in it is proven.</p>
