<h2 id="what-was-found">What did the 2026 inspection find in Zicam swabs?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: germ test results "out of limit," at the outside plant that makes the swabs.</p>

<p>You want to stop a cold before it takes hold. Nothing wrong with wanting that. The problem is the box Church &amp; Dwight sent to the store.</p>

<p><a href="https://api.fda.gov/drug/enforcement.json?search=recall_number:%22D-0790-2026%22">The federal recall record gives the reason: "out of limit results for microbiological testing."</a> That means the tests for germs came back outside the limit. It turned up in an inspection at the outside plant that makes the swabs.</p>

<p>Three lots are named: UN52882, UN53431 and UN60411. They shipped nationwide.</p>

<p>In 2025 the first notice was a press release. In 2026 it was a letter, so this one was easy to miss.</p>

<h2 id="the-2025-recall">Wasn't Zicam already recalled in 2025?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. Every lot of the swabs, for mold in the cotton tips.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/church-dwight-co-inc-issues-voluntary-nationwide-recall-zicamr-cold-remedy-nasal-swabs-zicamr-nasal">On June 6, 2025, Church &amp; Dwight recalled every lot of Zicam Cold Remedy Nasal Swabs.</a> Its notice pointed to "fungi in cotton swab components." Fungi means mold.</p>

<p>The same notice says where the danger sits. It warns of "serious and life-threatening blood infections in users whose nasal mucosa may be compromised due to inflammation and mechanical injuries."</p>

<p>Nasal mucosa is the soft lining of your nose. Inflamed is how it feels on day one of a cold.</p>

<p>And so, by the company's own words, the risk sits in a sore nose. That is the nose you have when you reach for the box.</p>

<h3 id="the-molds">Aspergillus and Penicillium: which molds did Church &amp; Dwight flag?</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.churchdwightrecall.com/">Church &amp; Dwight's recall page names four molds it flagged in some swabs.</a> Three are Aspergillus and Penicillium types. The fourth is Exophiala. The page says the results are "not yet conclusive."</p>

<p>Mold on a shower curtain is one thing. Mold on a swab in your nose is another.</p>

<h2 id="before-2025">Was the Zicam swab in trouble before 2025?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. In 2009, over reports of lost smell. Church &amp; Dwight bought Zicam's owner in 2020.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/nasal-spray-can-cause-loss-smell-fda-warns-flna1c9451113">On June 16, 2009, federal regulators told shoppers to stop using Zicam's zinc nasal gel and swabs.</a> They said the products "can permanently damage the sense of smell," the Associated Press reported.</p>

<p>Regulators counted about 130 reports of lost smell since 1999. Those reports came from doctors and patients, "not the manufacturer," the AP wrote.</p>

<p>Health officials said the company had more than 800 lost-smell complaints on file. They asked to see them.</p>

<p>That company was Matrixx Initiatives. It said Zicam's safety was "supported by the cumulative science." <a href="https://www.proskauer.com/release/proskauer-represents-church-and-dwight-in-their-acquisition-of-zicam-brand-for-530-million">In December 2020, Church &amp; Dwight bought Matrixx for $530 million.</a></p>

<p>The 2026 recall record calls today's swab "Zinc-Free," so it is not the 2009 formula. It is still the Zicam name, and still a swab in your nose.</p>

<p>Three Zicam recalls in 14 months: the swabs twice, and elderberry drops once. Here they are with the 2009 warning.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Isn't a Class II recall the mild kind?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: milder than 2025, yes. Only three lots are named. But it is the second swab recall in 14 months.</p>

<p>Here is the part you are probably thinking. This time it is only three lots, and it is not the serious kind.</p>

<p>That is fair, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/industry-guidance-recalls/recalls-background-and-definitions">On the federal recall scale, Class II is one step below Class I.</a> The 2025 swab recall was Class I, the most serious kind.</p>

<p>In 2025 the company also said no serious harm had been reported.</p>

<p>So why would I still throw the box out?</p>

<p>Two swab recalls in 14 months, both over mold or germs.</p>

<p>You trusted that box to be clean. You do not owe it a third chance.</p>
