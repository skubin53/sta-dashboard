<h2 id="what-got-pulled">What got pulled, and who actually made it?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: eight separate recalls, 3,111,072 bottles, from one company you have probably never heard of.</p>

<p>The name on the recall is K.C. Pharmaceuticals, Inc., of Pomona, California.</p>

<p>You have never seen it because it does not sell under its own name. It fills the bottles that carry the Walgreens, CVS, Kroger, Rite Aid and a dozen other store labels.</p>

<p>On March 3, 2026 it recalled eight different eye drops at once. <a href="https://api.fda.gov/drug/enforcement.json?search=recalling_firm%3A%22K.C.+Pharmaceuticals%22+AND+recall_initiation_date%3A%5B20260101+TO+20261231%5D&amp;limit=10">The FDA lists all eight under one reason, "Lack of Assurance of Sterility," each sent nationwide and still ongoing as of April 8, 2026.</a></p>

<p>Add the eight together and it is 3,111,072 bottles.</p>

<p>This was not the first time.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/kc-pharmaceuticals-inc-654986-08032023">The FDA put the sterility problem in writing on August 3, 2023.</a> That year the same company pulled two eye drop recalls, more than 738,000 bottles between them. Less than three years later it pulled eight at once, 3,111,072 bottles. More than four times as many.</p>

<h2 id="why-eye-drops">Isn't everything made like this? Why start with eye drops?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: because a drop goes straight onto your eye, with no skin in the way.</p>

<p>You are right that you cannot rip out everything under your sink tonight, and half the pharmacy probably comes from the same few factories anyway.</p>

<p>So do not try to fix all of it. Start with the one thing you put on the wettest, most open part of your body, two or three times a day.</p>

<p>That is the eye drops.</p>

<p>Everything else you buy has skin in the way. A drop does not. It sits right on the surface of your eye, which is why the sterility of the bottle is everything.</p>

<p>And this is the company the FDA had already warned. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/kc-pharmaceuticals-inc-654986-08032023">The 2023 letter said the company's aseptic processing was "inadequately designed to prevent contamination of" its "ophthalmic drug products."</a> Ophthalmic means eye. That was the FDA's own word for it.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">The case against my own story</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: three fair points cut against me, and they are all true.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> The FDA called all eight of these Class II recalls, not Class I. That is not the most serious class, and no infections have been reported.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> "Lack of Assurance of Sterility" means the company could not prove the drops were sterile. It does not mean a germ was found in a bottle.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> The recalled batches all carried expiration dates between April and October 2026. So most of them are past date now anyway.</p>

<p>Those are real, and I am not going to pretend they are not.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because of what a drop is, and because this same company was told in writing in 2023 and it happened again, over four times bigger. The FDA already had the whole pattern on paper, years before these bottles ever reached your purse.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Is the store brand bottle in my purse part of this?</strong> The 2026 recalls named eight versions filled by K.C. Pharmaceuticals and sold under labels like Walgreens, CVS and Kroger, with expiration dates between April and October 2026. If yours is older stock, it could be one of them. That is about how those batches were made, not every bottle on the shelf today.</p>

<p><strong>Is Equate from Walmart on the list?</strong> No. Equate is not among the 2026 labels. It shows up in the same company's earlier recall, D-0898-2023, of 397,430 bottles of Dry Eye Relief started in June 2023.</p>

<p><strong>Did the drops actually have germs in them?</strong> The recall reason was "Lack of Assurance of Sterility," which means the company could not prove the drops were sterile. It does not mean a germ was found in a bottle, and no infections were reported.</p>

<p><strong>Why does it matter if it is only eye drops?</strong> Because a drop goes straight onto the surface of your eye with no skin to stop anything, and the FDA had already cited this same company for the same sterility problem in writing back in 2023.</p>
