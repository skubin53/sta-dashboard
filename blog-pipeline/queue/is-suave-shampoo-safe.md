<h2 id="the-keratin-kit">What happened with the Suave keratin kit?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it was pulled in May 2012. Then the owner agreed to pay $10.25 million to settle the hair and scalp claims.</p>

<p>Your hair gets drier and frizzier with the years. So a kit that promised smooth hair for 30 days sounds like a gift.</p>

<p>That was the pitch for the Suave Professionals Keratin Infusion 30 Day Smoothing Kit. Unilever owned Suave back then.</p>

<p><a href="https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca7/14-3009/14-3009-2016-03-25.pdf">A federal appeals court wrote in 2016 that "for some consumers, the Smoothing Kit was a disaster."</a> The suits said it "destroyed users' hair and burned their scalps."</p>

<p>The same court says the kit was pulled in May 2012. Shoppers in three states had sued.</p>

<p>The court named the active ingredient. It was thioglycolic acid.</p>

<p>The judges called it "extremely corrosive." They wrote that, left on long enough, it "can dissolve the hair and burn the scalp."</p>

<p>The case was settled. <a href="https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca7/14-3009/14-3009-2016-03-25.pdf">The deal set aside $10 million for injuries and $250,000 to pay back the cost of the kit.</a> By July 2014, 2,294 claims had come in, the court wrote.</p>

<p>So a kit sold as a treat for your hair ended in a fund for hurt scalps.</p>

<h2 id="the-preservative">What was the Suave shampoo lawsuit about?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a 2021 suit says an add-in that keeps Suave shampoo fresh gives off formaldehyde. It says that made hair fall out. Nothing is proven.</p>

<p>The kit was a once in a while thing. Shampoo is every day. It is on your scalp every morning.</p>

<h3 id="dmdm-hydantoin">DMDM hydantoin, the preservative in the suit</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.suave.com/pages/trust-in-suave">Suave's own website says "select Suave products use DMDM hydantoin."</a> It calls it a "stabilizing preservative." That is what keeps the bottle from going moldy.</p>

<p><a href="https://news.bloomberglaw.com/class-action/unilever-sued-over-formaldehyde-related-shampoo-preservative">On June 18, 2021, a shopper sued in federal court in Chicago, Bloomberg Law reported.</a> Her suit names Suave Professionals shampoo and conditioner. It says this add-in gives off formaldehyde. It says that caused hair loss and scalp irritation.</p>

<p>Those are her claims. No court has ruled that the shampoo hurt anyone.</p>

<p>Formaldehyde is not a small word. <a href="https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/formaldehyde/formaldehyde-fact-sheet">The National Cancer Institute says formaldehyde has been named a "known human carcinogen."</a> That means a cause of cancer in people.</p>

<p><a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6389437/">In a 2019 study, researchers tested two shampoos with DMDM hydantoin on the bottle.</a> Each held about 117 micrograms of formaldehyde per gram. The label said DMDM hydantoin. It did not say formaldehyde.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for Suave?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, a fair one. Suave says the add-in is safe and gone. And the brand has a new owner.</p>

<p>Here are the strongest points on the other side.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://www.suave.com/pages/trust-in-suave">Suave says safety reviewers, like the Cosmetic Ingredient Review board, deem DMDM hydantoin safe.</a> It also promised that "by the end of 2021, all Suave products will be DMDM hydantoin free."</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The brand has a new owner. <a href="https://www.beautypackaging.com/contents/view_breaking-news/2023-02-14/unilever-sells-suave-brand-in-north-america-to-yellow-wood-partners/">Unilever sold Suave in North America to Yellow Wood Partners, a Boston investment firm, in 2023.</a> <a href="https://yellowwoodpartners.com/news-article/yellow-wood-partners-portfolio-companies-suave-brands-and-elida-beauty-complete-merger-to-create-evermark-a-leading-global-platform-of-personal-care-brands">In January 2026 it was folded into a new company called Evermark.</a></p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because the promise comes from the same people who put it in. You are asked to take their word that it is gone.</p>

<p>And the bottle in your shower now answers to an investment firm. That is not who you want deciding what touches your scalp.</p>
