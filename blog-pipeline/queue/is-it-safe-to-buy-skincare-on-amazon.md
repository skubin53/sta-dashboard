<h2 id="what-was-sold">What did Amazon sell into California?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: skin-lightening face creams. The state says several held mercury and the listings carried no warning.</p>

<p>You noticed brown patches coming up along your jaw after 50. Everybody said it was hormones, or just your age.</p>

<p>So you searched for something for dark spots, read the reviews, and clicked buy. If it is on Amazon, somebody must have checked it.</p>

<p>The state says nobody warned you. <a href="https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/Complaint%20for%20Injunctive%20Relief%20and%20Civil%20Penalties.pdf">The state's complaint</a> names eight creams. One is Chandni Skin Whitening Cream.</p>

<p>It says Amazon sold or shipped them into California. It says the listings gave no clear warning.</p>

<h3 id="mercury">Mercury: why a dark spot cream would hold it</h3>

<p>Mercury fades skin. The complaint says it "interferes with the body's production of melanin." Melanin is what makes your skin darker.</p>

<p>And so the spots fade, for a while. That is why someone puts it in.</p>

<p>The state says "Mercury is capable of being absorbed through skin." It can also pass from your hand to what you touch. The state says it can then reach kids and others in your home.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/68/wr/mm6850a4.htm">In 2019 the CDC reported on a patient, 47, in Sacramento.</a> She used a cream from Mexico twice a day for seven years. It held 12,000 parts per million of mercury. That jar was not from Amazon. It shows what the number can do.</p>

<h2 id="why-a-lawsuit">Why did it take a lawsuit to stop it?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: because the first lawsuit was filed in August 2014. The settlement came more than ten years later.</p>

<p>The first lawsuit over these creams was filed on August 25, 2014. A second came in 2019. The state looked into it after that.</p>

<p>Then the state asked Amazon to stop. By the state's account, Amazon agreed to a "suppression rule." That is a filter that keeps those creams off its site.</p>

<p>Sit with that. Amazon built that filter after the lawsuits, not before.</p>

<p><a href="https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/pre-filed%20copy%20executed%20amazon%20face%20creams%20proposed%20cj.pdf">The proposed deal</a> lays out the rest. Amazon must hire an outside expert on these creams. The state says it must also list approved brands with no mercury.</p>

<p>Here is the math. The deal gives the state $218,560 in penalties, $278,942 in fees and $65,000 for future checks. The whole deal adds up to $6,187,502. Of that, $4,500,000 goes to the lawyers for the first two who sued.</p>

<p>So most of the money paid for a ten-year fight.</p>

<h2 id="still-2026">Is it still happening in 2026?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a new lawsuit says so. In April 2026 shoppers sued Amazon over sunscreens they say carry lead.</p>

<p>On April 30, 2026, two shoppers filed <a href="https://www.classaction.org/media/wolf-et-al-v-amazon-complaint.pdf">Wolf v. Amazon.com</a> in federal court in Washington state. The lawsuit alleges some sunscreens sold on Amazon carry lead and cadmium. In June 2026 a third shopper joined. It now also says some sunscreens had less SPF than the label.</p>

<p>It says Amazon sold them "with no warning" to children and adults. Those are claims, not rulings.</p>

<p>It also says Amazon writes some of the words on the listing. It alleges Amazon "inserts its own descriptions" with Rufus. That was an AI tool Amazon has since dropped.</p>

<p><a href="https://join.switchtoamerica.com/post/is-sunscreen-safe">What goes through your skin at the beach</a> is its own story.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for Amazon?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, three fair points. None of them puts a warning on the box.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> Amazon did not make these creams. The state says so itself: "Amazon, which does not manufacture these products."</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> Amazon denies it sold any of them. The deal says Amazon admits nothing.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> By the state's account, those creams were never legal to make, ship or sell in California.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p><strong>Because the jar came to your door in Amazon's box.</strong> The state says the listing never warned you. And it took ten years of lawsuits to get a filter built.</p>

<p>You cannot tell from the box. In California's case the problem was creams that should never have been sold at all, by the state's account.</p>

<p><strong>How do I know if my cream has mercury?</strong></p>
<p>You cannot count on the jar to say so. By the state's account, most packages in the California case did not list mercury. The creams in the California case were sold to lighten skin. If yours came from a seller you cannot reach, stop using it.</p>

<p>No. The deal says it is not an admission by Amazon, and Amazon denies the claims. It still agreed to pay and to keep a filter running.</p>
