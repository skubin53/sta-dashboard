<h2 id="what-was-recalled">Which Costco whipped cream was recalled?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: one batch of 15 ounce Kirkland Signature Real Heavy Whipped Cream cans, sold October 11 to 31, 2026.</p>

<p>On November 7, 2026, Alamance Foods sent a letter to Costco members.</p>

<p><a href="https://gdx-assets.costco.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:8dedd165-f61b-475a-b7bb-5f33f77a4675/original/as/FINAL_KS_Whipped_Cream_Member_Letter.pdf">In it, the company said one batch "may contain a small amount of a cleaning chemical."</a></p>

<p>Alamance Foods produces the cream. Costco sells it under the Kirkland Signature name.</p>

<p>The can is 15 ounces. It came in three-packs. The Costco item number is 1777000.</p>

<p>The best-by date is APR/02/27. The time stamps on the recalled cans run from 00:35 to 02:45.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/thanksgiving-staple-recalled-costco-over-possible-contamination-cleaning-chemical">Fox Business reported the cans were sold from October 11 to October 31.</a> That is the month you stock up for pies.</p>

<p>The 12 states are Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Missouri, Mississippi, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee and Wisconsin.</p>

<p>And so a can you bought for Thanksgiving could be in your fridge right now.</p>

<h2 id="chemical-taste">Why does my whipped cream taste like chemicals?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the company says a cleaning chemical may be in one batch, and buyers tasted it.</p>

<p>You take a bite of pie. The cream tastes wrong, sharp and soapy. You wonder if it is just you.</p>

<p>It is not just you. In the same letter, Alamance Foods wrote that "members have reported a chemical smell and taste."</p>

<p>So people were already eating it, and tasting it, before the letter went out.</p>

<p>Here is what the letter leaves out. It never names the chemical.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fox5ny.com/news/costco-whipped-cream-recalled-12-states-over-chemical-contamination-risk">Fox 5 New York reported the company did not say how it happened, or how many cans are affected.</a></p>

<p>So you know a cleaner may be in your cream. You do not know which one, or how much.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/food/story/whipped-cream-sold-costco-recalled-potential-presence-cleaning-138108269">When ABC News asked for comment, Alamance pointed back to its recall letter.</a> That letter is all you get.</p>

<h2 id="check-your-can">How do I check my can?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: match the best-by date and the time stamp, then take the can back to Costco.</p>

<p>Take the can out of the fridge. Find the best-by date and the time stamp printed on it.</p>

<p>If it says APR/02/27 and a time between 00:35 and 02:45, it is from the recalled batch.</p>

<p>Stop using it. The letter says to return it to your local Costco for a full refund.</p>

<p>The letter went out to members who bought the cream. It starts with "Costco records indicate that you" bought it. If that letter came to you, check every can you bought against the date and time stamps.</p>

<p>If you have questions, the letter gives an email for Alamance Foods: customer.care@alamancefood.com.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for the company?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, three fair points, and they are all true.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> It is one batch, from a short run of time stamps. It is not every Kirkland can.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The company says no serious harm needing medical care has been reported. <a href="https://www.live5news.com/2026/11/12/whipped-cream-sold-costco-recalled-dozen-states-ahead-thanksgiving/">CNN reported the same thing.</a></p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> Costco used its buying records to send the letter to members who bought it. I will give it credit for that.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because the company says a cleaning chemical may have ended up in the cream. And you still do not know its name.</p>

<p>Here is what my swap will not do. Cream you whip by hand will not keep in the fridge for weeks like a can. You make it the day you eat it.</p>

<h2 id="give-it-up">Do I have to give up whipped cream?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no, the cream is not the problem; a can from a factory line run by a company you never met is.</p>

<p>You do not have to skip the topping. Pie without cream is just sad.</p>

<p>And you do not have to fix your whole fridge tonight, either. That is not the point of this.</p>

<p>Cream comes from a cow. That part is simple.</p>

<p>The trouble starts when it goes into a can on a big factory line. You never see that line, or who runs it. Then one batch may hold a cleaning chemical, and you are told very little.</p>

<p><a href="https://agr.georgia.gov/recalls">Georgia's Department of Agriculture posted the recall on November 9, 2026, under "Contains Cleaning Solution."</a> That is not a phrase you want next to your pie.</p>

<p>And so the fewer hands your cream passes through, the more you know about it.</p>

<h2 id="what-instead">What do I put on my pie instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: real cream from a local dairy farm, a cold bowl and a whisk.</p>

<p>Start with the cream, because that is where this went wrong.</p>

<p>Find a dairy farm near you. Go and meet the farmer. Ask how the cows are kept and how the cream is handled.</p>

<p>Ask what they wash their equipment with, too. A farmer who is proud of the work will tell you.</p>

<p>Then buy a pint of their heavy cream. Keep it in the coldest part of your fridge until pie time.</p>

<p>Chill a bowl. Pour in the cold cream. Whisk until it holds soft peaks. Add a spoon of sugar if you like.</p>

<p>That is the whole recipe.</p>

<p>Make it with a friend the first time. Let her whisk while you talk. Tell her about the recall over the bowl.</p>

<p>Next time, she might drive out to the farm with you. A habit is easier to change with someone beside you.</p>
