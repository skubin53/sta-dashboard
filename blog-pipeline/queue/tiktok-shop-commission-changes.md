<h2 id="what-changed">What did TikTok Shop change in 2026?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a new line on TikTok's help page sets photo carousels at 60% of your video rate.</p>

<p>You hold the jar up to the phone for the fourth take, because the light keeps changing behind you. You post it at ten at night.</p>

<p>The sample box open on the rug, the tripod you fold up before bed, the phone you turn face down, the rate in your showcase that moved since Monday, that feeling that the app was running your week instead of the other way around.</p>

<p>No shame in that. TikTok wrote the rules, and it keeps rewriting them.</p>

<p><a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20260612144125/https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6077860360177451&lang=en">In June, TikTok's commission page said nothing about carousels.</a> The version dated September 14, 2026 does. <a href="https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6077860360177451">Carousels are "set at 60% of the commission rate for videos."</a></p>

<p>So there was no big cut to point at. Just one new line on a page you had no reason to open.</p>

<h2 id="who-decides">Who decides what one video earns?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the seller does. And the same video can earn two rates on the same afternoon.</p>

<p>TikTok does not set your rate. Its own creator terms say TikTok does <a href="https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6314510387906350">"not determine the amount or terms of any commissions."</a></p>

<p>The seller does, and can set a second, lower rate for sales that come through an ad.</p>

<p><a href="https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-setting-different-affiliate-commission-rates-for-tiktok-shop-ads">TikTok's own help page gives an example.</a> Your video earns 10%. The same video, run in an ad, earns 5%.</p>

<p><a href="https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6077860360177451">TikTok figures your commission on the price the shopper actually paid.</a> And so a lower price cuts your commission with it.</p>

<p>Same video. Same jar. You can be earning two different rates before lunch.</p>

<p>And when a seller lowers your rate, a clock starts. <a href="https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6077860360177451">You keep the old rate for 30 days, with a warning 2 days before it ends. On a one-to-one deal, the wait is 5 days.</a></p>

<p>A raise can reach you the same day. A cut waits 5 days to a month. Either way, it is not your call.</p>

<h2 id="take-back">What can TikTok take back after you earn it?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a full refund takes it to zero. One policy decision can freeze it for good.</p>

<p>The number you see after a sale is a guess. <a href="https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6077860360177451">TikTok's page says, "This estimated commission is not final."</a></p>

<p>If the shopper sends it back for a full refund, your commission is zero. And you wait. <a href="https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6077860360177451">TikTok says about 15 days after delivery, and sometimes 31 or more.</a></p>

<p><a href="https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6837869503317761">TikTok Shop says it "may permanently freeze all payments"</a> after a violation. Miss the 30-day appeal window, or lose the appeal, and the frozen commissions may be forfeited.</p>

<p>Who decides? TikTok does. In its words, "Decisions made by TikTok Shop are final once the appeal process is complete."</p>

<p>Your videos can come down, too. <a href="https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6314510387906350">The terms say TikTok "may remove your Creator Content at any time and for any reason."</a> It can do that "without any notice to you."</p>

<p>A seller can knock your link out too. <a href="https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6837838528120577">Say a seller deletes a product and adds it back at a new rate.</a> TikTok says that wipes the old rate record. It also pulls the product links from every creator's video.</p>

<p>You did the filming and the lighting. Somebody else holds the off switch.</p>

<h2 id="rate-or-customer">What is the difference between a rate and a customer?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a rate is one sale at this week's terms. A customer can keep reordering.</p>

<h3>The rate you rent</h3>

<p>A rate is a promise that lasts 30 days. Then you watch the showcase and hope the number holds.</p>

<h3>The name you keep</h3>

<p>Ask where your customer lives. Not her house. Her record. If it sits inside somebody else's app, you are renting her.</p>

<h3>The soap she runs out of</h3>

<p>This is the one I care about. She runs out of laundry soap. She orders more from the people who make it, and she is still your customer.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly. So here is the part I will not dress up.</p>

<p>TikTok is not a monster. <a href="https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=6837838528120577">Since at least July 2024, it has locked your rate for 30 days when a seller cuts it.</a> That protects you, and I will say so plainly.</p>

<p>The trouble is that the rules are theirs, and they can move them.</p>

<p>Reorders are not magic either. A customer can stop any month she likes, and she should be free to.</p>

<p>My own business is Switch to America. It sells straight from the maker, with no middlemen and no markup.</p>

<p>I will not give you a number to expect. Anybody who does is guessing, or selling.</p>

<h2 id="monday">What would I do first on Monday?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: write down every rate you are on, then who buys from you.</p>

<h3>Write down every rate you are on today</h3>

<p>Open your showcase. Next to each product, write the rate and the date. And so when one moves, you see it on paper first.</p>

<h3>Write down the people, not the videos</h3>

<p>Every name you can remember. The one who asks what you wash your towels in. The follower who messages you from the store aisle.</p>

<p>No seller can take those names away from you. They are yours, and they always were.</p>

<h3>Ask five questions of whatever comes next</h3>

<p>Including of me. Especially of me.</p>

<p>What does it cost to start, all in. Is there a monthly requirement. What happens to my customers if I stop. Where is the income disclosure. Who can change my terms, and how much notice do I get.</p>

<p>If somebody dodges one, that is your answer.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are women asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>Did TikTok Shop cut commission rates in 2026?</strong></p>
<p>I found no TikTok page announcing one cut to every rate. I did find a new line setting photo carousels at 60% of the video rate. Sellers set the rest, one product at a time.</p>

<p><strong>How long until TikTok pays my commission?</strong></p>
<p>TikTok says about 15 days after the order is delivered. It can run to 31 days, and a refund or a dispute holds it up. A full refund takes it to zero.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to quit TikTok Shop to do this?</strong></p>
<p>No. Nobody is asking you to delete a thing. Keep posting what you post, and let this sit beside it.</p>

<p><strong>Can TikTok keep money I already earned?</strong></p>
<p>Its own policy says it can freeze your commissions after a violation. If your appeal is late or denied, the frozen money may be forfeited.</p>
