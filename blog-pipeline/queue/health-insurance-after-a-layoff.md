<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>The meeting took ten minutes. And the first thing you thought about was not the paycheck. It was your prescriptions.</p>
<p>Here is the short version. You can keep the same plan for a while. A law called COBRA usually lets you keep it for <a href="https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/an-employees-guide-health-benefits-under-cobra-2022.pdf">18 months after a layoff</a>.</p>
<p>The catch is the bill. The plan can charge you <strong><a href="https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/health-plans/cobra">up to 102% of the full cost</a></strong>. The share your employer paid now lands on you.</p>
<p>For a family plan, that is about $2,294 every month, up from about $571. That is KFF's 2025 average, times 102%.</p>
<p>The job held your paycheck and your coverage in the same hand. One meeting let go of both.</p>

<h2 id="what-happens">What happens to my health insurance if I get laid off?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: you can usually keep the same plan for 18 months. You can be charged the whole bill yourself.</p>

<p>You walk out to the car with a box. You sit there a minute. Then you think of the pharmacy, and the doctor you finally like.</p>

<p>Breathe. The plan does not vanish that day. Say your employer had 20 or more workers. Then <a href="https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/health-plans/cobra">the Department of Labor says</a> its plan generally has to offer you COBRA.</p>

<p>Here is the clock, from <a href="https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/an-employees-guide-health-benefits-under-cobra-2022.pdf">the Labor Department's own guide</a>. Your employer has 30 days to tell the plan. The plan then has 14 days to send you an election notice.</p>

<p>You get at least 60 days to say yes. After you say yes, you get at least 45 days to make the first payment.</p>

<p>And so you have a little time. Not a lot. Enough to think it through at your own kitchen table.</p>

<h2 id="what-it-costs">What does COBRA really cost?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: about four times what you paid at work for a family plan. More than six times for just you.</p>

<p>While you worked, you only saw your share. <a href="https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/">KFF's 2025 survey</a> puts the average family plan at $26,993 for the year. The average worker paid $6,850 of it. The employer paid the rest.</p>

<p>For one person, the plan cost $9,325. <a href="https://files.kff.org/attachment/Employer-Health-Benefits-Survey-2025-Annual-Survey-Summary-of-Findings.pdf">The worker paid $1,440 of it</a>.</p>

<p>On COBRA, the employer's part is gone. Add the 2% the law allows on top, and here is the math.</p>

<p>Those are averages, not your plan. Your own number comes in the mail, inside the election notice. And that notice is where the trouble can start.</p>

<h2 id="starbucks">What did a lawsuit say Starbucks' COBRA notice left out?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: by the lawsuit's account, clear steps on how to sign up. Starbucks denies it, and agreed to settle for $325,000.</p>

<h3>The notice</h3>

<p>The election notice is the one page that tells you how to keep your coverage. It has to say how to sign up, and by when.</p>

<p>In 2020, <a href="https://news.bloomberglaw.com/employee-benefits/starbucks-cobra-notice-suit-will-stay-in-court-not-arbitration">the husband of a former Starbucks worker</a> sued Starbucks in federal court in Florida. The suit said the notice did not clearly tell him how to sign up for COBRA. That is how <a href="https://www.cobranoticeclassaction.com/">the court-approved settlement site</a> sums up the claim.</p>

<h3>The denial</h3>

<p>Starbucks denies it. It says its notice followed the law. The judge has not ruled on who is right.</p>

<h3>The settlement</h3>

<p>In January 2026, <a href="https://news.bgov.com/payroll/starbucks-settles-cobra-notice-suit-after-losing-arbitration-bid">Bloomberg Law reported</a> that Starbucks had agreed to settle. The fund is $325,000. It covers about 33,800 people. They got a notice from 2016 to the start of 2023, and they did not sign up.</p>

<p>Claims closed on December 2, 2026. A final court hearing is set for January 6, 2027.</p>

<p>So look at who writes that page. Not you. The company that just let you go, or the firm it hired. One company held your paycheck and your coverage, and it also wrote the steps for keeping one of them.</p>

<h2 id="first-week">What would I do the week the notice comes?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: write down two dates, price one other plan, then start one thing that is yours.</p>

<h3>Write down your two dates</h3>

<p>Find the last day to sign up, and the first payment date. At least 60 days, then at least 45. Put both on the fridge, not in an inbox.</p>

<h3>Price the marketplace before you say yes</h3>

<p>You do not have to take COBRA. <a href="https://www.healthcare.gov/unemployed/cobra-coverage/">HealthCare.gov says</a> losing the plan from your job gives you 60 days to pick a marketplace plan instead.</p>

<p>Open enrollment for 2027 also runs to <a href="https://www.healthcare.gov/quick-guide/dates-and-deadlines/">January 15</a>. A plan picked by then starts February 1.</p>

<p>One warning from that same page. If you drop COBRA early by choice, you may have to wait for the next open enrollment.</p>

<p>Then talk it through with a friend who tells you the truth.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are women asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>How long can I keep my health insurance after a layoff?</strong></p>
<p>If your employer had 20 or more workers, COBRA usually lets you keep the same plan for 18 months. You get at least 60 days to sign up. Then you get at least 45 more to make the first payment.</p>

<p><strong>How much does COBRA cost a month?</strong></p>
<p>The plan can charge up to 102% of the full cost. Based on KFF's 2025 averages times 102%, that is about $2,294 each month for a family plan. For one person, it is about $793. Your own notice has your real number.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to take COBRA?</strong></p>
<p>No. HealthCare.gov says you can compare it with marketplace plans first. Losing the plan from your job gives you 60 days to pick one. If you drop COBRA early by choice, you may wait for the next open enrollment.</p>

<p><strong>Does building something of my own replace health insurance?</strong></p>
<p>No. It is not a health plan, and it does not replace one. Sort out your coverage first. Then build beside it.</p>
