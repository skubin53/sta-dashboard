<h2 id="what-hcl-wrote">What did HCL America write about a 62-year-old applicant?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: by the EEOC's account, two words. "Too old."</p>

<p>You sent the application on a Sunday night, from the desk by the window. You checked your email every morning for two weeks. Nothing came back.</p>

<p>You almost never get to see why. In one case, the EEOC did. That is the federal job bias agency.</p>

<p>In July 2021, a 62-year-old man applied to be a sales director at HCL America. <a href="https://www.eeoc.gov/newsroom/hcl-america-pay-495000-eeoc-age-and-national-origin-discrimination-lawsuit">The EEOC's suit says</a> HCL turned him down, "stating in an email to the hiring team that he was 'too old.'" The EEOC says a younger candidate got the job.</p>

<p>The EEOC calls him qualified. It sued in August 2024. A federal court approved the settlement on April 2, 2026. HCL agreed to pay him $495,000.</p>

<p>That is the EEOC's account, and HCL chose to settle it.</p>

<h2 id="is-it-my-age">Is it really my age, or is it me?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it is not you. The EEOC has been counting this for years.</p>

<p>You start to wonder. Maybe your resume looks old.</p>

<p>No shame in asking. Now look at what the EEOC keeps finding.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.eeoc.gov/reports/state-age-discrimination-and-older-workers-us-50-years-after-age-discrimination-employment">In a 2018 report</a>, the EEOC said 6 out of 10 older workers "have seen or experienced age discrimination in the workplace."</p>

<p>That report points to a study of over 40,000 job applications. Applicants 64 to 66 got turned down for interviews more often than those 49 to 51. Women, even at middle age, faced more age bias than older men.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.eeoc.gov/older-women-work-intersection-age-and-sex-discrimination">In its 2020 to 2023 budget years, the EEOC got over 52,000 age discrimination complaints.</a> At least half were filed by women.</p>

<p>You did not lose your touch.</p>

<h2 id="spot-it">How can you tell when it is happening to you?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: watch the ad, the form and the silence, and write down what you see.</p>

<h3>The words in the ad</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/fact-sheet-age-discrimination">The EEOC's fact sheet</a> says the law "generally makes it unlawful to include age preferences" in job ads. Its 2018 report says experts told it about posts asking for "digital natives." That is a birth year wearing a nicer coat.</p>

<h3>The birth year box</h3>

<p>The law does not ban an employer from asking your age. But the EEOC says that question "may deter older workers from applying." Its report also flags online forms with birth or graduation dates you cannot skip.</p>

<h3>The silence</h3>

<p>Most of it happens out of sight. The same report says only 3 percent of older workers report making a formal complaint. Age bias is "often difficult to detect and to prove."</p>

<h2 id="this-week">What would I do first this week?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: write it down, know your deadline, then ask your own questions.</p>

<h3>Write down what happened, with dates</h3>

<p>Save the job ad before it comes down. Write down the date you applied and the name on every email. Paper you can look at beats a feeling you carry around.</p>

<h3>Know your clock</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.eeoc.gov/how-file-charge-employment-discrimination">The EEOC says</a> you usually have 180 days to file a charge. For age, it can be 300 days. That is only if your state has its own age law, and an agency enforcing it. If your deadline is close, the EEOC says to call 1-800-669-4000.</p>

<h3>Ask five questions of whatever comes next</h3>

<p>Including of me. Especially of me.</p>

<p>What does it cost to start, all in. Is there a monthly requirement. What happens to my customers if I stop. Where is the income disclosure. Who gets paid when I start, and how.</p>

<p>If somebody dodges one, that is your answer.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are women asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>Is 55 too old to get a job?</strong></p>
<p>No. The federal age law protects you from age 40 on, with no top age. It covers employers with 20 or more workers.</p>

<p><strong>Is age discrimination in hiring illegal?</strong></p>
<p>Yes. The law bans it in hiring, firing, pay and promotions. A job ad generally cannot state an age preference either.</p>

<p><strong>How do I prove age discrimination in hiring?</strong></p>
<p>It is hard, and the EEOC says so. Save the ad, your application date and every email. You usually have 180 days to file a charge, or 300 in some states.</p>

<p><strong>Is it harder to get a job after 50?</strong></p>
<p>It can get harder as you age. A study of over 40,000 applications, cited by the EEOC, found applicants 64 to 66 got fewer interviews than those 49 to 51. Women faced more age bias than older men did.</p>
