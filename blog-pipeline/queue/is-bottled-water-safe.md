<h2 id="what-is-floating">What is actually floating in a bottle of Dasani?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: tiny bits of plastic, and the top kind in the study matches bottle caps.</p>

<p>You stood in the garage longer than you meant to, one bottle in your hand.</p>

<p>You bought the case so there is always water to grab. No shame in that. It is stacked by the front door of the store.</p>

<p>The problem is what Coca-Cola puts it in.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.coca-cola.com/content/dam/onexp/us/en/media-center/dasani/water-quality-report.pdf">Coca-Cola's own Dasani report says most of its plants start with city water.</a> It gets filtered, and then minerals go back in for taste.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fchem.2018.00407/full">In 2018, a SUNY Fredonia team tested 259 bottles from 11 brands.</a> Plastic turned up in 93% of them.</p>

<p>Dasani was one brand. The lot bought in the US held about 165 bits per liter. All 10 bottles had some, and the worst had 303.</p>

<h3 id="polypropylene">Polypropylene, the plastic caps are made of</h3>

<p>The team checked the bigger bits one by one. Across all the brands, the top plastic "matches a common plastic used for the manufacture of bottle caps," the study says.</p>

<p>And so where does it come from? The study says some of it may come from the bottle. Some may come from the bottling, too. And that study did not test heat at all.</p>

<h2 id="hot-garage">What does a hot garage do to a case of water?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: heat speeds up a metal called antimony coming out of the bottle.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.bevnet.com/news/2022/coke-dasani-sprite-bottles-to-go-clear-for-sustainability-efforts">In 2022, Coca-Cola said most US Dasani would come in recycled PET plastic.</a> PET holds a little of a metal called antimony.</p>

<p><a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2007.07.048">In 2008, scientists at Arizona State tested nine bottled waters sold in Arizona.</a> At 72 degrees, the antimony stayed far under the EPA limit for three months.</p>

<p>Heat changed that. At 149 degrees, the water would go over the EPA limit in 38 days. That is the study's own math.</p>

<p>And where does it get that hot? The study names "cars, garages, and enclosed storage areas" in an Arizona summer.</p>

<p>Coca-Cola's Dasani report never says where to keep the case. It does not mention heat at all.</p>

<h3 id="antimony">Antimony, the metal in the bottle</h3>

<p><a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.watres.2007.07.048">The EPA caps antimony in drinking water at 6 parts per billion.</a></p>

<p>Back in 2008, the scientists said plastics that do not leach it "should be considered." In 2022, Dasani was still in PET. And heat is not even why Coca-Cola once pulled Dasani off the shelf.</p>

<h2 id="in-trouble-before">Has Coca-Cola been in trouble over Dasani before?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. A recall in Britain in 2004, and a county lawsuit over its bottles.</p>

<h3 id="bromate">Bromate, the reason Dasani was pulled</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.cnn.com/2004/WORLD/europe/03/19/uk.dasani/index.html">In March 2004, Coca-Cola recalled about 500,000 bottles of Dasani in Britain.</a> Tests found bromate over the UK limit.</p>

<p>Coca-Cola's own statement said the bromate "was formed at a level that exceeded UK legal standards." It blamed bromide in calcium it added for UK rules. Its ozone step turned that to bromate.</p>

<p>Britain's food agency said high levels over a long time could raise cancer risk. It also said there was no immediate risk.</p>

<p>That recall was only in Britain. But <a href="https://www.coca-cola.com/content/dam/onexp/us/en/media-center/dasani/water-quality-report.pdf">the US Dasani report still lists ozone as the last step.</a></p>

<p>The bottle is in court now, too. <a href="https://lacounty.gov/2024/10/30/la-county-sues-pepsi-and-coca-cola-over-plastic-beverage-pollution-and-deceiving-public-on-plastic-recycling/">On October 30, 2024, Los Angeles County sued Coca-Cola and PepsiCo over plastic bottles.</a> The county's news release named Dasani.</p>

<p>The county alleges they misled the public about recycling. It says the plastic breaks down into bits that end up in "the water we drink."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/21344/000162828026010047/ko-20251231.htm">Coca-Cola filed its yearly report in February 2026.</a> It says a judge refused to toss the case in September 2025. The company says it "believes it has strong defenses." As of that report, nothing had been proven.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Isn't bottled water still cleaner than my tap?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: not on plastic, and most Dasani plants start with city water anyway.</p>

<p>Here is the part I know you are thinking. Bottled water feels like the safe choice.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1097715">A 2025 Concordia University review looked at over 140 studies.</a> It compared bottled water to tap. Bottled water drinkers may swallow up to 90,000 more bits of plastic a year.</p>

<p>Now the other side.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> Nobody has proven Dasani made anyone sick. The Concordia team says the long-term effects are still poorly understood.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> Most of the Dasani bits were too small to test one by one. The 2018 team said they were "most probably plastic."</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because in that 2018 study, one brand of water came in both glass and plastic. In glass, the plastic count fell from 1,410 per liter to 204. The bottle is the part you can change.</p>

<p>And you are not the only one who stopped trusting the case. Los Angeles County says it sued after shoppers across the state complained.</p>

<h2 id="what-instead">What do you fill up with instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a glass jug in the fridge and a steel bottle by the door.</p>

<p>Stop buying the case. If one has sat in a hot garage all summer, I would not drink it.</p>

<p>Most Dasani plants start with city water. If your home is on city water, your kitchen tap starts there too. If you like it filtered, filter it at home.</p>

<p>Then pour it into glass. Keep a jug in the fridge, and fill a steel bottle for the car.</p>

<p>A jug will not fix your pipes. I will not pretend it does. It does take the plastic bottle and the hot garage out of it.</p>

<p>For the rest of the house, I point you to Switch to America. It is a private membership store. We are the manufacturer. No middlemen, no markup.</p>

<p>Start tonight. Fill one glass jug and put it in the fridge.</p>
