<h2 id="claim-form">Is there a Driscoll's settlement or claim form?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no. The case is four months old, and the court record shows no deal.</p>

<p>You stood at the sink longer than you meant to. The berries were under the water, and that post was still open on your phone.</p>

<p>No shame in that. You have bought the box with the yellow label for years, because the name meant something.</p>

<p>A claim form comes after a settlement or a win. <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73543306/berlinger-v-driscolls-inc/">The court record shows neither.</a></p>

<p>Six shoppers sued in federal court in San Jose on June 26, 2026. They are from New York, New Jersey, Massachusetts and Illinois. So any page offering a Driscoll's payout is offering money that does not exist yet.</p>

<h2 id="what-it-says">What does the lawsuit say is on the berries?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: 12 pesticides, and it calls eight of them PFAS. The box names none.</p>

<p><a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.473105/gov.uscourts.cand.473105.1.0_1.pdf">The complaint says lab tests found 12 pesticides on the berries.</a> Eight of them, it says, "are considered PFAS." PFAS break down so slowly they got the name forever chemicals.</p>

<p>The tests came from an online report in May 2026, the complaint says. Hold on to that part.</p>

<h3 id="the-eight">Fludioxonil and seven more: the eight it calls PFAS</h3>

<p>The complaint names all eight. The highest, it says, was fludioxonil, at 60 parts per billion. Two others, it says, were over limits set in Europe.</p>

<p>And the box? The complaint says nowhere on the clamshell does Driscoll's tell you about PFAS. It says "Only the Finest Berries."</p>

<p>The six are suing over what they paid. Each one says they would have paid less, or not bought the berries at all.</p>

<h2 id="ceo">Why did Driscoll's change its CEO?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: Driscoll's gave no reason. Brie Reiter Smith, from the founding family, took over from Soren Bjorn "effective immediately."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2026/09/berry-company-ceo-steps-down-amid-pesticide-whirlwind/">Food Safety News reported the change on September 2, 2026.</a> It said the switch "comes amid a growing legal storm."</p>

<p>Part of that storm comes from inside. The class action points to a former Driscoll's food safety manager. He filed his own suit in Ventura County.</p>

<p>He alleges his bosses put the brand first. By his account, they cared more about "plausible deniability" than fixing pesticide problems. He says he was fired for refusing to go along. Those are his claims, not findings.</p>

<p>So who checks the berries before they reach your kitchen? Driscoll's says it does.</p>

<h2 id="safe-to-eat">Are Driscoll's strawberries safe to eat right now?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no court has called them unsafe, and none has called them clean. The parasite scare was not about strawberries.</p>

<p>Rule out the parasite first. Your search bar may have put "cyclospora" next to Driscoll's.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.cdc.gov/cyclosporiasis/outbreaks/07-26/index.html">The CDC tied this summer's outbreak to iceberg lettuce from Mexico.</a> It counted 12,883 sick in 21 states. It says the outbreak "has ended," and strawberries are not on that page.</p>

<p>Now the pesticides. <a href="https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/07/21/878294.htm">Driscoll's told Insurance Journal</a> it has strict food safety programs and works to follow the rules. Meeting a rule is one thing. Telling you what is on the berry is another.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for Driscoll's?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, three fair points. The biggest is that the test was one box.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://blackdotresearch.sg/are-driscolls-strawberries-laced-with-hundreds-of-pesticides-and-confirmed-to-cause-cancer/">The fact-checkers at Black Dot Research found</a> the residues came from a test of one box. One box is not every box. I am not going to pretend it is.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> Posts on your feed said the brand is "directly tied to cancer." Black Dot rated that claim false. Finding a residue, it wrote, "does not establish a cancer risk."</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> Driscoll's calls the claims "without merit," and no court has found otherwise. Nobody in either shoppers' suit claims a berry made them sick.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p><strong>Because Driscoll's has pulled its own strawberries over pesticides before.</strong> <a href="https://api.fda.gov/food/enforcement.json?search=product_description:Driscoll%27s&limit=50">US recall records show</a> 3,095 cases in May 2015, for naled over the limit. Then 8,222 crates in January 2022, for etoxazole over the limit.</p>

<p>Five more of its berry recalls from 2018 to 2022 were over pesticides too. One test is thin. That record is not.</p>
