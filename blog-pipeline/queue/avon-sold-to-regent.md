<h2 id="who-owns-avon">Who owns Avon now?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: Regent, since September 1. That makes it the third owner of Avon in the US since 2016.</p>

<p>It is late. One lamp is on over your kitchen island. The laptop is shut, the stool is pushed back, and half a glass of water sits there going warm.</p>

<p>The brochures in your trunk, the customer who texts you for her lipstick, the order due Tuesday, the group chat asking what the sale means. And that feeling that someone else was running your business, not you.</p>

<p>Avon in the US was split from the rest of Avon in 2016. An investment fund called Cerberus took control of it.</p>

<p>Then LG, a Korean company, bought it in 2019. <a href="https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10850497">It paid $125 million, the Korea Herald reports.</a></p>

<p>On August 24, 2026, LG signed to sell it to Regent. <a href="https://www.avonworldwide.com/news/Avon-International-and-Avon-North-America-reunite-for-the-first-time-in-a-decade">Avon's own news page</a> called the deal "a more connected opportunity for employees, Representatives and Ambassadors."</p>

<p>That is you. The plan was about you, and it was signed before anyone told you.</p>

<h2 id="going-out-of-business">Is Avon going out of business?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no. It was sold to a firm that already ran Avon on three other continents.</p>

<p>Regent bought Avon in Europe, Asia and Africa first. <a href="https://api.mziq.com/mzfilemanager/v2/d/9e61d5ff-4641-4ec3-97a5-3595f938bb75/a86eb54f-925c-bd99-7e84-83bcf57c288d?origin=2">Natura, the company from Brazil that sold it, says that sale closed on December 31, 2025.</a></p>

<p>Eight months later, Regent bought the US business too. And so the US business and Avon International have one owner again.</p>

<p>Here is the part I will not argue with. A buyer who wants the whole brand back together may be good for Avon. <a href="https://www.avonworldwide.com/news/Avon-International-and-Avon-North-America-reunite-for-the-first-time-in-a-decade">Avon's own news page</a> lays out the plan. Better products, and a stronger tie to the people who sell them. That is a real point.</p>

<p>But good news for the owner and a say for you are two different things.</p>

<h2 id="who-got-asked">Who made the sales, and who got a vote?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: sellers like you made three out of four sales last year. You got no vote on the sale.</p>

<p><a href="https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026082616190075114">The Asia Business Daily, a Korean paper, ran the numbers.</a> By its count, 75.9% of Avon's sales in the US and Canada last year came through its sellers. That is you.</p>

<p>LG paid $125 million in 2019. By The Asia Business Daily's account, LG agreed to sell for $6 million. The paper says LG would hand over Avon Canada's shares for $1. LG said the exact price would be set at closing.</p>

<p>Whatever the final price, you were not asked. The whole chain fits in one table.</p>

<h2 id="what-can-change">What can the new owner change for you?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: almost anything, even whether you stay. Avon's own rule book says so.</p>

<p>None of the sale news says what changes for you. But Avon's rule book says who decides.</p>

<p><a href="https://avon.com/magnoliaPublic/dam/jcr:8a19974e-7485-4b34-9050-3c1017a1b601/business-policies-and-procedures-avon-independent-ambassadors-us-en.pdf">The one on Avon's site took effect January 1, 2025.</a> It even says sellers used to be called Sales Representatives. Now the paper calls you an Ambassador.</p>

<h3>The rules can change whenever Avon likes</h3>

<p>The rule book says it "may be modified by Avon at any time in its sole discretion." It also has you agree "to abide by all future changes." So the paper has you saying yes to rules nobody has written yet.</p>

<h3>Your pay levels can move</h3>

<p>In Avon's words, "Commission Levels are subject to change at any time." What you keep from each order is not locked.</p>

<h3>Your spot can end for no reason</h3>

<p>Either side "may terminate the Ambassador Contract at any time, for any reason or for no reason at all." You can walk away too. But your place there was never yours to keep.</p>

<h3>Your customer can go around you</h3>

<p>The rules say a customer may choose "to work directly with The Avon Company without an Ambassador." The trust is yours. The checkout is Avon's.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly. I will tell you which part.</p>

<p>Avon is not a bad company for being sold. Companies get sold, and this one may go well.</p>

<p>Now me. Switch to America could change its rules too. Any company can.</p>

<p>Switch to America sells straight from the manufacturer. No middlemen and no markup. No minimums, no maximums. You pay what a brand new shopper pays.</p>

<p>Avon's rules also say this. You may not recruit Avon's sellers or customers into another direct sales business. That holds while you sell Avon, and for one year after. I would keep that rule to the letter.</p>

<p>I will not hand you a number to expect. Nobody honest can.</p>
