<h2 id="what-was-recalled">What did Ghirardelli recall?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a hot cocoa pouch and 12 other powdered drink mixes, pulled in April 2026.</p>

<p>The Ghirardelli Chocolate Company pulled 13 powdered drink mixes in late April 2026.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fox4news.com/news/ghirardelli-recalls-hot-cocoa-other-products-salmonella-concerns">A Fox station reported the 13 mixes on April 29, 2026, with hot cocoa on the list.</a></p>

<p>The hot cocoa on the list is the <strong>4/2lb Premium Hot Cocoa Pouch Bulk</strong>. That means two pound pouches, packed four to a case.</p>

<p>The rest are frappe mixes and sweet ground powders. Mocha, white mocha, vanilla, frozen hot cocoa. Some come in 30 pound bags.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.ghirardelli.com/voluntary-recall-notice">Ghirardelli's own notice lists every lot number and best-by date.</a> The dates run from February 2027 to January 2028.</p>

<p>So a pouch in your pantry today could still be well inside its date.</p>

<h2 id="where-it-started">Where did the Salmonella worry start?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: in milk powder from a dairy supplier, mixed by a company you never heard of.</p>

<p>The front of the pouch leaves out two companies.</p>

<p>Ghirardelli did not make the milk powder. <a href="https://www.ghirardelli.com/voluntary-recall-notice">Its notice says the powder came from California Dairies, Inc.</a> From there it went to a third-party manufacturer. That third company mixed it into the drinks.</p>

<p>So three companies handled that powder before your mug. You only ever saw one name.</p>

<p><a href="https://midvalleytimes.com/article/news/2026/07/14/california-dairies-major-milk-powder-recall-continues/">A California paper reported the milk powder recall.</a> About 2.7 million pounds were recalled on April 20, 2026. It called the Salmonella risk "potential, but unproven."</p>

<p>That same powder went to dozens of companies. Chips, trail mix, cheese bread and pork rinds were pulled too.</p>

<p>And so powder from one supplier ended up in foods you would never link to it.</p>

<h2 id="still-going">Is the recall still going?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the milk powder recall was still running in July. The April lot list is still the one to check.</p>

<p>On July 14, 2026, the Mid Valley Times said the milk powder recall was still going.</p>

<p>Now cocoa season is here. A pouch bought online could still be in your pantry.</p>

<p>If you have one, match the lot number on the pouch to Ghirardelli's list. If it matches, throw it out.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">The case against my own story</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: three fair points, and they are all true.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> Most of these were big bags for restaurants and other big kitchens. <a href="https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2026/04/ghirardelli-recalling-powdered-drink-mixes-because-of-salmonella/">Food Safety News said they were meant for restaurants and other institutions.</a></p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> Ghirardelli says no one has reported getting sick. It also says its own testing has not found any affected mixes.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> Ghirardelli pulled its mixes within about a week of the supplier. It called the move caution. I will give it credit for that.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because you paid for the good name. The name was not the whole chain. A dairy you never met and a mixer you never heard of were both in that mug.</p>

<p>Here is my own limit, too. Real cocoa made in a pan will not taste like the pouch at first. It is less sweet, and it takes five more minutes.</p>

<h2 id="is-cocoa-bad">Is hot cocoa bad for you, then?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the cup is not the problem; a mix built from strangers' powder is.</p>

<p>You do not have to give up your cozy mug. That is not the point of this.</p>

<p>Cocoa comes from a bean, and milk comes from a cow. That part is simple.</p>

<p>The trouble starts when the milk is dried to a powder. Then it is shipped in bulk. A company whose name is not on the bag blends it.</p>

<p>And so the more hands a food passes through, the less you know about it.</p>

<h2 id="what-instead">What do I make instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: real milk from a local dairy, plain cocoa powder, and a saucepan.</p>

<p>Start with the milk, because that is where this recall began.</p>

<p>Find a dairy farm near you. Go and meet the farmer. Ask how the cows are kept and how the milk is handled.</p>

<p>That one talk tells you more than any pouch ever could. Ask if you can pick up a gallon each week.</p>

<p>Then keep a jar of plain cocoa powder, just cocoa, and a bag of sugar.</p>

<p>Warm a cup of the farm milk in a pan. Stir in a spoon of cocoa and a spoon of sugar. Taste it. Add more of what you like.</p>

<p>That is the whole recipe.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Was the regular Ghirardelli hot cocoa at the store recalled?</strong></p>
<p>The recall names the 4/2lb Premium Hot Cocoa Pouch Bulk and 12 other large mixes. Ghirardelli says they were mainly for restaurants, but some could be bought online. Match your lot number to the company's list.</p>

<p><strong>What do I do if my pouch is on the list?</strong></p>
<p>Do not drink it. Throw it out. Ghirardelli says to fill out the Contact Us form on its website if you think you drank some.</p>

<p><strong>What if I already drank some?</strong></p>
<p>Food Safety News says Salmonella usually brings diarrhea, cramps and fever, 12 to 72 hours after eating. If you feel sick, call your doctor and tell her about the mix.</p>

<p><strong>Is homemade hot cocoa hard to make?</strong></p>
<p>No. Warm a cup of milk from a local dairy in a pan. Stir in a spoon of plain cocoa and a spoon of sugar. It takes about five minutes.</p>
