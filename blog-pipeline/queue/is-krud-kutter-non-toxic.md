<h2 id="the-lawsuit">What did the Krud Kutter lawsuit say?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: that a cleaner sold as Non-Toxic was toxic to people, animals and the earth. That is the complaint's claim. Rust-Oleum denies it.</p>

<p>You kneel on the bath mat on a Saturday morning. You spray the shower tile and scrub with your face close to the grout. The window stays shut because it is cold out.</p>

<p>Your knuckles split in the cold now. So you picked the bottle that said Non-Toxic.</p>

<p>No shame in that. The word sat big on the front, right where Rust-Oleum put it.</p>

<p>You want a cleaner that cuts soap scum and does not bite back. That is fair. The lawsuit says the problem is the word Rust-Oleum used to sell it.</p>

<p><a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.359488/gov.uscourts.cand.359488.1.0.pdf">A California shopper sued Rust-Oleum on May 13, 2020.</a> His complaint says the "Non-Toxic," "Earth Friendly" formula "is toxic to humans, animals, and the environment."</p>

<h3 id="potassium-hydroxide">Potassium hydroxide, the caustic potash in the Original</h3>

<p>The complaint names three ingredients in Krud Kutter Original. One is potassium hydroxide, or caustic potash.</p>

<p>It calls this one "corrosive to tissue" and says it can burn skin and eyes. That is the complaint talking, not a court.</p>

<p>And Rust-Oleum's own paperwork has words for that bottle too.</p>

<h2 id="safety-sheet">What does Rust-Oleum's own safety sheet say?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: "Warning." It says the Original causes skin and eye irritation, and it asks for gloves.</p>

<p><a href="https://images.thdstatic.com/catalog/pdfImages/59/599a1865-8dc9-423d-8d0e-dc8a19e6e551.pdf">Rust-Oleum's safety data sheet for Krud Kutter Original is dated July 9, 2024.</a> The case was still open then.</p>

<p>It is marked "Warning." It says the cleaner "Causes skin irritation" and "Causes eye irritation."</p>

<p>It says to "Wear protective gloves." If it gets on your skin, the sheet says to flush with water "for at least 15 minutes."</p>

<p>It also says, "Use only with adequate ventilation." That is not what Non-Toxic sounds like.</p>

<p><a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.359488/gov.uscourts.cand.359488.186.0.pdf">Rust-Oleum told the judge its front label said "Caution: Eye and Skin Irritant" next to the words "Non-Toxic."</a> Both, on the bottle you held up to the tile.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.rustoleum.com/-/media/6602A8A5951A467296ED22563E70FED2.ashx">A Krud Kutter Original data sheet on Rust-Oleum's own website still calls it a "non-toxic cleaner."</a> The same sheet says to flush eyes or skin "for at least 15 minutes."</p>

<h2 id="the-judge">Did a judge rule the label was false?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no. But the judge would not end the case early, and Rust-Oleum settled before any jury heard it.</p>

<p>Rust-Oleum asked the judge to rule for it without a trial. <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.359488/gov.uscourts.cand.359488.186.0.pdf">On January 26, 2024, the judge said no.</a></p>

<p>On "Non-Toxic," the judge wrote, "There is at least a genuine dispute of fact here." So a jury could have decided it.</p>

<p>On "Earth Friendly," the back of the bottle had a note. The judge wrote that it "is in small type and the defendant's own surveys provide evidence that most consumers do not read it."</p>

<p>The company's own surveys. That does not sound right, does it?</p>

<p>Then Rust-Oleum settled. <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.359488/gov.uscourts.cand.359488.236.0.pdf">On October 2, 2025, the judge gave the deal final approval.</a> "Non-Toxic" comes off all Krud Kutter labels and marketing for good.</p>

<p>"Earth Friendly" can stay with an asterisk. The asterisk sends you to a line on the back.</p>

<p>Here is the front, next to the paperwork.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for Rust-Oleum?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, a fair one. No court found anyone was hurt, and the caution was on the front.</p>

<p>I know what you are thinking. Maybe you scrubbed with Krud Kutter for years and never got a rash. That is not nothing.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://www.classaction.org/media/bush-v-rust-oleum-corporation-settlement-agreement.pdf">Rust-Oleum "denies all of the allegations made in the Action."</a> The deal is "not an admission of guilt or wrongdoing of any kind."</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> No jury heard the case. Nobody proved the cleaner hurt anyone.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> The caution sat right on the front. Rust-Oleum argued that told you enough.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because a bottle that says Caution should never say Non-Toxic too. And the judge wrote that the opinion of the shopper's expert "goes beyond eye and skin irritation."</p>

<p>This post will not tell you your cracked knuckles came from that bottle. Nobody proved that, and I will not pretend they did. What is settled is the word.</p>
