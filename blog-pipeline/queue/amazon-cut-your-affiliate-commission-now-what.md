<h2 id="what-did-amazon-change">What did Amazon actually change?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: four rules at once, all on one day, in one memo.</p>

<p>Amazon posts its own change log. Almost nobody reads it.</p>

<p>Here is the line that did the damage: <a href="https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement">"Updated calculation of onsite commission income to apply only to Direct Qualifying Purchases of the same ASIN variant as the linked Product detail page."</a></p>

<p>In plain words: you get paid on the thing she clicked. Not on the four other things in her cart.</p>

<p>For a lot of creators, those four things were the income.</p>

<p>Three more rules moved the same day. Amazon added a 180 day limit to get paid at all.</p>

<p>It cut off pay for anyone sent through a boosted post. In its words, "regardless of whether prohibited keywords are used."</p>

<p>And your page now has to carry "commentary, analysis or transformation."</p>

<p>Four changes, one Tuesday, and nobody asked you.</p>

<h2 id="why-did-my-pay-drop">Why did my pay drop when my traffic did not?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: you were paid on a moment, and somebody else owns the moment.</p>

<p>An affiliate link drops a cookie. She clicks. She buys. You get paid once.</p>

<p>Then the window shuts and you are back at zero.</p>

<p>You wake up Monday needing a brand new click. Same dollar, all over again.</p>

<p>That is not a content problem. That is the shape of the deal.</p>

<p><strong>And you never had a say in it.</strong> The terms live on Amazon's server. They can be rewritten in an afternoon. By somebody who has never heard your name.</p>

<p>You did the work of the recommendation. They kept the customer.</p>

<h2 id="click-or-customer">What is the difference between a click and a customer?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: one pays you for a second. The other pays for as long as she shops.</p>

<h3>The cookie</h3>
<p>It pays once, on one item, and then it is gone. Next month starts empty.</p>

<h3>The cart</h3>
<p>That part quietly held a lot of creator income together. Since April it is not yours.</p>

<h3>The customer</h3>
<p>She sets up her own shop. She runs out of laundry soap in March and orders more. You stay credited for as long as she keeps ordering.</p>

<p>You did the work once and it keeps counting. That is the only real difference.</p>

<p>The rest is a table. The row that matters is the last one.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: getting paid on reorders is not magic. I will not pretend it is.</p>

<p>Here is the part I know you are thinking, because I thought it too. This is the point where somebody shows you a screenshot and a car.</p>

<p>That is fair. You have been pitched by people with nothing behind it. So this is what I will do instead.</p>

<p><strong>I will not show you an income number.</strong> <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guidance-concerning-multi-level-marketing">The FTC wants "reliable, empirical evidence, not subjective beliefs or personal anecdotes."</a> It also says a "results not typical" line at the bottom is not enough. A number with no study behind it is not honesty. It is decoration.</p>

<p>Now the part that hurts my own side of the argument.</p>

<p>The FTC read 70 of these companies' own income disclosures. Most people in them <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/09/ftc-staff-report-analyzes-70-mlm-income-disclosure-statements">made $1,000 or less per year</a>. That is under $84 a month, before costs. In 17 of those companies, most people made nothing.</p>

<p>Companies built on reorders also fold. Modere sold for 23 years and then closed with no notice. <a href="https://www.directsellingnews.com/2025/04/11/modere-announces-closure-after-23-years/">On April 11, 2025 it told its people this.</a> It had "made the difficult decision to close our doors." Dashboards went dark that day.</p>

<p>So the model is not the answer by itself. What matters is whether real people reorder because they want the stuff. And whether the company lasts another ten years.</p>

<p><strong>And this is not for everybody.</strong> Want your income back to April's number by October? This will not do that. It builds slowly or not at all. You cannot tell which in three weeks.</p>
