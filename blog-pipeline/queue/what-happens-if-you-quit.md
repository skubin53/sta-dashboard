<h2 id="contract">What does Amazon's contract say about your customers?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: that they were Amazon's from the first tap, and they stay Amazon's after you go.</p>

<p>You know your people. The one who asked about your shower filter at eleven at night. The one whose mother just moved in with her. The one who orders whatever coffee you order. The one who never comments and buys every time.</p>

<p>Amazon's contract has a word for every one of them.</p>

<p>Section 3 is called "Amazon Customers." Its first line is the one at the top of this page. They are Amazon's, not yours.</p>

<p>Its last line goes further. <a href="https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement">"You will not handle or address any contacts with any of our customers."</a> If she asks you about her order, you send her back to Amazon.</p>

<p>Then the clock. <a href="https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/G9SMD8TQHFJ7728F">Amazon's own help page</a> says your link covers what she puts in her cart "within 24 hours." It shuts early if she checks out, or taps someone else's Amazon link.</p>

<p>After that, in Amazon's words, "you will not earn commission income on any subsequent purchases."</p>

<p>So she was never yours. You had a day with her.</p>

<h2 id="the-day-you-quit">What happens the day you quit?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: seven days later the deal is over, and the customers stay put.</p>

<p>Section 6 lets either side end the deal "at any time, with or without cause." It ends seven days after notice.</p>

<p>Then this line. "Upon any termination of this Agreement, all rights and obligations of the parties will be extinguished."</p>

<p>Extinguished means put out, like a candle.</p>

<p>A few sections live on after the end. Section 3 is one of them. So after you go, she is still Amazon's customer, in writing.</p>

<p>Amazon can also end it for you the same day. One reason on its list: Amazon believes it "may become subject to tax collection requirements."</p>

<p>That has happened. On June 29, 2011, Amazon emailed its California Associates. <a href="https://www.geekwire.com/2011/full-text-amazon-cancels-affiliate-program-california-gov-brown-inks-tax-law/">"Contracts with all California residents participating in the Amazon Associates Program are terminated effective today, June 29, 2011."</a></p>

<p>Not one of them quit. A state tax law passed, and every California account ended in one email.</p>

<p>Every link they had posted stopped counting that same day.</p>

<h2 id="wait-it-out">Why doesn't staying put protect you?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: because staying is how you say yes to every change.</p>

<p>Section 13 lets Amazon change the terms "at any time and in our sole discretion." That means their call alone.</p>

<p>If you stay past the date of a change, you have agreed to it. If you do not like it, the contract says "your only recourse is to terminate this Agreement."</p>

<p>Recourse means way out. So your only way to say no is to leave. And when you leave, she stays Amazon's.</p>

<p>The rules still move. <a href="https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/compare">Amazon's own change page</a> lists new terms effective April 14, 2026. One of them is about creator content on Amazon's own pages. It now pays only when she orders the exact version it is tied to.</p>

<p>It is not new, either. In April 2020, Amazon gave Associates one week's notice of lower rates. <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2020/04/14/amazon-slashes-commission-rates-for-affiliate-program.html">CNBC reported the rate on furniture fell from 8% to 3%.</a></p>

<p>You did nothing wrong in any of this. The rules were just never yours to set.</p>

<h2 id="what-you-keep">So what do you actually get to keep?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the trust, and any way to reach her that you built yourself.</p>

<p>Set the contract down for a minute. Look at what is still in your hands.</p>

<h3>Her trust</h3>

<p>She did not order because of Amazon. She ordered because you said it worked in your house. Section 3 cannot touch that.</p>

<h3>A way to reach her</h3>

<p>A follower count is not a way to reach her. A phone number or an email she chose to give you is. Ask for it plainly, and tell her why.</p>

<h3>A reorder that stays with you</h3>

<p>This is the one I care about. She runs out of laundry soap and orders more. She still counts for you while you are in. There is no 24 hour clock on it.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly, and here is the part I will not dress up.</p>

<p>Amazon is not a monster. Its 2011 letter said fees already earned would be "paid in full." That October <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20111007071802/https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/gp/associates/help/t48">it invited the California accounts back</a>. Nothing they sent Amazon while they were shut out was ever paid.</p>

<p>Here is my own admission. If you stop, her account does not walk out the door with you. Not here either. It stays with Switch to America.</p>

<p>What changes is how long she counts for you while you are in, and whether you are the one she calls.</p>

<p>Switch to America sells straight from the manufacturer. No middlemen and no markup.</p>

<p>You pay what a brand new shopper pays. Not a penny less. I have no special rate and I would not offer you one.</p>

<p>I will not hand you a number to expect either. Nobody honest can.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are women asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>If I quit Amazon, do I lose what I already earned?</strong></p>
<p>No. Money already owed to you still has to be paid. Amazon can hold it "for a reasonable period of time" first, to cover returns.</p>

<p><strong>Can I take my Amazon customers with me?</strong></p>
<p>No, because the contract says they were never yours. Section 3 even lives on after the contract ends. What you take is the trust, and any way to reach her that you set up yourself.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to quit Amazon to build something else?</strong></p>
<p>No. Keep your links if they help you. This sits beside that work, not on top of it.</p>

<p><strong>Is it the same with every company?</strong></p>
<p>Ask each one. With me, if you stop, her account stays with Switch to America. The difference is how long she counts for you while you are in.</p>
