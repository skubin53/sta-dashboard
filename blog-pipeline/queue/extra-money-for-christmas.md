<h2 id="who-is-hiring">Who is actually hiring for Christmas this year?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: by October 1, only two retailers had put a number on it, Challenger says. One of them sells Halloween costumes.</p>

<p>It is eleven at night. The kitchen is dark except the one light over the island. You have added up December twice.</p>

<p>The plane tickets. The gift list taped inside the pantry door. The bigger grocery run for the 24th. The tires you put on the card in September. December is spending you, not the other way around.</p>

<p>So you look at who is hiring. Challenger, Gray &amp; Christmas tracks what employers announce. By October 1, it counted only two stores that had given hiring numbers. Spirit Halloween and Michaels, about 62,000 jobs between them.</p>

<h2 id="walmart">What does the FTC say Walmart's app left out?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: by the FTC's account, sometimes the full tip. The complaint says Spark showed drivers pay and tips they did not always get.</p>

<p>You want money on your own hours, in your own car. Nothing wrong with wanting that. The trouble is the number on the screen.</p>

<p>Walmart runs a delivery app called Spark Driver. You pick up orders at a Walmart store and drive them to the door. Each offer shows the pay and the tip before you take it.</p>

<p>In February 2026, the FTC and 11 states sued Walmart over that number. <a href="https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/WalmartSparkDriverComplaint.pdf">The complaint</a> says that since at least 2021, the screen and the pay did not always match.</p>

<p>It says Walmart split some orders between drivers, then split the tip. Each driver, it says, had been shown the full tip.</p>

<p>It says Walmart pulled orders out of a batch after a driver said yes. The pay and tips went with them, it says, often without a word to the driver.</p>

<p>It says Walmart told shoppers that 100% of tips go to drivers. In its words, "in numerous instances, Walmart charges Customers for tips that Drivers do not receive."</p>

<p>And it quotes a Spark manager on the tip splitting. The system, the manager said, was "working as designed."</p>

<p>Walmart agreed to a $100 million judgment to settle. <a href="https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/2323055walmartsparkdriverenteredorder.pdf">The order</a> says Walmart "neither admits nor denies" the charges.</p>

<p>None of that is about how hard a driver works. It is about who sets the number. You can ask about it before you start.</p>

<h2 id="options">What are my real options before Christmas?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a store, an app, or your own closet. Each has one question to ask first.</p>

<h3>A seasonal job</h3>

<p>Apply this week, not in December. Before you say yes, ask if the job ever stays on after the holidays.</p>

<p>It can. Michaels said nearly half of last year's seasonal store roles became regular jobs, <a href="https://www.challengergray.com/blog/2026-holiday-hiring-outlook-after-lowest-season-since-2008-will-retailers-stock-up-on-workers/">Challenger reported</a>.</p>

<h3>Driving for an app</h3>

<p>Ask one thing first: can the pay drop after you accept? Under the Walmart order, Spark cannot cut an offer after a driver says yes. There are some exceptions. Ask any app the same. If it dodges, that is your answer.</p>

<p>Then take out gas, insurance and wear on your car. What is left is what the driving really pays.</p>

<h3>Selling what you already own</h3>

<p>The coat with the tags on, the bread machine in the hall closet, the second slow cooker, the boots from a trip you never took. Your stuff, your price.</p>

<p>Ask one thing first: would you buy it again today? If not, it goes.</p>
