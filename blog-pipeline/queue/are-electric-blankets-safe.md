<h2 id="what-jkmax-recalled">What did JKMAX just recall?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: every heated blanket and pad it sold on Amazon. The trouble sits inside the fabric.</p>

<p>You lay it on the bed after dinner. You set the remote to high before you brush your teeth.</p>

<p>You stood by the bed longer than you meant to, a hand flat on the blanket, waiting for the warm.</p>

<p>No shame in that. It was right there on Amazon, in four colors.</p>

<p>You wanted to be warm. That is a fair thing to want. The recall points at what JKMAX put inside the blanket to get you there.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2026/JKMAX-Recalls-Heated-Blankets-and-Heating-Pads-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards">The US Consumer Product Safety Commission posted recall 26-778 on September 17, 2026.</a> It covers "all heating pads and heated blankets sold by JKMax." They sold on Amazon from September 2022 through July 2026.</p>

<p>The notice says JKMax is aware of 577 reports "of blankets smoking, melting or burning." It counts 53 burn injuries.</p>

<h3 id="heating-wire">The heating wire, the part you never see</h3>

<p>A wire runs under the fabric. That wire makes the heat. <a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2026/JKMAX-Recalls-Heated-Blankets-and-Heating-Pads-Due-to-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards">The recall says it "can overheat, posing fire and burn hazards."</a></p>

<p>And so the part the recall names is the part you cannot see or touch. And it runs all night.</p>

<h2 id="all-night">Is it safe to leave a heated blanket on all night?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: not a JKMAX, not one more night. A burn nurse calls falling asleep on a heating pad a burn risk too.</p>

<p>The cold sheets, the wool socks, the remote on high, the cord behind the nightstand, that feeling the blanket ran your nights instead of the other way around.</p>

<p>All night is hours of heat with you asleep under it.</p>

<p><a href="https://healthcare.utah.edu/the-scope/health-library/all/2018/11/heating-pads-can-cause-second-degree-burns">Brad Wiggins was a nurse manager at the University of Utah Health Burn Center.</a> He was talking about heating pads, and JKMAX recalled its pads too.</p>

<p>He named the danger plainly: "falling asleep on it." He said a pad can cause "a second degree burn over just a few hours."</p>

<p>He named age too. "Maybe as you get older you don't feel as much," he said.</p>

<p><a href="https://health.ucdavis.edu/burncenter/newsletter/2018/0218BurnNet.pdf">The UC Davis burn center wrote about blankets in its February 2018 newsletter.</a> It told a burn survivor to use the low setting. That cuts the chance of "a contact burn you cannot feel." Its list says to turn warming bedding off "when not in use."</p>

<p>And so the safest heat is the heat you switch off before you sleep.</p>

<h2 id="check-yours">How do I check mine and get my money back?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: look on the back of the white remote. The refund starts with a pair of scissors.</p>

<p>Turn the white remote over. The model number is on the back, and it starts with JKMAX. It is also on a white label on the box.</p>

<p>The recall names models JKMAX-002, JKMAX-7284B, JKMAX-6284B, JKMAX-10090B and JKMAX-8490B. Any JKMAX heated blanket or pad is on it.</p>

<p>Stop using it tonight. The CPSC says to contact JKMax "for a full refund."</p>

<p><a href="https://jkmaxrecall.com/recall">JKMAX's own recall page opens with "We offer our sincere apologies."</a> Then it hands you the work.</p>

<p>First you cut the whole power cord. You write "Recalled" and your last name on the remote in permanent marker. Then you email photos, your Amazon order number and the price you paid.</p>

<p>Then you wait. The page says customer service is "currently very busy" and will answer "within 2 weeks." A label comes. You ship it all back, and then the refund comes.</p>

<p>One click to buy it. Scissors, a marker, photos and two weeks to send it back.</p>

<p>The record on JKMAX, in order. Start with the last column.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for keeping your heated blanket?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, two fair ones, and neither one covers a JKMAX.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://health.ucdavis.edu/burncenter/newsletter/2018/0218BurnNet.pdf">The UC Davis burn center told a reader, "I wouldn't necessarily trash that electric blanket just yet."</a> It said newer blankets have a shutoff to cut the chance of overheating.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The recall notice counts 53 burn injuries, not 577. That is the number, and I am not going to make it bigger than it is.</p>

<p>So why would I still tell you to unplug it?</p>

<p>Because the JKMAX blankets were the newer kind, sold from 2022 to 2026. That same burn center says never to use warming bedding on anyone "insensitive to heat."</p>

<p>You are far from the only one who trusted it. About 195,000 of them went out. If your mother sleeps under one too, check hers the same night.</p>

<h2 id="what-instead">What keeps you warm instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a plain wool blanket on top, with no wire inside and no plug.</p>

<p>Unplug the JKMAX tonight and cut the cord the way the recall page asks.</p>

<p>Then put a heavy blanket on the bed, plain wool or thick cotton. It holds your own heat. Nothing in it can overheat, because nothing in it plugs in.</p>
