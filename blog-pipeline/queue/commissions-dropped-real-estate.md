<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>Your broker is half right.</p>
<p>Home sales are stuck at a 30-year low. That half really is the market.</p>
<p>The other half is a rule you never voted on. For 28 years it decided how a buyer's agent got paid, and NAR wrote it. The appeals court put it plainly: <strong><a href="https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/26/08/243444P.pdf">"NAR first adopted this rule in 1996."</a></strong></p>
<p>NAR gave the rule up to settle a lawsuit, and it denies any wrongdoing. On August 19, 2026, a federal appeals court upheld that deal.</p>
<p>Markets turn. That rule is gone. The average fee held, but now you ask for yours.</p>

<h2 id="just-the-market">Is your broker right that it is just the market?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: half right. 2025 had the fewest home sales since 1995. That is the half you were told about.</p>

<p>You kick your heels off under the stool at nine at night. The bowl on the island is full of keys to other people's houses. Two buyers signed with you. Neither one has closed.</p>

<p>The gas for the showings, the open house nobody came to, the buyer who went quiet after six tours, the check that came in smaller than last spring's, that feeling that the business is running you instead of the other way around.</p>

<p>And so you asked your broker what happened. Your broker said it is the market.</p>

<p>Your broker is not making it up. <a href="https://www.pbs.org/newshour/economy/2025-home-sales-stuck-at-30-year-low">In 2025, 4.06 million existing homes sold.</a> The Associated Press called it a 30-year low.</p>

<p>That half is the market. It is real, and it has nothing to do with how hard you work.</p>

<h2 id="the-rule">What did NAR's rule have to do with your paycheck?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it put your fee in the MLS before any buyer showed up. Then a jury found NAR liable over it.</p>

<p>You remember how it worked. The listing went live, and the buyer's side fee sat right there in the MLS.</p>

<p>That was not a habit. It was a rule. <a href="https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/26/08/243444P.pdf">The court says every NAR MLS had to enforce it.</a> The seller's side had to offer the buyer's agent a fee, or it could not use the MLS.</p>

<p>In April 2019, a group of Missouri home sellers sued NAR and four big brokerage brands. They alleged the rule was part of a price-fixing conspiracy.</p>

<p>In October 2023, a jury in Kansas City found NAR liable. It awarded $1.785 billion, and the law allows that to be tripled.</p>

<p>NAR settled before its challenges to that verdict were decided. <a href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/national-association-of-realtors-reaches-agreement-to-resolve-nationwide-claims-brought-by-home-sellers-302090431.html">It agreed to pay $418 million over about four years</a>, and to end the rule. Its own press release says NAR "continues to deny any wrongdoing."</p>

<p>It is still paying. <a href="https://www.nar.realtor/press-releases/national-association-of-realtorsr-reaches-agreement-to-resolve-nationwide-homebuyer-claims">In April 2026, NAR agreed to pay another $52.25 million</a>, this time to settle claims from home buyers.</p>

<p>You were not in that courtroom. Your fee was.</p>

<h2 id="what-changed">What changed in how you get paid?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: since August 17, 2024, you settle your fee in writing, with each buyer, before the first tour.</p>

<h3>Your fee left the MLS</h3>

<p>No fee for you can be offered in the MLS now. <a href="https://www.nar.realtor/news/real-estate-news/nar-practice-changes-to-take-effect-august-17">NAR set August 17, 2024 as the day that changed.</a> A seller can still offer one outside it. The court says the seller has to okay that offer in advance.</p>

<h3>The paper comes before the showing</h3>

<p>You sign a written agreement with your buyer before you tour a single house. It has to spell out your fee and who pays it. And your fee can't go over the sum your buyer agreed to.</p>

<h3>The word negotiable, said out loud</h3>

<p>You have to say it to every client, too. In the court's words, "brokers must tell clients that commissions are negotiable."</p>

<p>And so the fee you used to see in the MLS is now a talk at somebody's kitchen table. Here is the whole change, side by side.</p>

<p>Every row on that table was decided by NAR or by a court. Not one of them was decided by you.</p>

<h2 id="monday">What would I do first on Monday?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: count your closings, then write down the people.</p>

<h3>Count closings, not showings</h3>

<p>Pull last year's closing papers and this year's. Count the closings in each. Put both numbers on the fridge. It stops living in your stomach once it lives on paper.</p>

<h3>Ask your broker two questions</h3>

<p>What did my split do this year? What came out of my check before it reached me? If both answers are "the market," that is your answer.</p>

<h3>Write down the people, not the listings</h3>

<p>Every past client who still sends you a Christmas card. The couple whose first house you found. The neighbor who asks what the place down the street sold for. The friend who keeps asking about downsizing.</p>

<p>No rule change can take those names off you. They were never in the MLS.</p>

<p>Then ask five questions of whatever comes next, me included. What does it cost to start? Is there a monthly requirement? What happens to my customers if I stop? Where is the income disclosure? Who gets paid when I start?</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are women asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>Did the NAR settlement cut my commission?</strong></p>
<p>Not on paper. No rule set a lower fee. Redfin says the average buyer's agent fee is back near where it was in early 2024. What changed is that you now agree on it in writing, with each buyer, before the first tour.</p>

<p><strong>Can a seller still pay the buyer's agent?</strong></p>
<p>Yes. A seller can still agree to pay you. The offer just cannot go in the MLS, and your written buyer agreement caps what you can take.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to give up real estate to do this?</strong></p>
<p>No. Nobody is asking you to hang up your license. Keep your clients and keep showing houses. This sits beside that work, not on top of it.</p>
