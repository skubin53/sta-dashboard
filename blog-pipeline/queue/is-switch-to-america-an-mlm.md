<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p><strong>No. Switch to America is not an MLM.</strong></p>
<p>The FTC describes an MLM as <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/multi-level-marketing-businesses-and-pyramid-schemes">"selling products to family and friends and recruiting other people to do the same."</a></p>
<p>That is not how this works. Nobody sells anything. People shop for their own homes. Beef, snacks, vitamins, laundry soap, makeup. Over 575 products, delivered to their door.</p>
<p>No inventory. Nothing to stock. No buy-in to build the business.</p>
<p>The money comes from real customers. They keep reordering the soap, vitamins and cleaners they use up.</p>

<h2 id="why-people-ask">Why do some people ask?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: because people can earn by sharing it. From the outside, that looks familiar.</p>

<p>If you love a store, you tell a friend. With us, when your friend keeps shopping, you get paid for bringing them. Some friends tell their friends too.</p>

<p>From the outside, that can look like a network. But the parts that make it one are missing.</p>

<p>Nobody buys inventory. Nobody sells to their friends. Nobody buys their way in.</p>

<p>The money comes from products real families buy, use up and order again.</p>

<p>Some people also mix us up with other websites that have similar names. <a href="https://join.switchtoamerica.com/post/is-switch-to-america-the-same-as-ameriswitch">Here is how to tell which site is ours</a>.</p>

<h2 id="pyramid">Is it a pyramid scheme?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no. In a pyramid scheme, the money comes from recruiting. Here, it comes from real products people use up and order again.</p>

<p>The FTC puts it plainly. In a pyramid scheme, <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/multi-level-marketing-businesses-and-pyramid-schemes">"Your income would be based mostly on how many people you recruit, not how much product you sell."</a></p>

<p>The FTC also says it looks at <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guidance-concerning-multi-level-marketing">how a business really works and what its pay plan rewards</a>.</p>

<p>Here, income comes from real customers. They keep shopping for their own homes.</p>

<h3>Start with Johnson &amp; Johnson</h3>

<p>J&amp;J made the baby powder many of us grew up with.</p>

<ul>
<li>In 2020, J&amp;J stopped selling its talc baby powder in the US and Canada. <a href="https://www.jnj.com/our-company/johnson-johnson-consumer-health-announces-discontinuation-of-talc-based-johnsons-baby-powder-in-u-s-and-canada">J&amp;J's statement</a>. In 2023 it switched to cornstarch worldwide. <a href="https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-consumer-health-to-transition-global-baby-powder-portfolio-to-cornstarch">J&amp;J's announcement</a>.</li>
<li>Tens of thousands of people have sued, saying the talc powder caused their cancer. In July 2026, J&amp;J offered $5.5 billion to settle about 76,000 ovarian cancer claims. The deal is not final yet. <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/200406/000020040626000155/exhibit991.htm">J&amp;J's filing</a>. J&amp;J denies the claims and says its talc is safe.</li>
<li>In December 2025, a Baltimore jury awarded more than $1.5 billion to a woman who said the company's talc gave her mesothelioma, a rare cancer. J&amp;J is appealing. <a href="https://thedailyrecord.com/2025/12/23/baltimore-jury-johnson-johnson-talc-mesothelioma-verdict/">The Daily Record</a>.</li>
<li>In 2021, J&amp;J recalled five Neutrogena and Aveeno spray sunscreens after its own tests found low levels of benzene in some samples. Benzene is known to cause cancer. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/johnson-johnson-consumer-inc-issues-voluntary-recall-specific-neutrogenar-and-aveenor-aerosol">The recall notice</a>.</li>
</ul>

<p>J&amp;J's old baby and drugstore brands now belong to <a href="https://www.jnj.com/media-center/press-releases/johnson-johnson-announces-final-results-of-exchange-offer-and-finalizes-separation-of-kenvue-inc">Kenvue</a>, which split off from J&amp;J in 2023. Kimberly-Clark, the manufacturer of Huggies and Kleenex, has <a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/55785/000162828026052348/kmb-20260630.htm">agreed to buy Kenvue</a>.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Is Switch to America an MLM?</strong></p>
<p>No. In an MLM, people buy products and sell them to family and friends. Here, nobody sells anything. People shop for their own homes, straight from an American manufacturer. There is no inventory and no buy-in to build the business.</p>

<p><strong>What can people buy?</strong></p>
<p>Over 575 products. Beef, snacks, baking mixes, supplements, vitamins, laundry detergent, makeup, cleaners, skin care and more. New products come out every month.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to sell anything?</strong></p>
<p>No. There is nothing to sell. Shoppers order what their own home uses. It ships straight to them.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to buy inventory?</strong></p>
<p>No. There is nothing to stock and nothing to resell.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to recruit anyone?</strong></p>
<p>No. You can simply shop, and that is a win. If you want to earn, you can refer people who want to shop for their own homes too.</p>

<p><strong>Is it a pyramid scheme?</strong></p>
<p>No. The FTC says a pyramid scheme pays mostly for recruiting, not for product sold. Here, the money comes from real products that real customers buy, use up and order again.</p>

<p><strong>Who is Switch to America up against?</strong></p>
<p>Not other home businesses. The giant companies behind the brands on store shelves, like Procter &amp; Gamble, Unilever and Nestl&eacute;. And the big box stores that sell them.</p>

<p>So if anyone asks whether Switch to America is an MLM, the answer is no.</p>
