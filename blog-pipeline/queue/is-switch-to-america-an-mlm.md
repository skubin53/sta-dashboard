<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p><strong>No. Switch to America is not an MLM.</strong></p>
<p>The FTC describes an MLM as <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/multi-level-marketing-businesses-and-pyramid-schemes">"selling products to family and friends and recruiting other people to do the same."</a></p>
<p>That is not how this works. Nobody sells anything. People shop for their own homes, straight from an American manufacturer. It ships to their door.</p>
<p>No inventory. No boxes in the garage. No buy-in to build the business.</p>
<p>The money comes from real customers. They keep reordering the soap, vitamins and cleaners they use up.</p>

<h2 id="what-it-is">What is Switch to America?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it is my own business. I help families switch to safer, American made versions of what they already buy. It comes to their door.</p>

<p>Think dish soap, laundry soap, the cleaner under the sink, shampoo, vitamins. The things a home uses up every month.</p>

<p>Families shop online from an American manufacturer. It has been in business for 41 years. In 2026, Forbes ranked it number 2 on its list of America's Best Brands for Value. Only Costco ranked higher.</p>

<p>So we do not compete with network marketing companies. We compete with stores like Costco, Walmart and Target.</p>

<h2 id="why-people-ask">Why do some people ask?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: because people can earn by sharing it. From the outside, that looks familiar.</p>

<p>If you love a store, you tell a friend. With us, when your friend keeps shopping, you get paid for bringing them. Some friends tell their friends too.</p>

<p>From the outside, that can look like a network. But the parts that make it one are missing.</p>

<p>Nobody stocks a garage. Nobody sells to their friends. Nobody buys their way in.</p>

<p>The money comes from products real families buy, use up and order again.</p>

<h2 id="pyramid">Is it a pyramid scheme?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no. In a pyramid scheme, the money comes from recruiting. Here, it comes from real products people use up and order again.</p>

<p>The FTC puts it plainly. In a pyramid scheme, <a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/multi-level-marketing-businesses-and-pyramid-schemes">"Your income would be based mostly on how many people you recruit, not how much product you sell."</a></p>

<p>The FTC also says it <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guidance-concerning-multi-level-marketing">looks closely at where the money really comes from</a>. Real customers who want the products? Or recruiting?</p>

<p>Here, income comes from real customers. They keep shopping for their own homes.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else might you be wondering?</h2>

<p><strong>Is Switch to America an MLM?</strong></p>
<p>No. In an MLM, people buy products and sell them to family and friends. Here, nobody sells anything. People shop for their own homes, straight from an American manufacturer. There is no inventory and no buy-in to build the business.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to sell anything?</strong></p>
<p>No. There is nothing to sell. Shoppers order what their own home uses. It ships straight to them.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to buy inventory?</strong></p>
<p>No. There is nothing to stock and nothing to resell. No boxes in the garage.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to recruit anyone?</strong></p>
<p>No. You can simply shop, and that is a win. If you want to earn, you can refer people who want to shop for their own homes too.</p>

<p><strong>Is it a pyramid scheme?</strong></p>
<p>No. The FTC says a pyramid scheme pays mostly for recruiting, not for product sold. Here, the money comes from real products that real customers buy, use up and order again.</p>
