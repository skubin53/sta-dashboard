<h2 id="in-the-tube">What is in the tube you use twice a day?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: for years, some of it was plastic.</p>

<p>In March 2014 a dental hygienist named Trish Walraven wrote a short post.</p>

<p>She had been pulling blue specks out of her patients' gums for months. Not off the surface. Down in the channel where the gum meets the tooth.</p>

<p>So she took a tube home. She mixed a pea sized blob with water and strained out what would not dissolve.</p>

<p>Then she soaked the specks in nail polish remover. Then in alcohol. Overnight.</p>

<p>Nothing happened. They were polyethylene. The plastic in a grocery bag.</p>

<p>She went looking for a reason. <a href="https://www.dentalbuzz.com/2014/03/04/crest-imbeds-plastic-in-our-gums/">Crest's own website gave one, and she quoted it: "Polyethylene plastic is in your toothpaste for decorative purposes only."</a></p>

<p><strong>Color.</strong> That was the whole reason.</p>

<p>Then she listed the Crest tubes it was in. 3D White. Pro-Health. Sensitivity.</p>

<p>Hygienists across the country started saying the same thing.</p>

<figure style="margin:28px 0;">
<img src="https://scan.ismyhometoxic.com/blog-images/iscresttoot-villain-v1.webp" alt="A red tube of Crest toothpaste standing on a bathroom counter beside a toothbrush loaded with paste, cups of family toothbrushes at one end and a dark window behind" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px;">
</figure>

<p>Six months later Procter and Gamble gave in. <a href="https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2014/09/crests-parent-company-says-microbeads-in-toothpaste-will-be-removed/">It said the beads would be gone from Crest by March 2016.</a></p>

<p>And then Congress went further.</p>

<p>A federal law signed on 28 December 2015 banned plastic beads in rinse off products. <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-114publ114/html/PLAW-114publ114.htm">The law spells out who it means: "the term 'rinse-off cosmetic' includes toothpaste."</a></p>

<p>Read that again. A law about plastic litter had to name toothpaste.</p>

<h2 id="sued-now">What is Crest being sued over right now?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the stuff that makes the foam.</p>

<p>Sherrie Oliver and Michael Testone sued Procter and Gamble on 2 July 2026, in a California federal court.</p>

<p>The tubes named are the Crest Pro-Health gum line. Gum Detoxify Plus. Advanced Gum Restore. Gum and Sensitive.</p>

<p>Every one of those is sold to fix your gums.</p>

<p>The filing says they hold sodium lauryl sulfate. That is the detergent that makes toothpaste foam up.</p>

<p>In their words it is <strong>"a harsh detergent and known irritant"</strong> that at these levels "can damage gum and mouth tissue, inhibit wound healing, trigger inflammation."</p>

<p>And then the line that does it.</p>

<p><strong>"These are the very conditions P&amp;G claims the Products prevent or reduce."</strong></p>

<p>Sherrie Oliver says her teeth and gums hurt after using one of them.</p>

<p>Procter and Gamble has not answered in court. Nothing is proven yet.</p>

<h2 id="every-toothpaste">Isn't this in every toothpaste?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: plenty of them, yes. That is not a reason to keep this one.</p>

<p>You are right. That foaming detergent sits in a lot of tubes.</p>

<p>And so the question was never really the chemical. It is the company.</p>

<p>This is the company that put plastic in your mouth and called it color.</p>

<p>It took a hygienist and an act of Congress to get it out.</p>

<p>Look at what else they were doing. On 23 November 2021 they pulled Old Spice and Secret sprays off the shelves. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/pg-issues-voluntary-recall-specific-old-spice-and-secret-aerosol-spray-antiperspirants-and-old-spice">Their own recall notice says why: benzene. "Benzene is classified as a human carcinogen."</a></p>

<p>Four weeks later, on 17 December, <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/pg-issues-voluntary-recall-aerosol-dry-conditioner-spray-products-and-aerosol-dry-shampoo-spray">they recalled their dry shampoo sprays for the same thing.</a> Pantene. Herbal Essences. Aussie.</p>

<p>Different aisle. Same company. Same year.</p>

<p>You cannot redo the whole bathroom tonight. So pick the thing with the most hours on it.</p>

<p>Two minutes, twice a day, for the rest of your life. Here that is the tube.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">The case against my own story</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: here is what cuts the other way.</p>

<p>I will not hide the parts that do not help me.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> The hygienist said it herself. "I am not saying that polyethylene is causing gum problems. I'd be jumping too soon to that conclusion without scientific proof."</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The beads are gone. That part is finished and it stayed finished.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> The 2026 case is a filing. No judge has agreed with one word of it yet.</p>

<p><strong>Four.</strong> On both benzene recalls the company said the amounts found "would not be expected to cause adverse health consequences."</p>

<p>So why does this still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because of who keeps finding it.</p>

<p>A hygienist found the plastic. Lawyers filed the suit. A lab found the benzene.</p>

<p><strong>Not once did the company find it first and come and tell you.</strong></p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Does Crest still have plastic beads in it?</strong> No. Procter and Gamble said they were out by March 2016. A federal law then banned plastic beads in rinse off products, and it names toothpaste. This part is closed.</p>

<p><strong>What is sodium lauryl sulfate doing in toothpaste?</strong> It is a detergent. It makes the foam. The July 2026 filing calls it "a harsh detergent and known irritant" at the levels used in the Crest gum tubes.</p>

<p><strong>Has a court found Crest unsafe?</strong> No. The case was filed on 2 July 2026 and that is all it is. Every line in it is an allegation until a judge or a jury says otherwise.</p>

<p><strong>Is the whole ingredient list printed on the tube?</strong> Often not. The hygienist who found the beads in 2014 noted the full list was on the box, not the tube. Throw the box out and it is gone.</p>
