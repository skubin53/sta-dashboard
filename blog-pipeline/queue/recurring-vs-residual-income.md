<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>They are not the same thing. Recurring is a charge that repeats until somebody cancels. Residual is a payment each time finished work gets used again.</p>
<p>Amazon Prime is a recurring charge. <strong><a href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-secures-historic-25-billion-settlement-against-amazon">In September 2025, Amazon agreed to pay $2.5 billion to settle FTC charges over Prime.</a></strong> The FTC alleged Amazon made it hard to cancel on purpose. Amazon did not admit it.</p>
<p>So when somebody offers you recurring income, ask one thing. Who has to keep the customer from leaving?</p>

<h2 id="the-difference">What is the difference between recurring and residual income?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: one repeats until your customer leaves. The other is pay each time work is used again, or money left over.</p>

<p>Start with recurring. Think of a gym, or a box on the porch each month.</p>

<p>The charge comes back on the same day. It stops the day your customer clicks cancel. And so every month has one quiet job inside it. Keep her from clicking.</p>

<p>Residual means something else. <a href="https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/residual">The Cambridge dictionary</a> calls it "a payment made to an actor, singer, writer, etc. for repeated uses of their work."</p>

<p>She films the show once. It airs again, and she is paid again.</p>

<p>The same page gives a second meaning. Money "left after some other payments have been made." That is your checking account after the bills are paid.</p>

<p>So ask anybody who says it which one they mean.</p>

<h2 id="amazon-prime">What did the FTC say Amazon did to keep Prime recurring?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the FTC alleged Amazon signed people up without clear consent. It said the way out was built to stop them.</p>

<p>A charge that renews is legal. The trouble, by the FTC's account, was how Amazon kept people in. <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/ftc-takes-action-against-amazon-enrolling-consumers-amazon-prime-without-consent-sabotaging-their">The FTC sued in June 2023.</a> It charged that the main purpose of the cancel process "was not to enable subscribers to cancel, but to stop them."</p>

<p>First you had to find the cancel page, the FTC said. Then, it said, page after page asked you to stay, at a lower price or with auto-renew off.</p>

<p>The FTC pointed to news reports that Amazon called this path the "Iliad." That is Homer's poem about a war that lasted ten years.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-secures-historic-25-billion-settlement-against-amazon">In 2025 the FTC quoted Amazon's own papers</a>: "subscription driving is a bit of a shady world."</p>

<p>Amazon settled in September 2025. The FTC says that meant a $1 billion penalty and $1.5 billion in refunds, for about 35 million people. <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Amazon-ROSCA-Order-Filed_0.pdf">The settlement order says Amazon does not admit or deny the charges.</a></p>

<p>The deal even bans one button by name, the FTC says. Amazon can no longer show one that reads "No, I don't want Free Shipping."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/09/ftc-announces-additional-payments-consumers-stemming-ftcs-amazon-prime-settlement">On September 17, 2026, the FTC said refunds would widen.</a> Refunds go out to millions more members from October 1, 2026, with no claim needed.</p>

<p>The charge only repeats if she stays. And so, by the FTC's account, the effort went into hiding the door. Your business is far smaller. It still needs her to stay.</p>

<h2 id="every-month">What does recurring income ask of you every month?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the same work again, plus the work of keeping your customer. And leaving keeps getting easier.</p>

<p>Say you sell a monthly box. You pack it every month. Say you teach a weekly class. You teach it every week.</p>

<p>The charge repeats because the work repeats. Recurring income is usually recurring work.</p>

<p>The tape gun, the flat boxes by your elbow, the labels on Sunday night, the email from the one who wants to pause, that feeling that the calendar owns you instead of the other way around.</p>

<p>Now the way out. Amazon's order says she must be able to leave the same way she came in.</p>

<p>Europe got there first. <a href="https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_22_4186/IP_22_4186_EN.pdf">The European Commission said in July 2022</a> that Amazon agreed to let people there cancel Prime "with just two clicks."</p>

<p>That is fair to her. It also puts recurring income a click or two from gone.</p>

<h2 id="side-by-side">How do recurring and residual compare side by side?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: recurring ends with one click. The reorder kind of residual ends when your customer stops wanting the thing.</p>

<p>This is what each one asks of your Sunday nights. The last row is the one that matters.</p>

<table class="sta-table">
<tr><th>&nbsp;</th><th>Recurring</th><th>Residual</th></tr>

<tr><td>What keeps it going</td><td>She has not canceled yet</td><td>She chooses the thing again</td></tr>

<p>Neither column is a promise. Both can stop. What differs is what keeps her there. A charge she has to remember to stop, or a thing she chooses again.</p>

<h2 id="recruiting-word">Isn't residual income just a recruiting word?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it gets used that way. So make whoever says it name the meaning. Mine is the reorder.</p>

<p>I know what you are thinking. "Residual" is all over your feed. You are right to roll your eyes.</p>

<p>And a reorder can stop too, any month. I will not hand you a number to expect. Nobody honest can.</p>

<p>Amazon has a side too. <a href="https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-response-on-ftc-agreement">Its statement says</a> "Amazon and our executives have always followed the law." It says it works "incredibly hard" to make signing up or canceling "clear and simple."</p>

<p>A monthly charge you are glad to pay is fine. The FTC's case was about the way in and the way out.</p>

<p>When I say residual, I mean the reorder. Switch to America is the maker. No middlemen and no markup. I would rather lose a customer than trap one.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do you want to know?</h2>

<p><strong>Is recurring income bad?</strong></p>
<p>No. A charge your customer is glad to pay is fine, and plenty of good businesses run on one. The trouble starts when the business needs her to forget she is paying.</p>

<p><strong>What does the Prime order say about canceling now?</strong></p>
<p>The 2025 order says the way out must not be "difficult, costly, confusing, or time consuming." It has to work through the same channel you used to sign up.</p>

<p><strong>Am I owed a Prime refund?</strong></p>
<p>Maybe. <a href="https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/refunds/amazon-refunds">The FTC says</a> it covers US members who signed up through certain Amazon pages, or tried to cancel online and could not, from June 23, 2019 to June 23, 2025. You also had to use 20 or fewer Prime benefits in a year. No claim is needed, and payments run through April 2027.</p>
