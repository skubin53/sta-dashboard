<h2 id="what-happened">What happened to Trunori?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: on September 28, 2026, it told promoters it was closing. The founders say their manufacturing partner stopped the orders.</p>

<p>It is late. One lamp is on in the kitchen. Your laptop is shut and your chair is pushed back.</p>

<p>You know the list. The customer who texted to ask where her ProPara is, the kit box still in the hall closet, the group chat that will not go quiet, the launch post still pinned to your page, and that feeling that somebody else's choice was running your business instead of you.</p>

<p>Here is what the founders wrote. Their shipping side was told "by our manufacturing co-founder to stop fulfilling trunori orders."</p>

<p>And so the doors closed. All open orders, they wrote, "will be refunded in full."</p>

<h2 id="one-partner">How could one partner stop everyone's orders?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: Trunori's name was on the bottle. By the founders' account, the shipping sat in a partner's hands.</p>

<p>Read the notice slowly. Nothing in the published notice blames the product, and nothing blames you. It points at one person, on the side that made the product.</p>

<p>The notice <a href="https://behindmlm.com/companies/trunori-collapses-manufacturing-partners-blamed/">does not name that partner</a>. It does not say what went wrong between them either.</p>

<p>So, by the founders' account, the name on the bottle and the hand on the shipping were not the same. They said it plainly: "we cannot guarantee that an order placed will actually be fulfilled."</p>

<p>From your side of the screen, you had no way to see that. Nobody gave you a key to that room.</p>

<p>Here is the part I know you are thinking. Maybe it was just bad luck.</p>

<p>The founders wrote, "We were not preparing to close trunori. We did not see this coming." The watchdog site that reported the notice is not so sure. It asks how, if money was the problem, they did not see it coming. I take them at their word.</p>

<p>And that is the point. If the people at the top could not see it coming, nobody selling it could. Nobody hands you these questions on day one. Here they are.</p>

<p>Ask all three of any company you look at. Ask them of me.</p>

<p>Here is Trunori held up against those three. The middle column is the founders' own notice, as published.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly, and here is the part I will not dress up.</p>

<p>The founders were building, by their own account. Their notice says new products were on the way. "Those products are real. Those plans were real."</p>

<p>Switch to America could close too. Any company can. I will not pretend otherwise.</p>

<p>Here is my answer to question one. We are the manufacturer. No middlemen and no markup. No minimums, no maximums. Ask me the other two on the call. If I dodge one, that is your answer.</p>

<p>You pay what a brand new shopper pays. I will not hand you a number to expect. Nobody honest can.</p>

<h2 id="monday">What would I do first on Monday?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: look after your customers' money first, then write down their names.</p>

<h3>Call every customer who had an order open</h3>

<p>Tell her what the founders wrote. All open orders "will be refunded in full." Ask her to tell you when it lands.</p>

<p>Federal <a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-435">rules for online and mail orders</a> back her up. Once a seller tells her it will not ship, the order counts as canceled. The seller owes her a "prompt refund."</p>

<p>If she paid by credit card and nothing shows up, <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1666">federal law</a> counts goods "not delivered" as a billing error. Her letter has to reach the card company within 60 days. The clock starts when the first bill showing the charge was sent. So do not wait long.</p>

<p>She will remember that you called.</p>

<h3>Save every order record you can still find</h3>

<p>Order emails, receipts, texts from the company. Put them in one folder with the date on it. If a refund or a card letter is needed, that folder is your proof.</p>

<h3>Check your own card for the monthly fee</h3>

<p>If you paid a monthly promoter fee, find it on your statement. Make sure it stopped.</p>

<h3>Write down the people, not the products</h3>

<p>Each name you can think of. The one who asked you about protein. Her sister. The neighbor who tried it twice.</p>

<p>No notice can close those. They are yours. They always were.</p>

<p>Then talk it over with a friend who has nothing to sell you.</p>
