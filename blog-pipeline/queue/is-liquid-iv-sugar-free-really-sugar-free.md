<h2 id="what-the-suit-says">What does the Liquid I.V. lawsuit say?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it says a sugar called allulose is the first ingredient in every Sugar-Free stick. And it says a note on the pack shows the company knew allulose adds sugar.</p>

<p>You want more water in your day, and that is fair. The suit is about the word on the stick.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.classaction.org/media/liquid-iv-complaint.pdf">On August 12, 2026, two shoppers sued The LIV Group, the company behind Liquid I.V. They filed in federal court in Northern California.</a> It covers the Sugar-Free Hydration Multiplier and Energy Multiplier, every flavor.</p>

<p>The front says Sugar-Free. The first ingredient is allulose, <a href="https://www.classaction.org/media/liquid-iv-complaint.pdf">"four or five grams" a serving, by the complaint's count.</a> The panel says 0g Total Sugars.</p>

<p>"Allulose is a sugar," the complaint says.</p>

<h3 id="allulose">Allulose, the first ingredient in the stick</h3>

<p>The complaint calls allulose "a rare sugar," made of the same parts as fruit sugar. It is about 70% as sweet as table sugar. So it takes grams of it to sweeten a drink.</p>

<p>"Allulose is not a common household name," the complaint says. You should not need a chemistry class to buy a drink mix.</p>

<p>So how does a stick with 4 grams of allulose show a zero?</p>

<h2 id="zero-grams">How can the panel say 0 grams of sugar?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: guidance from 2020 lets companies leave allulose out of the sugar lines on the panel. The rule itself was never rewritten, the suit says.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-seeks-input-nutrition-labeling-certain-sugars-and-issues-final-guidance-allulose">In October 2020, federal food regulators put out guidance on allulose.</a> They would not enforce counting it in Total Sugars or Added Sugars while they work on a rule. It still counts in Total Carbohydrate.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.classaction.org/media/liquid-iv-complaint.pdf">The rule itself still counts every simple sugar as sugar, with no exception for allulose, the complaint says.</a></p>

<h3 id="sugar-free-rule">What sugar free means in the rule</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/101.60">The federal rule sets a line for sugar free. A serving must have "less than 0.5 g of sugars."</a> By the complaint's math, 4 grams is eight times that line.</p>

<p>The rule allows a small note by an ingredient: "adds a dietarily insignificant amount of sugar." Liquid I.V. prints that note by the allulose, the complaint says.</p>

<p>The suit says that note is only allowed when the sugar is under half a gram. It says the note shows the company knew allulose adds sugar.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for Liquid I.V.?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, a fair one. The grams of allulose are printed right on the pack.</p>

<p>I know what you are thinking. It says allulose right there on the pack. That is fair.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://www.classaction.org/media/liquid-iv-complaint.pdf">The pack says "4g Allulose Per Serving" or "5g," the complaint says.</a></p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The 0 on the panel matches the 2020 guidance.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74638230/ulrich-v-the-liv-group-inc/">No judge has ruled against the company. As of October 13, 2026, the court file shows no ruling on the claims.</a></p>

<p>And this suit does not say allulose hurt anyone. I am not saying that either.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because the front says Sugar-Free, and by the suit's account the first ingredient is a sugar. The front is what you paid for.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/07/28/879370.htm">A suit over Chobani Zero Sugar yogurt is further along. On July 27, 2026, a federal appeals court in Chicago let it go forward.</a> That yogurt has 4 grams of allulose. Judge Thomas Kirsch wrote that the court did "not find that allegation implausible."</p>

<p>The court did not say who wins. It said the shoppers can try to prove it.</p>
