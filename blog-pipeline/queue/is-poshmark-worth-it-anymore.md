<h2 id="what-changed">What changes at Poshmark on October 26?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it stops paying for a box that weighs more than its label. That cost now comes out of your sale.</p>

<p>It is late, and the wool coat is finally in the box, taped shut on your office floor.</p>

<p>You printed the same prepaid label as always. Under a new Poshmark rule, that box can cost you.</p>

<p>Poshmark posted it on its blog on October 8. <a href="https://blog.poshmark.com/2026/10/08/shipping-heavier-items-how-label-upgrades-work/">Starting October 26, "if you ship a package over 5 lbs without upgrading your label, the required upgrade cost will be deducted from your earnings."</a></p>

<p>The standard label covers up to 5 pounds. That counts the box, the padding and the tape. Poshmark itself says coats, boots, handbags and bundles are more likely to go over.</p>

<h2 id="charges">Why is Poshmark charging me $10 or $15?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the box weighed more than its label. The bigger the gap, the bigger the charge.</p>

<p>Here is <a href="https://blog.poshmark.com/2026/10/08/shipping-heavier-items-how-label-upgrades-work/">Poshmark's own list</a>. From 5.01 to 10 pounds, the upgrade is $5. From 10.01 to 15 pounds, it is $10.</p>

<p>Ship more than 15 pounds on one label, and $15 comes out, up to 25 pounds. Past 25, you pay the "full USPS shipping cost."</p>

<p>It is not the first shipping change this year. In February, Poshmark cut its upgrade tiers from five to two, <a href="https://www.valueaddedresource.net/poshmark-ends-overweight-shipping-protection/">Value Added Resource reported on October 17</a>.</p>

<p>The same report says Poshmark "has not explained why it is ending either protection." And so you get the bill, and no reason.</p>

<h2 id="hazmat">What is the $50 hazmat fee, and why is it yours now?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it is a USPS charge on a box that breaks its hazmat rules. Since October 19, Poshmark no longer covers it.</p>

<p>USPS started charging it on July 12, 2026, Poshmark says, and Poshmark "covered most of this fee for sellers."</p>

<p>On October 5, it gave you 14 days' notice. <a href="https://blog.poshmark.com/2026/10/05/usps-hazmat-fee-update-how-to-avoid-the-50-fee/">From October 19, "sellers will be responsible for the $50 fee if USPS flags their package."</a></p>

<p><a href="https://www.usps.com/ship/shipping-restrictions.htm">USPS calls it a "HAZMAT noncompliance fee."</a> It applies to commercial shipments "improperly declared, improperly labeled," or outside its rules some other way.</p>

<p>Poshmark says the most common cause "isn't the item inside." It is a reused box with an old hazmat mark on it. Watch for the small Limited Quantity diamond on boxes from beauty products, aerosols and cleaning supplies.</p>

<p>The items have USPS rules too, for perfume, nail polish and aerosols. Perfume with alcohol cannot go by air, only by ground.</p>

<h2 id="buyer">Who does your buyer belong to?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: on paper, her contact details are for Poshmark business only. It is written into Poshmark's terms.</p>

<p>You found her. You took the photos by the window at noon, and you answered her question about the hem.</p>

<p><a href="https://poshmark.com/terms">Poshmark's terms</a> cover "the contact information of any User of the Service (including Buyers)." You may not use it for anything but the service and "any transactions between Users."</p>

<p>And so the shopper who loved your closet stays a buyer in Poshmark's system. You may not write to her about anything but Poshmark.</p>

<p>The label is not yours either. Poshmark "reserves the right to discontinue providing Labels to any or all Users at any time and for any reason."</p>

<p>And the rules move. The same terms say, "We may update this Agreement at any time by posting updated Terms."</p>

<p>They have moved before. In October 2024, Poshmark split its fees between buyers and sellers. Weeks later it went back, on October 24, 2024. <a href="https://www.valueaddedresource.net/poshmark-reverts-to-original-fee-structure/">Its CEO, Manish Chandra, wrote,</a> "The outcome of the change did not meet our expectations."</p>

<p>Poshmark is not a monster. It kept its upgrade prices the same this season. In February, it cut the upgrade on a 10 pound box from $22.50 to $5, <a href="https://www.valueaddedresource.net/poshmark-ends-overweight-shipping-protection/">Value Added Resource reported</a>.</p>

<p>In 2024 it took back a fee change and said sorry.</p>

<p>Keep your closet. Nobody is asking you to delete a thing.</p>

<p>Now me. Switch to America could change its rules too. Any company can.</p>

<p>Switch to America is the manufacturer. No middlemen and no markup. No minimums, no maximums.</p>

<p>A coat sells once. A shopper who likes her laundry soap buys it again. That reorder is the part I care about.</p>

<p>You pay what a brand new shopper pays. I will not hand you a number to expect. Nobody honest can.</p>

<h2 id="monday">What would I do first on Monday?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: weigh the box, strip the old marks, then write down the people you know by name.</p>

<h3>Weigh every box after you pack it</h3>

<p>Put it on a kitchen scale with the tape on. Over 5 pounds? Upgrade before you ship, under Sales, then the order, then Upgrade Shipping Label.</p>

<h3>Clean every box you reuse</h3>

<p>Peel off or cover every old hazmat mark. Not sure? Poshmark's own advice is a new box or mailer.</p>

<h3>Write down the people you know by name</h3>

<p>Not your Poshmark buyers. Its terms keep their details for Poshmark business. I mean the friends who ask where you found that jacket. Your sister. The neighbor who borrows your good boots.</p>

<p>No company blog post can change those names. It is not a list to sell to. I do not teach that.</p>

<h3>Ask the next company four questions</h3>

<p>Including me. Who owns the customer list? Who can change the fees, and with how much notice? Who pays when the mail goes wrong? Where is the income disclosure?</p>

<p>If somebody dodges one, that is your answer.</p>
