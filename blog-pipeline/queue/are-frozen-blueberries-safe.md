<h2 id="what-got-pulled">What exactly got pulled from Walmart and Trader Joe's?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: six lots of Great Value organic berries and one Trader Joe's organic blend. The lot code is printed on the bag.</p>

<p>Go get the bag. Look for one of these three.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Great Value Organic Blueberries, 10 ounces.</strong> Lot 6 019 01, 6 040 01 or 6 163 01, on the front.</li>
<li><strong>Great Value Organic Triple Berry Blend, 10 ounces.</strong> Lot 6 031 01, 6 041 01 or 6040 01-6, on the front.</li>
<li><strong>Trader Joe's Organic Mixed Berry Blend, 12 ounces.</strong> Lot 6 051 01, on the back.</li>
</ul>

<p>If your code is there, do not eat it. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/frutas-y-hortalizas-del-sur-sa-expands-recalls-include-additional-lots-great-value-frozen-organic">The notice says to throw it out or take it back "to Walmart or Trader Joe's for a full refund."</a></p>

<p>Shop at Publix? <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/publix-recalls-all-lots-greenwise-organic-frozen-blueberries-and-whole-mixed-berries-due-potential-e">It recalled every lot of its GreenWise organic frozen blueberries and mixed berries in July.</a></p>

<p>The bags carry store names. The recall carries the supplier's name: Frutas y Hortalizas del Sur S.A., of San Carlos, Chile.</p>

<h3>E. coli O145, the germ named in the recall</h3>

<p>This is not a stomach bug you sleep off. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/frutas-y-hortalizas-del-sur-sa-expands-recalls-include-additional-lots-great-value-frozen-organic">The notice says it can cause "severe stomach cramps, diarrhea (which may be bloody), and vomiting."</a></p>

<p>Most healthy people get better in a week, it says. Some get a kind of kidney failure called HUS.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/outbreak-investigation-e-coli-o145h28-frozen-blueberries-july-2026">Symptoms can start a few days after you eat it. Or up to nine days later, the outbreak report says.</a> Ate from a recalled bag and got bad cramps or bloody diarrhea? Get medical care that day.</p>

<p>And the word organic on the front did not stop any of this.</p>

<h2 id="didnt-organic">Didn't organic mean they were safe?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no. The USDA says its own organic seal "does not convey a message of food safety."</p>

<p>You paid extra for organic. That is a fair thing to want.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/NOP%20Labeling%20Preamble.pdf">The USDA's own organic rules say the seal covers how the food was grown and handled.</a> Then they say it flat out. "The seal does not convey a message of food safety or more nutritional value."</p>

<p>Nobody printed that on the bag. And so you paid extra, and that line stayed in a government rulebook.</p>

<p>Frozen berries carry one more problem. You do not cook them. <a href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cms_ia/importalert_1128.html">A federal import alert now names this supplier. It says produce is often eaten "without further treatment" to kill germs.</a></p>

<p>So the berry goes straight from the bag to your bowl.</p>

<h2 id="every-bag">Do I have to throw out every frozen berry I own?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no. Only the listed lots are recalled. The next bag you buy is where you get to choose.</p>

<p>No, you cannot trace every bag back to a field in Chile. So do not try.</p>

<p>Toss the recalled lots, then change where your everyday bag comes from.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">The case against my own story</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the outbreak is over and no deaths were reported.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/outbreak-investigation-e-coli-o145h28-frozen-blueberries-july-2026">The CDC declared the outbreak over on September 21, 2026. It counted 17 sick in Florida and Georgia, 6 in the hospital, and no deaths reported.</a></p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> <a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/outbreak-investigation-e-coli-o145h28-frozen-blueberries-july-2026">Every sample federal investigators pulled tested negative, the outbreak report says. One sample Publix tested did match the outbreak germ.</a></p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> Only the listed lot codes are recalled. If your code is not there, your bag is not in this.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because the list kept growing. The newest lots went on it October 2, eleven days after the outbreak was declared over.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/frutas-y-hortalizas-del-sur-sa-expands-recalls-include-additional-lots-great-value-frozen-organic">The supplier calls that "a precautionary measure following traceback information."</a> None of its three notices says how the germ got in.</p>

<p>In July, its frozen blueberries also went on a federal import alert. <a href="https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cms_ia/importalert_1128.html">The alert is for produce that "appears to have been prepared, packed or held under insanitary conditions."</a> That is its title, not a court ruling.</p>

<h2 id="what-do-i-buy">Where do I get blueberries I can trust?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: from a farm near you, picked by a farmer whose name you know.</p>

<p>You want berries on your yogurt every morning. Nothing wrong with wanting that.</p>

<p>The problem is the chain. Your bag came from a supplier in Chile, crossed a border, took a store's name and sat in a freezer case. Nobody in that line ever had to look you in the eye.</p>

<p>So make the chain short. Find one blueberry farm near you, a pick-your-own field or a farm stand.</p>

<p>Get to know the farmer. Call this week. Ask how they grow them and who picks them.</p>

<p>Go in the summer with a friend. Pick a bucket each, and freeze them on a sheet pan at home.</p>

<p>A farm can get it wrong too. But you can call the farmer and ask.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Are frozen blueberries safe to eat right now?</strong></p>
<p>Most bags are. Only the recalled lots are the problem. If your lot code is on the list, throw the bag out.</p>

<p><strong>Can I cook or rinse a recalled bag and still use it?</strong></p>
<p>Do not try. The notice says not to eat the recalled lots at all.</p>

<p><strong>I already ate some. What should I watch for?</strong></p>
<p>Bad stomach cramps, diarrhea that may be bloody, and vomiting. They can start a few days to nine days after. If that is you, get medical care that day.</p>
