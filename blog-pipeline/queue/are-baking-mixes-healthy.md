<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>The front of the box is an advertisement. It is not a report.</p>
<p><strong>In June 2025 Texas passed a law about this. <a href="https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB00025F.htm">It names 44 ingredients that will need a warning, and two of them are "bleached flour" and "bromated flour."</a></strong></p>
<p>Both are in ordinary baking flour today. Neither has to be on the front of the box.</p>

<h2 id="whats-in-the-box">What is in a box of mix?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: flour, and flour is not one thing.</p>

<p>Saturday morning. The box comes down. Add water, stir, pour.</p>

<p>It is mostly flour. No surprise there.</p>

<p>Here is the surprise. Flour comes in kinds. The kinds are not the same.</p>

<p>Look at how a big mill sells flour to bakeries. <a href="https://www.generalmillsfoodservice.com/products/56573000-gold-medal-superlative-flour-bakers-enriched-bromated-bleached">Its own page calls it an "enriched bromated bleached option."</a></p>

<p>Three words there do work.</p>

<p><strong>Enriched</strong> means they put vitamins back in. That is good news. I will say so.</p>

<p><strong>Bleached</strong> means it was treated to go white fast.</p>

<p><strong>Bromated</strong> means a rising helper went in.</p>

<p>None of it is a secret. It is on the page I just linked.</p>

<p>It is not on the front of the box in your kitchen.</p>

<h2 id="why-so-little">Why does the front say so little?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: because the front is where the selling happens.</p>

<p>The front of a box is not a rule book. It is a poster.</p>

<p>Take the word healthy. You would think it is nailed down.</p>

<p>The FDA did change the rule. It came out on <a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-finalizes-updated-healthy-nutrient-content-claim">19 December 2024</a>.</p>

<p>Now read the FDA's own line on who has to follow it.</p>

<p><strong>"Manufacturers can voluntarily use the claim on a food package if the product meets the updated criteria."</strong></p>

<p>Nobody is made to put it on. That is what voluntary means.</p>

<p>And a box can say warmer things instead. Wholesome. Homestyle. Made with real buttermilk.</p>

<p>Those words have no test to pass.</p>

<p>Then there is the plain question. Is the box even right?</p>

<p>In January 2025 a pancake mix was pulled. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/quaker-issues-limited-recall-undeclared-milk-pearl-milling-company-original-pancake-waffle-mix">The FDA notice says the boxes "may contain undeclared milk."</a></p>

<p>Milk in the box. Not on the box.</p>

<p>If you cannot have milk, that matters a lot.</p>

<p>Now read how they found out. The notice says it came "after being alerted by a retail partner."</p>

<p>A shop told the maker. Not the other way round.</p>

<h2 id="what-texas-did">What did Texas just do?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it wrote a warning label, and set the clock for 2027.</p>

<p>This is the newest thing on it, and almost nobody has read it.</p>

<p>Texas Senate Bill 25 names 44 things. Bleached flour is number six. Bromated flour is number nine.</p>

<p>Here is the warning the law would put on the box, word for word.</p>

<p><strong>"WARNING: This product contains an ingredient that is not recommended for human consumption by the appropriate authority in Australia, Canada, the European Union, or the United Kingdom."</strong></p>

<p>Read what that does. It does not say America found harm.</p>

<p>It says four other countries did not want it.</p>

<p>The fine has teeth. Up to <strong>fifty thousand dollars a day</strong>, per product.</p>

<p>Now here is the line that decides how much of this you ever see.</p>

<p>The law "applies only to a food product label developed or copyrighted on or after January 1, 2027."</p>

<p>Only new labels. A box drawn before that date is not covered.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">The case against my own story</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: four things cut against me. Here are all four.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> Enriched flour is a real good. Vitamins go back in. That has helped people for eighty years.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The Texas warning does not say America found a problem. It says four other places said no. Not the same thing.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> The recall was small and it was handled. The notice says plainly: "No allergic reactions related to this matter have been reported to date."</p>

<p><strong>Four.</strong> The Texas law can switch itself off. If the FDA rules on one of the 44, the bill says the state rule "has no effect."</p>

<p>So this is not a story about poison in a pancake.</p>

<p>It is a story about a gap you cannot close from where you stand.</p>

<p>The good word on the front is optional. How the flour was made is not on the front at all. The one law that would change that starts with next year's boxes.</p>

<p>And when a box went wrong, a shop caught it.</p>

<h2 id="what-do-i-use">What do I keep in the house instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: pancakes from scratch take four minutes. I timed it.</p>

<p>This is the easiest swap in the house. Start here.</p>

<p>Flour, baking powder, salt, an egg, milk, a bit of melted butter. That is the mix.</p>

<p>That is close to what is in the box too, minus the shelf life.</p>

<p>I keep the dry part in a jar. Saturday I add the wet part and stir.</p>

<p>It is cheaper. A bag of flour makes far more pancakes than a box does.</p>

<p>And I am not sending you to compare boxes on the shelf.</p>

<p>Those two words were not on the box I could see. They were on a page written for bakeries.</p>

<p>So the only question left is who made it, and whether they would tell you before a state made them.</p>

<p>That is why my family buys through a private membership store instead. We are the manufacturer. No middleman, no markup. One name on it, and a person who picks up the phone.</p>

<p>Do the small thing first. Make one batch from scratch this weekend.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Is bromated flour banned in America?</strong> No. It is legal to sell and use. Texas put it on a list that needs a warning on new labels from 2027. That is a warning rule, not a ban.</p>

<p><strong>Does my bag of flour say bromated on it?</strong> Most bags of all purpose flour are not bromated. Bleached and unbleached are both sold. The bromated kind shows up most in flour sold to bakeries.</p>

<p><strong>Is a mix worse than making it from scratch?</strong> The main difference is what you can see. From scratch, you chose all six things. From a box, you chose the box.</p>

<p><strong>Does the word healthy on a box mean anything now?</strong> The FDA changed the rule in December 2024. Using the word is optional, so a box can just say something else.</p>
