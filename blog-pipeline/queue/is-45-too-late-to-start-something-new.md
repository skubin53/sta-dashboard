<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>No. And there is real research on it.</p>
<p>Four researchers went and looked up the age of the people who started America's fastest growing new companies. Not a survey. Actual government records.</p>
<p><strong><a href="https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faeri.20180582">Their finding: "The mean age at founding for the 1-in-1,000 fastest growing new ventures is 45.0."</a></strong></p>
<p>Mean age just means average age. The average was 45.</p>

<h2 id="who-checked">Who checked, and how?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: four researchers, using tax and Census records, not opinions.</p>

<p>You know the picture in your head. A business gets started by somebody in their twenties. Somebody in a hoodie. Somebody with time.</p>

<p>That picture came from the news. It did not come from the numbers.</p>

<p>In 2020 a study was published in a journal called <em>American Economic Review: Insights</em>. A journal is where research goes after other experts check it. The four authors were from MIT, Northwestern, the University of Pennsylvania, and the US Census Bureau.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2018/adrm/carra-wp-2018-03.html">They did not ask anybody anything.</a> They pulled the real records. Firms, workers, and owners. Then they looked up how old each founder was.</p>

<p><strong>Here is the line that matters.</strong> They wrote that "successful entrepreneurs are middle-aged, not young."</p>

<h2 id="what-about-50">What about 50? Or 55?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: 50 beat 30, by a lot.</p>

<p>The same team ran one more test. They compared founders by age.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.nber.org/papers/w24489">Their words: "Conditional on starting a firm, a 50-year-old founder is 1.8 times more likely to achieve upper-tail growth than a 30-year-old founder."</a></p>

<p>Let me say that in plain English.</p>

<p><strong>Upper-tail growth</strong> means the business took off. It grew fast. It ended up near the top.</p>

<p><strong>Conditional on starting a firm</strong> means they only counted people who actually started one. So this is not about who tries. It is about what happens after you try.</p>

<p><strong>1.8 times more likely</strong> means close to twice as likely.</p>

<p>So put those together. A woman of 50 who starts something is close to twice as likely to see it take off as a man or woman of 30.</p>

<p>Nobody told you that. It did not fit the story.</p>

<h2 id="what-you-actually-have">What do you actually have at 45 that you did not have at 25?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: four things, and the study says one of them is the big one.</p>

<h3>You know the work</h3>
<p>The study says it plainly. "Prior experience in the specific industry predicts much greater rates of entrepreneurial success." Prior experience just means you have done it before. At 25 you had none. Now you have twenty years of it.</p>

<h3>You know people</h3>
<p>Every job, every school run, every church, every group chat. That is a network. A network is just a list of people who would take your call.</p>

<h3>You can spot a bad deal</h3>
<p>You have been sold to for thirty years. You know the voice. You know when a promise is too smooth. That is not cynicism. That is a skill, and it is expensive to buy.</p>

<h3>You are not guessing about yourself</h3>
<p>At 25 you did not know what you were good at. You do now. You also know what drains you. Knowing both saves years.</p>

<h2 id="what-the-study-does-not-say">What does the study NOT say?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it does not say you will win. Read this part twice.</p>

<p>I am going to argue against my own headline now. You deserve that.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> That number 45 describes a very small group. One in a thousand. These are the fastest growing new firms in the country. That is not most businesses. It is not a side thing at the kitchen table either.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The study says age lines up with success. It does not say age causes it. Those are different. The thing doing the real work is probably the experience, not the birthday.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> Most new businesses do not last. <a href="https://www.bls.gov/bdm/entrepreneurship/entrepreneurship.htm">The US Bureau of Labor Statistics tracks new businesses year by year</a> and says it plainly: some of them will fail. That is the honest risk and I am not going to hide it.</p>

<p>So what is left after all that?</p>

<p>This. Your age is not the reason to say no. It never was. If you decide not to start something, decide it for a real reason.</p>

<h2 id="where-do-i-start">So where do I start?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: with the thing you already know, and with something small.</p>

<p>Do not go looking for a big new idea. The study points the other way.</p>

<p>Start with what you already know. Twenty years of doing something is the asset. Ask one question. What do people already ask me for?</p>

<p>Then make it small. Small is not weak. Small is how you find out if the thing works before it costs you anything you cannot spare.</p>

<p>I did not have a plan when I started. I had a house I wanted to clean up and a stubborn streak. That is honestly it.</p>

<p>The first thing I ever did was look at my own cupboards. So that is what I would tell you to do first. It costs nothing, it takes three minutes, and it tells you something true about your own house today.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Does this study cover women?</strong> It covers founders in the United States and does not carve out women on its own. The age finding is about age. What it does say is that experience in the field matters most, and a woman of 45 has plenty of that.</p>

<p><strong>What if I have never run anything?</strong> Then you are the normal case, not the odd one. The study points at industry experience, not at having been a boss. Doing the work for years is the thing it measures.</p>

<p><strong>Is 60 too late?</strong> The study measured a rise through the forties and fifties. It did not find that things fall off a cliff after that. It also did not follow a big group past 60, so nobody should claim more than the numbers show.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to quit my job?</strong> No. Nothing here says that and I would not say it either. Small and slow next to a paycheque is a normal way to begin.</p>
