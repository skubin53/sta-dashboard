<h2 id="two-offers">What did Centene and Microsoft offer in 2026?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: Centene let staff apply, with no promise. Microsoft set a number.</p>

<p>You read the email at your desk. You read it again in the parking lot with the engine off. Then you counted the years to Medicare on your fingers.</p>

<p>On June 15, 2026, <a href="https://news.bloombergtax.com/insurance/centene-offers-most-staff-buyouts-after-obamacare-member-losses">Bloomberg News said</a> Centene would offer buyouts to most of its 61,000 staff. Membership in its health plans had dropped.</p>

<p>Staff could apply, but a spokesperson said the offer "isn't guaranteed or automatic." And the story says layoffs "could follow if Centene doesn't meet its target through voluntary exits."</p>

<h2 id="voluntary">Why doesn't voluntary feel voluntary?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the word is true. The company still picked the date, and a layoff can sit behind it.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/qa-understanding-waivers-discrimination-claims-employee-severance-agreements">The EEOC's guide to severance agreements</a> calls this an "exit incentive program." It aims to "persuade them to voluntarily resign and sign a waiver," your promise not to sue.</p>

<p>Its teaching example is a bank that offers 50 tellers six months of pay to resign. Then "only 30 tellers voluntarily resign," and the bank "involuntarily lays off 10 tellers."</p>

<p>That bank is made up. It says nothing about Centene or your company. It does say why your stomach dropped, and so you ask about the buyout and the layoff.</p>

<h2 id="in-writing">What should you get in writing before you sign?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the pay, your coverage, your 401(k), what a no means and your real deadline. Each needs a name and a date.</p>

<p>Email each one, so the answer comes back in writing.</p>

<h3>What does the pay really come to?</h3>

<p>Ask the amount and the dates. <a href="https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525">The IRS says</a>, "You must include in income amounts you receive as severance pay." And so the number in the letter is not the number in your account.</p>

<h3>When does my health coverage end?</h3>

<p>Ask the day, and who pays after that. <a href="https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/an-employees-guide-health-benefits-under-cobra-2022.pdf">The Labor Department's guide</a> says COBRA runs 18 months after a job ends. You may pay up to 102 percent of the plan's cost. Losing coverage also opens <a href="https://www.healthcare.gov/glossary/special-enrollment-period/">a HealthCare.gov window</a>, usually 60 days before or after it ends.</p>

<h3>What happens to my pension or 401(k)?</h3>

<p>Ask what you keep, and whether your last day changes it. Leaving in the year you turn 55 or later? <a href="https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-exceptions-to-tax-on-early-distributions">The IRS</a> then drops the extra 10% tax on that job's 401(k). An IRA does not get that. <a href="https://theshannonnicole.com/post/laid-off-at-55-now-what">The rule of 55 is here.</a></p>

<h3>What happens if I say no?</h3>

<p>Is my job safe? If layoffs come later, is there a package? You may not get a promise. That is an answer too.</p>

<h3>Is that date to apply, or to sign?</h3>

<p>Are you 40 or over? Does the paper ask you not to sue over your age? Then the EEOC says a group program owes you "at least 45 days to consider the waiver." You get seven more days to take your signature back. The paper must also tell you in writing to see a lawyer. <a href="https://theshannonnicole.com/post/severance-at-52-what-next">Severance after you sign is here.</a></p>

<p>"If you are being rushed, ask for more time," the EEOC says. "Put your request in writing."</p>

<h2 id="the-bridge">How long is the bridge from a buyout to Medicare?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: at 56, nine years. COBRA usually covers 18 months of it.</p>

<p>Here is the math. <a href="https://www.medicare.gov/basics/get-started-with-medicare">Medicare is for people "65 or older,"</a> nine years out.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/section-404.409">The federal rule</a> lets you take Social Security, reduced, "at age 62." <a href="https://theshannonnicole.com/post/is-full-retirement-age-changing-in-november">Born on January 2, 1960 or later, your full age is 67.</a> That is six years to a first check and eleven to a full one.</p>

<p>Take COBRA's 18 months off the nine, and seven and a half are left.</p>

<p>Write your ages down a page, from today to 65. Beside each one, write what pays the bills and what covers your doctor. An empty line is your next question.</p>

<p>A buyout is a company deciding how big your job is. It can be a fair deal, and it is still their decision.</p>

<p>The bridge is yours. That is the part no memo can shrink. A new job, your savings, a spouse's plan, a HealthCare.gov plan. Those come first. Anything you start sits beside them.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly, and here is the part I will not dress up.</p>

<p>Centene is not a monster, and neither is Microsoft. Centene's spokesperson <a href="https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/centene-offering-staff-buyouts-navigates-murky-aca-waters">told Fierce Healthcare</a> the program was "to support employees who may be considering a transition." For you, it may be exactly that.</p>

<p>Now me. Switch to America is a private membership store that sells straight from the manufacturer. No middlemen and no markup. You pay what a brand new shopper pays, not a penny less.</p>

<p>What you build with me is not a paycheck, and it is not health insurance. If you need either one, go get it first.</p>

<p>I am not after your buyout money. And I will not hand you a number to expect. Nobody honest can.</p>

<h2 id="monday">What would I do first on Monday?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: send the questions, then write down the people.</p>

<h3>Send the questions</h3>

<p>Put the date on the fridge calendar. Email the questions to HR, and keep a copy off their laptop. Let a lawyer and a tax pro read the paper while the date is still far off.</p>

<h3>Write down the people, not the job title</h3>

<p>Every name that would still take your call. The one you trained, the client who asked for you by name. Those are your references.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else are women asking me about this?</h2>

<p><strong>Should I take the buyout or wait for a layoff?</strong></p>
<p>I cannot answer that without your paper. Ask in writing what a layoff package would pay. Then you are comparing two real things, not a deal and a rumor.</p>

<p><strong>Can I get unemployment if I take a buyout?</strong></p>
<p><a href="https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/unemployment-insurance">The Labor Department says</a> each state sets its own rules. You usually must be out of work through no fault of your own. Call your state office before you sign.</p>

<p><strong>Will starting something of my own cover my health insurance?</strong></p>
<p>No. Owning a business does not come with a health plan. Sort out your coverage first, then build.</p>

<p><strong>Am I overreacting to a voluntary offer?</strong></p>
<p>No. That email is about every year between you and Medicare. Six questions is not being difficult.</p>
