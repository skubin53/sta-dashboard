<h2 id="what-was-recalled">Which Clear Eyes drops were recalled in 2026?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: two kinds, 48 days apart. Maximum Itchy Eye Relief in July, then Redness Relief in September.</p>

<p>You want your eyes white and calm by lunch. Nothing wrong with wanting that. The problem is the bottle Prestige put on the shelf.</p>

<p><a href="https://api.fda.gov/drug/enforcement.json?search=recall_number:%22D-0766-2026%22">On July 29, 2026, Prestige recalled 39,060 bottles of Clear Eyes Maximum Itchy Eye Relief.</a> It was one lot, 2552A, a sterility recall over "potential contamination."</p>

<p>Then came September 15. <a href="https://api.fda.gov/drug/enforcement.json?search=recall_number:(%22D-0870-2026%22+%22D-0871-2026%22)&limit=2">The federal record lists 17 lots of Redness Relief, 2,356,248 bottles, sent all over the country.</a> Every lot code starts with RL25.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.cvs.com/content/dam/enterprise/cvsretail/help-assets/recall_pdf/Medtech_ClearEyesRednessRelief_318160_Lotsupdated.pdf">CVS told shoppers in writing they "should immediately discontinue use of it."</a> That is the little bottle in your purse.</p>

<h2 id="every-day">Can you use Clear Eyes Redness Relief every day?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: not by Prestige's own directions. Up to four times a day, and stop if symptoms last more than 72 hours.</p>

<p>Your heat runs all winter. Your drops were written for three days.</p>

<p><a href="https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a26ef66e-2def-4c98-9959-d40d4cfe3108">Prestige's drug facts for Redness Relief say "up to four times daily."</a> They say to stop if "symptoms last for more than 72 hours." And they warn that "overuse may produce increased redness of the eye."</p>

<p>And so, in its own words, too much of the drop that clears the red can bring more red.</p>

<h3 id="naphazoline">Naphazoline: the part that makes the red fade</h3>

<p>Naphazoline squeezes the tiny blood vessels in the white of your eye, so the red fades.</p>

<p><a href="https://doi.org/10.1001/archopht.1997.01100150036004">In 1997, eye doctors at Baylor College of Medicine wrote up 70 patients.</a> Drugstore redness drops had inflamed their eyes, and naphazoline was one of three drugs named. The typical patient had used the drops daily for three years. Once they stopped, the typical eye took four weeks to clear.</p>

<h2 id="pulled-before">Hasn't Clear Eyes been pulled before?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, in 2019 and 2023. After 2019, Prestige sued the plant that made it.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/altaire-pharmaceuticals-inc-issues-voluntary-recall-multiple-over-counter-ophthalmic-products-sold">In July 2019, Altaire Pharmaceuticals, a plant that made Clear Eyes for Prestige, recalled Redness Relief.</a> Its notice warned of "serious and potentially life threatening infections or death" from a drop that is not sterile.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/altaire-pharmaceuticals-inc-586153-03122020">A 2020 federal warning letter to that plant tells of a germ test plate logged as "0."</a> By the letter's account, it held "colonies too numerous to count."</p>

<p><a href="https://westfaironline.com/courts/prestige-brands-demands-1-9m-from-clear-eyes-maker/">In 2022, Prestige sued that plant for $1,878,313, what it says the recall cost.</a> It alleged the drops were not fit for their intended purpose.</p>

<p>Read that again. Prestige told a court its own Clear Eyes were not fit for use.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001295947/000129594726000042/pbh-20260630.htm">On August 6, 2026, Prestige told investors its sterile eye care plants had "undergone inspections by health authorities."</a> It said it was working to "strengthen quality systems." That was eight days into the July recall. Forty days later came the 2,356,248 bottle recall.</p>

<p>Three Clear Eyes recalls, from the federal record.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Isn't a Class II recall the mild kind?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: milder, yes, and Prestige says no germ was confirmed in July. It is still two sterility recalls in 48 days.</p>

<p>Maybe you are thinking, <em>I have used Clear Eyes for years and my eyes are fine.</em></p>

<p>That counts, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. <a href="https://www.healio.com/news/optometry/20260824/potential-contamination-prompts-recall-of-more-than-39000-bottles-of-eye-drops">For the July recall, a Prestige vice president told Healio contamination was not confirmed.</a> He said no serious harm had been reported.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.cbsnews.com/news/clear-eyes-eye-drops-recall-contamination/">Class II means harm that is "temporary or medically reversible," CBS News explained.</a> <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/s0161-6420(84)34140-9">In a 1984 study, 11 volunteers used naphazoline for ten days.</a> Stopping did not make their eyes redder.</p>

<p>So why would I still tell you to throw it out?</p>

<p>Because a drop goes straight onto your eye, with no skin in the way. Two sterility recalls in 48 days is enough for me.</p>

<h2 id="what-instead">What can you use when the heat dries your eyes?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a plain wetting drop in single use vials, and a warm washcloth at night.</p>

<p>Start with the air. Run a humidifier where you sit at night.</p>

<p>Before bed, lay a warm, wet washcloth over your closed eyes for five minutes.</p>

<p>Still want a drop? Pick a plain wetting drop with nothing to whiten the red. Buy single use vials. Each one is thrown out after one use. No bottle sits open in your purse for a month.</p>

<p>Will your eyes be bright white by lunch? I am not going to promise that. A plain drop just wets your eye.</p>
