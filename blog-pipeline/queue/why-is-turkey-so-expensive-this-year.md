<h2 id="who-is-butterball">What did Butterball agree to pay?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: $34 million, signed August 19, 2026, to settle a lawsuit brought by turkey buyers.</p>

<p>You stood at the meat case longer than you meant to, the bird cold in both hands, counting chairs.</p>

<p>You want a big, golden bird on the table for the people you love. Nothing wrong with wanting that. The problem is what the buyers who sued say Butterball did to the price.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.butterball.com/about-us/corporate-information">Butterball calls itself "the most recognized and loved" turkey brand in the United States.</a></p>

<p>On August 19, 2026, it signed a deal to pay $34,000,000. The buyers who sued are businesses that bought turkey straight from the big companies. The court still has to give its final approval.</p>

<p>It was the last company those buyers were still suing. Every other one had already settled with them.</p>

<p>So what do those buyers say Butterball did?</p>

<h2 id="what-accused">What do the buyers say Butterball did?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: they say it worked with other turkey companies to fix prices for seven years, from 2010 to 2016.</p>

<p>The deal spells out the claim. The buyers say Butterball joined a plan "to fix, raise, maintain, and stabilize the price of Turkeys."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/16610105/1864/in-re-turkey-antitrust-litigation/">In July, the court set a jury trial against Butterball for October 8, 2026.</a></p>

<p><a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/16610105/1913/in-re-turkey-antitrust-litigation/">On July 31, the court wrote that both sides "are strongly encouraged to settle their claims now."</a></p>

<p>Butterball signed less than three weeks later. <a href="https://www.turkeylitigation.com/assets/docs/Butterball/2026-08-21%20%281956%29%20Memo%20ISO%20DPP%27s%20Mot.%20for%20Prelim.%20Approval%20of%20Butterball%20Settlement.pdf">The buyers' lawyers told the court the deal came "less than two months before trial."</a></p>

<p>So the buyers' case never reached a jury. None of it explains this year's price. That is where bird flu comes in.</p>

<h2 id="bird-flu">Isn't it just the bird flu this year?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: bird flu is back, and USDA still expects about 6% more turkey this year than last.</p>

<p>You heard it was the bird flu.</p>

<p><a href="https://ers.usda.gov/publications/115633">USDA counted bird flu in 11 commercial turkey flocks in September, 544,900 birds.</a> August added 2 more flocks and 119,300 birds.</p>

<p>And yet USDA expects about 5.1 billion pounds of turkey this year. In 2025 it was about 4.8 billion.</p>

<p>The price went up anyway. USDA points to fall buying and calls it "ongoing demand strength."</p>

<p>Here it is on paper, next to the bird you put back.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Didn't Butterball deny all of it?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. It denies the claims in the deal itself, and no court has decided whether it did anything wrong.</p>

<p>I know what you are thinking. A company can settle just to make a lawsuit go away.</p>

<p>That is fair, and I will not pretend otherwise.</p>

<p><a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ilnd.371822/gov.uscourts.ilnd.371822.1865.0.pdf">In July, the judge threw out part of the buyers' case against Butterball.</a> A jury could still side with them on the rest, the judge wrote, but the evidence "is far from overwhelming."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.turkeylitigation.com/assets/docs/AgriStats/Exhibit%201%20-%20Revised%209-4-26%20Long%20Form%20Notice%20-%20Final%2009042026.pdf">The court's own notice says it "has not decided whether" Butterball "did anything wrong."</a> And this deal will not take a cent off your bird this year. It covers 2010 to 2016.</p>

<p>If you bought your turkey at a store, you are not in it. The money goes to businesses that bought straight from the companies. Their claims were due October 30, 2026.</p>

<p>It is not over, either. A second group of buyers was still suing in October. <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/16610105/1987/in-re-turkey-antitrust-litigation/">On October 19, 2026, the court ordered them and Butterball into "significant and earnest settlement negotiations."</a></p>

<p>Here is why I still would not hand them my holiday.</p>

<p>Butterball was the last one left, weeks from a jury. The court had told both sides, in writing, to weigh its view of the evidence. Butterball chose to pay $34 million instead of facing that jury. You do not need a jury to choose who sets the price of the bird on your table.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Why is turkey so expensive this year?</strong></p>
<p>USDA expects the wholesale price of a frozen hen turkey to average 175.5 cents a pound in 2026, up from 135.8 cents in 2025. Bird flu hit 13 commercial turkey flocks in August and September, but USDA still expects more turkey. It points to strong demand.</p>

<p><strong>Did Butterball admit it fixed turkey prices?</strong></p>
<p>No. It denies the claims, and the deal says it is not an admission. The court has not decided whether Butterball did anything wrong.</p>

<p><strong>Can I get money from the Butterball turkey settlement?</strong></p>
<p>Not for a turkey you bought at a store. The money goes to businesses that bought turkey straight from the companies from 2010 to 2016. Their claims were due October 30, 2026.</p>

<p><strong>What if there is no turkey farm near me?</strong></p>
