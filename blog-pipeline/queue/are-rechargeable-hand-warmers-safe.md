<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>Your fingers ache on the steering wheel by seven. So the little warmer rides in your coat pocket, charged the night before.</p>
<p><strong>If yours is a HotHands rechargeable, model H163650, stop using it today.</strong> HotHands' company, Kobayashi, recalled about 77,000 of them on October 1, 2026. <a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2027/Kobayashi-Recalls-Rechargeable-Hand-Warmers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-from-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards">The federal notice says the battery "can overheat and ignite."</a></p>
<p>No one has reported a burn from a HotHands. But its own notice says it uses the same battery as OCOOPA's warmers. <a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2026/OCOOPA-Direct-Recalls-1-5-Million-Rechargeable-Hand-Warmers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-or-Death-from-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards-One-Death-Reported-Imported-by-Shenzhen-Street-Cat-Technology">That recall counts 350 burns.</a></p>

<h2 id="what-hothands-recalled">What did HotHands just recall?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: its rechargeable hand warmer, model H163650. The trouble is sealed inside the case.</p>

<p>You charge it on the kitchen counter at night. In the morning you click it on and drop it in your pocket.</p>

<p>You stood by the back door longer than you meant to, the warmer in your fist, waiting for the heat.</p>

<p>It came two to a box with a charging cord, and it worked.</p>

<p>You wanted warm hands. No shame in that. The recall points at what HotHands put inside it.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2027/Kobayashi-Recalls-Rechargeable-Hand-Warmers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-from-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards">The US Consumer Product Safety Commission posted recall 27-011 on October 1, 2026.</a> It covers about 77,000 of these warmers. They sold in stores and on Amazon from July 2025 through August 2026, for $30 to $40.</p>

<p>The notice tells you to stop using it right away. Kobayashi owes you "a full refund."</p>

<h3 id="the-battery">The lithium-ion battery in your pocket</h3>

<p>A small battery sits sealed inside the plastic case. It makes the heat. <a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2027/Kobayashi-Recalls-Rechargeable-Hand-Warmers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-from-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards">The recall says it and the parts around it "can overheat and ignite."</a></p>

<p>And so the part on the recall is the part you hold in your bare hand. Or carry in a coat pocket, right against you.</p>

<h2 id="why-recall">Why recall it if no one got hurt?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: HotHands says its battery matches the one in another brand's warmers. That recall lists a death.</p>

<p>The HotHands recall page opens with "Product safety is our top priority." Then it gives its reason.</p>

<p><a href="https://hothands.com/recall-notice-rechargeable">It points to the OCOOPA recall first.</a> Then it says its own warmers "share the identical lithium-ion battery and electrical components."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2026/OCOOPA-Direct-Recalls-1-5-Million-Rechargeable-Hand-Warmers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-or-Death-from-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards-One-Death-Reported-Imported-by-Shenzhen-Street-Cat-Technology">OCOOPA's recall lists 350 burn injuries and the death of an 83-year-old in San José, California, in February 2026.</a></p>

<p>Those are OCOOPA's numbers. HotHands reports none.</p>

<p>Now look at the dates. OCOOPA's recall went up on July 30, 2026. <a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2027/Kobayashi-Recalls-Rechargeable-Hand-Warmers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-from-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards">HotHands warmers sold through August, the CPSC says.</a> Their recall came on October 1.</p>

<p>And so two months passed between the two recalls. By HotHands' own words, the same battery sat in both.</p>

<h2 id="check-yours">How do I check mine and get my money back?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: turn it over and look for H163650. The refund starts with a permanent marker.</p>

<p>Turn the warmer over. <a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2027/Kobayashi-Recalls-Rechargeable-Hand-Warmers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-from-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards">The model number, H163650, is printed on the bottom with the batch number.</a> The white HotHands logo sits on top.</p>

<p>The recalled ones are white with orange. They came two to a pack, and the pair snaps together with magnets.</p>

<p>Then the work starts. <a href="https://hothands.com/recall-notice-rechargeable">The HotHands recall page</a> gives you a claim number first. You write "Recalled" and that number on the warmer in permanent marker.</p>

<p>Then you send photos of the marked warmer, the model number and the four-digit batch number. Add a photo of your receipt if you still have it.</p>

<p>One click to buy it. A marker, a claim number and a set of photos to give it back.</p>

<p>Do not toss it in the kitchen trash, even when you are done. The page says to keep it until your claim is complete.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.epa.gov/recycle/used-lithium-ion-batteries">The EPA warns that a crushed battery in the trash is a fire hazard.</a> So it goes to your household hazardous waste center. Call first and ask if they take it.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for keeping yours?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: two fair points, and neither one is a reason to keep model H163650.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2027/Kobayashi-Recalls-Rechargeable-Hand-Warmers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-from-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards">The CPSC lists no injuries for the HotHands warmer.</a> HotHands calls the recall a step taken "out of an abundance of caution."</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The recall is narrow. <a href="https://hothands.com/recall-notice-rechargeable">HotHands says it "does not involve any HotHands Air-activated hand warmers."</a></p>

<p>Both are true.</p>

<p>So why would I still tell you to stop today?</p>

<p>Because HotHands wrote down its own reason. In its words, the battery is "identical" to OCOOPA's.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2026/OCOOPA-Direct-Recalls-1-5-Million-Rechargeable-Hand-Warmers-Due-to-Risk-of-Serious-Injury-or-Death-from-Fire-and-Burn-Hazards-One-Death-Reported-Imported-by-Shenzhen-Street-Cat-Technology">OCOOPA sold its warmers for close to eight years.</a> HotHands sold its warmer for about one.</p>

<p>And so a year with no reported burns only tells you the warmer is new. It is not a safety test.</p>

<p>You are far from the only one who trusted it. About 77,000 went out. If you gave a two-pack to your mom, check hers the same day.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Are HotHands rechargeable hand warmers safe to use right now?</strong></p>
<p>Not model H163650. The recall says to stop using it now and ask Kobayashi for a full refund. No one has reported a burn. But HotHands says its battery matches the one in OCOOPA's warmers.</p>

<p><strong>How do I know if my rechargeable hand warmer is recalled?</strong></p>
<p>Turn it over. HotHands model H163650 is printed on the bottom. If yours says OCOOPA on top, look on the bottom for UT3053, UT3056, ZLS-118, ZLS-118S, ZLS-118D, H01 or H01(PD). All seven are recalled. If it says Gobi Heat, look for UT3053.</p>

<p><strong>Are the air-activated HotHands packs recalled too?</strong></p>
<p>No. HotHands says this recall does not involve them. That tells you what was recalled, and nothing more. For cold hands this winter, plain wool gloves have nothing inside to recall.</p>

<p><strong>Can I just throw my old one in the trash?</strong></p>
<p>No. The EPA warns that a crushed battery in the trash is a fire hazard. HotHands says to keep it until your claim is done. Then call your household hazardous waste center and ask if you can drop it off.</p>
