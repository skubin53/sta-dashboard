<h2 id="what-did-the-court-decide">What did the Supreme Court decide about Roundup?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a state jury cannot make Monsanto add a cancer warning.</p>

<p>You stand on the driveway in flip flops, wand in hand. The breeze pushes the mist back onto your ankles.</p>

<p>That is how everybody's mother did it. The jug never said otherwise.</p>

<p>In 2019 a Missouri man named John Durnell sued Monsanto. He said he used Roundup for about 20 years. He said it gave him non-Hodgkin lymphoma. <a href="https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1068_n7ip.pdf">A jury agreed and awarded him $1.25 million, the dissenting justices wrote.</a></p>

<p>Bayer bought Monsanto in 2018. It took the case all the way to the Supreme Court. The court reversed the verdict.</p>

<p>The reason was simple. The EPA approved the label, and so a state jury cannot ask for another one.</p>

<p>Federal law, the court said, makes Monsanto sell Roundup with the label the EPA approved. That label has no cancer warning.</p>

<p>The two justices who disagreed wrote the plain fact down. Roundup's label "did not (and still does not)" warn about cancer.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.npr.org/2026/06/25/g-s1-120233/supreme-court-monsanto-roundup-decision">Bayer's chief executive, Bill Anderson, said in a statement that the ruling "brings overdue justice," NPR reported.</a> So who is left to warn you?</p>

<h2 id="is-my-jug-the-same">Is the jug in my garage the same Roundup sold today?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: maybe not, and Bayer's chief executive at the time said why in 2021.</p>

<h3>Glyphosate, the ingredient Bayer took out of the home jug</h3>

<p>For decades the weed killer inside Roundup was glyphosate.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.iarc.who.int/featured-news/media-centre-iarc-news-glyphosate/">In March 2015 the World Health Organization's cancer agency classed glyphosate as "probably carcinogenic to humans."</a> That means it probably causes cancer in people.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.agweb.com/news/crops/crop-production/bayer-pull-glyphosate-us-lawn-and-garden-markets">In July 2021 Bayer said it would pull glyphosate from home Roundup, as early as January 2023.</a> Its chief executive at the time, Werner Baumann, said over 90% of the claims came from that home market.</p>

<p>He also said why. It was "exclusively geared at managing litigation risk and not because of any safety concerns."</p>

<p>Read that again. Not safety. Lawsuits.</p>

<p>Farms still get glyphosate. And a jug on your shelf from before 2023 may be the old formula. That is the one the lawsuits were about.</p>

<h2 id="why-did-bayer-fight">Why did Bayer fight so hard over one label?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: tens of thousands of cancer claims and a $16 billion bill.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/georgia-law-roundup-maker-bayer-lawsuits">By May 2025, more than 67,000 of 181,000 Roundup claims were still open, the AP reported.</a> Bayer had set aside $16 billion to settle cases.</p>

<p><a href="https://thedailyrecord.com/2026/10/30/bayer-wins-st-louis-trial-roundup-cancer-claims/">By October 2026, Bayer faced around 65,000 claims, and a proposed $7.25 billion settlement was under review by a judge in St. Louis, Reuters reported.</a></p>

<p>In March 2025 a Georgia jury awarded nearly $2.1 billion to a man. He alleged Roundup caused his cancer. Bayer insists glyphosate is safe.</p>

<p>Bayer did not wait on the courts. The AP says it joined farm groups to push for laws in the states and in Congress. It ran billboards, TV and radio ads too.</p>

<p>North Dakota passed the first law shielding pesticide companies that follow the federal label. Georgia passed the second, starting January 1, 2026. The AP says it will not affect cases already filed.</p>

<p>It is written to protect any pesticide company that follows the federal label, the AP reported. Not just Bayer.</p>

<p>And so the label was never a warning. Now it is a shield.</p>

<h2 id="best-case-for-roundup">What is the best case for Roundup?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: three fair points, and I am going to print all three.</p>

<p>Here is what you might be thinking. Your mother sprayed the fence line for years and she is fine. That is fair.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> <a href="https://www.epa.gov/ingredients-used-pesticide-products/glyphosate">In 2017 the EPA said glyphosate is "not likely to be carcinogenic to humans."</a> That is what the EPA says. I am printing it so you have it.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> By 2025, home Roundup no longer used glyphosate, the AP reported.</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> The Supreme Court never said Roundup causes cancer. Those claims are allegations, and Bayer disputes them.</p>

<p>And tossing the jug is not about the springs behind you. It is about this one.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me? That same EPA page says a federal court threw out the health part of its glyphosate decision. That was June 2022. And Bayer changed the home formula over lawsuits, in its own words.</p>

<h2 id="what-do-i-use-instead">What do I use on the driveway instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a kettle, a small hand tool, and the old jug gone.</p>

<p>You want clean cracks and a tidy fence line. Nothing wrong with that. The problem is what Bayer used to get you there.</p>

<p>So start with the jug. Get rid of it. <a href="https://www.epa.gov/safepestcontrol/safe-disposal-pesticides">The EPA says never pour pesticides down the sink, toilet, sewer or street drain.</a> Take it, cap on, to your county's hazardous waste drop-off.</p>

<p>Then the cracks. Pour boiling water from the kettle slowly into each one. Use a small wooden tool after a rain, when the roots slide out whole.</p>

<p>It is slower than a spray. It is also you, in the sun, with nothing drifting onto your ankles.</p>

<p>The rest of the house runs on the same question. Who made it, and would they tell you?</p>

<p>That is why my family buys our cleaners through a private membership store. We are the manufacturer. No middlemen, no markup. One name on the bottle, and a person who picks up the phone.</p>

<p>Do the small thing first. Tonight, carry the jug from the garage to the trunk of your car.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Does Roundup still have glyphosate in it?</strong></p>
<p>The home lawn and garden Roundup did not as of 2025, the AP reported. Bayer started the switch as early as 2023. Farm Roundup still uses it, and an older jug in your garage may too.</p>

<p><strong>Did the Supreme Court say Roundup causes cancer?</strong></p>
<p>No. It ruled only on the label. It said a state jury cannot demand a cancer warning the EPA did not require.</p>

<p><strong>How do I get rid of an old jug of Roundup?</strong></p>
<p>Take it to your county's household hazardous waste drop-off with the cap on. Never pour it down the sink, toilet or street drain.</p>

<p><strong>What can I use on driveway weeds instead?</strong></p>
<p>Boiling water poured slowly into the crack, and a small hand tool after a rain. It is slower than a spray, and nothing drifts back onto you.</p>
