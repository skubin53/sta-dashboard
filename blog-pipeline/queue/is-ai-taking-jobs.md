<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>Some, yes. And nobody asks you first.</p>
<p>This year AI was the top reason US companies gave for cutting jobs.</p>
<p>Through September, employers announced 573,195 job cuts. AI was named in 120,136 of them. That is about one in five, by <a href="https://web.archive.org/web/20261002191842/https://www.challengergray.com/blog/job-cuts-fall-in-september-hiring-plans-up-3-over-2025-on-weak-early-seasonal-hiring/">the October 1 count from Challenger, Gray &amp; Christmas</a>.</p>
<p>And Amazon put it in writing. In June 2025, its CEO told staff he expected AI to <strong>"reduce our total corporate workforce."</strong></p>
<p>A memo can move your job. It cannot move the people who trust you.</p>

<h2 id="the-numbers">How many jobs did AI take this year?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: companies named AI in 120,136 cuts through September. In September alone, it came fifth.</p>

<p>You read it on your phone at the kitchen counter, then read it again. Another company, another round, and the word AI in the second line.</p>

<p>Challenger, Gray &amp; Christmas counts the layoff plans US employers announce. So far this year, AI leads its list of reasons.</p>

<p>Then September got quieter. Employers announced 43,281 cuts, the fewest for a September since 2022. AI came fifth, with 3,961.</p>

<p>The government count was quiet too. In September, employers added 29,000 jobs and unemployment sat at 4.2%. <a href="https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/empsit_10022026.htm">The Bureau of Labor Statistics</a> said both "changed little."</p>

<p>And jobs like yours? In March, <a href="https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/new-ilo-data-confirm-women-face-higher-workplace-risks-generative-ai-men">the International Labour Organization</a> looked at jobs held mostly by women. In the countries it studied, they are almost twice as likely as men's jobs to be exposed to AI. It named secretaries, receptionists, payroll clerks and accounting assistants.</p>

<p>Exposed means AI can do part of the work. It does not mean the job is gone.</p>

<p>The new tool your boss keeps bringing up, the training video you watched twice, the reorg email at five on a Friday, the young hire who asks you how the old system worked, that feeling that the job is learning from you so it can do without you.</p>

<h2 id="amazon">What did Amazon tell its own staff about AI?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: that it expected AI to shrink its office staff. Then came two rounds of cuts, about 30,000 roles.</p>

<p>You want a job that values what you know. Nothing wrong with wanting that. The trouble is what one memo can do to it.</p>

<p>In June 2025, Amazon's CEO, Andy Jassy, wrote to his staff about AI. <a href="https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-ceo-andy-jassy-on-generative-ai">"We will need fewer people doing some of the jobs that are being done today,"</a> he wrote.</p>

<p>He expected it to "reduce our total corporate workforce" in the next few years.</p>

<p>In October 2025, <a href="https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-workforce-reduction">Amazon announced about 14,000 corporate roles cut</a>. That memo asked the question for you: "Some may ask why we're reducing roles when the company is performing well."</p>

<p>In January 2026, <a href="https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-layoffs-corporate-jan-2026">about 16,000 more</a>. That memo asked if cuts every few months were the new rhythm. Its answer: "That's not our plan."</p>

<p>On August 31, KOMO News <a href="https://komonews.com/news/local/amazon-to-permanently-lay-off-121-workers-in-seattle-bellevue-amid-ongoing-ai-push-artificial-intelligence-agi-employment-jobs-unemployed-models-reorganization-seattle-washington-jeff-bezos-customer-shopping-prime-economy">reported a Washington state filing</a> for 121 more Amazon layoffs in Seattle, Bellevue and Sumner. Permanent, starting October 1. The notice did not say which departments.</p>

<p>You do not get a vote on a memo. And the reason inside it can change.</p>

<h2 id="the-reason">Is AI really why the jobs are going?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: sometimes. Two days after announcing 14,000 cuts, Amazon's CEO said they were "not even really AI driven."</p>

<p>You may already have a doubt, and it is fair. Maybe the headlines are bigger than the change.</p>

<p>On October 30, 2025, Jassy was asked about the cuts on Amazon's earnings call. <a href="https://www.cnn.com/2025/10/30/tech/amazon-layoffs-andy-jassy-ai-culture">He told them</a> it was "not really financially driven, and it's not even really AI driven, not right now. It's culture."</p>

<p>So the June memo said AI. The October answer said culture. Either way, the cuts went ahead.</p>

<p>And some companies are walking it back. In <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/01/employers-who-laid-off-workers-for-ai-are-reversing-their-decisions.html">Robert Half data sent to CNBC</a>, 32% of US hiring managers said they cut a role mainly because of AI. Later, they hired for the same job, or one like it.</p>

<p>The job they cut was the job they had to fill again. What you know still counts.</p>

<p>So you close the laptop and look out the window for a minute. Not to quit. To work out what is yours.</p>
