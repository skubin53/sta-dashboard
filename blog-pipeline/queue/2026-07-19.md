<p>Here is the short version, with the receipts. On July 13, 2026, Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier <a href="https://www.myfloridalegal.com/newsrelease/attorney-general-james-uthmeier-launches-investigation-potassium-bromate-floridas-food">announced his office had launched an investigation</a> into potassium bromate in Florida's food supply, issuing a civil investigative subpoena to General Mills as <a href="https://www.news4jax.com/news/local/2026/07/13/florida-ag-uthmeier-subpoenas-general-mills-over-potassium-bromate-in-flour/">reported by News4JAX</a>. The subpoena is part of an inquiry under the Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act into the supply chain of products containing the flour additive, <a href="https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/07/15/florida-bromate-probe-puts-general-mills-flour-under-scrutiny/">according to BakeryandSnacks</a>. This is an investigation and a request for information, not a filed lawsuit, and no wrongdoing has been proven. As the Attorney General himself put it, "Nobody is getting sued today, yet."</p>

<p>I want to walk you through this gently and factually, because the headlines can feel scary, and you deserve the calm version with the sources so you can decide for yourself.</p>

<h2>What actually happened?</h2>

<p class="qa"><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Florida's Attorney General opened an investigation and sent General Mills a civil subpoena over potassium bromate in certain Pillsbury and Gold Medal bakery flours. It is an inquiry and a request for records, not a lawsuit, and no wrongdoing has been proven.</p>

<p>The Attorney General's office issued a civil investigative subpoena to General Mills Inc. and General Mills Operations LLC, <a href="https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/07/15/florida-bromate-probe-puts-general-mills-flour-under-scrutiny/">per BakeryandSnacks reporting</a>. The subpoena named four specific bromated flour products by brand, all sold in 50-pound bulk sacks: Pillsbury Potentate High Gluten Flour, Pillsbury Best Bakers Patent Flour, Gold Medal All Aces Bakery Flour, and Gold Medal Superlative Bakers Flour. Pillsbury and Gold Medal are both General Mills brands, <a href="https://www.news4jax.com/news/local/2026/07/13/florida-ag-uthmeier-subpoenas-general-mills-over-potassium-bromate-in-flour/">as News4JAX noted</a>. The company itself was the party subpoenaed, not the brands separately.</p>

<p>Attorney General Uthmeier framed the effort around transparency for families. "Floridians have a right to know what is in the food they buy and feed their children," <a href="https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/07/15/florida-bromate-probe-puts-general-mills-flour-under-scrutiny/">he said, as reported by BakeryandSnacks</a>. He added, "This investigation is about protecting Florida families and providing transparency to our consumers," <a href="https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/07/15/florida-bromate-probe-puts-general-mills-flour-under-scrutiny/">in the same remarks</a>.</p>

<p>He was also careful about where things stand. "Nobody is getting sued today, yet. We want to learn more information," <a href="https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/07/15/florida-bromate-probe-puts-general-mills-flour-under-scrutiny/">he said</a>. On the fact that the additive is federally allowed, his office noted that FDA approval "doesn't necessarily make it right," <a href="https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/07/15/florida-bromate-probe-puts-general-mills-flour-under-scrutiny/">also per BakeryandSnacks</a>. In his press remarks, Uthmeier described potassium bromate as genotoxic and said it can cause oxidative stress and DNA damage, citing links to kidney, thyroid, and abdominal cancer, <a href="https://www.wfla.com/news/florida/florida-attorney-general-announces-subpoenas-against-general-mills-other-companies-over-flour-ingredients/">as covered by WFLA in Tampa</a>. Those are the Attorney General's characterizations, not settled findings.</p>

<h2>What is potassium bromate, and why the concern?</h2>

<p class="qa"><strong>Quick answer:</strong> It is a dough conditioner that IARC calls "possibly carcinogenic to humans" and California lists under Prop 65, yet the FDA still permits it in the United States while many other countries do not.</p>

<p>Potassium bromate is a dough conditioner. Bakers have long used it to strengthen dough and help bread rise higher. The concern comes from how different authorities classify it.</p>

<p>The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), part of the World Health Organization, classifies potassium bromate as Group 2B, "possibly carcinogenic to humans," <a href="https://www.inchem.org/documents/iarc/vol73/73-17.html">in its Monographs Volume 73 (1999)</a>. It is important to read that classification precisely: IARC based it on sufficient evidence in experimental animals and inadequate evidence in humans. In plain terms, the animal data raised a flag, and the human data was not conclusive.</p>

<p>In California, potassium bromate has been listed under Proposition 65 as a chemical known to the state to cause cancer since January 1, 1990, <a href="https://oehha.ca.gov/proposition-65/chemicals/potassium-bromate">according to the California Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment</a>, with a consumer-facing summary on the <a href="https://www.p65warnings.ca.gov/chemicals/potassium-bromate">official Prop 65 warnings site</a>.</p>

<p>Here is the part that surprises a lot of moms: it is still legal in US food. The FDA continues to permit potassium bromate, codified for the malting of barley at <a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-172/subpart-H/section-172.730">21 CFR 172.730</a>, and allowed as an optional dough-conditioning ingredient in standardized bakery products such as bread, up to 75 ppm by weight of flour, under <a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-136/section-136.110">21 CFR 136.110</a>.</p>

<p>Meanwhile, many other places restricted it long ago. Potassium bromate has been banned for food use in the <a href="https://www.food-safety.com/articles/8939-california-food-safety-act-signed-into-law-officially-banning-four-toxic-additives-by-2027">European Union since 1990</a>, and it is not permitted in food in the United Kingdom, Canada, Brazil, and China, among other countries, even as it remains legal in the US, a broader picture also reflected in the <a href="https://www.inchem.org/documents/iarc/vol73/73-17.html">IARC record</a>. And in California, <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB418">Assembly Bill 418, the California Food Safety Act</a>, will bar the sale of food containing potassium bromate in the state effective January 1, 2027.</p>

<h2>Is it in my food, and how can I tell?</h2>

<p class="qa"><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Read the ingredient label. If it says "bromated flour" or lists potassium bromate by name, it is in there. Flour marked "unbromated" does not contain it.</p>

<p>The most reliable way to know is to read the ingredient label. Potassium bromate, when present, is listed by name, and flour treated with it is often labeled "bromated flour" or "enriched bromated flour." If you see either phrase, that product contains it. Unbromated flour will usually say "unbromated" or simply will not list potassium bromate at all.</p>

<p>Because the FDA permits it in the US, the presence of potassium bromate is not itself a recall or a violation. It is a labeled, legal ingredient. The Florida investigation is about transparency and the supply chain, <a href="https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/07/15/florida-bromate-probe-puts-general-mills-flour-under-scrutiny/">as BakeryandSnacks described</a>, not a finding that any specific bag on a shelf is unsafe.</p>

<h2>The bigger pattern</h2>

<p>This flour story is one of several recent food-safety actions worth knowing about. Here are a few, each with a source.</p>

<p>Beginning in April 2026, California Dairies Inc. recalled bulk powdered milk and buttermilk over potential Salmonella contamination, an event that grew to affect dozens of downstream products sold at major retailers, <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/major-product-recalls/2026-recalls-food-products-associated-powdered-milk-california-dairies-inc-due-potential-salmonella">as tracked on the FDA's Major Product Recalls page</a>.</p>

<p>In February 2026, Ajinomoto Foods North America recalled frozen fried rice products, including items sold at Trader Joe's, over possible glass fragments. The recall began with about 3.3 million pounds and was later expanded to roughly 37 million pounds, <a href="https://www.fsis.usda.gov/recalls-alerts/ajinomoto-foods-north-america-inc--recalls-chicken-fried-rice-products-due-0">according to the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service notice</a>, and was <a href="https://www.consumerreports.org/health/food-recalls/fried-rice-ramen-dumpling-recall-glass-trader-joes-ajinomoto-a1204133892/">reported by Consumer Reports</a>.</p>

<p>The same pattern shows up in the jar of food you spoon into a baby's mouth. In February 2021, a <a href="https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/oversight-subcommittee-staff-report-reveals-top-baby-foods-contain-dangerous">U.S. House oversight subcommittee reported that top baby foods are tainted with dangerous levels of four toxic heavy metals</a>: inorganic arsenic, lead, cadmium, and mercury. A <a href="https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/imo/media/doc/ECP%20Second%20Baby%20Food%20Report%209.29.21%20FINAL.pdf">follow-up report in September 2021</a> found dangerous levels in even more products, including brands that had first withheld their own test data. This matters because the <a href="https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/all-around/Pages/Blood-Lead-Levels-in-Children-What-Parents-Need-to-Know.aspx">American Academy of Pediatrics says there is no known safe blood lead level in children</a>, and the FDA only <a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-issues-final-guidance-industry-action-levels-lead-processed-food-intended-babies-and-young">finalized action levels for lead in processed baby food in January 2025</a>.</p>
<p>Parents have taken this to court. As of July 1, 2026, <a href="https://www.jpml.uscourts.gov/sites/jpml/files/Pending_MDL_Dockets_By_Actions_Pending-July-1-2026.pdf">473 cases were pending in a federal baby-food lawsuit</a> consolidated in the Northern District of California. Those families allege that heavy metals in these products caused autism, ADHD, or other neurodevelopmental harm. That causation claim is an allegation that has not been proven. In fact, on February 27, 2026, the judge overseeing the case <a href="https://www.aboutlawsuits.com/court-excludes-expert-witnesses-metal-baby-foods-autism-adhd/">excluded the plaintiffs' general-causation experts</a>, finding they had not reliably shown that baby food causes autism or ADHD. There has been no trial, no jury verdict, and no settlement. The manufacturers deny their products caused any injury. What is documented, and not in dispute, is that Congress found the metals in the food and that pediatricians say there is no safe level of lead for a child.</p>

<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>

<h3>Is General Mills being sued over potassium bromate?</h3>
<p>No. As of July 2026 this is a civil investigative subpoena and an investigation, not a filed lawsuit. Attorney General Uthmeier said, "Nobody is getting sued today, yet," <a href="https://www.bakeryandsnacks.com/Article/2026/07/15/florida-bromate-probe-puts-general-mills-flour-under-scrutiny/">according to BakeryandSnacks</a>. No wrongdoing has been established.</p>

<h3>Does potassium bromate definitely cause cancer?</h3>
<p>Not established in humans. IARC classifies it as Group 2B, "possibly carcinogenic to humans," based on sufficient evidence in animals and inadequate evidence in humans, <a href="https://www.inchem.org/documents/iarc/vol73/73-17.html">per its 1999 monograph</a>. California lists it under <a href="https://oehha.ca.gov/proposition-65/chemicals/potassium-bromate">Prop 65</a>.</p>

<h3>If it is banned elsewhere, why is it legal here?</h3>
<p>The US FDA still permits it, codified at <a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-172/subpart-H/section-172.730">21 CFR 172.730</a> and <a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-136/section-136.110">21 CFR 136.110</a>, even though the EU, UK, Canada, Brazil, and China restrict it. California's <a href="https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240AB418">AB 418</a> bars its sale in the state starting January 1, 2027.</p>

<h3>How do I know if my flour has it?</h3>
<p>Check the label. Products made with it are often marked "bromated flour," and potassium bromate is listed by name in the ingredients when present.</p>
