<h2 id="what-changed">What actually changed in your affiliate pay?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the pay got smaller and the window got shorter.</p>

<p>You post. She clicks. A cookie lands on her phone. If that turns into an order, you get paid once. Then it is gone.</p>

<p>That was already thin. Then this year happened.</p>

<p><a href="https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/compare">Amazon narrowed onsite pay to the exact item clicked.</a> Not the rest of her cart. Not the mixer she added after your kettle post.</p>

<h2 id="already-done">Why does the cut land on work you already did?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: you agreed they could change it whenever they like.</p>

<p>One line does most of the damage. It sits in the Amazon Associates agreement.</p>

<p><a href="https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/operating/agreement">"We reserve the right to modify any of the terms and conditions contained in this Agreement at any time and in our sole discretion."</a></p>

<p>Then read the next bit. If you do not like the change, your one move is to quit.</p>

<p>That is not a partnership. That is a notice board.</p>

<p>TikTok is plainer about it. A rate drop gets you two days of warning. Your old rate holds for thirty days. Then it does not.</p>

<p>And so the video you filmed in March gets paid on June's rules. Your March is gone. It cannot be re-earned.</p>

<p>You did the hard half. You built the trust. They kept the customer.</p>

<h2 id="cookie-or-customer">What is the difference between a cookie and a customer?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: one pays you once. One pays you every time she runs out.</p>

<h3>The click</h3>

<p>A cookie is a few days long. Then you are back at zero. You go and find another stranger, and you do it again tomorrow.</p>

<h3>The list</h3>

<p>Ask where your customer lives. Not her house. Her record. If it sits on a system you cannot log into, you are renting her.</p>

<h3>The reorder</h3>

<p>This is the one I care about. She runs out of soap. She orders more. You stay credited. You are the one who brought her here. You did the work once and it keeps counting.</p>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly, and here is the part I will not dress up.</p>

<p>Getting paid on reorders is not a magic word. Companies built on reorders fall over too.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.directsellingnews.com/2025/04/11/modere-announces-closure-after-23-years/">Modere closed in April 2025 after twenty three years.</a> Twenty three years is not a startup. So the model on its own guarantees you nothing.</p>

<p>What matters is whether real women reorder because they actually want the stuff. If they do not, the pretty word residual means nothing.</p>

<p>Here is what I can tell you about here. We are the maker. No middleman and no markup. There is no monthly requirement, and nothing to hit to stay active.</p>

<p>You pay what a brand new customer pays. Not a penny less. I do not have a special rate and I would not offer you one. Anybody who discounts you on day one is buying you, not building with you.</p>

<p>And there is no list of ten people you know. I do not teach that and I do not have one.</p>

<p>I will not hand you a number to expect either. Nobody honest can.</p>

<h2 id="monday">What would I do first on Monday?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: count what you own, then write down the people.</p>

<h3>Count what you actually own</h3>

<p>Open your dashboard. Write down what pays you next month if you post nothing at all. For most link work that number is zero. Put it on paper. It stops living in your stomach and starts living somewhere you can look at it.</p>

<h3>Write down the people, not the links</h3>

<p>Do not skip this. It is the only thing you still own outright. Every name you can remember. The one who asks you what shampoo. Her sister. The one from the school run.</p>

<p>Nobody switches those women off from an office in another state. They are yours. They always were.</p>

<h3>Ask five questions of whatever comes next</h3>

<p>Including of me. Especially of me.</p>

<p>What does it cost to start, all in. Is there a monthly requirement of any kind. What happens to my customers if I stop. Where is the income disclosure and can I read it. Who makes money when I join, and how.</p>

<p>Take those five to every offer put in front of you. If somebody dodges one, that is your answer. You just saved yourself a year.</p>

<h2 id="faq">Questions women are asking me about this</h2>

<p><strong>Did Amazon warn anyone?</strong></p>
<p>It published the changes on its updates page. That page is public and anyone can read it. No email landed in your inbox to say your pay was about to shrink. Nothing in the deal says one had to.</p>

<p><strong>Can I keep my affiliate links?</strong></p>
<p>Yes. Nobody is asking you to delete a thing. Keep posting what you post. This sits beside that work, not on top of it.</p>

<p><strong>What if my audience is small?</strong></p>
<p>A cookie needs a crowd, because it pays once. A reorder does not. Thirty women who buy their own soap every month beat thirty thousand who scroll past.</p>

<p><strong>Do I have to keep stock in my garage?</strong></p>
<p>No. She orders, it ships to her door. Nothing goes through you and there is no starter pack to buy up front.</p>

<p><strong>What if I never want to sell anything again?</strong></p>
<p>Then don't. That is a fair answer. Keep being the one they text about what is safe to use. Let that be enough.</p>
