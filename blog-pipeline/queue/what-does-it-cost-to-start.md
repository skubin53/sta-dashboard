<h2 id="what-lularoe-charges">What does LuLaRoe charge to start?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: $499 on its own page, and the same page is already selling you more.</p>

<p>You have stood in a friend's spare room between two racks of leggings. You bought a pair you did not need, because she needed the sale.</p>

<p>So I read <a href="https://join.lularoe.com/onboarding-packages">LuLaRoe's own page</a> to see what it costs to start now. It sells a "Retailer Business Starter Pack." "Priced at only $499, it includes 33 pieces of clothing."</p>

<p>Then it says, "The more you stock, the more you save." The add-on packs are offered "for 3 days after your initial order."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/ag-ferguson-sues-lularoe-over-pyramid-scheme">Washington sued LuLaRoe in 2019.</a> The state said the starting fee ran "$2,000 to $9,000."</p>

<p>And the state says you did not pick what came in the box. <a href="https://agportal-s3bucket.s3.amazonaws.com/uploadedfiles/Another/News/Press_Releases/2019_01_23Complaint_Stamped.pdf">The state's complaint alleges</a> the stock came in 33-piece "pods." It says sellers could not choose the size or the print.</p>

<p>In 2021 <a href="https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/lularoe-pay-475-million-resolve-ag-ferguson-s-lawsuit-over-pyramid-scheme">the state put the packs at</a> $500 to $5,000. Today the floor is $499. You still pay for 33 pieces before you sell one.</p>

<h2 id="what-else-it-costs">What else does it cost that nobody puts on the first page?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: racks, bins, a label printer and even a trailer, on a list a court made LuLaRoe publish.</p>

<p>Before the first box lands, you clear a shelf for it. Then a closet. Then the spare room.</p>

<img src="https://scan.ismyhometoxic.com/blog-images/whatdoesitc-villain-v1.webp" alt="A hall closet at night, every shelf full of ordinary household things except one, wiped bare and empty with a folded cloth left on its edge" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;height:auto;border-radius:10px;">

<p>In 2021 LuLaRoe settled with Washington for $4.75 million. <a href="https://agportal-s3bucket.s3.amazonaws.com/uploadedfiles/Another/News/Press_Releases/2021_02_01ConsentDecreeSigned.pdf">The court order</a> told it to publish a plain statement for sellers, in a set form. That form lists the other costs of starting.</p>

<p>Racks and hangers. Storage bins and mannequins. A printer for shipping labels. And right there on the list, a "vehicle/trailer to transport merchandise."</p>

<p>The same form lists the costs that keep coming, down to gas to "deliver orders."</p>

<p><a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/multi-level-marketing-businesses-and-pyramid-schemes">The FTC puts it in one line</a>. "Even if the start-up costs seem low, additional expenses can add up quickly."</p>

<p>Picture it. The racks in the spare room, the bins in the hall, the printer on the kitchen counter, the back seat full of boxes. And that feeling that the stock owns your house instead of the other way around.</p>

<p>And so the $499 is just the first check you write.</p>

<p>Getting out cost something too. <a href="https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/ag-ferguson-sues-lularoe-over-pyramid-scheme">The state says</a> LuLaRoe raised its refund to 100 percent in April 2017. Five months later, by the state's account, it went back to 90 percent "without notice."</p>

<p>The state quoted a seller who wrote while her returns were still on the road. "I have $16,740 in inventory in a truck on the way to their warehouse."</p>

<p>Close the laptop on any offer that shows you the pack and hides the trailer.</p>

<h2 id="is-this-a-pitch">Isn't this just another pitch?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: partly. So here is the part that cuts against me.</p>

<p>I know what you are thinking. LuLaRoe fixed some of this, so why keep pointing at it?</p>

<p>It did fix some. Under the 2021 order, new Washington sellers who quit within 45 days could return all their first stock. Full refund, shipping paid. And it now puts its price up front.</p>

<p>LuLaRoe also denied "any and all wrongdoing of any kind whatsoever," the order records.</p>

<p>My side has limits too. A company built on reorders can close. Any of them can. And I will not hand you a number to expect, because nobody honest can.</p>

<p>Washington used a simple test, and I want you to use it on me. Where does the money come from? <a href="https://www.atg.wa.gov/news/news-releases/lularoe-pay-475-million-resolve-ag-ferguson-s-lawsuit-over-pyramid-scheme">The state says</a> if it comes mainly from signing people up, not from sales, it is a pyramid scheme.</p>

<p>Ask it of me. Ask it of anyone, with a friend beside you if that helps. A straight answer does not need a closed door.</p>

<h2 id="before-you-pay">What should you ask before you pay anyone to start?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: get the whole list in writing, and ask what you must keep buying to stay in.</p>

<h3>Ask for the whole list, not the pack</h3>

<p>Write down every cost to start, all in. The pack, the shelf, the printer, the gas. If you only have the price of the pack, you do not have the price yet.</p>

<h3>Get the refund rule in writing</h3>

<p><a href="https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/multi-level-marketing-businesses-and-pyramid-schemes">The FTC says</a> to "get the company's refund policy in writing." Then ask if it is full or partial, and how long it takes.</p>

<h3>Ask what you must keep buying to stay in</h3>

<p>The FTC warns you may be "required to buy a certain amount of product at regular intervals." Ask it straight. Ask me too.</p>

<p>If anybody dodges one of these, that is your answer. You just saved yourself a garage full of boxes.</p>
