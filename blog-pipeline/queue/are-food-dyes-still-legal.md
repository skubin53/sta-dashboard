<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>Yes. Today, in 2026, they are still legal.</p>
<p><strong><a href="https://www.fda.gov/industry/color-additives/fdc-red-no-3">The FDA pulled Red No. 3 on 15 January 2025. Then it gave the makers two years: "Manufacturers who use FD&amp;C Red No. 3 in food and ingested drugs will have until January 15, 2027 or January 18, 2028, respectively, to reformulate their products."</a></strong></p>
<p>Red 40 and Yellow 5 were not touched at all. They are still approved.</p>

<h2 id="still-legal">Is it still legal in my cupboard?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. And it can stay there after 2027 too.</p>

<p>Open the snack cupboard. The bright bag. The cereal box.</p>

<p>You heard the news in 2025. Red dye got banned.</p>

<p>Here is the part that did not travel. The ban has a start date, and it is not yet.</p>

<p>Food makers have until <strong>15 January 2027</strong>. Drug makers get until 2028.</p>

<p>Now read the next line on that page. It is the one that got me.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/industry/color-additives/fdc-red-no-3">"Consumers could see FD&amp;C Red No. 3 as an ingredient in a food or drug product on the market past the effective date in the order if that product was manufactured before the effective date."</a></p>

<p>In plain words. Make it before the date, sell it after.</p>

<p>The shelf empties when the boxes run out. Not on a Tuesday in January.</p>

<p>And that is one dye. Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6 and Blue 1 were not in it at all.</p>

<h2 id="what-took-so-long">What took thirty five years?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: they took it out of lipstick in 1990 and left it in food.</p>

<p>Go back to 1990. A company asked the FDA to approve Red 3 for makeup.</p>

<p>The FDA said no. Here is why, in its own words.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/industry/color-additives/fdc-red-no-3">"Due to the existence of data demonstrating that the use of FD&amp;C Red No. 3 caused cancer in rats, the FDA denied the petition."</a></p>

<p>So in 1990 it came out of the lipstick. It stayed in food for thirty five more years.</p>

<p><strong>Same dye. Same rat data. Same agency.</strong></p>

<p>The law behind both is older than that. It is called the Delaney Clause and it dates to 1960.</p>

<p>The FDA says it "prohibits FDA authorization of a food additive or color additive if it has been found to induce cancer in humans or animals."</p>

<p>The rule existed the whole time. Someone had to file paperwork to make it apply.</p>

<p>That took until 2022. The order came in 2025.</p>

<h2 id="who-made-them-stop">Who actually made them stop?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: not the FDA. A state lawyer with a subpoena.</p>

<p>The other dyes are still legal. So what moved them?</p>

<p>Two things happened in 2025. Neither was a rule.</p>

<p><strong>June 2025. General Mills.</strong> The Texas Attorney General opened an investigation.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/general-mills-agrees-remove-toxic-artificial-dyes-its-products-following-investigation-and-legal">Read what his office wrote about the promise before it: "The company previously pledged to remove these harmful dyes from its products in 2015. However, it only briefly removed them before again reselling cereals containing artificial colorings."</a></p>

<p>Read that again. They promised in 2015. They took it out. Then they put it back.</p>

<p>Nobody sent you a letter about that.</p>

<p><strong>August 2025. Kellogg's.</strong> Same office. Same kind of story.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-secures-historic-legal-agreement-kelloggs-remove-toxic-artificial-dyes-its">His office says the investigation started "after Kellogg's claimed it would remove petroleum-based food colorings in the United States, but did not do so. Instead, it removed these toxic ingredients in Canada and Europe while continuing to put different types of blue, red, and yellow dyes in American cereals."</a></p>

<p>The same box. A different country. A different recipe.</p>

<p>They already knew how to make it without the dye. They just did not do it here.</p>

<p>Kellogg's has now signed. It has until the end of 2027.</p>

<p>These are the state's claims, not a court's finding. I will say that plainly.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">The case against my own story</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the FDA does not agree with the scary version, and I have to say so.</p>

<p>I will give you the strongest words against me. They come from the FDA.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> On the rats: "The way that FD&amp;C Red No. 3 causes cancer in male rats does not occur in humans."</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> On people: "there is no evidence showing FD&amp;C Red No. 3 causes cancer in humans."</p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> On the headlines: "claims that the use of FD&amp;C Red No. 3 in food and in ingested drugs puts people at risk are not supported by the available scientific information."</p>

<p>That is a flat no. I am not going to soften it.</p>

<p>The FDA acted because the 1960 law left it no choice. Its words: "as a matter of law."</p>

<p>So the honest version is not that a dye is poisoning you. It is about the gap.</p>

<p>A rule from 1960. Data good enough to keep it out of lipstick in 1990. Food untouched until 2025. A recipe already sold without it in Canada.</p>

<p>Nobody was lying to you. Nobody was in a hurry either.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Is Red 40 banned too?</strong> No. Only Red No. 3 was revoked. Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6, Blue 1 and Blue 2 are still approved for use in food.</p>

<p><strong>Why is it still on shelves if it was banned in January 2025?</strong> Because the order gave food makers until 15 January 2027 to change their recipes. The FDA also says product made before that date can still be sold after it.</p>

<p><strong>Are the dyes listed on the label?</strong> Yes. The FDA requires certified colours to be named in the ingredients. That is how a state could prove what was in the box.</p>

<p><strong>Do European versions use different colours?</strong> The Texas Attorney General's office says Kellogg's removed the colorings in Canada and Europe while keeping dyes in American cereals. Kellogg's has now signed an agreement to change here too.</p>
