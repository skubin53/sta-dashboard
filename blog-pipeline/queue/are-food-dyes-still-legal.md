<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>Yes. Today, in 2026, they are still legal.</p>
<p><strong><a href="https://www.fda.gov/industry/color-additives/fdc-red-no-3">Red No. 3 was pulled on 15 January 2025. The makers were then handed two years: "Manufacturers who use FD&amp;C Red No. 3 in food and ingested drugs will have until January 15, 2027 or January 18, 2028, respectively, to reformulate their products."</a></strong></p>
<p>Red 40 and Yellow 5 were not in that order. They are still legal, and still in your food.</p>

<h2 id="still-legal">Is it still legal in my cupboard?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes. And it can stay there after 2027 too.</p>

<p>Open your snack cupboard. The bright bag. The cereal box.</p>

<p>You heard the news in 2025. Red dye got banned.</p>

<p>The ban has a start date. That date is not here yet.</p>

<p>Food makers have until <strong>15 January 2027</strong>. Drug makers get until 2028.</p>

<p>Now read the next line on that page. It is the one that got me.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/industry/color-additives/fdc-red-no-3">"Consumers could see FD&amp;C Red No. 3 as an ingredient in a food or drug product on the market past the effective date in the order if that product was manufactured before the effective date."</a></p>

<p>In plain words. Make it before the date, sell it after.</p>

<p>Your shelf empties when the boxes run out. Not on a Tuesday in January.</p>

<p>And that is one dye. Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6 and Blue 1 were not in the order at all.</p>

<h2 id="what-took-so-long">What took thirty five years?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: they took it out of lipstick in 1990 and left it in your food.</p>

<p>Go back to 1990. A company wanted Red 3 kept in makeup for good.</p>

<p>The answer was no. Here is the reason, on the record.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/industry/color-additives/fdc-red-no-3">"Due to the existence of data demonstrating that the use of FD&amp;C Red No. 3 caused cancer in rats, the FDA denied the petition based on the Delaney Clause."</a></p>

<p>So in 1990 it came out of the lipstick. It stayed in your food for thirty five more years.</p>

<p><strong>Same dye. Same rat data. Same desk.</strong></p>

<p>The law behind both is older still. It is called the Delaney Clause and it dates to 1960.</p>

<p>It bars a colour once that colour has been found to cause cancer in animals.</p>

<p>So the rule sat there the whole time. Somebody just had to file the paperwork.</p>

<p>That took until 2022. The order came in 2025.</p>

<p>Nobody knocked on your door in 1991 to mention any of it.</p>

<h2 id="who-made-them-stop">Who actually made them stop?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: not a regulator. A state lawyer with a subpoena.</p>

<p>The other dyes are still legal. So what moved anybody at all?</p>

<p>Two things happened in 2025. Neither one was a rule.</p>

<p><strong>June 2025. General Mills.</strong> The Texas Attorney General already had them under investigation.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/general-mills-agrees-remove-toxic-artificial-dyes-its-products-following-investigation-and-legal">His office wrote this about the promise before it: "The company previously pledged to remove these harmful dyes from its products in 2015. However, it only briefly removed them before again reselling cereals containing artificial colorings."</a></p>

<p>Read that again. They promised in 2015. They took it out. Then they put it back.</p>

<p>And in 2025 they promised again. They still have not signed anything.</p>

<p>Nobody sent you a letter about that.</p>

<p><strong>August 2025. Kellogg's.</strong> Same office. Same shape of story.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-paxton-secures-historic-legal-agreement-kelloggs-remove-toxic-artificial-dyes-its">His office says the investigation started "after Kellogg's claimed it would remove petroleum-based food colorings in the United States, but did not do so. Instead, it removed these toxic ingredients in Canada and Europe while continuing to put different types of blue, red, and yellow dyes in American cereals."</a></p>

<p>The same box. A different country. A different recipe.</p>

<p>They already knew how to make it without the dye. They just did not do it for you.</p>

<p>Kellogg's has now signed. It has until the end of 2027.</p>

<p>These are the state's claims, not a court's finding. I will say that plainly.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Is Red 40 banned too?</strong> No. Only Red No. 3 was pulled. Red 40, Yellow 5, Yellow 6, Blue 1 and Blue 2 are all still legal in your food.</p>

<p><strong>Why is it still on shelves if it was banned in January 2025?</strong> Because the order gave food makers until 15 January 2027 to change their recipes. Boxes made before that date can still be sold after it.</p>

<p><strong>Are the dyes listed on the label?</strong> Yes, by name. That is how a state lawyer proved what was in the box. Hunting for it is still not your job.</p>

<p><strong>Do European versions use different colours?</strong> The Texas Attorney General's office says Kellogg's removed the colorings in Canada and Europe while keeping dyes in American cereals. Kellogg's has now signed an agreement to change here too.</p>
