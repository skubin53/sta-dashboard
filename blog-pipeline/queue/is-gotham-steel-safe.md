<h2 id="what-the-suit-says">What does the Gotham Steel class action say?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it alleges pans sold as "Toxin Free" held things the box never named. The company being sued, E. Mishan, denies it.</p>

<p>You threw out the old scratched pan. You picked the copper one because the box said non-toxic. You hung it by the stove. You fried two eggs in it this morning.</p>

<p>Now a lawsuit says that box left things out.</p>

<p>No shame in that. The word was printed big, right where you were meant to trust it.</p>

<p>You wanted a pan that puts nothing into your food. That is fair. The trouble, the suit says, is what E. Mishan &amp; Sons, Inc. sold you to get it.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.stainless-steelcookwaresettlement.com/assets/uploads/settlement-agreement.pdf">The case, number CVRI2505343, was filed September 8, 2025, in Riverside County, California.</a> It covers Gotham Steel, Granite Stone and Bell &amp; Howell cookware with stainless steel parts.</p>

<p>The suit lists the words it says sold them, like "Non-toxic," "Safe," "Toxin Free" and "PFOA Free." <a href="https://www.stainless-steelcookwaresettlement.com/">It alleges that was "despite the undisclosed presence of chromium, iron, nickel, lead, cadmium, PFOA, PFAS, PTFE, or PFOS."</a></p>

<p>Those are claims in a lawsuit. No court has tested them, and so I will keep calling them claims.</p>

<h2 id="is-it-non-toxic">Is Gotham Steel non-toxic?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: its home page still says "Non-Toxic, Non-Stick, and Safe." A page linked at the very bottom lists five metals.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.gothamsteel.com/">On October 20, 2026, the Gotham Steel home page read "Non-Toxic, Non-Stick, and Safe."</a></p>

<p>At the very bottom sits a link marked "CA AB1200." <a href="https://www.gothamsteel.com/pages/california-ab-1200">That page says "Gotham Steel Cookware may contain" iron, chromium, nickel, manganese and aluminum.</a></p>

<p>A big word on the front. A small list behind a footer link.</p>

<p><a href="https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/AB1200%20Enforcement%20Advisory%20%281%29.pdf">A California law says pan labels must list certain added chemicals, starting January 1, 2024.</a></p>

<h3 id="the-new-label">Iron, chromium and nickel on the new label</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.stainless-steelcookwaresettlement.com/assets/uploads/settlement-agreement.pdf">In the deal, E. Mishan agreed to print "This product contains: iron, chromium, and nickel" on labels and product pages.</a> It would start within 180 days of a final deal and last up to three years.</p>

<p>Those three are what most stainless steel is made of. I am not calling steel poison.</p>

<p>The trouble is the order. By the suit's account, "Toxin Free" came first, and the metals never made the box.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/260.10">The FTC's guide for green claims says "non-toxic" likely means "non-toxic both for humans and for the environment generally."</a> And so the word is a promise, and E. Mishan is the one who picks it.</p>

<h2 id="who-is-covered">Who does the Gotham Steel settlement cover?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: only buyers in California and Colorado, and the claim deadline passed on September 25, 2026.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.stainless-steelcookwaresettlement.com/assets/uploads/long-form-notice.pdf">The court notice</a> covers buyers in California or Colorado from September 8, 2021, to July 6, 2026.</p>

<p>A valid claim pays $6.00 per pan, pot or set, up to two per home.</p>

<p>Nothing is paid unless the judge gives final approval and any appeals end. The notice says the November 2 hearing "may be moved."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.stainless-steelcookwaresettlement.com/assets/uploads/gotham_pans_final_approval_motion_and_supporting_declarations_1791911865.pdf">An October 9, 2026, court filing counts 38,433 valid claims and no objections.</a></p>

<p>If you bought yours anywhere else, no check is coming.</p>

<h2 id="safe-to-cook-with">Is Gotham Steel safe to cook with?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: no court has said. But the same company recalled a Granitestone pan in April 2026. A metal cap can shoot off when hot.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2026/E-Mishan-Recalls-Granitestone-Diamond-Pro-Blue-Stainless-Saute-Pans-Due-to-Impact-and-Burn-Hazards">The US Consumer Product Safety Commission posted recall 26-377 on April 2, 2026.</a> It covers about 740,000 Granitestone Diamond Pro Blue saut&eacute; pans.</p>

<p>The metal cap on the handle screw "can become detached and forcefully eject when heated," the notice says. It counts at least 98 incidents, and one report of bruises and burns.</p>

<p>The danger in this recall is a hot piece of metal.</p>

<p>If you have one, stop using it. The CPSC says to contact E. Mishan "for a full refund."</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for keeping your pan?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: three fair points. None of them changes what the box said.</p>

<p><strong>First,</strong> nobody here says a pan made anyone sick. The suit "does not allege that the Products caused any physical or personal injury to anyone," the notice says.</p>

<p><strong>Second,</strong> E. Mishan denies it. The notice says the deal "does not mean Defendant broke the law."</p>

<p><strong>Third,</strong> the metals on the new label are in most stainless steel, whatever the brand.</p>

<p>All three are true. I am not going to pretend otherwise.</p>

<p>So why would I still tell you to swap it? Because you paid for one word, and the company picked it. A list of metals behind a footer link is not the same promise.</p>

<p>And you are not the only one asking. Two buyers took it to court.</p>

<h2 id="what-instead">What do I cook in instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a plain cast iron skillet made in America, with no nonstick coating.</p>

<p>Cast iron has no coating to wear thin. The pan is iron, all the way through, and the name says so.</p>

<p>Start with your egg pan, not because it is the worst one, but because it is the one you reach for every morning.</p>

<p>Heat it slowly. Add a little oil or butter before the eggs. Wash it in hot water, dry it on the burner and wipe on a thin coat of oil.</p>

<p>The eggs may stick at first. The more you cook in it, the slicker it gets.</p>
