<h2 id="whats-in-the-box">What is actually in a box of Stove Top?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: bread crumbs, oil and seasoning, plus BHA and BHT, two of the three preservatives Kraft Heinz names last. BHA has been on a federal cancer report since 1991.</p>

<p>You brine the turkey. You peel the potatoes. You get the good plates down. You make the stuffing from the box because the oven is full and one burner is free.</p>

<p>You stand at that burner longer than you mean to, stirring crumbs into hot butter. The kitchen smells like every November you remember.</p>

<p>You want it fast, and you want it to taste like home. That is a fair thing to want. The problem is what Kraft Heinz adds so the oil in that mix stays fresh.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.kraftheinz.com/stove-top/products/00043000285558-stuffing-mix-for-turkey">Kraft Heinz lists enriched wheat flour first and high fructose corn syrup second.</a> There is soybean oil in it too. Then, at the very end, come BHA and BHT.</p>

<h3 id="bha">Why is BHA on a cancer report?</h3>

<p><a href="https://ntp.niehs.nih.gov/sites/default/files/ntp/roc/content/profiles/butylatedhydroxyanisole.pdf">The National Toxicology Program calls BHA "reasonably anticipated to be a human carcinogen."</a> It has been on that report since 1991.</p>

<p>Its full name is butylated hydroxyanisole. The listing rests on animal studies. Rats, mice and hamsters fed BHA grew tumors, the report says. They were in the front part of the stomach.</p>

<h3 id="bht">What is BHT doing in the same box?</h3>

<p>BHT sits right next to BHA on the list. Its full name is butylated hydroxytoluene.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/13/2026-09507/butylated-hydroxytoluene-bht-request-for-information">A federal notice called for safety data on BHT on May 13, 2026. It asks "if BHT remains safe under its current conditions of use in food."</a> It says BHT is used "to prevent the spoilage of fats and oils."</p>

<h2 id="the-review">What did the federal review open?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: two notices this year, one for each preservative, asking for safety data. There is no ruling yet on either one.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/11/2026-02761/butylated-hydroxyanisole-bha-request-for-information">The BHA notice came first, on February 11, 2026.</a> Comments closed April 13. The BHT notice followed on May 13, with comments due July 13.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/30/2026-15429/butylated-hydroxytoluene-bht-request-for-information-reopening-of-comment-period">Then the BHT window was reopened to August 31.</a> Someone had asked for more time, "particularly due to supply chain communications." The notice does not say who asked.</p>

<p>Both notices ask food companies to send in how much they use. The BHT notice calls it "voluntary submission of data." And so the companies that use BHT decide whether to share it.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/food/food-chemical-safety/list-select-chemicals-food-supply-under-fda-review">The government keeps a list of food chemicals under review. As of October 29, 2026, it shows no action on either one.</a> Both are still legal in food today.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for Stove Top?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: yes, three fair points. The review is not a finding. The cancer data come from animals, and both are legal today.</p>

<p>I know what you are thinking. Your family has eaten this every November, and everyone is fine. I get it.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> Being picked for review is not a safety finding. Neither notice names Stove Top, and nothing has been ruled.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The BHA listing comes from animal studies. <a href="https://ntp.niehs.nih.gov/sites/default/files/ntp/roc/content/profiles/butylatedhydroxyanisole.pdf">The same report calls the human data "inadequate." One Dutch study found no rise in stomach cancer at typical intake.</a></p>

<p><strong>Three.</strong> Both are legal in food today. Kraft Heinz is allowed to use them.</p>

<p>So why does it still sit wrong with me?</p>

<p>Because the box is on your counter now, and the answer is not. More than eight months after the BHA notice, <a href="https://www.kraftheinz.com/stove-top/products/00043000285558-stuffing-mix-for-turkey">the turkey mix still lists it</a>.</p>

<p>Stuffing made in your own kitchen does not need a preservative at all. You make it in the morning and eat it that afternoon.</p>

<h2 id="what-instead">What do you make on Thursday instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: stuffing from a loaf you bake yourself. Add celery, onion and sage from a farm near you. Thanksgiving is November 26, so there is time.</p>

<p>You might be thinking it is in everything, so why bother with one dish. You are right that you cannot fix it all this month. So start with the one dish on Thursday's table.</p>

<p>Bake a plain loaf on Monday. Cut it into cubes on Tuesday, and let them dry overnight on a sheet pan.</p>

<p>Then find a farm near you for the celery, the onion and the sage. Go meet the farmer. Ask how they grow it, and how long ago it was picked.</p>

<p><a href="https://join.switchtoamerica.com/post/why-is-turkey-so-expensive-this-year">Already calling a farm about the turkey?</a> Ask about the celery and sage on the same call.</p>

<p>On Thursday, cook the onion and celery in butter until they go soft. Toss in the bread, the sage and broth you simmered from the turkey neck. Bake it while the turkey rests, until the top turns golden.</p>

<p>Start with the stuffing. It is one dish on one day, and you will taste it.</p>

<p>Ask your sister-in-law, or a friend, to come over on Tuesday and cube the bread with you. Two cutting boards and an hour of talk.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Are BHA and BHT banned?</strong></p>
<p>No. Both are legal in food sold here today. The reviews are looking at whether that should change, and nothing has been ruled yet.</p>

<p><strong>What is in Stove Top stuffing?</strong></p>
<p>Kraft Heinz lists enriched wheat flour first and high fructose corn syrup second. BHA and BHT close the list, as preservatives. Stuffing from your own bread, celery, onion and sage has none of them.</p>

<p><strong>Why does boxed stuffing need preservatives?</strong></p>
<p>The mix has oil in it, and it waits in a box. The federal notices say BHA and BHT keep fats and oils from spoiling. Stuffing you bake on Thursday is gone by Thursday night.</p>

<p><strong>Am I overreacting about one box of stuffing?</strong></p>
<p>No. Two federal notices asked about BHA and BHT this year, and you are asking about your own table. You are just the first one at the table to ask.</p>
