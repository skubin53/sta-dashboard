<h2 id="the-short-answer">The short answer</h2>
<p>Your premium is going up again, and nobody asked you. The extra tax help ended, and care keeps costing more.</p>
<p>For 2027, insurers on the marketplace asked for a median 15% more. That is <a href="https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/how-much-and-why-aca-marketplace-premiums-are-going-up-in-2027/">KFF's count across 276 insurers</a>, the second double-digit year in a row.</p>
<p>One asked for far more. <strong><a href="https://www.medicaldaily.com/unitedhealthcare-exits-georgia-aca-marketplace-2027-479682">UnitedHealthcare asked Georgia for 54.3%</a></strong> on its marketplace plans.</p>
<p>The plan at work is climbing too. Marsh puts the 2027 rise in employer health costs at 8.2% per worker, <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/health-insurance-premiums-2027-inflation-aca-obamacare.html">the most since 2003</a>.</p>
<p>The price gets set in a filing you never see. Your plan for it gets set at your own kitchen table, this November.</p>

<h2 id="why-going-up">Why is my health insurance going up so much?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: the extra tax help ended on December 31, 2025. And the people who stayed cost more to cover.</p>

<p>The email comes in the fall. Or the letter from work. You open it at the kitchen counter, and the number is bigger again.</p>

<p>Last year you had more help. The extra federal tax credits on marketplace plans ran out at the end of 2025.</p>

<p>And so, many healthier shoppers left. <a href="https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/brief/how-much-and-why-aca-marketplace-premiums-are-going-up-in-2027/">KFF says</a> insurers expect that smaller, sicker group to add about 4 percentage points to 2027 prices.</p>

<h2 id="unitedhealthcare">What did UnitedHealthcare ask Georgia for?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: 54.3% more on its marketplace plans for 2027. And it is leaving that marketplace.</p>

<p>You want a plan that is still there next year, at a price you can plan around. Nothing wrong with wanting that. The trouble is what one filing can do to it.</p>

<p>For 2027, UnitedHealthcare filed for a 54.3% increase on its Georgia marketplace plans. <a href="https://www.medicaldaily.com/unitedhealthcare-exits-georgia-aca-marketplace-2027-479682">Medical Daily reported</a> it was the highest request in Georgia.</p>

<p>Georgia's insurance office says it pushed back. In its words, "we didn't feel like that was fair for Georgians." By the state's account, United then said it would leave.</p>

<p>United's own statement did not mention the rate fight. <a href="https://thecurrentga.org/2026/08/31/georgia-aca-rates-on-track-for-20-or-more-increase-for-2027-coverage/">The state's list for 2027</a> shows UnitedHealthcare leaving that marketplace. If you had one of those plans, you need a new one for January 1.</p>

<p>It is not only Georgia. In New York, <a href="https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/brief/how-much-and-why-aca-marketplace-premiums-are-going-up-in-2027/">UnitedHealthcare's own filing</a> names a cause. The end of the extra tax help and a federal rule change "account for 12.7% of the requested rate change."</p>

<p>That same summer, UnitedHealth Group reported <a href="https://www.unitedhealthgroup.com/content/dam/UHG/PDF/investors/2026/unh-reports-second-quarter-2026-results.pdf">$8.0 billion in earnings from operations</a> for April through June. Its insurance arm made $3.9 billion of that, up from $2.1 billion a year before.</p>

<p>Part of the reason, in its own words: "pricing discipline and benefit design changes." In plain words, the price and the plan's terms. Both land on you, and the plan at work is moving too.</p>

<h2 id="at-work">What is happening to the plan at work?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: it is going up too. More of it may land on you as a higher deductible.</p>

<p>Family coverage at work cost $26,993 for 2025, <a href="https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/">KFF found</a>. The average worker paid $6,850 of it. The employer paid the rest.</p>

<p>For 2027, <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/health-insurance-premiums-2027-inflation-aca-obamacare.html">Marsh expects</a> employer health costs to rise 8.2% per worker. That is the biggest jump since 2003. And 59% of employers plan cost cuts, like higher deductibles.</p>

<p>A higher deductible means you pay more before the plan starts to pay. You feel it the first time you go to the pharmacy in January.</p>

<h2 id="before-november">What would I do this November?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: find your 2027 number in two places, then write the gap on paper.</p>

<h3>Find the number at work</h3>

<p>When the enrollment email comes from work, find two lines. The 2027 premium, and the deductible. Write both on a pad, not a screen.</p>

<h3>Find the number on the marketplace</h3>

<p>From November 1, you can see 2027 plans on <a href="https://www.healthcare.gov/quick-guide/dates-and-deadlines/">HealthCare.gov</a>, or your state's own site. Pick by December 15 for a January 1 start. Open enrollment ends January 15. Some state sites run later.</p>

<p>Look up the plans where you live and write down the price. Now you have two numbers. The gap is what your plan at work is worth.</p>

<p>Then talk it through with a friend who tells you the truth.</p>

<p><strong>Why is my health insurance going up so much in 2027?</strong></p>
<p>The extra federal tax credits ended at the end of 2025, and healthier shoppers left. Insurers also name rising care prices as the main driver. For 2027, marketplace insurers asked for a median 15% more.</p>

<p><strong>What if I make too much for a tax credit?</strong></p>
<p>Since the extra credits ended, there is no tax credit over about four times the poverty line. For one person in 2026, that was roughly $64,000. You pay the full price.</p>

<p><strong>When can I change my marketplace plan for 2027?</strong></p>
<p>On HealthCare.gov, open enrollment runs November 1 to January 15. Pick by December 15 for a plan that starts January 1.</p>
