<h2 id="which-foods">Which bettergoods foods were recalled?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: two foods. One lot of pistachio nut butter, and all of one frozen pasta.</p>

<p>You stood at the pantry shelf longer than you meant to, the little jar in your hand.</p>

<p>You wanted the cleaner choice. No shame in that. These recalls point at Walmart's name on the jar and the bag, not at you.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/boticelli-foods-recalls-bettergoods-pistachio-nut-butter-because-possible-health-risk">Botticelli Foods announced a recall of one lot of bettergoods Pistachio Nut Butter on August 3, 2026.</a> It is the 6.7 ounce glass jar, UPC 194346207961, lot LB028ACP04.</p>

<p>It went to Walmart stores in 19 states. <a href="https://api.fda.gov/food/enforcement.json?search=recall_number:%22H-1273-2026%22">The recall record, H-1273-2026, counts 16,200 units.</a></p>

<h3 id="salmonella">Salmonella, in three jars the state tested</h3>

<p>The state of Florida tested three jars at a Walmart store on a routine check. It found Salmonella, the notice says.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/boticelli-foods-recalls-bettergoods-pistachio-nut-butter-because-possible-health-risk">Walmart learned of the test on July 17, 2026.</a> <a href="https://api.fda.gov/food/enforcement.json?search=recall_number:%22H-1273-2026%22">The record says the recall started that same day.</a> The notice for shoppers went up August 3, 17 days later.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/gias-foods-inc-recalls-bettergoods-authentic-italian-lemon-alfredo-fettuccine-because-possible">On September 15, Gias Foods announced a recall of two lots of frozen bettergoods Lemon Alfredo Fettuccine.</a> It is the 22 ounce yellow bag, UPC 194346442706, lots L6079C and L6080C.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/gias-foods-inc-recalls-bettergoods-authentic-italian-lemon-alfredo-fettuccine-because-possible-0">On October 1, Gias pulled all lot codes.</a> <a href="https://api.fda.gov/food/enforcement.json?search=recall_number:%22H-0014-2027%22">The recall record names a third lot, L6081A.</a></p>

<h3 id="listeria">Listeria, found in state food tests</h3>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/gias-foods-inc-recalls-bettergoods-authentic-italian-lemon-alfredo-fettuccine-because-possible">Gias says state tests in Washington and Florida showed the pasta may contain Listeria.</a></p>

<p>Gias says Listeria can cause "serious and sometimes fatal infections" in young children and frail or elderly people.</p>

<p><a href="https://api.fda.gov/food/enforcement.json?search=recall_number:%22H-1396-2026%22">The recall record ranks the pasta Class I</a>, and <a href="https://api.fda.gov/food/enforcement.json?search=recall_number:%22H-1273-2026%22">the jar too.</a> <a href="https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-7/subpart-A/section-7.3">Under federal rules, Class I is the most serious of three.</a></p>

<h2 id="who-made-it">Who made the food, if Walmart did not?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: a factory in Italy made the jar. The pasta came from Italy too, and Gias Foods of New York pulled it.</p>

<p>Walmart launched bettergoods on April 30, 2024. <a href="https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2024/04/30/walmart-launches-bettergoods-a-new-private-brand-making-elevated-culinary-experiences-accessible-for-all">Its launch page calls it "Walmart's largest private brand food launch in 20 years."</a></p>

<p>The page says Walmart "collaborated with suppliers across the globe." It does not name one.</p>

<p>The recall notice does. <a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/boticelli-foods-recalls-bettergoods-pistachio-nut-butter-because-possible-health-risk">Botticelli says Gustibus Alimentari Srl of Assoro, Italy made the nut butter, and Walmart sold it.</a></p>

<p><a href="https://api.fda.gov/food/enforcement.json?search=recall_number:%22H-1396-2026%22">The pasta bag says "MADE IN ITALY," per the recall record.</a> Walmart's name sits under "DISTRIBUTED BY."</p>

<p><a href="https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/treehouse-foods-announces-expansion-voluntary-recall-include-all-waffle-and-pancake-products-due">In October 2024, TreeHouse Foods pulled three bettergoods protein waffles over possible Listeria.</a></p>

<p>The same list held Great Value waffles. TreeHouse said all of it came from one plant.</p>

<p>And so better is a word on the box. The better waffles and the everyday waffles came out of one plant.</p>

<h2 id="check-yours">How do I check my jar or bag?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: match the UPC and the lot code. If they match, do not eat it, and get your money back.</p>

<p>Find the lot code on the jar. The recalled one reads LB028ACP04, with an expiration date of January 28, 2027. The UPC, the number under the barcode, is 194346207961.</p>

<p>The pasta is simpler. Each 22 ounce bag with UPC 194346442706 is pulled, no matter the lot.</p>

<p>If yours matches, do not eat it. Each notice offers a full refund where you bought it.</p>

<p>Questions on the jar go to Botticelli at 631-543-7000, extension 203. Questions on the pasta go to sales@giasfoods.com.</p>

<h2 id="the-other-side">Is there a case for keeping the brand?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: two fair points for the brand. Neither one is a reason to keep lot LB028ACP04 or the pasta.</p>

<p><strong>One.</strong> No one has reported getting sick. The nut butter recall is one lot. That is real, and I am not going to pretend it is not.</p>

<p><strong>Two.</strong> The state tests did their job, and both firms pulled the food.</p>

<p>So why would I still not trust the name?</p>

<p>Because the name did none of the catching. A Florida test found the jar at a Walmart store, already for sale. State samples found the pasta. And so the word better did nothing for you here.</p>

<h2 id="what-instead">What goes in the pantry instead?</h2>

<p class="quick-answer">Quick answer: food you can trace to a person. Make the nut butter yourself, and find a farm near you for the rest.</p>

<p>Do the small thing tonight. Match the jar and the bag. If they match, make the return.</p>

<p>Then think about the next jar. This one came from a factory in Italy by way of a firm in New York, and you never saw a step of it.</p>

<p>Nut butter is nuts, ground until they turn smooth. Get the nuts from a farmer you know. Grind them in your own kitchen, and you know each step.</p>

<p>For the rest of the pantry, find a farm near you and get to know the farmer. Ask how they grow it. That is how you know whose hands were on your food.</p>

<p>A farm will not hand you a bag of frozen pasta for a busy night. That is the honest trade.</p>

<p>You are far from the only one with that jar. <a href="https://api.fda.gov/food/enforcement.json?search=recall_number:%22H-1273-2026%22">There were 16,200 of them.</a> If a friend keeps one too, check hers over coffee.</p>

<h2 id="faq">What else do people ask?</h2>

<p><strong>Is my bettergoods pistachio butter recalled?</strong></p>
<p>Only lot LB028ACP04, the 6.7 ounce jar with UPC 194346207961 and a January 28, 2027 date. The notice says no other lots are part of this recall.</p>

<p><strong>Which bettergoods pasta is recalled?</strong></p>
<p>All of the frozen Lemon Alfredo Fettuccine, 22 ounces, UPC 194346442706. Gias Foods pulled all lot codes on October 1, 2026.</p>

<p><strong>I already ate some. What now?</strong></p>
<p>All three notices say no one has reported getting sick. Between them, they list fever, headache, stiffness, nausea, vomiting, belly pain and diarrhea. If you feel any of that, call your doctor and say what you ate. If yours matches, take the rest back for the refund.</p>

<p><strong>Is all bettergoods food recalled?</strong></p>
<p>No. These three notices cover two foods. In 2024, three bettergoods protein waffles were in a TreeHouse Foods recall that is now closed. That tells you what was pulled, and nothing more.</p>
