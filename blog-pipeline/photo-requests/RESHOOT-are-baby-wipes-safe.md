# Three frames to reshoot. The post is LIVE and the pictures name the wrong company.

**THE PROBLEM, in one line: the post accuses TARGET, and every product shot in it is a
clearly branded PAMPERS pack.**

Pampers was not recalled. Pampers is not mentioned anywhere in the copy. The recall was
Target's own store brand, **Up & Up**, in two versions, Fragrance Free and Fresh Cucumber
Scented.

So the words blame one company and the photographs show another. The hero frame is also the
share card, so that is the Pampers pack showing on Facebook next to a headline about a
recall.

Two of the five frames are FINE and must not be touched:
`arebabywipe-belonging-v1` and `arebabywipe-freedom-v1` carry no branding at all.

---

## THE ONE RULE FOR THIS SET

**No Pampers. No Huggies. No brand that was not recalled.**

Two ways to solve it, and either is acceptable:

1. **Best: a plain unbranded wipes pack.** A soft plastic pouch with the oval flip lid, in a
   plain pale colour, no wordmark, no logo, no legible text anywhere on it. The story is
   carried by the recall copy next to it, not by the pack.
2. **Also fine: a Target-style Up & Up pack**, if it can be rendered convincingly. That IS
   the recalled product and it is the honest choice. Only do this if the wordmark comes out
   clean and correct. A misspelled or garbled wordmark is worse than no wordmark.

**Do NOT render a legible brand name unless it is Up & Up.** If in doubt, plain pack.

There is a second, separate defect in the current turning-point frame: the pack reads
**"Lansitive"** instead of "sensitive". Garbled text on a product is the classic AI tell and
it has to be gone. This is another reason to prefer a plain pack with no text on it at all.

---

## Continuity to keep

The current set is good on everything except the brand. Keep it:

- **The woman is in her fifties**, grey or silver hair, teal blue knitted jumper, jeans.
  She reads as a grandmother, which is right. Do not make her younger.

- A baby appears in the set and that is fine here. This is a baby wipes post and she is the
  grandmother. The audience rule about not building a hook on a baby is satisfied because
  the copy is aimed at HER, at her own hands and her own face, not at a new mother.
