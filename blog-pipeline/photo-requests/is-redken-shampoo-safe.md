WHY THIS POST EXISTS: Her hair changed. It went dry and flat and the gray came in wiry, so she stopped buying the drugstore bottle and started paying thirty-eight dollars at the salon because the stylist said the cheap stuff was the problem. She has been buying safety. That is what the price tag was for.

THE VILLAIN, and it is the COMPANY and the product, never the shopper and never the label: L'Oreal USA. New York capped 1,4-dioxane in personal care at 1 ppm. Instead of meeting it, L'Oreal USA applied to the state for waivers on 69 salon products and got them, listing Redken All Soft Shampoo at 4.5 ppm, Redken Color Extend Graydiant at 3.67 ppm, Kerastase Densifique Women Bain at 4.6 ppm, and L'Oreal Professionnel Serie Expert Volumetry at 7.23 ppm. That is seven times the legal limit, in writing, on a government form, signed by the company.
