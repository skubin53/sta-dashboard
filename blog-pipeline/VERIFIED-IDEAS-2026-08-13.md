## SHOPPER, ready to write

### 1. Who Decides a New Ingredient Is Safe to Eat? (2026)
- **Villain:** Daily Harvest
- **Answer to the title:** the company selling it. Since 1958 a manufacturer could hire its own
  experts, declare its own ingredient safe, and never tell FDA it exists.
- **Proof:** FDA Constituent Update, 15 May 2024, tara flour does not meet the GRAS standard and
  is an unapproved food additive. The 2022 Daily Harvest outbreak: 393 ill, 133 hospitalised.
  Be precise, FDA says it has not established tara flour CAUSED it, the outbreak prompted review.
  News peg: HHS/FDA proposed rule 10 Aug 2026 making GRAS notification mandatory.
- **Say it is a PROPOSED rule.** Natural News wrote it in past tense. Nothing is closed.

### 2. Is Red Dye 40 Actually Banned Yet? (2026)
- **Villain:** Mars (M&M's, Skittles)
- **Angle:** she heard they banned the dyes, so the bowl on the counter stayed. Nobody banned
  anything, they asked nicely.
- **Proof:** Dunford et al., J Acad Nutr Diet, June 2025, 39,763 products: 19% contain synthetic
  dyes, 28% of child-marketed products, Mars 52%. FDA revoked Red No. 3 on 15 Jan 2025 with a
  15 Jan 2027 deadline. The April 2025 six-dye phase-out is VOLUNTARY, no regulation behind it.
  Texas SB 25 labels from 1 Jan 2027. West Virginia HB 2354 was BLOCKED by a federal judge in
  Dec 2025. Product data is from 2020, so it predates reformulation pledges.

### 3. Why Has Nobody Recalled the Steam Cleaner That Burned 48 People? (2026)
- **Villain:** Dovety / Shenzhen Kaisennic Intelligent Technology
- **Why this one is the strongest of the four:** the villain is COMPANY BEHAVIOUR, not chemistry.
  The seller refused to do a recall. That makes "one company that makes it and can be held to it"
  a stronger frame, instead of inviting the reader to audit our own ingredients.
- **Proof:** CPSC Product Safety Warning 26-474, 7 May 2026, model TMSC-001. 52 reports of hot
  water ejecting, at least 48 burn injuries. CPSC: "The Chinese seller... has not agreed to a
  recall." Sold on Amazon and TikTok Shop.
- **Three fixes required before writing:**
  1. Do NOT claim it is still purchasable unless the listings are checked on the day. Provable
     version: no recall, no refund, no redesign, and the units already bought are in homes.
  2. Say "no refund on record from the seller". CPSC speaks to the seller, not Amazon's guarantee.
  3. Cite CPSC only. Four plaintiff firms rank high on this search. None is a regulator.
- **Strengthener:** her obvious out is "I will buy a US-brand steamer". BISSELL recalled 3.4M
  Steam Shot handhelds in 2024 for the same hazard, WITH refunds. That closes the door and proves
  the accountability point at once.

## WHY 36 DIED, because the pattern matters more than the losses

**1. The proof did not say what the idea claimed.** Most common failure by far. The Listerine
study's own abstract contradicted the claim in four places. A failed-replication paper was cited
as if it proved the thing it failed to replicate.

**2. The wrong entity was named.** Orgain was never sued, only Costco. A settlement was credited
to Perrier that belonged elsewhere. 11,877 lawsuits across a whole docket were pinned on L'Oreal.

**3. The product on her shelf had already changed.** Residential Roundup has had no glyphosate
since 2023. The bottle in her garage is not the bottle in the lawsuit.

**4. THE SELF-INDICTING FRAME. 6 of 19 in the second pass.** This is the one worth reading twice.

Ingredient-level attacks kept collapsing because the ingredient is a common one. A post attacking
d-limonene in Dove, or anisyl alcohol in eos, or "unscented is not unscented" in Tom's of Maine,
invites the reader to go and check our own catalogue for the same class of ingredient. The
verifier called eos "FATAL: the swap indicts her own shelf harder than it indicts eos."

Same disease on the builder side. Frames like "who owns the customer", "who sets your ceiling",
"what would you still have if this company changed its terms tomorrow" all indict a referral
model as hard as they indict Uber or an employer.

**The move that works:** attack COMPANY BEHAVIOUR, not chemistry. A refused recall, a hidden
document, a regulator action, a company that cannot be reached. Those make the "one company, no
middlemen" frame stronger. That is exactly why the Dovety idea survived and the ingredient posts
did not.

**BLOCKED ON SHANNON:** to run ingredient-level attacks safely, someone has to confirm which
ingredient classes our own catalogue is genuinely clean of. Only she can answer that. Until she
does, ingredient posts are a risk and behaviour posts are not.
