THE SUBJECT IS TIME, which is as hard to photograph as an income figure, and CLOCKS ARE BANNED IN THIS SET because a clock face is numbers. So the hours are shown by what an evening leaves behind instead. Built to the realism standard: rooms full of colour and honest clutter, furniture against walls, none of the retired prop kit. ONE woman locked across all five: about 51, fair hair going grey to her shoulders, soft petrol blue jumper. THE KITCHEN IS WARM AND FULL: painted units, a wall of colourful crockery, a crammed bookshelf, ferns, a patterned runner, a jumper over the chair. Hero is her at the worktop early in the day, hand-writing in a plain notebook while she works something out, capable and unhurried, an ordinary productive morning. VILLAIN IS THE FRAME THAT CARRIES THE ARGUMENT and there is nobody in it: the same kitchen at half past eleven at night lit by one lamp, a dinner plate pushed aside with half a meal gone cold and congealed on it, a fork laid across it, a glass with the ice long melted, the chair pushed back at an angle and left, a cardigan slumped over its back, black window. She looked up and the evening had gone. Turning point has her close the notebook and set it down with the biro on top, take her hand off it and turn to the darkening window, settled. BELONGING IS A NEW SETTING AGAIN per Shannon instruction not to repeat rooms: an outdoor street market, one of them holding a bunch of carrots mid-conversation, crates of vegetables, a striped awning, wet cobbles. ONE DEVIATION TO FLAG: the brief says exactly two people and no face in the background, and this frame has blurred shoppers behind them. A market with nobody else in it would have looked fake, so the two women are the only faces in focus and everyone else is soft and turned away. Freedom is the warmest: notebook and paper gone, low golden evening light, her sideways on a chair by the window with her feet up and a paperback face down on her knee, laughing at somebody in the next room, the evening actually hers."
