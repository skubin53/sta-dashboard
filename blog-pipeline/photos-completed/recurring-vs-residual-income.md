FTC rule holds: no legible text, no money, no clocks, no screens facing the camera, every cardboard box completely plain with no printing. ONE woman across the set: about 49, dark curly hair twisted up in a claw clip, warm brown skin, olive green shirt, dark jeans. THE OBJECT THAT CARRIES THE STORY is the same job done again every single week: plain brown shipping boxes and a tape gun. THE ROOM is the modern dining room she works from: light oak table against a wall of windows, black pendant lights, mustard velvet chairs, a big rubber plant, a sideboard of colourful ceramics. Hero is her taping up a box at the table in the morning, quick and practised, good at this and with no idea anything is wrong. VILLAIN CARRIES THE ARGUMENT with nobody in it: the same table at night under one lamp, a fresh stack of flat unmade boxes waiting, the tape gun on its side, scissors, a chair pushed back and left. The pile that builds again the moment it is cleared. Turning point has her set the tape gun down, push the boxes to the far end and turn to the window, settled. BELONGING IS A NEW SETTING: two clearly different women in their fifties sitting on a wooden jetty over a lake, shoes off, laughing (the first two takes were rejected, one for fused fingers on gesturing hands and one because the second woman rendered about thirty; the fix was hands at rest and explicit fifties ageing). Freedom is the warmest: her barefoot on a garden swing seat in golden evening light, a paperback face down on her knee, laughing at a neighbour over the fence."
