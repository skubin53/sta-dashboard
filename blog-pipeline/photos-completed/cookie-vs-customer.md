THE OVERRIDING RULE WAS THE FTC ONE: no legible text of any kind in any frame and nothing anywhere that suggests money, earnings or income. Every parcel, sheet of paper and card in the set is deliberately unprinted, and in the freedom frame the handwriting is soft distant texture with no readable words. The request file's boilerplate line about showing a recognisable branded product does NOT apply, since a builder post has no product. SIXTH distinct woman and SIXTH house in this run of builder sets. THE IDEA THE SET IS BUILT ON: a cookie is a click that expires, a customer is a person who writes back, so the whole arc is carried by ordinary post. ONE woman locked across all five: about 55, silver-grey pixie cut, olive green cardigan over a plain cream top. Hero is her at her own kitchen table on an ordinary morning, absorbed, wrapping small parcels by hand in plain brown paper and tying them with twine, care taken over something small. VILLAIN IS THE FRAME THAT CARRIES THE ARGUMENT and it contains nothing at all: a plain metal letterbox beside an ordinary front door, its little door hanging open and the inside completely empty, dead leaves blown onto the mat, flat grey morning, nobody in frame. Nothing came back. It is deliberately unremarkable rather than dramatic. Turning point has her set the last parcel down and let go of it, hand flat on the table beside it, looking away toward the window, settled, decision already made, NOT reading or holding anything. Belonging is exactly two women on the doorstep, properly mid-conversation and laughing, one plain parcel passing between them almost forgotten because the conversation matters more, no transaction and no price tag. Freedom is the warmest and it answers the villain directly: the wrapping put away, full golden sun across the table, and a small untidy handful of real post that actually arrived, one card open in her hand while she laughs at it, a second mug already out so the house is not empty. Redone 2026-10-12 for check 15. The old set was British: a small kitchen with roller blinds, mugs and flowers in vases, a wall letterbox on brick, and a British doorstep. New room is a big lived-in kitchen in a suburban house in Madison, Wisconsin: pale blue-grey shaker cabinets, butcher block counters, a big farmhouse table, stainless French-door fridge, dishwasher, gas range with full oven door and a tea towel, floor-length cream linen curtains on black rods at every window, same cupboards in every kitchen frame. Real clutter: laundry basket, dog leash on a hook, a jacket over a chair. Hero was edited to curtain the sink window and make the paper stack plain kraft; the fridge dispenser lights were painted out by hand in hero, turning point and freedom. Villain is new: an empty black curbside mailbox on a wooden post, door hanging open, dead maple leaves, grey morning, no numbers. Turning point and freedom are edits of the hero: last parcel set down, hand flat, looking out the window; parcels put away, golden sun, her laughing over a handwritten card with no readable words and a handful of real post. Belonging is new: her and a neighbour laughing on a white clapboard front porch with a plain parcel between them.
