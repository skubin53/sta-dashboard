That carton was used as the product reference and the label kept to the oval FARMHOUSE EGGS badge only. ONE woman across the set: white, about 57, short curly salt-and-pepper hair, round glasses, chambray denim shirt with sleeves rolled. THE ROOM is her real kitchen: soft mint green shaker lower cabinets with bronze cup pulls, warm white closed wall cupboards with bronze knobs, walnut butcher-block worktop with tomatoes, a loaf, a crock of wooden spoons and chives, glossy olive green handmade tiles, plain mustard yellow full-height linen curtains. Hero built first and used to lock the room for the villain, turning point and freedom. Hero: her cracking a brown egg into a speckled bowl, the carton open beside her. Villain: the carton alone on the worktop in cold flat early light. Turning point: her setting the closed carton down and looking out of the window. Belonging, a new setting: two clearly different women in a backyard by a small chicken coop, one handing the other a plain wicker basket of fresh brown eggs, laughing. Freedom: warm morning sun, her laughing over a pan of scrambled eggs, the wicker basket beside her, no carton anywhere. FIXED TO PASS THE LIST: gingham curtains read as country cottage so they became plain mustard linen; a second small garbled badge on the hero carton painted out and the carton composited back from the first render because the curtain edit garbled its badge; the turning point badge came back as gibberish so the clean hero badge was fitted onto it; the turning point and freedom were warmed so the measured light runs villain coldest to freedom warmest."
