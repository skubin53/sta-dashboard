note: "Nano Banana Pro 4K, two variants per beat. BUILDER TRACK so there is no villain product and the show-the-real-brand rule does not apply. THE OVERRIDING RULE WAS THE FTC ONE: nothing in any frame may suggest money, earnings or income, and no legible text anywhere, because the copy carries no income figure and a photograph implying income IS an earnings claim. Every frame was written against that. Hero has her at her own kitchen table looking straight down the lens, mug in hand and the laptop CLOSED beside her, level and plain-spoken with no held smile, someone about to tell you the truth rather than sell you something. VILLAIN IS THE BEST FRAME IN BOTH BUILDER SETS and it works by subtraction: a phone lying face-up on a dark counter at eleven at night, screen lit but thrown completely out of focus so it is a blank glowing rectangle with nothing readable on it at all, cold blue light spilling onto one abandoned mug and a few crumbs, the rest of the kitchen swallowed in dark. Nobody in frame. The emptiness of the screen does more than any message would, and it keeps the beat legally clean at the same time. Turning point has her at the table with a BLANK notepad and the pen resting on it untouched, hands loose, looking off past the camera, settled and unhurried and deliberately NOT yet decided. Belonging is two clearly distinct women in their fifties over mugs and a plate of biscuits, one talking with her hands and the other genuinely listening, with NO paperwork, laptop, phone, brochure or product between them, because the brief was explicit that it must read as a conversation and not a pitch. Freedom is the warmest frame: unpacking a plain brown delivery box onto the counter in morning light, lifting out unbranded amber and white bottles, crumpled packing paper, nothing celebratory, a normal Tuesday that is just a bit lighter than it used to be. This set was split across two days because Higgsfield hit its daily generation cap after three beats. Redone 2026-10-12 for check 15 and the builder rules. The old set was a small British kitchen with radiators, UK sockets and a mug in four frames, and the villain showed a lit phone screen, which builder sets do not allow. New room is one big lived-in kitchen in a suburban house in Grand Rapids, Michigan: white shaker cabinets, black counters, a long butcher block island, stainless French-door fridge, dishwasher, gas cooktop with a full oven door below and a tea towel, floor-length natural linen curtains on black rods at every window, same cupboards in every frame. The oven display was painted to plain dark glass and the dishwasher and mixer badges blurred by hand in every frame. Hero is her at the island looking straight at the camera, level and plain-spoken, laptop and mug gone. Villain is new and has no screen: the same island late at night under one cold light, covered in rows of plain unlabelled bottles and sealed plain boxes waiting to go out, packing paper on the floor, nobody there. Turning point: a blank notepad with the pen untouched, her looking past the camera. Belonging: her explaining something to a friend at the island, both laughing, a plate of cookies, no mugs. Freedom: golden morning, her smiling as she unpacks a small delivered box of plain unlabelled bottles. Measured R/B ramp: villain 0.89, hero 1.15, turning point 1.31, belonging 1.55, freedom 1.79."
