note: "BUILDER TRACK. Nano Banana Pro on ImagineArt, each frame enhanced to 2752px then delivered at 1600px webp. FTC rule holds throughout: no legible text in any frame and nothing suggesting money, earnings or income. THIS SET WAS BUILT TWICE AND THE FIRST VERSION WAS SCRAPPED ENTIRELY. Shannon killed it on 2026-08-25 and the reasons are now rules in the photo guide. The first attempt put the creator at a chrome clothes rail in the middle of a bare white room, steaming seven identical beige linen shirts, with a camera on a tripod and three boxes. Her words: nobody has their clothes in the middle of a room on a rack, all the clothes were the same colour and style, and the whole thing looked depressing and unreal. She also killed a freedom frame of a woman at a desk marooned in the middle of an empty floor, and banned the prop kit I had been reusing in nearly every frame across thirteen sets: the mug of coffee, the jug of flowers, the second mug set out so the house is not empty. She then sent me to do image research on real modern home offices, which is now the standing rule: pull real reference photos before inventing a room. THE REBUILD USES A REAL HOUSE. Hero is shot from the bedroom doorway with the door edge in frame: she is at her own built-in wardrobe lifting a jumper off the rail, and the clothes are an honest jumble of mustard, teal, navy, a floral dress squashed between two coats, mismatched wooden and wire hangers, shoes heaped on the wardrobe floor. A charger cable trails to the socket, the bed is cropped at the edge, a plant needs watering. VILLAIN IS THE FRAME THAT CARRIES THE ARGUMENT: shot low along a hallway floor just inside the front door, five plain taped boxes of returns shoved against the skirting where they are in the way, one balanced at an angle, tape and scissors on the boards, coats cropped above, shoes in a heap. Nobody in frame. The work went out and it all came back. Turning point returns to the wardrobe with her hand flat on the open door, one mustard jumper hanging from her other hand, head turned right away toward the window, settled. Belonging is two women on the front steps of a modern house, laughing, nothing in their hands. Freedom is the warmest and the most deliberately COLOURFUL: a deep teal sofa pushed against the window wall with mismatched mustard and rust cushions, a patterned rug, a walnut sideboard crammed with framed photos and books at different heights, big plants, sheer linen curtains and low afternoon sun, her sideways in the corner of the sofa with her legs tucked up laughing hard. No coffee, no flowers, no styling anywhere in the set."
