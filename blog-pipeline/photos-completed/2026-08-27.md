So the VILLAIN beat carries the brand: it is a close product shot and MAYBELLINE NEW YORK reads perfectly clean and correct on the barrel there. The hero is the natural human frame and the tube in it is simply a lipstick, with no gibberish text on it. ONE hallway locked across hero and turning point (walnut console, round wooden mirror, bowl of keys, stack of post, coats and a leopard scarf on hooks, fern, glass panelled front door). Hero is her about 50, jacket half on and bag already on her shoulder, putting a deep berry lipstick on at the mirror on a weekday morning. Villain is the coldest frame and nobody is in it: a real bathroom counter on hard flat desaturated light with the Maybelline bullet lying twisted all the way up and worn at an angle, its cap off to one side, an open gloss wand beside it, and a crumpled tissue carrying a heavy smear of the same berry red, surrounded by honest counter clutter (hairbrush with hair in it, toothbrush in a mug, hair tie, folded flannel, cotton buds). Turning point has her lower the lipstick, cap in her other hand, looking at her own reflection, settled, decision already made, jacket now off the shoulder, NOT reading anything. Belonging is deliberately built to look nothing like a getting-ready shot: low camera angle, charcoal and plum bedroom, her mid-conversation and laughing with a clearly different woman of the same age, no products and no text in frame. Freedom is the warmest: outdoors on a sunlit street in full golden light, bare lips, no makeup at all, laughing hard at something a friend just said. She is never made to look vain or silly anywhere in the set. Villain is the product and the company."
