THE BAGS ARE DELIBERATELY UNBRANDED: plain white and grey carrier bags with no printing, because the villain companies (Novolex, Inteplast, Mettler) make bags that carry the RETAILER's name, and rendering a retailer brand would point the finger at the wrong company. The branded-product line in the request is not satisfiable here and was skipped on purpose. ONE woman across the set: about 50, dark hair in a low ponytail, raspberry red fleece (a tiny chest wordmark on the hero fleece was retouched out), grey jeans. THE ROOM is her cosy cream shaker kitchen with butcher block, red kettle and gingham cafe curtains. Hero is her kneeling at the open cupboard under the sink hauling out a wad of saved bags into one big bag to take back to the store, good humoured, no idea anything is wrong. VILLAIN CARRIES THE ARGUMENT with nobody in it: one bag stuffed tight with dozens of bags hanging on the back of the pantry door. Months of dutiful saving. Turning point has her set the bag down by the back door and turn to the window, settled. BELONGING IS A NEW SETTING: two clearly different women filling a willow basket in a pick-your-own apple orchard. Freedom is the warmest: her unpacking carrots and bread from a plain canvas tote in the same kitchen in full morning sun, laughing (cropped slightly to lose labelled jars and fridge papers). REDONE 2026-10-11 FOR CHECK 15 (kitchens must be big, real and American, with an oven, a fridge and a dishwasher, same cupboards in every frame). The old kitchen was a small British galley with gingham cafe curtains and no fridge in view, and the villain was a pantry door in another room. New room: a big open-plan Wisconsin family kitchen, cream shaker cabinets with wooden knobs, butcher block worktops, a white tiled backsplash, a stainless French-door fridge, a stainless dishwasher, a farmhouse sink under a double-hung window, a stainless range with a full oven door and a red kettle, a big butcher block island with wooden stools, oak floor, glass back doors, floor-length linen curtains on black rods at every window and door, a denim jacket on a stool and boots by the door. Shot as a candid phone snapshot. Hero rendered first and locked; villain, turning point and freedom made as edits of it. Villain: one plain bag stuffed with saved bags hanging from the cupboard under the sink. Turning point: the big bag set down by the back door, her looking out. Freedom: unpacking carrots and bread from a plain canvas tote in morning sun. Belonging (apple orchard) kept. Every bag plain, no retailer printing. FIXED BY HAND: a tiny wordmark on her fleece and a soap bottle by the sink removed in the hero; text-like marks showing through the stuffed bag in the turning point softened. Light measured R/B: villain 0.96, hero 1.26, turning point 1.31, belonging 1.61, freedom 1.70. All 15 checks written out per frame before committing."
