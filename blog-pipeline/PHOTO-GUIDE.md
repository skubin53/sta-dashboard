# PHOTO-GUIDE.md: Switch to America Blog Photography

**For:** Cameron and the photo Claude, every day.
**Job:** Make the images carry the same story and the same realism as the writing. A reader should feel the whole arc, fear to free, by scrolling the pictures alone, before she reads a single word.

**Model:** Nano Banana Pro. **Ratio:** 16:9 or 3:2 (never square for the blog). **Count:** 5 to 6 images per post, roughly one every 300 words.

---

## 1. The four rules that never bend

1. **The reader is the hero.** The woman in the photo IS the reader: a real mom in her own real kitchen, farm, or market. Not a model in a set. She is usually looking at her task, the light, or another person, rarely straight into the lens. Shannon is only the guide and appears in at most one image per post.
2. **Real, not stock.** True-to-life skin with pores and lines, a different real person in every single image (vary age, ethnicity, body, hair), lived-in homes with honest clutter, dressed windows with curtains, natural window light, legible real labels. If it could be a catalog or an ad, it is wrong.
3. **The set walks fear to free.** The images follow the Story Framework beats in order. Fear lives ONLY at the start (villain and receipt). Every frame after that gets warmer, softer, and calmer than the one before.
4. **End on warmth and hope, never fear.** The last image a reader sees is always the transformation: real food, a calm home, freedom. Never close on the villain, never leave the set cold.

---

## 2. Light and color do the storytelling (not scary props)

You never need a smokestack, a hazmat suit, a green glow, or a warning symbol. The same ordinary kitchen simply feels different under different light. Treat the whole post as one **temperature ramp**:

| Beat | White balance feel | Light quality | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Ordinary world | Cool-neutral (~5200-5600K) | Soft window, slightly flat | Calm, faintly uneasy, low contrast |
| 2 Villain | Coldest (~4800K) | Harder single source, long shadow, low fill | Clinical, tight, desaturated |
| 3 Receipt | Cool-neutral, newspaper-gray | Flat, sober, documentary | The most un-pretty frame in the post |
| 4 Objection defused | Warming (~5000 climbing to 5400K) | Window light returning as ally | Color returns to skin and room |
| 5 Guide (Shannon) | Warm (~5400K) | Warm side light | Relaxed, secondary, never hero light |
| 6 Not alone | Warm interior / golden | Layered ambient, caught moment | Connection, warmth |
| 7 Transformation | Warmest (~5600-6500K), golden | Soft wraparound window light | Rich, saturated-but-natural, hopeful peak |

**Iron rule of the ramp: no later frame may be colder than an earlier one. The arc only thaws.**

Villain dread comes from cinematography (cooler white balance, harder light, longer shadows, less fill, a clinical evenness), safety comes from soft wraparound window light and gentle shadow rolloff. Honest, never gimmicky.

---

## 3. The realism kit (bake into every prompt)

**Light**
- One big soft window as the key, placed 30 to 90 degrees to the SIDE (3/4 back-side), never flat frontal. Flat front light is the number-one AI/stock tell.
- Diffuse harsh sun with a sheer or curtain, then let the opposite side genuinely fall into shadow (negative fill). Real rooms have a dark side; "everything evenly lit" is the sterile studio look Shannon bans.
- Let corners fall into honest shadow. Meter for the highlights. Slightly dim and moody reads as a real morning; bright-and-even reads as an ad set.

**Color grade**
- Warm, earthy, restrained saturation. Greens go sage/olive, whites go cream not paper. Muted earth palette (wood, terracotta, butter, sage, oat).
- Soft filmic contrast with a real black point. Avoid both flat grey mush and the over-sharpened HDR "clarity maxed" crunch. Add subtle grain and a mild natural vignette.

**Composition**
- Vary the angle across the 5 to 6 images: 3/4 overhead (~45 degrees) for a plate/board, straight-down flat-lay for a table or the receipt, eye-level side-on for a loaf's height or rising steam.
- Off-center subject, breathing room, shallow believable depth of field (35/50/85mm feel, one plane sharp, background soft). No edge-to-edge razor focus.
- Shoot from the reader's POV: over-shoulder or first-person looking down at HER hands and HER counter.

**Hands and people**
- Hands mid-action: kneading, dusting flour, tearing bread, pouring, holding a mug, holding a phone. One clear simple action per hand. Real working hands, short unpolished nails, maybe a worn ring or a little flour.
- Candid, often NOT looking at camera. Relaxed shoulders, a genuine half-smile or focused calm, everyday clothes. Sell freedom and ease, never chore-face or fear.
- Engineer variety on purpose: name a concrete distinct person each image ("a 58-year-old Filipina woman with reading glasses and grey roots," "a heavyset Black woman in her 40s with locs," "a freckled redhead in her early 30s with a gap tooth"). Rotate age 30s to 60s, ethnicity, body, hair, and the home itself.

**Skin and camera**
- Prompt for "natural skin texture, visible pores, fine lines, peach fuzz, uneven tone, a few freckles, flyaway hairs, documentary photo, no retouching, shot on 35mm at f2.8." Over-smoothed poreless skin is the loudest AI tell.

**Set dressing**
- Lived-in, not showroom: knife-scarred wood boards, a wrinkled linen towel, a chipped enamel mug, a starter jar, dishes in the rack, a kid's drawing on the fridge, mail on the counter, a fruit bowl with one browning banana. Matte natural surfaces (linen, raw wood, stoneware, cast iron, glass), never glossy chrome or white subway-tile showroom.
- Windows always dressed: curtains, sheers, a linen shade, or a valance, plus a plant on the sill. Never bare glass.

**Labels and food**
- Product labels legible and plausible, or turned partly away / thrown soft with shallow depth. Never leave gibberish type sharp and centered. When credibility matters, generate the scene then composite the real label in after.
- Real food is irregular: dented apples, dirt on carrots, uneven crumb, matte surfaces, real steam. Airbrushed-perfect food reads as "processed," the opposite of the message.

## 4. The anti-AI realism checklist (run before approving any image)

Zoom to 100% and check each. If any fails, regenerate.

- [ ] **Skin** has pores, lines, texture, uneven tone. Not waxy or airbrushed.
- [ ] **Hands** have exactly five fingers, natural knuckles, no fusing or melting into objects.
- [ ] **Faces** are all different people across the post. No repeated "stock mom."
- [ ] **Labels/text** are legible and plausible, or intentionally soft/turned. No melted letters or fake logos sharp in frame.
- [ ] **Light** is directional with a genuinely dark side. Not flat, shadowless, or HDR-haloed.
- [ ] **Windows** are dressed. No bare glass.
- [ ] **Kitchen** is lived-in with real clutter. Not an empty gleaming showroom.
- [ ] **Grade** is warm, earthy, filmic. Not cold all-white, not candy-saturated.
- [ ] **Focus** is shallow with soft falloff. Not edge-to-edge razor sharp.
- [ ] **Appliances/architecture** are physically sane (aligned knobs, one faucet spout, hinges present, windows that tile). Check junctions and reflections.
- [ ] **Food** is matte and irregular. Not shellacked, rubbery, or glass-like.
- [ ] **Emotion** sells calm/freedom (coffee at the window). Not anxious scrubbing or performed cheer.
- [ ] **Expression** is asymmetric and candid, gaze usually off-camera.
- [ ] **No fear-porn**: no hazmat, gas masks, biohazard/skull symbols, neon chemicals, or crying/sick children.
- [ ] **Arc holds**: this frame is not colder than the one before it, and the LAST image is the warmest.

## 5. Beat-by-beat shot map (which photo serves which beat)

Every post is 5 to 6 images. Aim to hit these beats in order; combine 4 and 5 if you only have five shots.

1. **ORDINARY WORLD**: her real kitchen this morning from HER POV (over-shoulder or looking down at the counter). Coffee half-drunk, a cereal bowl, mail, the factory product sitting innocently among normal groceries. Cool-neutral, slightly flat, curtains half-drawn. It must look FINE; the unease is only subtle. 3:2.
2. **THE VILLAIN**: the factory product or ingredient in honest macro on HER counter, never a lab. Colder white balance, harder single light, longer shadow. Legible real label facing camera, or one damning ingredient line sharp in shallow focus. Dread from light and framing, not props. 16:9.
3. **THE RECEIPT**: the proof photographed like evidence: a printed lawsuit/recall/court notice, or the product beside a phone showing an FDA/news page. Flat, cool-neutral, newspaper-gray, top-down flat-lay of facts. Reading glasses, a pen, her hand nearby. The coldest, most un-pretty frame. On-screen text plausible and blurred-but-real. 4:3 or 3:2.
4. **HER OBJECTION DEFUSED**: same woman pausing, leaning on the counter, hand to chin, dawning clarity not panic. Temperature starts warming, color returns to her skin. Real micro-expression (skeptical, thinking, relieved), not a smile-for-camera. Medium shot, shallow depth. 3:2.
5. **THE GUIDE, SHANNON (rare)**: Shannon only here and only as mentor. Environmental and secondary: slightly out of focus behind the reader, or hands-only passing a jar, or beside the woman both looking at the solution together. Warm side light, relaxed mid-50s authenticity. Never centered, never out-lit or out-dressed the reader. 3:2.
6. **NOT ALONE / COMMUNITY**: two to four DIFFERENT real moms together (varied age, ethnicity, body, dress) at an island, a market stall, a porch, or a phone text-thread held in frame. Candid mid-laugh or mid-conversation, hands busy. Warm ambient/golden light, layered depth. This frame carries connection. 3:2.
7. **THE TRANSFORMATION**: end on warmth and freedom: real food from a farm/mill/market (a crate of veg, flour dusting a mill counter, eggs in a bowl, tallow rendering) or the reader relaxed in her calm lived-in home, feet up, kid nearby. Warmest grade, golden, rich natural saturation. The emotional peak. This is always the LAST image. 16:9 or 3:2.

## 6. Prompt-writing recipe (turn an [IMAGE:] brief into a Nano Banana Pro prompt)

Take the brief from the post and expand it into this order. Every prompt gets a realism tail.

**Formula:** `[SPECIFIC PERSON] + [ACTION, candid, gaze] + [HER SPACE, lived-in details] + [DRESSED WINDOW + LIGHT DIRECTION + WHITE BALANCE for this beat] + [GRADE] + [CAMERA] + [REALISM TAIL] + [RATIO]`

**Realism tail (paste into every prompt):**
> natural skin texture, visible pores, fine lines, flyaway hairs, no retouching, documentary photo, single soft window light from the side with real shadow falloff, dressed window with curtains, lived-in kitchen with a little clutter, warm earthy filmic grade, shallow depth of field with soft background, subtle film grain, shot on a 35mm mirrorless lens at f2.8, anatomically correct five-fingered hands, legible real-looking labels

**Worked micro-example (Beat 2, villain):**
> A close macro of a bottle of factory seed oil sitting on a worn butcher-block counter in an ordinary older kitchen, label facing camera and legible, backlit from a curtained window on the left so the oil looks faintly off, hard single-source light, long shadow, cool white balance ~4800K, clinical and tight, desaturated filmic grade, shallow depth of field, subtle grain, no retouching, documentary photo, 16:9

**Do not** write: "beautiful woman, perfect kitchen, bright even lighting, professional studio photo." Every word there is an AI tell.

---

## 7. Delivery rhythm

Within each post vary the shot types so it reads as photographed by a real person over a real morning: one hero (finished food or the mom in her kitchen), one process/hands, one ingredient flat-lay or market/farm, one detail macro, one wide lived-in room, one human moment. Vary the person, the home, the time of day, the crop, and the lens feel across the set. Shannon in at most one frame. Deliver 5 to 6 images at 16:9 or 3:2.
