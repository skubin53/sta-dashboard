## 1. How she actually talks. This is evidence, not opinion.

Built from 36 recordings, 32,225 words of her speaking.

**Her nine-word thesis.** Everything she believes, in the shortest thing she has ever said:

> "Why are we sick? It's not normal. Why are we aging? It's not normal. We're not aging. We are
> slowly being poisoned."

**The mechanics, measured:**

| what | how she does it |
| --- | --- |
| sentence length | median **11 words**, one in five under six |
| her hinge | **"and so"**, 55 times. Where a writer puts "therefore". |
| who she addresses | **you 673, we 415, I 334.** One woman. Never an audience. |
| geography | **"here" 182 times.** "Here" is the new place. "Over there" and "these stores" is the enemy. It is how she names the supplier without naming it. |
| how she prices an ask | **in minutes, never dollars** |
| how she closes | on **meeting the person**. Never the product. Never fear. |

**Five moves a writer would not invent:**

1. Asks a question and answers it herself in the next breath. *"That doesn't sound right, does it? Why do we do that?"*
2. Tiny yes-questions before the ask. *"Do you like saving money?"*
3. Reframes doubt instead of arguing. *"That's not confusion. That's a pattern."*
4. Inverts the risk. *"That's not risk. The real risk is staying with the products you already know are toxic."*
5. Domestic images for abstract things. *"It'll smooth them out like a freshly ironed shirt. Your cellulite will be packing its bags."*

## 2. Hard rules. Breaking one of these is a rewrite, not a note.

- **Never write the letters FDA** except to name a specific recall, fine or lawsuit. Never as an
  authority, never "the FDA has not evaluated this". Their approval is the red flag, not the
  reassurance. Say instead: *nobody is checking that bottle, and the company selling it is the
  only one deciding what goes in it.*
- **Never name the supplier.** Not once, anywhere.
- **Never "MLM", "downline", "house parties", "warm market".**
- **Never name a rival brand as the replacement.** The swap is always described plainly.
- **Never an income claim**, no dollar figure anyone might earn.
- **Never tell anyone to stop or change a prescribed medicine.**
- **No em dashes or en dashes.** Commas, full stops, or the word "to".
- **No P.S. blocks.**
- **Never build a hook on a baby, a high chair, a nursery or a toddler.** About 7% of her readers
  have one. Aim at HER body: tired, joints, hormones, "just your age".
- **Never say "the wellness company".**

## 3. Voice rules learned this week, from her corrections

**Write TO her, never ABOUT her.** The single most common fault.

> Wrong: "So a woman goes in exhausted, and gets told it is her age."
> Right: "So you go to the doctor. You say you are tired all the time. And she tells you it is your age."

**Grade five.** Short sentences, ordinary words, one idea at a time. Past about fifteen words,
cut it in two.

**Name the feeling, not the idea.** Not "low B12 looks like ageing". Write what she feels: worn
out, fuzzy head, hands and feet tingling.

**Never leave a blank.** Every verb needs its place. Not "so you go in". Go in *where*? Say
"so you go to the doctor".

**No stitching phrases.** "Here is why that matters more than it sounds", "it is worth noting",
"the reality is". Nobody says those out loud.

**No jargon of any kind.** Not medical, not marketing. "Your blood work", never "serum levels".
"The end of your gut", never "the terminal ileum".

**When something is bad, say get rid of it.** Do not soften. The one-swap-a-week pacing is about
a whole HOUSE. Never apply it to one item somebody just asked about.

**Ask before you assume.** If the answer depends on which version she has, ask first, not last.

## 4. The two registers. Both are hers. The calendar needs both.

### The villain register, which is what the blog does now

Name a villain, prove it with a recall or a lawsuit, offer the swap. *Is Fabuloso Safe? What Was
Growing Inside 4.9 Million Bottles.*

**This is for ACQUISITION.** A stranger types "is Fabuloso safe" into Google at eleven at night
and this post is the answer. It should not change.

### The gentle register, which she has none of

Study-led, calm, no villain, no selling, no fear. Six beats in about 350 to 1,200 words:

1. One plain sentence of context
2. **The study, named up front.** Journal, month, researchers, university. Never "studies show".
3. What they did, in numbers. How many people, how old, how long.
4. What they found, in the order they found it
5. Why it happens, when the mechanism is known
6. One bolded sentence at the end worth remembering

Then, sometimes, one practical tip. No product, no link.

**This is for RETENTION.** It goes to somebody who already bought and hands her something
interesting so she feels clever for knowing it.

**Print the weakness of your own argument.** Their best move. *"Most of the research was
self-reported at a single point in time, so this is a strong pattern rather than proven cause
and effect."* Saying that is what makes the rest believable.

**Their topic map is seven areas: physical, social, financial, intellectual, mental, spiritual,
environmental. Shannon's blog covers one of them.**

## 5. The gates. Every post must pass all 40.

Run: `python blog-gates.py <slug> --links`

The ones most often missed:
- Title is a question and ends with (2026)
- 60% or more of H2s are questions
- meta_description under 155 characters
- FAQ uses `<p><strong>Question?</strong></p>`, not H3s, 3 to 5 of them
- `.sta-author` block, `.sta-keep` block with 3+ internal links
- Article @graph schema with Article, Person, Organization, BreadcrumbList, plus FAQPage
- **Word count 1,200 to 2,000.** Not 2,500.
- Reading grade 5.5 or below, sentences averaging 11 words or fewer
- 4+ outbound citations, all returning 200
- Shopper posts drive to the scan. **Builder posts drive to the 20 minute Zoom and must never
  mention the scan.**
- First call to action inside the first 20% of the post

## 7. Copy you may not touch, and a cap that contradicts itself

### Three sentences are locked. They are not up for review.

They live in the Shannon's Advice box and repeat on every post, so an edit here repeats
everywhere. `CHECKLIST` gate **A9a** locks the first two and `SHANNONS-ADVICE-SPEC.md` says
Shannon confirmed them and they **must not be quietly softened.**

1. *"I have spent the last 15 years helping women get off prescription medications."*
   Confirmed three times. It is **not** the never-stop-a-medicine rule: that rule is about telling
   THE READER to come off a prescription, and this is Shannon, first person, describing her own
   work. **The not-a-doctor disclaimer is BANNED** alongside it. Never add one.
2. *"They are poison centers meant to do you and your family harm."*
   She removed the "in my opinion" frame herself and that was deliberate.
3. *"The proof is in my blog posts, the recalls, the lawsuits and the fines..."*
   Her shipping copy. Her "never say I have written about it" rule was scoped to the CHAT.

**On 2026-08-26 a voice rule written that morning flagged the first one and it was softened
across 65 files before anybody read the spec.** All reverted. `blog-voice-audit.py` now carries a
`LOCKED_COPY` list and no rule fires inside these sentences. If you think one of them is wrong,
that is a conversation with Shannon, not an edit.

**The one permitted variation:** a BUILDER post drops *"and in the app I built called The Family
Home Toxic Scorecard"* from sentence 3, because a builder post may never point at the home scan.
It keeps every other word, including "my blog posts", and the closing line becomes the twenty
minutes instead of the scan.

### The two word-count rules contradict each other. Here is how to land it.

- The brief asks for **1,200 to 2,000 words of body prose.**
- Gate **A13b caps the WHOLE FILE at 2,000 words.**
- The mandatory furniture, the Advice box, the table, the Proof section, the calls to action, the
  Keep Reading block and the author bio, is **about 870 words before a single sentence of the post
  exists.**

So prose can never reach 1,200 inside a 2,000 word file. Both numbers cannot be satisfied.

**Until Shannon moves one of them, A13b wins, because it is the gate that blocks publication and it
carries her date on it.** Aim for **1,900 to 1,990 total**, which lands around 1,100 to 1,200 words
of real prose. Every post in the queue sits there. Do not pad to hit 1,200 prose and do not cut a
receipt to hit 2,000 total. If the argument genuinely needs more room, say so rather than
quietly trimming the proof.
