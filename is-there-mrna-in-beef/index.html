Is There mRNA in Beef?

What beef groups say about mRNA vaccines for cattle, as of October 2026. Plus the beef with a no mRNA promise in writing.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years. Published October 22, 2026. Facts as of October 2026.

Short answer: beef and farm groups say no mRNA vaccine is licensed for US beef cattle. Riverbend Ranch goes one step further. It puts a promise in writing: its Black Label cattle "Never receive any mRNA vaccines."

The question is not about the mRNA in every living cell. It is about mRNA vaccines.

What does "mRNA in beef" really mean?

Quick answer: people mean mRNA vaccines.

mRNA is short for messenger RNA. Tennessee Farm Bureau says messenger RNA "occurs naturally in all cells." So when people ask about mRNA in beef, they mean one thing. Were the cattle given an mRNA vaccine?

Are mRNA vaccines used in US beef cattle today?

Quick answer: beef and farm groups say no mRNA vaccine is licensed for US beef cattle.

  • NCBA, the National Cattlemen's Beef Association, in April 2023: "There are no current mRNA vaccines licensed for use in beef cattle in the United States."
  • The Idaho Beef Council, on a page with no date: "there are currently no mRNA vaccines licensed for use in Beef Cattle."
  • Tennessee Farm Bureau, in a July 2026 paper: "Currently, none of the commonly used vaccines licensed by USDA for livestock utilize mRNA technology."

NCBA, the Idaho Beef Council and Tennessee Farm Bureau are beef and farm groups. So this is what the industry itself says.

Cattle do get other vaccines. NCBA said in 2023 that none of them used mRNA.

Sources: NCBA statement on National Beef Wire, April 2023; Idaho Beef Council, mRNA Vaccines in Beef Cattle (no date shown); Tennessee Farm Bureau, 2026 Policy Development: mRNA Vaccines, July 2026. All checked October 22, 2026.

Who decides which vaccines cattle can get?

Quick answer: USDA. A new vaccine for cattle needs USDA's OK first.

Tennessee Farm Bureau says mRNA vaccines need "significant review and approval from USDA" first. The group also says the USDA Center for Veterinary Biologics oversees that licensing.

Research is going on. Tennessee Farm Bureau says research into mRNA vaccines for farm animals "has been ongoing for more than a decade."

Source: Tennessee Farm Bureau, 2026 Policy Development: mRNA Vaccines, July 2026.

Could mRNA vaccines be used on cattle later?

Quick answer: yes, one day. That is why this page has a date on it.

I checked every source on this page on October 22, 2026. Research keeps going, so a new vaccine could be licensed later.

Would a label tell you? Maybe not. Tennessee Farm Bureau's policy says food made with approved farm technologies should not have to name them on the label. That is why a promise in writing matters.

Source: Tennessee Farm Bureau, 2026 Policy Development: mRNA Vaccines, July 2026, food safety policy section.
