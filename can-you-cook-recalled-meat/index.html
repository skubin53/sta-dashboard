Can You Cook Recalled Meat to Make It Safe?

What "temperature abuse" means in a meat recall, why heat in your own kitchen is not the fix, who to call, and what to do instead.

What does "temperature abuse" mean in a meat recall?

Quick answer: it means the meat may have been kept too warm while it was stored, before it ever got to you.

On October 17, 2026, the USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) said Kayem Foods, of Chelsea, Massachusetts, is recalling about 1,584,361 pounds of ready-to-eat meat and poultry. The reason, in the agency's words: the products "may have experienced temperature abuse during storage."

FSIS said the company looked into it and found that it had "stored various RTE meat and poultry products in a warehouse cooler that experienced temperature deviations." RTE means ready-to-eat.

Here is how it came to light. FSIS says the company got a complaint from a store about warm meat at delivery on October 1, 2026.

Kayem's own notice, dated October 16, 2026, says the cause was "a cooling system malfunction" at one of its warehouses. FSIS says the meat was made from about March 31 to October 7, 2026, and shipped to stores and food service places nationwide.

How serious is a Class I recall?

FSIS calls this a Class I recall. It defines Class I as "a health hazard situation where there is a reasonable probability that the use of the product will cause serious, adverse health consequences or death."

Here is the other side, straight. FSIS also says "There have been no confirmed reports of illness due to consumption of these products." Kayem's CEO, Matt Monkiewicz, called it a voluntary recall made "out of an abundance of caution." He said, "We have learned from and corrected the issue and will not allow this to happen again."

Sources: USDA FSIS, Kayem Foods Inc. Recalls Meat and Poultry Products Due to Possible Temperature Abuse, October 17, 2026; Kayem Foods, Voluntary Recall Information, October 16, 2026.

Can I cook meat recalled for temperature abuse to make it safe?

Quick answer: no. The USDA did not say to cook it. It said not to eat it.

FSIS put it plainly. "Consumers who have purchased these products are urged not to consume them. These products should be thrown away or returned to the place of purchase."

So why not just cook it hot? Two general food safety facts from the CDC help show why heat is not a sure fix:

  • Germs grow fast when food is too warm. The CDC says "Bacteria can multiply rapidly if left at room temperature or in the 'Danger Zone' between 40°F and 140°F."
  • Some germs leave something behind that heat does not undo. The CDC says staph "can multiply in contaminated food and make a toxin that causes food poisoning." It adds, "Even though cooking can kill Staph, it does not destroy the toxin in the food."

To be clear, the Kayem recall does not name any germ. Neither the USDA nor Kayem has said any Kayem product had staph or any other germ in it. These are general facts about food kept too warm. They are not a finding about Kayem.

Why does it matter that this meat is ready-to-eat?

Kayem calls these "fully cooked" products. You warm them, or you eat them cold. Kayem's own list of recalled items includes hams and turkey breasts, the kind of meat many of us slice and serve cold.

The CDC says "Foods that are not cooked after handling are especially risky if contaminated with Staph." It names sliced meats among them.

Does freezing or reheating it take it off the list?

No. The recall is about how the meat was stored before it reached you. Nothing you do in your kitchen changes that. The USDA's word is the same for every pack on the list: do not eat it.

Sources: USDA FSIS, recall notice, October 17, 2026; CDC, Preventing Food Poisoning, November 24, 2025; CDC, About Staph Food Poisoning; Kayem Foods, list of recalled items.

What if I already ate some?

Quick answer: the USDA's notice says there have been no confirmed reports of illness. If you are worried, call your healthcare provider.

FSIS says "Anyone concerned about an illness should contact a healthcare provider." Then deal with what is left. Throw it out, or take it back to where you bought it.

Who can I call about a recalled pack?

  • Kayem Foods Customer Service: 800-426-6100, Monday to Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. Eastern.
  • USDA Meat and Poultry Hotline: 888-674-6854 (888-MPHotline), for food safety questions.

What should I do instead?

Quick answer: make the chain from the farm to your freezer short.

This meat went from a plant, to a warehouse cooler, to a truck, to a store, to your fridge or freezer. Kayem says the problem was a cooler in its own warehouse. FSIS says a store complained when warm meat showed up at delivery. Nobody in that long line ever had to answer to you by name.

So make the line short:

  1. Find one farm near you that raises the meat your family eats.
  2. Get to know the farmer by name. Ask how the animals are raised.
  3. Ask how the meat stays cold until you pick it up.
  4. Buy a few packs and freeze them yourself. Then you know where it has been.

A farm can get it wrong too. But you can call the farmer and ask.
