Are Dryer Sheets Bad for You?

What is really in a dryer sheet, what a study found in the air from dryer vents, and the simple swap that costs less over time.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping over 100 women switch to safer, American made home products.

Dryer sheets and liquid fabric softener are not worth panicking over, but they work by leaving a scented, waxy film on your clothes, and that film is mostly added fragrance plus a softening chemical called a quat. For most people the real issues are skin irritation, indoor air, and towels that slowly stop absorbing, not a proven disease. Here is the honest version, and the swap that is both safer and cheaper.

What is actually in a dryer sheet?

Two things do the work. The first is a fabric-softening quaternary ammonium compound, a "quat," which is the waxy coating that makes fabric feel soft. It is the same buildup you scrape off your lint screen. The second is added "fragrance," a single word that can legally stand in for dozens of undisclosed ingredients. Liquid fabric softener works the same way. The soft feel and the clean-laundry smell are a residue left behind on purpose, not a sign the clothes came out any cleaner.

The clean-laundry smell is not cleanliness. It is a coating of added fragrance the label never has to name.

Are the chemicals in dryer sheets dangerous?

For a healthy adult using them now and then, this is a low-stakes choice, not an emergency. The honest concern is fragrance and indoor air, especially for anyone with asthma or sensitive lungs. A University of Washington study published in the journal Air Quality, Atmosphere and Health tested the air coming out of dryer vents while fragranced laundry products ran, and found more than 25 volatile organic compounds venting outside. Seven of them are classified as hazardous air pollutants, and two of those, acetaldehyde and benzene, are carcinogens with no established safe level of exposure. The researchers noted that these vent emissions are essentially unregulated.

That is not proof a dryer sheet will harm you. It is a solid reason to skip the added fragrance where you can, and doing so costs you nothing.

Source: Steinemann et al., "Chemical emissions from residential dryer vents during use of fragranced laundry products," Air Quality, Atmosphere & Health (2011). Read the study.

Do dryer sheets ruin towels and affect safety?

The same coating that softens fabric also repels water, so towels feel plush but dry you less well the longer you use softener. On some fabrics that residue can reduce flame resistance too, which is why the care tags on children's sleepwear often say not to use fabric softener. If your towels have gone from thirsty to barely there, the softener is usually the reason, and it reverses after a few washes without it.

What should I use instead?

The simplest swap is wool dryer balls. They soften and cut static with no coating and no fragrance, they shorten drying time, and one set of American made wool balls lasts for years and replaces hundreds of single-use sheets, so the safer option is also the cheaper one. For static-prone loads, taking things out while barely damp helps as well.

Where to start

Laundry is one of the easiest first swaps because you use it every week and the whole family wears the result. You do not need to throw anything out today. Change the one product you run most, see how it feels, and go from there.
