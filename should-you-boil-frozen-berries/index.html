Do You Need to Boil Frozen Berries Before You Eat Them?

What Ireland's food safety agency says to do with imported frozen berries, and why. Plus what to do with a recalled bag, and a simple way to know where your berries came from.

By Shannon Nicole, founder of Switch to America. Seven years helping women switch to a toxin-free home, and a health coach for over 20 years. Published October 17, 2026.

Do you need to boil frozen berries?

Quick answer: Ireland's food safety agency says to boil imported frozen berries for one minute.

You shake them over yogurt. You drop them in a smoothie. You never cook them. That is the whole issue.

The Food Safety Authority of Ireland, called the FSAI, "recommends boiling imported frozen berries for one minute before consumption." It gives the reason plainly. There have been outbreaks of norovirus and hepatitis A virus in imported frozen berries across Europe.

This is not new. The FSAI first gave the advice in 2013. That year, a hepatitis A outbreak in Ireland was linked to imported frozen berries. It "turned out to be part of a multi-state outbreak, with over 1,000 cases reported in 12 EU countries."

It has kept happening. Between 2014 and March 19, 2025, the FSAI counts 9 alerts across Europe for hepatitis A and 52 alerts for norovirus in imported frozen berries.

Why frozen berries? The FSAI says more outbreaks in Europe have been linked to imported frozen berries than to other berries. And it says freezing "preserves viruses such as norovirus and hepatitis A virus." The cold does not destroy them.

Source: Food Safety Authority of Ireland, Advice to boil imported frozen berries.

How does a virus get into a bag of frozen berries?

Quick answer: it can happen on the farm or on the packing line, the FSAI says.

The FSAI names a few ways it could happen. Farm water that has sewage in it. Workers who are sick. Machines and surfaces that touch the berries while they are frozen, mixed and packed.

Virus outbreaks tied to frozen berries have happened in the US, too. In 2023, the FDA and CDC looked into a hepatitis A outbreak linked to frozen organic strawberries imported from Baja California, Mexico. Ten people got sick in four states. Four went to the hospital. No deaths were reported.

Sources: Food Safety Authority of Ireland, Advice to boil imported frozen berries; FDA, Outbreak Investigation of Hepatitis A Virus Infections: Frozen Strawberries (February 2023).

Is a quick warm-up enough?

Quick answer: no. The advice is a full boil for one full minute.

The FSAI says boiling for one minute "will destroy viruses, if present."

Warm is not the same as boiling. The CDC says noroviruses "can survive temperatures as high as 145°F." It also says quick steaming "will not heat foods enough to kill noroviruses."

So a few seconds in the microwave to thaw the berries does not match the advice. Bring them to a boil. Then keep them boiling for one minute.

Sources: Food Safety Authority of Ireland, Advice to boil imported frozen berries; CDC, How to Prevent Norovirus.

Will boiling fix a bag from the 2026 US berry recall?

Quick answer: do not try. Throw it out or take it back.

The 2026 US recall was not about a virus. It was about E. coli O145:H28. Ireland's boil advice was written for viruses. The FDA's advice for the recalled lots is to throw them out or return them for a refund.

Here is what the FDA's outbreak page says, as updated October 5, 2026:

  • The CDC declared the outbreak over on September 21, 2026. It counted 17 people sick in two states, six in the hospital, and no deaths reported.
  • On October 2, 2026, the supplier, Frutas y Hortalizas del Sur S.A., of San Carlos, Chile, expanded its recall. It added more lots of Great Value frozen organic berries and one lot of Trader Joe's frozen organic mixed berries.
  • The FDA says that expansion "follows new information obtained by FDA investigators during an onsite foreign inspection."
  • The FDA also says that during the outbreak investigation, "the firm did not provide all records to FDA that the Agency had asked the firm to voluntarily provide."

Only the listed lots are recalled. That tells you which bags to toss. It does not tell you how any other bag was grown or packed.

Which berries does Ireland's advice not cover?

Quick answer: fresh berries, and berries you grow and freeze yourself.

The FSAI says its advice is for imported frozen berries only. It does not cover fresh berries. It "does not apply to berries grown in your own garden and frozen after picking."

How do I know if my frozen berries are imported?

The FSAI says that if the bag does not state the country of origin, you should assume the berries are imported. The store may be able to tell you.

In the US, the USDA's country of origin rule covers fresh and frozen fruits. But the rule treats a mix of foods as a "processed food item." It lists "fruit medley" as an example of an item it leaves out. A mixed berry blend is a mix of fruits.

Sources: Food Safety Authority of Ireland, Advice to boil imported frozen berries; USDA Agricultural Marketing Service, Country of Origin Labeling (COOL); 7 CFR 65.220.

What should I do instead?

Quick answer: make the chain from the field to your freezer short, so you know where your berries came from.

  1. Grow a few berries of your own. A couple of blueberry bushes or a strawberry patch is a good start. Pick them, freeze them, and you know exactly where they came from.
  2. No garden? Find a farm near you. Go to a pick-your-own field or a farm stand in season. Ask the farmer how they grow and pick them.
  3. Freeze them yourself. Rinse the berries and pat them dry. Spread them on a sheet pan and freeze. Then pour them into a bag.
  4. Already have an imported bag? If you want to follow Ireland's advice, boil the berries for one minute. They turn into a warm sauce for oatmeal or yogurt.
  5. Is a bag on the 2026 recall list? Throw it out or take it back. Do not cook it to save it.
