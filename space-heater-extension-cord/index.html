Can I Plug a Space Heater Into an Extension Cord?

What the federal fire agency says about cords, power strips and wall outlets, why it matters, and the one check to do before the first cold night.

Can I plug a space heater into an extension cord?

Quick answer: no. The fire agency says the wall outlet, every time.

It is a cold night. The outlet by the bed is behind the dresser. So you grab the extension cord from the junk drawer.

That is the one thing the US Fire Administration says not to do. Its space heater tips say: "Plug space heaters directly into wall outlets and never into an extension cord or power strip."

The same agency gives a reason on its appliance page. That page is about major appliances, like stoves and dryers. It says to plug them straight into a wall outlet, and it warns, "The extension cord can overheat and start a fire." Its space heater tips give a space heater the same wall outlet rule.

Sources: US Fire Administration, Space Heater Safety Tips, page last reviewed Oct. 4, 2021; US Fire Administration, Appliance and electrical fire safety, page last reviewed May 4, 2023.

Is a power strip any better?

No. The fire agency names the power strip in the same line as the extension cord. A strip with a lot of outlets is still not the wall.

Why does the fire agency care so much about space heaters?

Quick answer: heater fires are a small share of heating fires, but a big share of the deadly ones.

Here are the agency's own words: "Home portable heater fires were only 3% of all home heating fires annually for 2017-2019, but they accounted for 41% of fatal heating fires in homes."

So a small share of heating fires. And a big share of the ones where someone died.

Source: US Fire Administration, Heating fire safety, page last reviewed May 4, 2023.

What should I check before the first cold night?

Quick answer: unplug it, then look at the cord and the plug.

  1. Unplug the heater. Do the check with it cold and unplugged.
  2. Look at the cord. The agency says to inspect for "cracked or damaged cords and broken plugs."
  3. If you find damage, stop using it. For any appliance with "a cracked, damaged or loose cord," the agency says to replace the appliance.
  4. Check the outlet. The agency says to replace wall outlets if plugs do not fit snugly.
  5. Keep the cord out from under the rug. The agency says to avoid putting cords where they can be "damaged or pinched, like under a carpet or rug."

Sources: US Fire Administration, Space Heater Safety Tips, page last reviewed Oct. 4, 2021; US Fire Administration, Appliance and electrical fire safety, page last reviewed May 4, 2023.

What can I do instead?

Quick answer: move the heater to the wall, or skip the heater by the bed.

  • Move the heater, not the cord. Find a wall outlet with open floor around it. The agency says to keep anything that can burn at least 3 feet from space heaters.
  • Turn it off at bedtime. The agency says, "Turn space heaters off when leaving the room or going to bed."
  • Warm yourself, not the room. Wrap up in a plain wool throw, or lay one more heavy blanket on the bed. There is no cord and no plug.

Common questions about space heaters and extension cords

Can I plug a space heater into an extension cord or a power strip?

No. The US Fire Administration says, "Plug space heaters directly into wall outlets and never into an extension cord or power strip." If the outlet is hard to reach, move the heater to a wall outlet with clear space around it.

What if my space heater cord is cracked or the plug is broken?

Do not use it. The US Fire Administration says to inspect your space heater for cracked or damaged cords and broken plugs. For any appliance with a cracked, damaged or loose cord, it says to replace the appliance.

Can I leave a space heater on when I go to bed?

The US Fire Administration says, "Turn space heaters off when leaving the room or going to bed." A plain wool throw or one more heavy blanket keeps you warm with no cord at all.

How dangerous are space heater fires?

The US Fire Administration says, "Home portable heater fires were only 3% of all home heating fires annually for 2017-2019, but they accounted for 41% of fatal heating fires in homes."

Sources for these answers: US Fire Administration, Space Heater Safety Tips; US Fire Administration, Appliance and electrical fire safety; US Fire Administration, Heating fire safety.

What can I do this week?

Walk to each room where you use a heater. Unplug it. Look at the cord and the plug. Then put it on a wall outlet with clear space around it, and put the extension cord back in the drawer.
