What "Product of USA" Really Means on Beef

The label used to have a loophole. Here is what it means now, and how to know your beef was actually born and raised in America.

If you buy beef because you want to support American ranchers, the label matters. For years "Product of USA" could appear on beef from cattle raised in other countries, as long as the meat passed through a US plant. A new USDA rule closes that loophole. Here is the plain version.

What does "Product of USA" mean now?

Quick answer: since January 1, 2026, the "Product of USA" label can only go on meat from animals born, raised, slaughtered and processed in the United States. All four steps happen here. USDA made the rule in March 2024.

What did "Product of USA" used to mean?

Quick answer: much less than shoppers assumed. Under the old rule, imported meat could say "Product of USA" if it was only repacked or lightly processed here. So the label did not tell you the animal was American.

Is there imported beef in my ground beef?

Quick answer: there may be, and the label may not tell you. You have a right to know where your beef came from.

This fall the US is letting in more imported lean beef at a low import tax. On August 26, 2026, the President signed a proclamation that adds 300,000 metric tons this year. That is about 660 million pounds of lean beef trimmings, the kind that gets mixed into ground beef. The extra beef comes in three batches of 100,000 metric tons. The first batch ran in September. The second started October 1. The third is set for October 31 to November 30. US Customs lists the third batch's first day as Monday, November 2.

Where does imported beef go? USDA says most of the beef the US imports is blended into ground beef.

Here is the catch. Since December 2015, sellers no longer have to tell you what country beef came from. Imported beef that comes already packed for shoppers must still name its country. But when imported beef is ground or repacked in a US plant, the label does not have to name the other country.

The new rule helps a little. Since January 1, 2026, imported beef cannot carry the words "Product of USA," even when it is ground here. But no company has to use that label. A pack without it may not tell you where the cattle were raised.

How do you know your beef is actually American?

Quick answer: look for a promise in writing, not a slogan. "Product of USA" now means born, raised, slaughtered and processed here. But no company has to use that label. The clearest answer is beef from a ranch that tells you in writing where its cattle were raised.
